# MEDIAN — *The Three Story Colonies*  
## Codex evaluation and inline markup

**Source reviewed:** `story_colonies.md`  
**Canon checked against:** `MEDIAN_GDD_v0.4.5.md`  
**Method:** Claude’s complete original text appears below, unchanged. My notes are inserted between passages and always begin with **CODEX NOTE**.

### Note key

- **KEEP** — already strong; protect this feature in revision.
- **VOICE** — prose/register observation rather than a canon problem.
- **CLARIFY** — meaning or presentation needs sharpening.
- **CANON** — conflicts with, or inaccurately states, current v0.4.5.
- **DECISION** — attractive new material that would settle something currently open or extend canon; approve deliberately rather than allowing it to enter by implication.
- **STRUCTURE** — concerns the function or placement of the material in the eventual book.

### Overall evaluation

This is a very good first pass. More importantly, it proves that the three-story-colony device works. The colonies do not merely illustrate species bonuses: each turns its signature system into a civic temperament and its founding loss into a different mode of memory. New Elderbank remembers as contested story, Morningside as unresolved argument, and Rushbottom as enumerated record. That is the draft’s central achievement and should survive intact.

Its weakness is not lack of invention but over-completion. The highly regular casting—three founders, later citizens, one perfectly matched Guest, four Tier snapshots, one Saying, three named things—sometimes reveals the design matrix beneath the fiction. A second pass should introduce slight asymmetry rather than more content. Let one colony lack a category, let another preserve something that does not fit the system cleanly, and let at least one mechanically “wrong” choice remain part of its identity.

There are also several substantive canon issues. The squirrel and mouse Founding Escapes diverge from the universal campaign opening; Morningside reaches the Rest-Stop Metropolis too early; New Elderbank’s Far-hole exceeds plausible Reach geometry; Rushbottom attributes universal citizen naming to itself; and the mouse births quietly settle the explicitly open body-unit growth question. The draft also turns imperfect oral inheritance into a discoverable objectively correct text, which weakens one of MEDIAN’s richest ambiguities.

With those issues repaired, this is close to the right register for the eventual sourcebook: specific, humane, melancholy without being continuously mournful, and capable of explaining systems through remembered lives.

# The Three Story Colonies — first sketch

**Nothing here is settled.** A proposal to deep-read, cut into, or replace. What *is* settled is the structure: three colonies, one per core species, each taking one of the three colony-naming registers (§23.3).

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / STRUCTURE:** Correctly frames the pass as authored content rather than smuggled canon. The three-colony/three-register structure is canonical and productive. For publication, remove this workshop preface; for development, retain it.

## A note on terminology

**Character** is designer-facing. **Citizen** is what the game says.

Sharpnose is a *Citizen* to a player who has embodied New Elderbank — one of the animals they know by name. To us, Sharpnose is a *Character*: a piece of authored content that recurs through the book, carries examples, and has a fate we decide.

This is the same split canon already runs at §23.1 — *the designer-facing term for this field is the agnomen; After-name is what the game says.* Worth stating once in the appendix guide so the two words stop being used interchangeably, and worth remembering when writing: the book says **Citizen**, and only our own notes say **Character**.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY / STRUCTURE:** Useful house rule, but it does not need to become reader-facing doctrine. *Agnomen / After-name* distinguishes a technical authoring label from an in-world term for the same field. *Character / Citizen* is looser: “character” can still be ordinary critical language in a sourcebook (“three recurring characters”) without breaking the fiction. Keep this in the authoring guide if it prevents drift; do not spend appendix space teaching the reader not to use a normal word.

## The three

Every colony has **two names** — the one its founders authored, and the one the corridor has always used for the ground. That rule is canon (§7.2) and has never been demonstrated. These demonstrate it.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** The underlying distinction is exactly right, but technically the *colony* does not have two names. The colony and its Home Median are two different things, each with one name. Suggested conceptual wording: “Every story colony stands in a Reach with a different name—the name its founders chose for their community, and the older name the corridor gives the ground.”

| Species | Colony | Reach | Biome | Register | Lost home |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rabbit** | **New Elderbank** | Ashcross | Wooded Median | **Tribute** | the Ancestral Warren, **Elderbank** |
| **Squirrel** | **Morningside** | Elmwater | Creek Split | **Aspirational** | the Ancestral Den, **Nine Beeches** |
| **Wood Mouse** | **Rushbottom** | Pipe End | Culvert Garden | **Grounded** | the Ancestral Burrow, **Chaff End** |

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** The name set is excellent. Each pair makes colony/ground legible at a glance, the three colony registers are unmistakable without labels, and the biomes reinforce rather than merely decorate species identity. *New Elderbank / Ashcross* is the strongest pair; *Rushbottom / Pipe End* is the most efficient demonstration of the naming distinction.

**New Elderbank, on Ashcross.** The founders named the new place for the old one in the plainest way displaced people ever do. Ashcross is named for an old ash standing where a service crossover breaks the median — so the Reach is named for a boundary and the colony for a memory.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** This is sourcebook voice at its best: one physical fact, one cultural fact, and the relationship between them.

**Morningside, on Elmwater.** Named by squirrels who build upward, for the side of the wood that takes the light first. Elmwater is a Creek Split: two channels, elms along both, and a colony whose central problem is getting caches across moving water.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** Strong image, but “getting caches across moving water” can sound as though the Cache Network is a hauling puzzle rather than the convenience-versus-resilience system. Consider making the water *force* the colony’s central argument: one secure bank or redundant stores on both.

**Rushbottom, on Pipe End.** The mice named the plants; the corridor named the concrete. Wet, rich, prone to flooding, with the culvert mouth as its Anchor Point.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** “The mice named the plants; the corridor named the concrete” is compact, memorable, and fully MEDIAN. Preserve verbatim if possible.

# I · NEW ELDERBANK

*Rabbit. Tribute. The colony that carries the full Founding Escape.*

Six citizens, all of them already in canon. **Bramble, Twig, Sharpnose, Nutmeg, Daisy** and **Fennel** appear in §18.1's bond examples and §22.1's event feed, and nobody has ever decided who they are. They arrive with three bonds already written:

> *Bramble and Twig — best friends*
> *Sharpnose and Nutmeg — partners in crime*
> *Daisy and Fennel — inseparable since the culvert*

Adopting them costs nothing and makes every worked example in the book land in one place.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / CLARIFY:** This is exactly the right consolidation move. Strictly, the six *names and three displayed bonds* are already in canon; the citizens as biographies are not. That distinction matters only in the working draft, where “all of them already in canon” can make later inventions look pre-approved.

> **CODEX NOTE — STRUCTURE:** Concentrating existing examples in one colony will give the book continuity, but do not let New Elderbank absorb every rabbit example by default. The story colonies should recur, not make the wider world feel as though it contains only seventeen people.

## The founding trio

Adolescents on the last morning at Elderbank — not the children being drilled in the nursery, which is why they were out at the peripheral store when the machines came.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** Fully aligned with §35.1. The only missing element is the player’s modified Choice-Event Card—the responsible or naughty free-afternoon choice that places the trio at the outskirts. The colony treatment need not reproduce the tutorial specification, but the eventual full Founding Escape narrative must embody that choice rather than explain their location as fixed backstory.

**Bramble.** The one who said what to do next, and kept saying it for four days. Not the bravest of the three; the one who would not stop moving. Became the colony's first Teacher because somebody had to, and has been teaching the Laws slightly wrong ever since without knowing it.
*Keepsake:* a knotted bootlace, used at Elderbank to measure tunnel runs.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Excellent character construction. “Not the bravest… the one who would not stop moving” gives leadership without heroic inflation. The bootlace binds personality, old material culture, and Warren Flow in one object.

**Twig.** The smallest, and the one who remembers the old warren best — can describe rooms the others cannot picture, including some that may never have existed. Bramble's Hearth-mate. Daisy's parent.
*Keepsake:* a scrap of blue tarp, carried out in the teeth.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / CLARIFY:** The unreliable rooms are superb and should be protected; they make imperfect memory emotional rather than merely instructional. “Daisy’s parent,” however, makes Bramble’s relationship to Daisy oddly ambiguous when Daisy is later born of Bramble and Twig’s Hearth. Canon records descent as “born of [Hearth],” not individual biological parentage. “Daisy was born of their Hearth” would align more cleanly and avoid inviting a genetic reading the GDD explicitly refuses.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** *Hearth-mate* is a good new in-world compound, but it is not currently in the canonical vocabulary. Approve and add it to the hyphen-compound bank, or use “member of Bramble’s Hearth.”

**Sharpnose.** Was mockingly reciting the Second Law — *the mowing is fire* — to needle the others about having to memorise it, and then it was true. Has not repeated a Saying since. The colony's first real expedition citizen, and its most reckless.
*Keepsake:* none. Refused to carry anything out.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / VOICE:** This is the sharpest capsule biography in the set. “Keepsake: none” makes absence function as an object without sentimentality. “Expedition citizen” is mechanically intelligible but sounds like document vocabulary; “scout,” “pathfinder,” or simply “the first to volunteer for the road” would keep the same fact inside the world.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** Sharpnose mocked a **Law**, then refuses to repeat a **Saying**. If the distinction is intentional—she rejects all formal recitation—make that breadth explicit. Otherwise use “Law.”

## The three who came after

**Nutmeg.** Arrived years into the campaign, and was one of the children being drilled in the nursery on the last morning — which the trio had believed impossible. Remembers the Laws **exactly**, which is how New Elderbank discovered it had been teaching them wrong. Bonded to Sharpnose: the one who mocked the lesson and the one who was actually listening.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / CANON:** Nutmeg is an excellent realization of the canonical “flagship” wanderer—another survivor who disproves the trio’s certainty and carries a disputed Law. Her pairing with Sharpnose is elegant and earned.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** “Remembers the Laws exactly” closes a much larger question than it appears to. Current canon makes the colony’s Laws imperfect and lets the Metropolis hold contradictions, “some” of which may be corrections. Giving one child perfect recall creates an objective master text and risks turning folklore into patch notes. More MEDIAN-like: Nutmeg remembers a different version with fierce certainty; one or two corrections are persuasive, while others remain contested. That preserves the argument without making Bramble simply wrong.

**Daisy.** The first citizen born at New Elderbank, of Bramble and Twig's Hearth. Has never seen Elderbank and does not miss it, which is its own kind of loss and the reason the colony's name means less to her than to anyone else in it.

> **CODEX NOTE — VOICE:** Strong generational function, but “does not miss it, which is its own kind of loss” tells the reader how to mourn on Daisy’s behalf. She has not lost Elderbank; she has inherited other people’s loss. That is more interesting and less coercive. Her relationship to the name could be distance, impatience, or the conviction that “New Elderbank” now means the only home she has known.

**Fennel.** Found half-drowned in a culvert on a Margin raid, alone, with a story nobody has been able to verify. Daisy was on the party that brought him in.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON / CLARIFY:** This gives the canonical bond phrase “inseparable since the culvert” a real event. However, §19.1 already marks a created visual titled **“Fennel, after the culvert”** to demonstrate a permanent visible change. “Half-drowned” alone does not account for that. Either give Fennel the lasting impairment the image marker implies, or revise the marker so the book does not promise a transformation the colony story omits.

> **CODEX NOTE — VOICE:** “Margin raid” is slightly martial in a setting careful about weaponization. The GDD does use “Margin raid,” so this is not a canon error; it is worth deciding whether the sourcebook’s narrative register prefers “forage,” “run,” or “expedition.”

**Guest — WHOOT.** A barred owl with a permanently weak wing, who can no longer hunt on the move and has become a stationary watcher instead (§21.2). Canon's one named Guest; he lives here.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Clean adoption of the existing template Guest. Assigning WHOOT to a specific story colony turns an abstract roster entry into a relationship and a place.

## The four tiers

**Tier I — Scavenger Camp.** Three adolescents in a scrape under the ash, eating what the verge gives and sleeping in shifts. Bramble measures everything with the bootlace. The first crossing to the Margin takes nine days to work up to. Nothing is named yet except the colony, and the colony is named for a place that no longer exists.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** Ashcross already has an older world-given name, but the trio may not know it yet. “They know no name for the ground yet” preserves the intended isolation while respecting §7.2. Also, colony naming occurs with the first civic structure and welcome sign during the tutorial; make sure “except the colony” occurs after that beat rather than at the instant of arrival.

**Tier II — Fortified Settlement.** The warren has four ways out and Bramble is still digging a fifth. Daisy is born; the Nursery is the first structure built for someone who does not exist yet. Sharpnose scouts the first Reach downcorridor and comes back with a folk name for it and a limp. WHOOT arrives and stops being a threat halfway through the second night.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** “The Nursery is the first structure built for someone who does not exist yet” is outstanding. It explains growth, civic confidence, and attachment-first construction in one sentence.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** WHOOT’s transition from threat to citizen is enticing but currently skips the decisive act. Because the Owl is an Ambient Guest, not an encounter escort, “halfway through the second night” could become a miniature story of mutual observation rather than a generic recruitment beat.

**Tier III — Independent Colony.** Two Reaches held. Nutmeg arrives and the Story Circle stops being a place where the Laws are recited and becomes a place where they are argued about. Fennel is brought in from the culvert. The colony can feed itself through a winter without a single Margin raid, and chooses to raid anyway.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** The Story Circle changing function is precisely how a Tier advance should read: not a bigger number, but an institution becoming culturally complex.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** If the colony is food-independent, its reason for returning to the Margin is probably scrap, artifacts, intelligence, or choice rather than sustenance. Naming that desire would make “chooses to raid anyway” less like irrational risk and more like the expedition loop continuing after basic survival.

**Tier IV — Sovereign Network.** A chain of outposts reaching most of the way to the rest stop, and a Warren with more exits than anyone can hold in their head at once — including the Far-hole, which comes up in a hedge two Reaches away and which only four citizens have ever used. Bramble is old and still Teacher. The Laws are taught correctly now, and something has been lost in that.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** The Far-hole cannot plausibly surface two Reaches away. Reaches are separated by service crossovers—open pavement and exposure—and each Reach is a unified geographic segment. Warren Flow’s “distant” exit is visible in the Home Reach’s Field layer; it does not imply a multi-Reach subterranean megaproject. Put the Far-hole at the distant end of Ashcross, perhaps in a hedge near the service crossover.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** “The Laws are taught correctly now” inherits the objective-text problem from Nutmeg. The loss is more haunting if the colony teaches an agreed version while knowing agreement is not certainty. That would retain the intended elegy—standardization costs something—without solving folklore.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** “Most of the way to the rest stop” is correct Tier-IV preparation if this is the Rest-Stop Metropolis, but use the canonical capitalization when it is the named destination.

## From the Chronicle

*The Remembering, New Elderbank.*

> **The First Day.** *They came to the ash at evening, and they were three, and they had been three for four days. Bramble said: the air smells good here. And they stopped.*

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** This is the strongest Chronicle excerpt. Its repetition has oral weight without becoming faux-archaic, and it makes the canonical line feel discovered rather than assigned.

**First Saying:** *The air smells good here.* Entered the Chronicle at the moment the Records began, and is said at New Elderbank whenever anything is settled — a building finished, an argument ended, a citizen come home.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Excellent cultural afterlife for the line. “Whenever anything is settled” also turns an arrival sentence into a colony-specific civic formula.

**Places that earned a name.**

- **Sharpnose's Rest** — a node on the far Margin. Named in the Chronicle's voice; what happened there is not yet written.
- **The Fifth Hole** — Bramble's fifth bolt-hole, dug in a season when nobody could see the need for it, and the reason forty citizens lived through one particular Road Work.
- **Nutmeg's Crossing** — the stretch of Highway she came over, alone, in the dark, having walked the corridor from a place the colony did not know still existed.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** These are exemplary event-conferred place names. Each name implies a story without summarizing it.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** “Forty citizens” prematurely establishes a population scale while the population study and species ceilings remain open in Appendix D. “The reason the colony lived through…” preserves the story and avoids accidental numeric canon.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** *Sharpnose’s Rest* strongly suggests a death-site, especially beside the open question about Sharpnose’s fate. That may be desirable, but it risks telegraphing the answer long before the narrative pass. “Rest” can also mean refuge or recovery; ensure the eventual event earns one reading rather than relying on ambiguity alone.

**What the colony believes that is wrong.** Two of the five Laws are misremembered — the trio were adolescents and nobody quizzed them for years. Nutmeg's corrections are still not fully accepted. Bramble taught the wrong version for a long time, and some citizens prefer it.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** Current canon says the Laws are imperfect but does not fix a count. “Two of five” is a new commitment. It is a reasonable one, but should be approved explicitly—especially because a precise number invites the future narrative to identify a definitive correct list. The best part here is not factual correction but the social truth that some citizens prefer Bramble’s version because it is theirs.

# II · MORNINGSIDE

*Squirrel. Aspirational. The colony that reaches.*

A den in nine beeches was cut down over two mornings for an interchange widening. Three young squirrels were out on the ground when it started, which is the only reason there are any.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** This is the largest conflict in Morningside. Every campaign uses the shared Founding Escape structure in §35.1: a mature ancestral home, the nursery lesson, the founding trio at a peripheral store because of the player’s social Choice-Event Card, a sudden machine catastrophe, and a played escape through the four registers. The squirrel version may be summarized rather than narrated in full, and its destruction can take two mornings, but its trio cannot survive for an unrelated fixed reason. Recast this as the brief summary of that same playable opening.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** “Cut down over two mornings” is worth retaining as the colony’s distinctive *experience* of loss. New Elderbank receives the blow; Morningside watches erasure proceed in stages. The shared mechanics need not produce identical memory.

## The founding trio

**Rowan.** Named the colony. Was the one who kept saying *there will be somewhere better than this* through eleven days of moving, and was right, and has never entirely been forgiven for it. Runs the Cache Network by instinct and cannot explain any of it.
*Keepsake:* a beech leaf, pressed flat and gone brown, from Nine Beeches.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Distinctive, contradictory, and systemic without sounding like a tutorial. “Was right, and has never entirely been forgiven” is especially good.

**Cobb.** The heaviest hauler in the colony and the least willing to leave it. Built the first bridge across the near channel and has rebuilt it eleven times. Believes, quietly, that Rowan's reaching is how a colony loses things.
*Keepsake:* a bent brass screw, worked out of the guardrail at Nine Beeches on the last morning.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Cobb embodies resilience without being reduced to “the conservative one.” The repeated bridge is an excellent material counterargument to Rowan.

**Gale.** The fastest, and the only one of the three who went back to look. Came back and would not say what was there. Scouts further than anyone sanctions and is the reason Morningside knows the corridor as far as it does.
*Keepsake:* nothing that was carried — a length of fishing line, found on the road on the second day, and kept because it was the first thing the new world gave them.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** “Nothing that was carried” creates a useful contrast with the other founders’ relics, and “the first thing the new world gave them” captures MEDIAN’s relationship to human detritus unusually well.

## The ones who came after

**Tassel.** A wanderer, taken in at the second winter, who had been living alone in a culvert for a season and does not talk about it. Became the colony's Caretaker and its best judge of when a cache has gone bad.

> **CODEX NOTE — VOICE:** Functional, but less individuated than the founders. It also repeats Fennel’s “alone in a culvert, unverifiable past” silhouette. Give Tassel a different displacement ecology or a positive habit unrelated to trauma; not every joiner needs to arrive as sealed grief.

**Wick.** Born of Rowan and Cobb's Hearth in the third spring, and the first citizen of Morningside who has never had to be told that a tree can be cut down.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Excellent. This does for Morningside what Daisy does for New Elderbank, but with a sharper species-specific fact.

**Guest — Slip.** A mink, met at the near channel as an obstacle and recruited two seasons later. Opens rescue from water, culverts and drainage; is worth almost nothing away from it. Morningside is built on a Creek Split, so Slip is worth a great deal.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / STRUCTURE:** A clear demonstration of the Guest option-opener model. It is almost *too* perfect a biome/Guest match, which contributes to the sense that each colony is an optimized specimen. Preserve Slip, but let the story show the cost or friction of reserving a scarce Guest slot for such a locally powerful specialist.

## The four tiers

**Tier I — Scavenger Camp.** Three squirrels in a drey lashed into the fork of the largest elm, and every acorn they own in one place because there is nowhere else to put it. Cobb hates this and says so daily. The near channel is uncrossable and the far bank is visibly better.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** A perfect Tier-I rendering of Cache Network: the problem is visible before the system has the infrastructure to solve it.

**Tier II — Fortified Settlement.** The first bridge. Caches split four ways on Cobb's insistence, which turns out to matter the first time the creek rises. Rowan names the colony at the welcome sign and half the colony thinks the name is too much. Slip is met, and is a problem.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / CLARIFY:** The welcome-sign beat is exactly canonical. “Half the colony” may be literally awkward at this population and sounds statistical; “not everyone” or naming the dissenter would keep the quarrel personal.

**Tier III — Independent Colony.** Caches across three Reaches and a genuine argument about whether that is a network or an overextension. Gale reaches the rest stop and comes back describing something nobody believes. Slip joins. Wick is born, and Morningside starts thinking in generations rather than seasons.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** If “the rest stop” means the Rest-Stop Metropolis, Gale reaches the campaign destination too early. Tier III prepares a corridor connection; Tier IV reaches the Metropolis, and the Special Journey is the campaign’s climax. Substitute a distant but lesser human site—a weigh station, service yard, or abandoned pull-off—or move this beat into Tier IV.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** “Thinking in generations rather than seasons” is an excellent Tier-III transformation and should remain.

**Tier IV — Sovereign Network.** The canopy runs unbroken over two Reaches and the caches are spread so wide that no single loss can hurt. Rowan is proved right in a way that is difficult to celebrate, because Cobb did not live to see the far bank held. Morningside is the colony that reached, and the reaching cost what reaching costs.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** “No single loss can hurt” overstates Cache Network’s promise; dispersal prevents one loss from wiping the stockpile, not from hurting. “No single loss can cripple the colony” keeps the mechanical point.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** Cobb’s death is powerful, but it should eventually arise from a concrete system-facing event, not function only as the price required by the paragraph’s symmetry. The draft correctly flags this below. Avoid making every colony’s mature identity depend on a pre-authored death; emergent histories need room to diverge.

## From the Chronicle

*The Remembering, Morningside.*

> **On the Naming.** *Rowan stood at the sign and said: we will call it for the light. And Cobb said: call it for the ground. And it is called for the light.*

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Very good Chronicle voice. It records disagreement without resolving or editorializing it.

**First Saying:** *There will be somewhere better than this.* Said with warmth and said with an edge, depending who is saying it, which is exactly what the colony is like.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON / DECISION:** §35.1 currently fixes *“the air smells good here”* as the founding line that enters the Chronicle when the Records begin, apparently for every campaign. This passage gives Morningside a different First Saying. That is an attractive change—colony-specific founding language is richer—but it is a canon change, not merely example content. Either revise §35.1 so each story colony has its own first line, or call this Morningside’s **defining Saying** rather than its first.

**Places that earned a name.**

- **Cobb's Bridge** — the near-channel crossing, rebuilt eleven times and then twelve.
- **The Far Bank** — not a folk name and not a place name, quite. It is what Morningside calls whatever it has not reached yet, and it moves.
- **Nine Beeches** — the lost den. Squirrels count what is gone.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Excellent set. It contains a structure, a moving metaphor, and a destroyed place—three different ways a name can hold memory.

**The argument that never ends.** Centralise or disperse. It is the Cache Network stated as a family quarrel, and it is the reason Morningside's Story Circle is louder than the other two.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** This is the colony concept in its final form. It turns mechanics into politics without inventing factions or a political subsystem.

# III · RUSHBOTTOM

*Wood mouse. Grounded. The colony that goes on.*

Chaff End was under a verge that got drainage work. There was no single morning; it took most of a season, and the mice kept moving deeper until there was no deeper. Three came out of the pipe at the far end.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** As written, this conflicts with the universal Founding Escape: a singular permanent apocalypse played through the same opening structure, not a season-long retreat whose survivors emerge through one pipe. Preserve the slow encroachment as Chaff End’s prehistory, but give it a final morning when the work reaches the ancestral Burrow and the canonical three are stranded at the periphery. That lets Rushbottom remember a season of warning while the game still begins at the required rupture.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** “Kept moving deeper until there was no deeper” is excellent and should survive the reconciliation.

## The founding trio

**Barley.** Counted. Knew how many left Chaff End, knows how many arrived, and has kept the number in the Almanac ever since — which is why Rushbottom's Records are the most complete of the three colonies and why nobody at Rushbottom has ever gone unnamed.
*Keepsake:* a bottle cap, carried out because it was already being carried and there was no moment to put it down.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** Every citizen in every MEDIAN colony is named from birth or recruitment; Rushbottom cannot uniquely ensure that “nobody… has ever gone unnamed.” Make its distinction that **no name has been lost** and that it preserves the names of the sixty-one, not that it grants a universal right other colonies lack.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON / CLARIFY:** The **Almanac** is the colony’s present-tense summary; the **Chronicle/Remembering** is its literary history. The list of Chaff End’s dead belongs in the Chronicle, a memorial roll, or a carried proto-record that later enters the Records. Also, the Records begin at settlement, so Barley can carry the count from before them but cannot literally have kept it “in the Almanac ever since” Chaff End.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** The bottle cap is beautifully anti-symbolic: it becomes sacred because there was no time to put it down.

**Thimble.** Made the first Relay work: stationed herself at the narrowest point of the pipe and passed everything through, for three days, without being asked. Rushbottom's whole logistics culture is a copy of what Thimble did that week.
*Keepsake:* a thimble. She was named for it long before she carried it, and nobody at Rushbottom finds this remarkable.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** The first Relay as remembered behavior rather than invented infrastructure is exactly how a signature system should become culture.

> **CODEX NOTE — VOICE:** The thimble joke is charming but slightly more arch than the rest of MEDIAN. It can stay if the book wants occasional authorial dry humor; otherwise it draws attention to constructed naming at the exact moment the reader should be inside the memory.

**Pip.** The smallest, the loudest, and the reason the other two kept going. Went back into the pipe twice for citizens who turned out not to be there.
*Keepsake:* a length of red twine, tied around the middle, which has been retied so often it is now three lengths knotted.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / CLARIFY:** Strong, especially the twine changing through repair. Eventually specify why Pip believed those citizens were there—a call, a mistaken count, an old station—so the act reads as desperate rescue rather than arbitrary pathos.

## The ones who came after

**Tuck and Seam.** Littermates, born of Barley and Thimble's Hearth in the first Nesting Season, and named together on the same day — because a mouse colony names two where the others name one.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION (MAJOR):** This settles the body-unit growth question that §12 and Appendix D explicitly leave open: whether mouse births and arrivals come in pairs while rabbit and squirrel growth comes singly. Showing it in fiction still settles it. The solution is elegant and probably correct for comparative pacing, but it must be approved as a system decision before this pair becomes canonical. Until then, mark the second clause provisional.

**Bran.** A wanderer taken in at the fourth season, who came down the corridor from a Reach nobody had scouted and who described it accurately enough that Rushbottom scouted it and found him right. Now the colony's most trusted Median Scout.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** A welcome low-melodrama arrival. Bran earns trust through testable geographic knowledge, which suits Rushbottom’s record culture.

**Guest — Sump.** A toad, resident in the culvert mouth since before the mice arrived, who was there first and made no objection to company. Reduces spoilage on stored food and steadies the wet ground around him. Has never once left the culvert.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Perhaps the most naturally embedded Guest of the three. “Was there first and made no objection” gives found family a register other than rescue or recruitment.

## The four tiers

**Tier I — Scavenger Camp.** Three mice in a root void, running a relay because three mice cannot carry anything alone. Barley starts the count. The colony's first structure is not a shelter, it is a store — the mice built the pantry before the bedroom, and were right to.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** Individual mice can carry **Light** items (capacity 6); two mice are required for a **Standard** item (weight 10). “Three mice cannot carry anything alone” is mechanically false. Suggested meaning: they run a relay because no one mouse can carry the loads on which a new colony depends.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Pantry before bedroom is exactly the sort of species-specific civic myth the book needs. The root void already supplies natural shelter, so the choice remains credible.

**Tier II — Fortified Settlement.** Tuck and Seam are born, and Rushbottom goes from three to five in a night. The micro-tunnel network reaches the culvert mouth and Sump is discovered to have been there the whole time. First flood. The store holds.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** The three-to-five jump inherits the unresolved growth-in-body-units decision. Apart from that dependency, “First flood. The store holds.” is outstanding compression.

**Tier III — Independent Colony.** The densest colony on the corridor, and the most legible — every citizen named, every citizen counted, every route mapped. Bran arrives with intelligence nobody asked for and everybody uses. Rushbottom starts scouting further than a colony its size has any business scouting, because it can afford to lose the time.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON / VOICE:** Again, every colony names every citizen. “Most legible” is a design-pillar phrase and sounds like the assembly process speaking through the fiction. Make Rushbottom “the most completely counted” or “the colony that can account for every citizen, store, and path.”

**Tier IV — Sovereign Network.** Rushbottom does not have the widest reach or the strongest caches. It has the most citizens, the fullest Chronicle, and a relay chain that runs the length of four Reaches without a single citizen ever carrying anything far. It is the colony that arrived at the Metropolis with a complete list of everyone who had ever lived there.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / DECISION:** The arrival at the Metropolis is correctly Tier IV and provides a wonderful culmination. “Most citizens” depends on still-open population ceilings, and the four-Reach Relay establishes corridor-scale operation not otherwise specified. Both are plausible consequences of the mouse design, but both deserve conscious approval.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** A literal continuous relay across four Reaches must cross exposed service crossovers. That can be an achievement, but the physical stations and handoff through those gaps need one sentence so the network does not feel magically continuous.

## From the Chronicle

*The Remembering, Rushbottom.*

> **The Count.** *Sixty-one went into the ground at Chaff End. Three came out of the pipe. Barley has written all sixty-one, and reads them at the Circle each spring, and it takes a long time, and nobody leaves.*

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / DECISION:** This is the emotional center of Rushbottom and possibly of the whole draft. Keep the ritual. The exact number sixty-one fixes an ancestral population scale while ceilings remain open; either approve the number as story-specific and non-prescriptive, or temporarily write “all their names.”

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** “Sixty-one went into the ground” can mean sixty-one died rather than sixty-one inhabited Chaff End. If that ambiguity is not deliberate, “There were sixty-one under Chaff End” is clearer.

**First Saying:** *Small hands, short road.* The Relay stated as a proverb, and Rushbottom's whole philosophy: no one carries anything far.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON / DECISION:** As with Morningside, this conflicts with the universal founding line in §35.1 if labeled the **First Saying**. It is excellent as Rushbottom’s defining or best-known Saying. If all three are to have distinct first Sayings, revise the campaign onboarding canon accordingly.

**Places that earned a name.**

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / STRUCTURE:** All three are strong names, but the heading breaks on purpose for *The Long Count*. **What Rushbottom has that the others do not.** A complete record. The rabbits remember Elderbank in stories and the squirrels count the beeches, but Barley wrote the names down, and Rushbottom is the only colony on the corridor that can say exactly who it lost.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** This cleanly completes the story/argument/list triad. “On the corridor” makes the claim very broad; “of the three” is safer unless the uniqueness is meant to be world canon.

# What the three demonstrate

Each colony was cast to make a different part of the design concrete, so that the same events read differently in three campaigns — which is the argument §4 has been making without evidence.

> **CODEX NOTE — STRUCTURE / VOICE:** Excellent internal authoring rationale, but “the argument §4 has been making without evidence” is process voice. In the published book, either omit this sentence or turn the table into a reader-facing comparative plate with no reference to document insufficiency.

| | New Elderbank | Morningside | Rushbottom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loss** | one morning | two mornings | most of a season |
| **What it kept** | a memory, imperfectly | a leaf and a grudge | a number |
| **Its problem** | the Laws are wrong | reach against resilience | density and legibility |
| **Its Records read** | as a story | as an argument | as a list |
| **Grief** | a blow — whole body-units, rarely | a blow, and a bridge nobody rebuilds | attrition — half-units, often |
| **Its Guest** | WHOOT, taken in | Slip, negotiated with | Sump, already there |

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** This table exposes the draft’s real success. “A memory / a leaf and a grudge / a number” and “story / argument / list” are the conceptual spine.

> **CODEX NOTE — CANON:** The different durations of loss are valuable, but they must be reconciled with the shared Founding Escape rather than replacing it. The period can describe how long destruction was anticipated or completed; the playable rupture still needs the canonical morning.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** “The Laws are wrong” is too absolute for New Elderbank; “the Laws are disputed” preserves uncertainty. “Density and legibility” uses design language for Rushbottom; “a complete count carried by many small hands” would stay nearer the colony’s own register.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** The grief row correctly demonstrates individual versus body-unit consequence, but “a bridge nobody rebuilds” currently pre-authors Cobb’s death and the player’s response. In a concept sourcebook this can be a canonical colony history; in a game example it risks implying that every Morningside campaign must reproduce that death. Decide whether the story colonies are fixed exemplary histories or representative possibilities.

**Growth reads three ways.** Daisy is born and New Elderbank gains a citizen. Wick is born and Morningside gains an heir. Tuck and Seam are born and Rushbottom gains two — which is the body-unit question §12 flags, shown rather than argued.

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION (MAJOR):** “Shown rather than argued” is still a decision. This is precisely how process leakage becomes canon without adjudication. Either approve growth in body-units and update §§12, 18.3, 18.4, and Appendix D, or leave Tuck and Seam’s joint birth as a story fact that does **not** establish the normal mechanical yield. The current sentence explicitly makes it the answer, so it cannot remain while the question stays open.

**Recruitment reads three ways.** The owl was taken in, the mink was an obstacle first, and the toad was there before anyone arrived and simply did not leave.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP:** Excellent. This expands found family without adding systems: rescue/accommodation, negotiated conversion, and prior residence.

# Naming banks, seeded

The names are bank *shapes* rather than lists — the first concrete content for the citizen-name appendix, and for the gap where §23.1 promises a species-specific component bank and nothing was ever deferred for it.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / STRUCTURE:** Good diagnosis of a real content gap. Keep the bank-generation work distinct from the colony biographies so changing a character does not silently change the naming grammar.

| Species | Bank shape | Seeded |
|---|---|---|
| **Rabbit** | flora and small plain nouns, plus one body-feature descriptor | Bramble · Twig · Nutmeg · Daisy · Fennel · Sharpnose |
| **Squirrel** | trees, weather and made things | Rowan · Cobb · Gale · Tassel · Wick |
| **Wood Mouse** | grain, cloth and small enclosed spaces | Barley · Thimble · Pip · Tuck · Seam · Bran |

> **CODEX NOTE — DECISION:** These are promising shapes, not yet sufficient banks. Test each against at least thirty generated names before canonizing the category rules. At present some seeds fit only by generous interpretation—*Cobb*, *Pip*, *Tuck*, and *Bran* can belong, but they do not yet prove the stated categories. Also watch overlap: flora, trees, grain, and small plain nouns can easily make the three species sound more alike than this table suggests.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** The single “body-feature descriptor” carve-out appears designed backward from Sharpnose. That may be fine, but a bank rule should produce a family of legal names rather than excuse one existing name. Define the pattern broadly enough to yield several credible rabbit names, or mark Sharpnose as a permitted exception.

**Guests take a different register.** WHOOT, Slip, Sump — sound and motion rather than flora. A Guest arrives already named, by a culture that is not the colony's, and the names should not sound like they came out of the same bank.

> **CODEX NOTE — KEEP / DECISION:** The principle is strong: Guest names should sound imported rather than generated from the colony’s species bank. The proposed “sound and motion” rule is not yet demonstrated—*Sump* reads as place/object more than sound or motion, while WHOOT is both sound and typography. Treat “non-colony register” as settled direction and the exact Guest bank as open.

> **CODEX NOTE — CLARIFY:** Canon says a Guest carries a Given Name from the first encounter and that nobody names themselves. “By a culture that is not the colony’s” is a reasonable inference, but it implies offstage animal communities beyond the Metropolis. That is fertile worldbuilding; approve it consciously rather than using it only to justify a generator.

## Recommended revision priorities

1. **Reconcile all three Founding Escapes with §35.1.** Keep their different durations and remembered textures, but preserve the shared playable structure and Choice-Event cause.
2. **Do not let Nutmeg prove a perfect master version of the Laws.** Make her memory corrective but not infallible.
3. **Move Morningside’s Rest-Stop arrival to Tier IV** or replace the Tier-III destination.
4. **Bring New Elderbank’s Far-hole back inside Ashcross.**
5. **Correct Rushbottom’s universal-name claims and Almanac/Chronicle usage.**
6. **Adjudicate mouse growth in body-units explicitly.** Do not allow Tuck and Seam to decide it by example while Appendix D says it is open.
7. **Decide whether first Sayings are universal or colony-specific**, then make §6.2, §35.1, and these examples agree.
8. **Preserve the story/argument/list triad**, the three forms of Guest arrival, and the strongest capsule lines. Those are not ornamental; they are the reason the story-colony apparatus earns its space.

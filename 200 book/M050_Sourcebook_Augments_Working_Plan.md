## 1. Purpose

The Augments Plan turns the complete theoretical game into a designed reading
experience. It identifies where prose needs a diagram, table, image, narrative
moment, or literal game presentation; where typography alone should carry the
page; and how the book can accumulate visual and narrative continuity without
becoming a slide deck, art portfolio, or second specification.

The plan must:

- make complex relationships easier to understand;
- let the reader imagine lived play rather than only read rules;
- establish the Corridor, Colonies, and Citizens as particular places and
  lives;
- create rhythm across a long landscape-format book;
- preserve exact reference depth in the Appendices;
- balance Mouse, Rabbit, and Squirrel civilization across the complete work;
- use recurring canon subjects so familiarity can accumulate;
- distinguish current canon from illustrative presentation; and
- produce bounded briefs that can guide layout, diagrams, mockups, writing,
  image generation, and image-to-image work.

## 2. Book-wide form

- Landscape 4:3, intended primarily for screen reading.
- The primary reading condition is one complete landscape page at a time on a
  laptop, moving vertically through the PDF. Every page therefore establishes
  its own context and completes its essential reading task without requiring
  an adjacent page to be visible.
- One unified Concept Sourcebook rather than a summary followed by a second
  Phonebook treatment.
- Every page designed; many pages intentionally contain no artwork.
- The page is the unit of composition. Prose, tables, diagrams, examples,
  narrative fragments, object cutouts, interface details, and partial
  illustration may share one page when they serve one coherent reading task.
  An augment does not own a page unless its mapping explicitly requires that
  scale.
- Continuous reading supported by strong hierarchy, running navigation,
  recurring visual grammar, and varied page density.
- Semantic text, labels, tables, diagram structure, captions, and interface
  information remain editable production elements.
- Generated imagery may provide a textless backplate or rendered scene but
  must not be the sole carrier of instructional text.
- Full-page artwork appears in two structural classes outside ordinary Chapter
  flow. **Part plates**, also called section plates during development, are
  wordless thresholds between Parts. **Chapter plates** are optional thresholds
  before selected Chapters and carry the Chapter title only.
- Text on a Chapter plate remains editable typography over a textless artwork
  source. Part plates contain no title, caption, Landscape Voice, interface
  text, or instructional label.
- Outside the Cover, End Plate, and Part 0's exceptional narrative treatment,
  only two augment classes ordinarily claim a complete page: structural artwork
  plates and full-page infographics. A full-page infographic may appear within
  a Chapter when its instructional function requires the complete page.
- The designed opening page after a wordless Part plate varies by need:
  sustained narrative, game presentation, recurring Citizen, diagram,
  typographic threshold, or silence.
- Narrative remains distributed and flexible rather than returning to a fixed
  Tale Panel format.
- Part 0 receives its own mapping pass after the regular book establishes its
  visual and narrative grammar.

Consecutive pages may answer, echo, or continue one another, and a later print
edition may reveal productive facing-page relationships. No governing
comparison, diagram, caption, reading order, or compositional subject may cross
a gutter or depend upon simultaneous spread view. In this plan, **spread** is
provisional shorthand for a short consecutive page sequence unless a record
explicitly states otherwise; pagination must convert each such sequence into
self-sufficient landscape pages.

## 3. Governing principles

### 3.1 Functional augmentation

Every augment must perform at least one defined function:

- orient the reader;
- explain a relationship;
- compare repeated fields;
- show sequence or state change;
- establish scale or spatial structure;
- demonstrate a Register or player decision;
- make a Citizen, Colony, object, or history particular;
- carry atmosphere or transition;
- support reference use; or
- create deliberate visual rest.

Decoration alone does not justify an augment. A prose page may be the fully
designed answer when another form would repeat rather than clarify.

### 3.2 One canonical owner

The GDD and its Appendices own game propositions. An augment may restate,
compare, demonstrate, or dramatize those propositions. Any new rule discovered
during augment work returns to authorial discussion and its proper GDD home
before the augment depends upon it.

### 3.3 Positive presentation

Augments show what MEDIAN is. They do not preserve removed mechanics, explain
developmental reconciliation, or retain obsolete labels merely to negate them.
Historical material can inspire form, subject, rhythm, or composition only
after its content has been checked against current canon.

### 3.4 Particularity and continuity

Narrative and visual augments prefer established Colonies, Citizens, Places,
objects, and remembered events over disposable examples. Each reappearance
respects incoming state and leaves any changed state available to later use.
Generic examples remain appropriate when particular identity would distract
from a pure comparison or formula.

### 3.5 Species balance

Rabbit carries the sustained Founding Escape. The rest of the book must answer
that prominence through strong, cumulative Mouse and Squirrel presence.
Balance is judged across the complete Sourcebook rather than page by page or
Part by Part.

### 3.6 Canon-image continuity

Existing canon imagery is mandatory input when a known Citizen or Colony is
rendered again. New composition, light, posture, activity, and emotion may
interpret the present scene; persistent body, markings, clothing, Keepsake,
Maiming, adaptive device, geography, and Colony form must follow current canon.
Appendix C and the GDD art doctrine govern reference-and-amend work.

### 3.7 Composite-page composition

Ordinary Sourcebook pages should feel authored as wholes rather than assembled
as alternating blocks of prose and pictures. A page may combine several
augment forms when hierarchy makes their relationship immediately legible:

- two or three prose measures with one partial illustration;
- a compact table or rule field nested within explanatory prose;
- object cutouts placed beside the definitions or histories they clarify;
- a wide environmental vignette sharing the page with continued reading;
- a literal game detail surrounded by editorial explanation; or
- typography, spacing, rules, and restrained background structure without art.

Composition follows function rather than a quota. One dominant element anchors
the page; supporting elements explain, particularize, compare, or provide
rest. Decorative accumulation does not substitute for hierarchy, and a useful
illustration does not require surrounding prose to vacate the page.

The visual result belongs to MEDIAN. Its typography, navigation, provisional
shared palette, highway ecology, animal-scale materiality, image treatment,
and restrained ornament govern the composition. Sourcebook precedents may
demonstrate how several elements coexist, but they do not supply MEDIAN's
fantasy language, page decoration, archetypal character treatment, or visual
identity.

Every composite page remains subject to single-page screen reading. It carries
its own heading, premise, semantic labels, and conclusion; no visual or textual
relationship depends upon an adjacent page being visible.

## 4. Augment vocabulary

### 4.1 Designed prose page

A page carried by typography, hierarchy, spacing, running navigation, pull
lines, sidebars, rules, and restrained graphic structure. It is the default
page class and an affirmative design choice, not an empty slot awaiting art.

### 4.2 Table or reference panel

An exact mapping, inventory, comparison, or constraint set. Tables remain
editable, sortable by the logic of the subject, and compact enough to scan.
Reference panels isolate definitions or recurring structures without
converting every paragraph into a card.

### 4.3 System diagram

A visual account of dependency, sequence, hierarchy, geography, or state
change. It is preferred when relationships matter more than atmosphere.

### 4.4 Infographic

A substantially authored visual explanation that combines diagram, labels,
selected illustration, and concise prose. An infographic may occupy a full
page when its instructional function and information hierarchy require that
scale. Unlike a structural plate, it remains an ordinary part of Chapter flow:
its prose, labels, table structures, arrows, keys, and diagram elements stay
editable and carry the page's teaching task. Smaller infographics remain
available inside composite pages.

### 4.5 Literal game presentation

A designed representation of an actual player-facing form: DWELL, TRAVEL,
RISK, MEET, EMBODY, Almanac, Chronicle, Tale, Community Board, Citizen view,
or another established interface. It demonstrates the form's information and
experience rather than promising a final software interface.

### 4.6 Partial-page illustration

A situated image sharing the page or spread with prose. It may establish body,
material, activity, place, relationship, scale, or atmosphere while remaining
subordinate to the section's reading task.

### 4.7 Structural plates

Full-page artwork may form a structural threshold in one of two classes:

1. **Part plate (section plate).** A wordless image placed between Parts. It
   creates visual rest, announces a change of scale or subject through imagery
   alone, and leaves the next page to name the new Part and Chapter. It carries
   no title, caption, Landscape Voice, interface text, or instructional label.
2. **Chapter plate.** An optional image placed before a selected Chapter. It
   carries the Chapter title and no other text. The title is an editable
   production element laid over a textless artwork source rather than generated
   into the image.

Neither class interrupts ordinary Chapter prose. Historical full-page divider
families, including the archived **Mouse** divider, may inform the Chapter
plate's scale, restraint, and title-over-image composition after their depicted
content has been remade to current canon.

### 4.8 Narrative augment

Narrative takes the form that best serves the governing section:

1. **Sustained narrative** for a sequence whose meaning depends on continuity.
2. **Recurring lived moment** for familiarity, relationship, or ordinary life.
3. **Worked play sequence** for choice, temporal order, and causal resolution.
4. **Literal game text** for Chronicle, Tale, Almanac, MEET, or another
   player-facing narrative form.
5. **Narrative-bearing environment** for recurring geography, physical traces,
   captions, and question-bearing detail.

There is no compulsory narrative slot per Part or Chapter.

### 4.9 Landscape Voice

Landscape Voice is the human narrator describing country, weather, movement,
and simple events. It may accompany a transition, map, environmental sequence,
or the designed page following a structural plate. It does not appear on a
wordless Part plate or a title-only Chapter plate. It does not speak as a
Citizen, protagonist, Chronicle, or mechanical notification.

### 4.10 Composite page

A designed landscape page combining two or more vocabulary forms under one
reader task and visual hierarchy. Composite page is a production relationship,
not permission to add content: every ingredient retains its canonical owner,
narrative function, asset lineage, and acceptance test. It is the expected
form of many ordinary pages even when one mapped augment supplies the dominant
subject.

## 5. Mapping record

Every proposed augment receives one mapping record before production.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| **Map ID** | Stable production identifier local to this plan |
| **GDD location** | Part, Chapter, subsection, Appendix, and relevant MSID |
| **Reader task** | What the reader should understand, recognize, compare, or feel |
| **Augment form** | One form from Section 4, or an approved combination |
| **Working subject** | Exact system, situation, Place, Citizen, object, or relationship |
| **Scale** | Inline, partial page, page, or spread |
| **Narrative function** | Teaching, continuity, recognition, consequence, atmosphere, or none |
| **Narrative treatment** | Sustained narrative, recurring lived moment, worked play sequence, literal game text, narrative-bearing environment, Landscape Voice, or an intentional none with its reason |
| **Register** | DWELL, TRAVEL, RISK, MEET, EMBODY, reference, or none |
| **Subjects** | Colony, Citizens, Species, counterpart, and environment |
| **Incoming state** | Facts already true before the augment begins |
| **Outgoing state** | Facts changed or established by the augment |
| **Canon inputs** | Mandatory Citizen, Colony, map, object, or visual references |
| **Semantic content** | Text, labels, data, table structure, and diagram elements that remain editable |
| **Asset lineage** | New, remake, adaptation, historical prompt, diagram, or typography-only |
| **Cross-reference** | Complementary body or Appendix treatment |
| **Priority** | Essential, valuable, or optional |
| **Approval state** | Proposed, discussed, approved, briefed, produced, integrated, or superseded |
| **Acceptance test** | The specific function by which the finished augment will be judged |

Mapping records remain concise. They specify the augment without pre-writing
final captions, prose, interface copy, or image prompts. **Scale** states the
minimum useful footprint and whether an augment must dominate; it does not
reserve the rest of a page unless the record explicitly calls for a full plate
or full-page explanatory form.

- Keep changing market facts visibly dated and separable from durable design
  comparison.

### 6.10 Part 0 — The Founding Escape

Part 0 is mapped after Sections 6.2–6.9 establish the book's ordinary visual
and narrative language.

- Preserve the uninterrupted literary account in the body.
- Follow Rabbit as the principal point of view and unstated tribute to
  *Watership Down*.
- Open on the Reach 4 flash-forward, return to the ancestral Home, and earn the
  later return to the same moment.
- Portray calm DWELL, a brief EMBODY opportunity, Chaos MEET, flight into
  TRAVEL, first Crossing, montage, final Reach 4 Crossing, Reach 5 arrival,
  Landscape Voice, and the Founding choice.
- Use instructional overlays sparingly to identify canonical Nouns without
  interrupting narrative continuity.
- Reserve full plates for actual cinematic thresholds.
- End on the blank ground that may become New Elderbank before Part I begins.
- Keep Mouse and Squirrel openings out of this sustained narrative; answer
  Rabbit's prominence through the later book-wide balance plan.

### 6.11 End Plate and colophon

- Give the Sourcebook a deliberate emotional ending after the Appendices.
- Echo or transform the Cover and Part 0 arrival image.
- Decide whether the plate remains silent or carries one short final line.
- Place credits, version information, and colophon before it rather than asking
  production metadata to end the book.

## 7. Narrative continuity plan

### 7.1 Story civilizations

The recurring narrative pool begins with:

- **New Elderbank** — Rabbit
- **Rushbottom** — Mouse
- **Morningside** — Squirrel

Their current Citizens, Tier rosters, Guests, identities, and histories live in
`200 book/M050_Story_Colonies_Citizen_and_Name_Outline.md`. Augment mapping
selects from that continuity rather than inventing a new cast for each system.

### 7.2 Narrative record fields

Every recurring narrative augment additionally records:

- the last book appearance of each subject;
- the facts the reader already knows;
- the new fact, relationship, or recognition established here;
- whether the event belongs to ordinary play, authored opening, Chronicle,
  Tale, or illustrative example;
- any object, Place, injury, Maiming, Guest, After-name, or visual state that
  must persist afterward; and
- the next plausible reuse, if any.

### 7.3 Narrative distribution

The book-wide pass checks that:

- ordinary life appears often enough for hazard to matter;
- narrative does not cluster only in Citizen and Away chapters;
- no story Colony exists only to demonstrate failure;
- no recurring Citizen becomes a universal tutorial mascot;
- danger changes particular lives before erasing them;
- Mouse and Squirrel acquire cumulative familiarity comparable to Rabbit; and
- narrative augments illuminate their governing systems rather than replace
  specification.

### 7.4 Two-stage narrative mapping

Narrative is conceived during Chapter mapping rather than attached after visual
pagination. Every mapping record identifies one of the approved narrative
treatments or states why none belongs there. This keeps image, information,
prose, and lived example answerable to the same reader task and prevents a
return to detachable Tale Panels.

After each Part is mapped, a horizontal narrative audit checks the resulting
rhythm across its Chapters. After the complete book is mapped, a second audit
checks the whole work for:

- Mouse, Rabbit, and Squirrel presence across sustained reading time;
- recurring Colony, Citizen, Place, object, and remembered-event continuity;
- variation among sustained narrative, lived moments, worked play, literal
  game text, narrative-bearing environments, Landscape Voice, and silence;
- attachment accumulating through recognition rather than being asserted;
- ordinary life, hazard, consequence, recovery, and return appearing in useful
  proportion;
- scenes teaching or deepening the system beside them; and
- intentional quiet preventing narrative from becoming a formula at every
  Chapter or Part threshold.

The later audit may move, shorten, fuse, or remove a narrative augment. It is a
composition and balance pass, not the first moment at which narrative is
invented.

## 8. Visual continuity and asset lineage

### 8.1 Current, remake, and new

The archive-wide review and provisional disposition of all 255 manifest entries
lives in `200 book/M050_Existing_Artwork_Disposition.md`. The historical
manifest remains unchanged; v0.5 judgments belong to this book-planning layer.

Each visual candidate is classified as:

- **Current reference** — usable as mandatory canon input.
- **Remake candidate** — valuable composition or subject with obsolete detail.
- **Historical prompt** — useful idea only; no visual authority.
- **New requirement** — no adequate existing concept.
- **Diagrammatic** — best built from editable shapes, text, and data.
- **Typography-only** — intentionally requires no image asset.

### 8.2 Image-to-image requirements

A brief for a known Citizen or Colony identifies every required source image,
the state that remains fixed, the state that changes, permitted compositional
interpretation, and the intended presentation. Canon input is mandatory for:

- dramatic images following a MEET;
- Register-change or loading presentations;
- Citizen panels and Roster views;
- Chronicle and Tale imagery;
- current Colony views;
- remembered earlier Colony states; and
- any scene whose recognition depends on an established body, object, or
  place.

### 8.3 Stale-detail audit

No historical image moves directly into layout. Every candidate is checked for
current Species, Citizen, Role, Practice, Place, Register, Resource, interface,
world, and language accuracy. A strong old plate is remade to current canon
rather than annotated with its errors.

## 9. Balance ledgers

The mapping phase maintains small derived ledgers for editorial review. They
do not become parallel content authority.

### 9.1 Core-Species presence

Track major plate, partial illustration, literal game presentation, narrative
appearance, worked sequence, and sustained-page presence for Mouse, Rabbit,
and Squirrel.

### 9.2 Story continuity

Track each recurring Colony and Citizen by appearance order, incoming state,
new information, and persistent outgoing state.

### 9.3 Register coverage

Track whether DWELL, TRAVEL, RISK, MEET, and EMBODY each receive enough clear

### 9.4 Augment-form rhythm

partial illustration, full plates, narrative, and intentional visual rest.

### 9.5 Body–Appendix complement

Track where the body establishes the reader-facing whole and the Appendix

## 10. Pagination and page rhythm

Pagination begins only after the first mapping pass. It will establish:

- prose density and column standards;
- heading and standfirst behavior;
- normal table, diagram, and sidebar footprints;
- full-page and spread cadence;
- Part and Chapter transition treatments;
- Appendix density and navigation;
- placement of recurring narrative subjects;
- adequate typographic rest; and
- the provisional total page count.

Page estimates remain consequences of approved content and rhythm, not quotas
that force augments into sections that do not need them.

## 11. Asset brief template

Once a mapping record is approved, its production brief contains:

1. Map ID and working title
2. Governing GDD location and MSID
3. Canonical proposition
4. Reader function
5. Augment form and page scale
6. Exact subjects, place, time, and state
7. Register or reference relationship
8. Composition or information structure
9. Mandatory canon references
10. Editable semantic content
11. Permitted invention and prohibited drift
12. Species and narrative-continuity requirements
13. Accessibility requirements
14. Intended page relationship
15. Acceptance criteria

Image prompts, diagram specifications, narrative prose, interface copy, and
layout directions are produced from this approved brief rather than embedded
prematurely in the master map.

## 12. Production sequence

1. Approve this granular skeleton.
2. Map Parts I–VII one Chapter at a time.
3. Map Appendices A–K.
4. Run species, story, Register, rhythm, and body–Appendix audits.
5. Map Part 0, Front Matter, Cover, colophon, and End Plate.
6. Establish provisional pagination and page rhythm.
7. Approve the essential/valuable/optional production slate.
8. Write asset briefs.
9. Prototype representative prose, table, diagram, infographic, game-view,
   narrative, partial-art, and full-plate pages.
10. Revise the design system from those prototypes.
11. Produce and integrate augments in approved order.
12. Audit canon, continuity, accessibility, balance, and reading rhythm across
    the assembled Sourcebook.

## 13. Acceptance audit

The completed Augments Plan must demonstrate that:

- every Chapter and Appendix has an explicit editorial treatment;
- every proposed augment performs a named function;
- every full plate occupies a structural threshold;
- every literal game presentation follows current Register rules;
- recurring narrative respects established incoming and outgoing state;
- canon Citizen and Colony imagery is mandatory input where applicable;
- stale art is remade rather than treated as authority;
- Mouse, Rabbit, and Squirrel receive balanced cumulative representation;
- Guest visibility supports rather than displaces Core-Species civilization;
- body and Appendix treatments complement one another;
- semantic information remains editable and accessible;
- unillustrated pages remain intentionally designed;
- page rhythm does not become a repeated Part formula;
- Part 0 remains narratively continuous while still introducing the game; and
- the final book reads as a complete Concept Sourcebook rather than a pitch for
  later software.

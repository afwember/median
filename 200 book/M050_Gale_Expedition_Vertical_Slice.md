# MEDIAN v0.5.0 — Gale's Expedition Vertical Slice

Status: Studio development document

Purpose: preserve one continuous Morningside expedition as narrative,
Story-Canon context, detailed system simulation, and earlier procedural
reference

Authority: mixed; each section states its own authority boundary

## How to read this document

This document holds four views of the same expeditionary subject:

1. an original narrative master from Launch through Homecoming;
2. the canonical Morningside history through Tier III that surrounds it;
3. a granular, current-baseline simulation of Home and Away as one system; and
4. the earlier cursory crunch transcript retained as developmental reference.

The Morningside history in Section 2 reproduces current Story Canon. The daily
Sustenance baseline in Section 3 is also current: **one Sustenance per total
resident Body Unit, including Citizens Away**. The expedition's exact incident,
the simulation's Stores, Places, Role assignments, Loads, yields, spoilage,
rolls, Wound duration, and other quantities remain provisional until adopted
elsewhere. Section 4 is reference, not a second authority; Section 3 supersedes
it wherever their precision differs.

This is a development vertical slice rather than a mandate to print the whole
ledger in the Sourcebook. Its value is that one set of named lives can test
DWELL, Launch, TRAVEL, RISK, MEET, Home accounting, Outpost use, Homecoming,
care, and persistent history without resetting state between systems.

# 1. Narrative master

Status: original development narrative; illustrative and available for later
adaptation, but not automatically a fixed event in Morningside's Story Canon

## The Far Bank

The morning began with a wind from the Roadway. It moved unevenly through
Morningside, stirring the high grass first and then descending among the linked
roofs and platforms in brief, unsettled breaths. The Web answered in its several
voices: a cord humming against wood, a ladder tapping twice against its rest,
dry leaves turning in the upper anchors.

Gale had been awake before the wind reached them.

From the eastern perch, the Squirrel watched pale traffic move beyond the
grass. A length of weathered fishing line was wound closely about Gale's chest.
It had been found during Morningside's first quiet inspection of Elmwater,
after the long escape from Nine Beeches, and Gale carried it without ornament
or explanation.

Below, the Colony was already gathering.

There was no report waiting for them—no known cache, no stranded traveler, no
promise of good ground. The country beyond Morningside remained what it had
been the night before: partly seen, incompletely understood, and interrupted by
the Roadway. That was reason enough to go.

Alder came by one of the lower connections, although three higher and quicker
ways stood open. Alder had been born at Morningside and treated height with none
of the reverence the Founders sometimes expected. A sound connection was useful
wherever it happened to be. A low one did not become shameful merely because
another animal could leap over it.

A smooth hardwood lever hung against Alder's side. Its surface had been gnawed
and worn until it sat securely against the body, but it remained the same Brace
Tool wherever the day's ground found a new use for it.

Reed waited beside the drainage run. The Mink's dark body seemed too long for
the made geometry of the Web, yet Reed had learned its ways without pretending
to be a Squirrel. Moving water remained the more natural country. It was how
Reed had first become known to Morningside, and it was the reason a safe
waterline had been kept as part of the Colony rather than treated as waste
ground below it.

The Launch was held where the lowest built connection met the open median.

Other Citizens were present. At Launch, three names were set together: Gale,
Alder, Reed. Their bodies made one complete party. Their ordinary
responsibilities remained theirs, but Morningside would go on for a time
without their present labor and Civic Shares.

Each took what had been prepared. Gale carried a single wrapped Offering as
well as the fishing-line Keepsake. Alder retained the hardwood Brace Tool and
a Binding Supply. Reed tested every fastening before settling a Remedy Supply
and the carried weight along the body.

They were told nothing about what they ought to find.

That was not the purpose of going.

When the choice was committed, the Colony changed at once, though little in its
appearance did. Three Citizens passed from Home into Away. Work that had rested
partly upon them settled upon those who remained. The expedition began without
a road laid before it.

Gale went first beyond the final connection.

Then, as at Nine Beeches long before, Gale stopped and looked back.

Morningside stood above the ground in branches, woven grass, salvaged wood,
wire, cloth, and the many small redundancies by which Cobb had taught it to
remain standing. Citizens moved within it. Nothing had become empty merely
because three lives were leaving.

Gale turned again.

They went north.

The Field was close country at first. Grass stems divided the view into narrow
passages. The drainage run bent beside them, sometimes visible as brown water
and sometimes betrayed only by the cooler air above it. Human fragments lay
where weather and traffic had thrown them: a white twist of plastic, a metal
cap sunk nearly level with the soil, a strip of dark fabric held under roots.

The party did not follow a marked route. Gale kept generally toward the
Roadway, turning when the ground required it. Alder favored low crossings over
the drainage cuts and tested any stem or fragment expected to bear weight.
Reed moved easily wherever water had shaped the earth, disappearing beneath
cover and returning a little farther ahead.

By late light, the shadow of the Sound Wall had drawn thin. Dry stems stood
pale on both sides of the channel. The three went north beneath them, keeping
close where the ground opened, until the sound of the Roadway gathered ahead
and every smaller sound was lost within it.

The Staging Post lay under the broken end of a concrete lip. From there, the
Roadway could be seen without entering it.

The place smelled of dust, oil, wet grit, and the hot bitterness left by tires.
Vehicles passed in streams whose order could be understood only for a little
while at a time. A gap appeared, narrowed, and vanished. One lane cleared while
another filled. Wind struck the concrete after each large vehicle and pulled
loose grass toward the road.

Gale watched the intervals.

Alder watched the ground.

The Squirrels did not seek a succession of shelters as a Mouse party might
have done, nor wait only for a single broad opening as Rabbits often did. Their
passage had to possess a flow: an initial movement, no more redirection than the
bodies could carry through at speed, and an anchor on the farther side.

Gale indicated the first line.

Alder altered it.

“There,” Alder said, pointing toward a low obstruction partway across. “If the
wind turns us, it turns us toward that. Not away from it.”

Beyond the lanes, a root had entered a crack beside the far curb. It offered
neither comfort nor safety, but it would hold.

Reed looked across the road.

“I cross with you,” Reed said.

The plan belonged to the party's Squirrel experience, and Reed would not be
given some imaginary Mink road hidden beneath the real one. Water Reach meant
something where water truly entered the situation. Here there were only lanes,
wind, moving vehicles, and the far-side anchor.

They waited through one false opening.

Then the Roadway opened in the form they had judged.

Gale went low and fast. Alder followed the initial line, struck the planned
redirection, and changed angle without stopping. Reed ran between them, long
body close to the pavement. The Roadway moved around their passage in noise,
air, and approaching mass, but the committed run allowed no new decision.

They reached the root together.

Only on the far side did the Crossing become consequence.

Their breathing had changed. The Roadway remained loud behind them, and the
country ahead no longer felt like an extension of Home. Nothing visible had
injured them. Nevertheless, each had crossed into a condition that the body
would remember before the mind found any reason to name it.

Gale waited until Alder and Reed had recovered.

Then they continued into the Margin.

The Node lay where runoff passed through a damaged outfall. It was not
announced by any sign or boundary. The ground merely became more exact as they
approached it. Water had bitten into one side of the bank. Roots hung exposed
above a narrow current. Several pieces of human material had lodged there over
time, and among them was a length of recoverable wire, partly buried and slowly
working loose.

The place already had an occupant.

A Raccoon stood on the dry bank above the outfall. One forepaw rested upon the
wire.

“That came down to me,” the Raccoon said.

“It has not come down yet,” Gale replied.

The wire shifted under the water and struck stone.

The Raccoon tightened its hold. “Then you had better leave it alone.”

No one had discovered a resource waiting politely to be gathered. They had
found a place, a changing condition, and another animal's claim. The wire would
soon be carried into the culvert. If anyone meant to keep it, something had to
be decided while it was still there.

The first decision was not to contest the Raccoon directly.

Alder found a position above the lower waterline where the hardwood lever could
be set against exposed root and broken concrete. Reed could reach the moving
wire from below without climbing onto the bank the Raccoon held. It was an
evasive answer to the claim: recover the material first, then settle what
followed.

Alder fixed the brace.

Reed entered the current.

For several moments the plan worked exactly as the ground suggested it might.
Reed moved beneath the hanging roots, found footing in the channel, and reached
the wire. Alder took the strain as the water pulled against them both. Gale
remained above, watching the Raccoon and the changing line.

“Now,” Alder said.

Reed heaved. Alder leaned into the Tool, turning the wire away from the culvert
mouth. Together they brought it onto a shelf of wet stone.

The shared work succeeded.

Then Reed's hind footing went.

The current turned the Mink sideways. Reed struck something hidden beneath the
brown water and came out hard against the bank. Alder held the wire. Gale
reached Reed's forelimb. Between them they brought both body and material onto
stable ground.

For a little while, no one spoke.

Reed rose, but not cleanly. One side remained guarded, and the forelimb trembled
when weight settled upon it. The injury belonged to Reed because Reed had
entered the current and struck the submerged edge. Alder's position at the
brace had shaped the action; it had not transferred its danger.

The Raccoon descended from the bank.

The wire no longer moved toward the culvert. It lay between the two parties,
recovered but unowned.

“You took it from under my paw,” the Raccoon said.

“We took it from the water,” Alder answered.

“And put it where you could carry it.”

Gale had said nothing since Reed was pulled free. Now Gale looked first at
Reed, then at the wire, and finally at the Raccoon.

The first choice had changed the situation. There was no reason to make it
again.

Gale unwrapped the Offering. Inside were flavorful berries selected at
Morningside for a meeting in which value might be recognized without either
side being defeated. Gale placed them on the stone beside the wire.

“Some for you,” Gale said. “Some for us. These besides.”

The Raccoon examined the berries and then the three travelers. Reed's Wound was
visible. Alder's brace still held the wet wire against the shelf.

“You did the dangerous part,” the Raccoon said.

“Reed did.”

“And you want half.”

“Less than half,” Gale replied. “And no quarrel when we come here again.”

That caused the Raccoon to look once more at the Offering.

The division took time. The longest useful portion remained with the Raccoon.
Morningside's party received a bounded share of Scrap that they could actually
carry. The berries did not return to Gale's Supply position. They had done what
the Offering had been brought to do and now belonged to another animal.

No friendship was declared. No enemy was made. The Node remained the same
outfall, though its history had changed. The wire had been removed from the
current and divided there. If the parties met again, neither would meet the
other for the first time.

They left before the light failed.

Reed could travel, though the Wound altered every piece of ground. Gale
shortened the lead. Alder selected connections that did not require Reed to
twist or climb sharply. None of this erased the injury. It merely allowed the
party to continue carrying it.

Morningside's first Outpost stood within reach before the next long movement
home.

It was a modest thing beneath a sound piece of discarded material, made dry by
careful fitting and strengthened with bindings that had been prepared at Home.
No Citizen lived there. No fire burned, and no attendant came out to receive
them. Yet the place held because Morningside had spent material, Workshop
capacity, and Builder commitment to make it hold.

That night they committed to Stopover.

The Stopover consumed time. It was not a pause taken outside the world.

They settled Reed where the Wound could remain still. The injury was cleaned
and bound through ordinary expedition care. By morning, Reed could travel
without the immediate worsening that had threatened on the previous ground.
The stabilization would hold for a limited journey. Home would provide the
actual recovery.

The Outpost gave them something less than sanctuary and more than exposed
sleep. The Roadway could still be heard. Wind entered through small openings.
Yet no animal had to wake at every movement in the grass.

They placed part of the recovered Scrap in the Outpost's marked dry storage.
The remainder stayed with the party. Gale's Offering remained spent. Alder's
brace remained usable. The fishing line stayed wound against Gale's chest.

At morning they turned toward Home.

There was no command by which return replaced travel. Gale led south because
south was where Morningside stood. They moved through the same country, crossed
the same kinds of ground, and approached the Roadway from its farther Staging
Post with the same bodies that had left—except that those bodies now carried a
Wound, a completed bargain, and the weight of what had been learned.

The return opening was not a generous one.

They accepted it because it was sufficient.

Gale committed first. Alder ran near Reed, prepared to take weight if the
stabilized limb failed. Traffic passed, air struck them, and the pavement
seemed longer than it had before. Still, the Crossing remained one complete
passage. None of them stopped in the Roadway. None was left between lanes while
the others reached safety.

They gained the Home Median together.

Only then did the land begin to look familiar in small ways. The drainage run
appeared beside them. A particular bent stem still touched the water. The Sound
Wall stood at its known angle beyond the grass. Farther on, the first made
connection of Morningside emerged from the vegetation.

They traveled until they reached it.

Homecoming began there.

The Colony received the travelers before it received their goods.

Gale was named first. Alder followed. Reed came last, moving carefully but
under their own strength. Those waiting saw the Wound before the Scrap. They
saw the empty place where Gale's Offering had been carried, the brace still at
Alder's side, the fishing line against Gale's chest, and the dirt and water
dried into every coat.

Only after the three had been recognized did Morningside count what had
returned.

The remaining Scrap entered the stores. Unspent Supplies were taken back into
the prepared pool. Expedition positions were cleared. The Offering remained
gone because it had been given. Reed's Wound remained because arriving Home
did not make it otherwise. Work, Role support, and Civic Balance changed again
as three Citizens became present.

A Hearth was available. Care could begin at once.

Reed looked toward it, then back at Gale and Alder.

“The wire?” Reed asked.

“Counted,” said someone from the stores.

“The Raccoon?”

“Not counted,” Alder said.

Gale looked outward once more, past the first connection and the open ground
beyond it. The Roadway was still there, carrying its weather of sound through
Elmwater.

Then Gale turned inward with the others.

For that night, the road was something heard beyond the walls.

# 2. Morningside Story Canon through Tier III

survival requires reaching farther. Cobb believes reach without redundancy
magnifies loss. Gale keeps returning with observations that neither position
can possess alone.

## Founding and First Anchors

Interchange work approaches Nine Beeches across two mornings before the
playable rupture reaches the ancestral Den. Rowan, Cobb, and Gale escape as
three Body Units. Rowan receives Focus and becomes the first Leader. Cobb tests
every uncertain foothold. Gale alone looks back.

After the final montage Crossing, the exhausted party enters Elmwater, a
Wooded Median shaped by mature trees, interrupted canopy, exposed gaps, and a
narrow drainage run. During the first quiet inspection of that ground, Gale
finds a length of weathered fishing line and keeps it as the first useful thing
the new Home gives them. Cobb carries a bent brass screw from Nine Beeches;
Rowan protects one pressed beech leaf.

The eastern side takes the light first. Rowan proposes **Morningside**. Cobb
would rather name the ground. Their argument enters the Colony before its first
Place.

Cobb leads the first Builder work. Workshop, Residence, and Garden occupy
distinct anchors whose connections make future alternatives visible. The first
Away return establishes the temptation of Squirrel Strained Carry: the party
accepts material beyond secured capacity, loses one loose piece to an immediate
Jostle result, and carries the rest Home. Morningside's recurring question is
already present—how much possibility can be carried before possibility itself
becomes unstable?

## Linked Ways

Tassel arrives with a cheerful account whose harmless details change in every
telling. Wick is born into a Colony where the Web is ordinary ground and Nine
Beeches inherited history. Vane's Crafter practice attends to wind, water, and
the behavior of fastenings. Lilt's Sparrow calls become familiar before the
Colony provides a concealed social perch and welcomes a new Leader.

Early construction extends more quickly than it reinforces. During a
heavy-wind Home MEET, one prominent connection becomes unusable while a modest
alternate way preserves access. The event makes Cobb's case materially
persuasive without making Rowan's outward ambition foolish. Subsequent work
closes loops as well as extending lines.

At seven Body Units, the Civic Dedication strengthens anchors, bindings, and
alternate ways throughout Home. Recognition names Morningside **Linked Ways**.

## Living Web

Larch arrives from a failed Squirrel Colony, proving both that communities
exist beyond Morningside and that connection alone guarantees nothing. Alder
prefers low dependable ways to the highest possible line. Rivet turns every
human-made fragment over before deciding whether it belongs.

Reed opens Mink Water Reach when current, runoff, or flooded ground matters.
Latch opens Raccoon Latchwork when an accessible human-made closure enters the
situation. Their Signatures expand situated choices without making exploration
safe or automatic.

Gale inspects the location proposed for Morningside's first Outpost and returns
with two inseparable facts: the ground would extend the Colony's practical
range, and its first approach offers poor shelter. Rowan hears possibility;
Cobb hears the cost of reaching it. The player chooses when Home is stable
enough to commit Builder shares and Workshop capacity. The completed Outpost
extends rest, storage, and Colony synchronization without stationing a Citizen
there.

At twelve Body Units, Morningside's separate branches have become loops with
credible alternatives. Recognition names the durable Colony a **Living Web**.
The Rowan–Cobb argument remains useful because neither one describes every
future choice.

## 3.1 Scenario assumptions

### Current premises

- Morningside is at Tier III, **Living Web**.
- Population is 12 Citizens and 12 Body Units.
- Gale, Alder, and Reed form a legal three-Citizen, three-Body-Unit party.
- Gale and Alder each contribute 10 secured Carry and 10 possible Strained
  Carry.
- Reed, as a Mink, contributes 10 secured Carry.
- Party capacity is therefore 30 secured Carry, 20 additional Strained Carry,
  and 50 absolute maximum Cargo.
- Daily Sustenance demand is one per total resident Body Unit. Away Citizens
  remain included.
- Four Day Bands form one day: Morning, Midday, Evening, Night.
- Time-based contributions accrue at one quarter of their daily value per
  eligible Band.
- Fractions remain hidden until DAWN releases whole amounts.
- Homecoming restores eligible Civic Shares immediately, without retroactively
  granting a full day of contribution.

## 3.18 What the exercise reveals

### One Sustenance per Body Unit creates a credible equilibrium

```text
Daily demand: 12
Daily Cultivation: 12
Structural daily balance: 0, before spoilage or disruption
```

A competent Tier III Colony can sustain ordinary life. Reserves and
Preservation then answer spoilage, winter, disrupted production, Guests,
Projects, and Pressure instead of constantly subsidizing an extreme structural
deficit. The original two-per-Body-Unit pass ended at 89 Sustenance; reducing
the baseline to one, together with the integrated Caretaker Provisioning model,
ends this six-DAWN exercise at 146.

### Role concentration makes Launch meaningful

Taking two Builders and the only Watchkeeper creates two exact Pressures while
leaving production intact. The player chooses which forms of civic
vulnerability Home accepts, rather than merely choosing “strong” travelers.

### Outpost transfer behaves cleanly

The selected 26 Cargo enters pooled Stores. No local cache, transport fiction,
stationed Citizen, or interface ceremony is required.

### Supply skinning appears robust

The Offering becomes berries in this MEET without creating berry inventory.
The replacement Offering prepared at Home remains anonymous and may receive a
different concrete form in a later situation.

### Persistent Tool identity also works

Alder's smooth hardwood Brace can brace a root, prop a loose covering, hold a
gap, resist movement, or support another authored response. The object remains
the same; only its situated use changes.

### Wound recovery is already represented by Caretaker Load

Reed's Wound raises ordinary Caretaker Load from one to two while Reed occupies
the available Hearth:

```text
Capacity 2 − Load 2 = Covered
```

That one balance change is the civic cost. Recovery needs time and a Hearth but
does not also remove a Caretaker from Capacity, consume a Project slot, or
reduce Storehouse Provisioning. Morningside loses its former Caretaker
Readiness without manufacturing a second charge for the same Wound. Had
Capacity been only one, the uncovered Patient Load would have become one
Caretaker Pressure while the occupied Hearth's recovery clock continued.

### Strained Carry may be forgiving

At six Strained Cargo out of twenty:

```text
3% Jostle chance per Squirrel
≈ 5.9% chance that at least one of the two checks loses a unit
```

Even maximum Strained Carry produces only a 10% check per Squirrel. That may be
correct for a small persistent risk, but the bulging-cheeks premise should be
tested across several expeditions before final tuning.

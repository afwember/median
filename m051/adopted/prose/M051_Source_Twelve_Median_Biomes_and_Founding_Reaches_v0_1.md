# MEDIAN m0.5.1 Source — Twelve Median Biomes and Founding Reaches

**Version:** 0.1

**Status:** Working Source for authorial and visual development

**Scope:** m0.5.1 development; outside the locked m0.5.0 compile

**Purpose:** Propose a twelve-biome corridor vocabulary and six curated founding relationships for visual exploration and later design review.

This document is a Source, not a finished specification. Its proposals may be revised after visual development. It does not change m0.5.0, establish final mechanics, revive the earlier procedural Anchor Point system, or make any biome the exclusive territory of a species.

---

## 1. Governing distinction

Every biome is available to every core species throughout the extended corridor. A biome does not belong to Mouse, Rabbit, or Squirrel.

A **biome** is the broad environmental grammar of a Reach: its ground, vegetation, water, exposure, infrastructure, spatial rhythm, and recurring forms of shelter and obstruction.

A **Reach** is one particular occurrence of a biome. It has its own arrangement, history, landmarks, and local conditions.

A **founding Reach** is a curated Reach whose local topology makes the selected species' colony grammar immediately legible. Founding compatibility belongs to the particular site configuration, not to exclusive biome ownership.

The same biome may therefore serve two very different functions:

- as a species-compatible founding Reach at the beginning of one campaign; or
- as an unchanged corridor Reach encountered by any other species later.

The world does not rearrange itself around the selected species. The founding sequence selects an appropriate place within the shared world.

> **Randomize the contents of the starting territory, not the spatial grammar that makes the species meaningful.**

## 2. Species-relative founding requirements

### 2.1 Mouse — JOIN

A Mouse founding Reach needs existing edges, enclosure, seams, cavities, or infrastructure capable of becoming one protected inhabited body. Expansion should plausibly proceed from room to wing to connected second house.

A visually successful Mouse site should suggest:

- continuous or joinable boundaries;
- sheltered low-level circulation;
- opportunities to thicken an inhabited envelope;
- distinctions between dry interior, wet or exposed exterior, and protected transition;
- a settlement that can become more connected as it grows.

The terrain should not force Mouse into a collection of detached picturesque huts. A bridge between otherwise unrelated clusters does not by itself create JOIN.

### 2.2 Rabbit — GATHER

A Rabbit founding Reach needs cover, several plausible household refuges, common safe ground, and multiple paths back into concealment. Expansion should plausibly proceed from a complete court to a daughter court and eventual connection between social centers.

A visually successful Rabbit site should suggest:

- a shared clearing or court;
- household-scale refuge around its perimeter;
- brush, banks, roots, deadfall, or other protective edges;
- more than one retreat path;
- room for communal life without monumental construction.

The court should feel protected by its surrounding terrain rather than exposed like a human village square.

### 2.3 Squirrel — CONNECT

A Squirrel founding Reach needs meaningful separation: multiple anchors, differences in height, gaps, interrupted surfaces, or structures that become useful only when joined by routes. Expansion should plausibly proceed from reach to reinforcement to a resilient network of loops.

A visually successful Squirrel site should suggest:

- several nodes rather than one dominant central building;
- vertical and horizontal movement;
- bridges, runs, trunks, ledges, posts, cables, or comparable route opportunities;
- alternate paths that can emerge as the colony develops;
- a settlement whose continuity resides in its network.

A central tree or tower may be a founding anchor, but it should not become a squirrel Town Center surrounded by incidental shelters.

## 3. Proposed twelve-biome roster

| # | Biome | Primary environmental identity | Founding relationship |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rootbank Meadow** | Grass, eroded banks, exposed roots, shallow hollows, and sheltered clearings | Rabbit |
| 2 | **Bramble Hollow** | Dense thorn and vine cover organized around narrow passages and protected openings | Rabbit |
| 3 | **Culvert Garden** | Drainage infrastructure, wet soil, raised banks, stone, and seasonal flow | Mouse |
| 4 | **Concrete Trench** | Retaining faces, hard seams, broken slabs, runoff channels, heat, and shade lines | Mouse |
| 5 | **Wooded Median** | Trunks, canopy, roots, fallen branches, shade, and interrupted visibility | Squirrel |
| 6 | **Rock Cut** | Shelves, fissures, ledges, sparse trees, height changes, and natural gaps | Squirrel |
| 7 | **Thin Grass Ribbon** | Narrow exposed ground, long sightlines, wind, and road proximity | Corridor |
| 8 | **Creek Split** | A persistent longitudinal stream dividing banks, islands, and crossings | Corridor |
| 9 | **Pond Hollow** | Standing water, reeds, saturated ground, seasonal margins, and concealed approaches | Corridor |
| 10 | **Interchange Expanse** | Broad and disorienting territory among diverging roads and scattered infrastructure | Corridor |
| 11 | **Overpass Shadow** | Columns, beams, abutments, dry recesses, echo, vibration, and artificial shade | Unusual corridor |
| 12 | **Abandoned Works** | Incomplete drainage, gravel beds, pipe, rebar, cut earth, and interrupted construction | Unusual corridor |

The six founding relationships above are proposed starting selections, not statements that the other species cannot inhabit or build within those biomes later.

## 4. Detailed biome notes

### 4.1 Rootbank Meadow

#### Environmental character

Rootbank Meadow is broad enough to read as open country but broken by low rises, erosion cuts, exposed roots, shallow banks, grass clumps, and irregular shrub cover. The Reach should alternate between visible common ground and immediate refuge rather than becoming an uninterrupted lawn.

The strongest founding instance contains a modest central clearing bounded on several sides by banks or roots. Those boundaries create household-scale pockets without completely separating them from one another.

#### Rabbit founding potential

Rootbank Meadow gives Rabbit a legible first court. The safe ground is already present, but its social meaning emerges through the placement of shelters, household edges, paths, stores, and communal Practices.

The colony should spread around and through the terrain rather than replacing it. The court remains visually primary. Later growth may establish a daughter court behind another bank or root mass.

#### Other species' corridor readings

- Mouse reads root seams, low hollows, and fallen material as discontinuous shelter opportunities rather than an immediately joined estate.
- Squirrel reads scattered shrubs, roots, and occasional posts or branches as a low network with limited vertical reach.

#### Visual cautions

- Do not make the Reach a manicured meadow.
- Do not place every shelter at equal intervals around a perfect circle.
- Avoid a human village-green composition with freestanding cottages facing an ornamental center.
- Preserve irregular cover and multiple escape paths.

### 4.2 Bramble Hollow

#### Environmental character

Bramble Hollow is dominated by honeysuckle, thorn, deadfall, leaf litter, interwoven stems, narrow animal runs, and pockets where the cover opens unexpectedly. Visibility is short. The Reach should feel larger from within than it appears from above.

Its defining contrast is between nearly impassable cover and small spaces of remarkable safety.

#### Rabbit founding potential

Rabbit makes a community from the hollow's protected openings. Several household refuges can face one shared interior without requiring rigid architecture. The surrounding thicket supplies both boundary and concealment.

The founding site should allow the first court to expand by clearing and tending passages rather than simply cutting the thicket away. A daughter court may appear beyond a dense screen while remaining connected by more than one protected route.

#### Other species' corridor readings

- Mouse reads the lower stem mesh as covered micro-runs, seed traps, and temporary wall texture.
- Squirrel reads the upper bramble and deadfall as a low secondary network that may connect to stronger anchors elsewhere.

#### Visual cautions

- The thicket must remain spatially legible enough for play.
- Do not turn the bramble into a decorative hedge around an exposed court.
- Do not overbuild; Rabbit habitation should remain partly discovered within the cover.

### 4.3 Culvert Garden

#### Environmental character

Culvert Garden centers on a drainage mouth or linked drainage structure, a narrow flow or seasonal trickle, damp soil, raised dry banks, stones, sedges, volunteer plants, and material deposited by runoff. The culvert is an active environmental organ, not a scenic tunnel.

Water level and flood history should be visible in staining, debris lines, scoured ground, and the distinction between ordinary flow and high-water refuge.

#### Mouse founding potential

The culvert may participate directly in JOIN. Buildings touching its structure or coherently connected through it can form one aligned estate. Mouse development may occupy the banks before, beside, above, and around the opening rather than flattening every room onto the culvert face.

The stream should leave meaningful dry player space. JOIN may move across the culvert, along its rim, and into attached rooms while maintaining protected internal circulation.

#### Other species' corridor readings

- Rabbit reads the culvert as severe-weather shelter, an escape mouth, a hard boundary, and a potentially dangerous deep pocket.
- Squirrel reads it as a low node or crossing understructure that needs routes upward to stronger anchors.

#### Visual cautions

- Do not make the settlement a flat dollhouse facade around the pipe.
- Do not turn ordinary flow into a broad river or bog.
- Keep flood pressure visibly credible.
- Preserve open buildable banks so colony placement remains a player choice.

### 4.4 Concrete Trench

#### Environmental character

Concrete Trench is a depressed or heavily hardened median with retaining faces, seams, drainage slots, broken slab, exposed aggregate, narrow soil pockets, reflected heat, sudden shade, and concentrated runoff. Vegetation survives opportunistically rather than covering the hardscape completely.

The Reach should feel engineered at human scale and involuntarily habitable at animal scale.

#### Mouse founding potential

Continuous walls and slab edges give Mouse a powerful JOIN grammar. Chambers can accrete against seams, beneath lips, inside drainage voids, and along connected protected runs. Growth should increasingly make the trench feel inhabited from within without disguising its concrete origin.

#### Other species' corridor readings

- Rabbit finds shade and some hard boundaries but limited digging, difficult concealment, and the danger of channelized escape.
- Squirrel finds climbable faces, seams, low ledges, and occasional infrastructure anchors, but fewer living routes.

#### Visual cautions

- Avoid post-apocalyptic ruin imagery.
- Retain live traffic, maintenance traces, drainage, salt, heat, and modern infrastructure.
- Do not transform the trench into a miniature stone city.

### 4.5 Wooded Median

#### Environmental character

Wooded Median contains mature or closely spaced trees, saplings, canopy breaks, roots, fallen branches, leaf litter, shade, and limited long sightlines. The highway remains perceptible through noise, flashes of vehicles, barriers, and openings between trunks.

#### Squirrel founding potential

Several trees or large branches provide the initial nodes. The colony begins with provisional reaches between them and develops through reinforcement, alternate paths, vertical separation, and loops.

The founding composition should not allow one magnificent tree to consume the entire spatial identity. The player needs multiple viable anchors and meaningful choices about which connection to establish first.

#### Other species' corridor readings

- Mouse reads roots, litter, fallen bark, and trunk bases as low cover and fragmented enclosure.
- Rabbit reads root pockets, deadfall, and brush clearings as refuge, but dense trunks and short sightlines may complicate shared ground.

#### Visual cautions

- Preserve the highway setting; do not let the image become generic woodland fantasy.
- Do not prebuild a complete rope-web before the colony exists.
- Bridges must terminate at credible nodes and later acquire alternate routes.

### 4.6 Rock Cut

#### Environmental character

Rock Cut is defined by layered stone, fissures, shelves, narrow channels, elevation changes, sparse vegetation, occasional wind-shaped trees, and abrupt gaps. It may sit where the roadway passes through or around exposed geology.

The rock should form usable but incomplete spatial islands. Height and separation are visible immediately.

#### Squirrel founding potential

Rock Cut expresses CONNECT without relying on a forest. Ledges become nodes; branches, roots, posts, and constructed runs bridge the gaps. The colony's identity emerges from making a discontinuous place traversable.

Player agency depends on having several plausible route alignments. The terrain should present choices rather than a single obvious bridge between predetermined platforms.

#### Other species' corridor readings

- Mouse reads fissures and undercuts as excellent individual shelters that may be difficult to join into one body.
- Rabbit reads shelves and pockets as refuge but may find shared courts constrained or exposed.

#### Visual cautions

- Avoid majestic mountain-country scale; this remains a highway median Reach.
- Keep roads, barriers, cuts, drainage, and regional vegetation materially present.
- Squirrels should inhabit routes and nodes, not merely pose on scenic rocks.

### 4.7 Thin Grass Ribbon

#### Environmental character

Thin Grass Ribbon is a narrow Reach with low vegetation, few substantial objects, strong wind, long sightlines, and constant awareness of both roads. Its apparent simplicity is its identity.

Minor features—a lone shrub, damaged delineator, tire fragment, shallow depression, or weed-thick drainage seam—carry unusual importance because so little else interrupts the ground.

#### Corridor value

This biome tests exposure, distance, observation, and the value of small cover. It makes ordinary roadside objects feel consequential. It should not compensate for its austerity by filling the Reach with picturesque debris.

#### Species-relative readings

- Mouse searches for continuous low edges that are scarce and therefore precious.
- Rabbit sees feeding ground and long warning distance paired with dangerous exposure.
- Squirrel sees isolated anchors and fragile connections with little vertical redundancy.

#### Visual cautions

- Sparse does not mean empty or featureless.
- Do not soften the road's proximity.
- Preserve enough navigation structure that Field movement remains readable.

### 4.8 Creek Split

#### Environmental character

Creek Split contains a persistent longitudinal stream that divides the Reach into banks, intermittent islands, crossings, undercut edges, deposits, and seasonal high-water routes. Unlike Culvert Garden, the watercourse organizes the whole Reach rather than emerging from one dominant structure.

#### Corridor value

The creek creates parallel territories and makes crossings locally important. Familiar paths may change with rain, erosion, obstruction, or seasonal flow. Ecology can be abundant without making travel easy.

#### Species-relative readings

- Mouse seeks bank seams, roots, deposited material, and protected crossings but confronts interruption of JOIN.
- Rabbit reads the banks as possible parallel cover systems with limited safe crossing points.
- Squirrel treats separated banks and islands as route nodes, especially where branches or infrastructure span the channel.

#### Visual cautions

- Keep the creek small enough to belong inside the median.
- Show longitudinal continuity rather than a decorative foreground stream.
- Do not turn every crossing into a built bridge before discovery.

### 4.9 Pond Hollow

#### Environmental character

Pond Hollow is a low basin containing standing water, reeds, saturated margins, seasonal mud, insects, amphibian life, concealed approaches, and patches of temporarily usable dry ground. Its shape and accessibility change more than its identity.

#### Corridor value

This biome emphasizes water margin, uncertain footing, concealment, disease or contamination possibility, and strong ecological presence. It can make Guests associated with water or damp ground feel native to the world rather than appended to it.

#### Species-relative readings

- Mouse finds rich edge material and shelter pockets but faces moisture spreading through low interiors.
- Rabbit finds cover and feeding opportunities while needing reliable dry refuge and escape ground.
- Squirrel uses reeds, shrubs, posts, and overhanging branches as a network above difficult terrain.

#### Visual cautions

- Avoid a picturesque woodland pond detached from the highway.
- Show runoff, barriers, litter drift, culvert influence, or another corridor cause.
- Preserve a clear distinction between ordinary wetland and flood event.

### 4.10 Interchange Expanse

#### Environmental character

Interchange Expanse is an unusually broad Reach created where roadways divide, merge, curve, or cross nearby. It contains scattered infrastructure, irregular barriers, long and conflicting sightlines, isolated vegetation clusters, and several apparent directions of travel.

Its defining pressure is not merely exposure but orientation: the Reach is difficult to read as one coherent place.

#### Corridor value

This biome supports route choice, mistaken direction, distant landmarks, multiple possible continuations, predator visibility, and the feeling that the highway system has briefly opened into a country of its own.

#### Species-relative readings

- Mouse sees widely separated shelter seams and a difficult problem of protected continuity.
- Rabbit sees possible courts of cover separated by extensive exposed ground.
- Squirrel sees many potential anchors, but their distance may make early routes fragile.

#### Visual cautions

- Do not represent the Expanse as a generic large meadow.
- The diverging highway geometry must explain its breadth and disorientation.
- Keep the animal-scale route legible despite the large view.

### 4.11 Overpass Shadow

#### Environmental character

Overpass Shadow exists beneath an overhead roadway or closely associated bridge structure. Columns, beams, abutments, ledges, expansion joints, drainage stains, protected dead spaces, echo, vibration, artificial shade, and sudden light boundaries dominate the Reach.

This is one of the roster's deliberately unusual biomes. It should feel like entering a different architectural climate while remaining continuous with the corridor.

#### Corridor value

The Reach supports vertical exploration, weather shelter, darkness, concentrated runoff, human debris, correspondence or flight encounters, and unusual routes above ordinary animal sightlines.

#### Species-relative readings

- Mouse reads abutment seams, drainage recesses, protected bases, and debris chambers as possible enclosure.
- Rabbit reads broad shade and sheltered edges alongside echo, hard ground, and potentially trapping boundaries.
- Squirrel reads columns, beams, seams, sign structures, and ledges as an artificial canopy.

#### Visual cautions

- Do not treat it as an underground dungeon or abandoned ruin.
- Traffic remains active overhead.
- Scale, vibration, water staining, and modern materials should remain unmistakable.
- Its exceptional visual language is a reason to encounter it later, not automatically a reason to use it as a squirrel start.

### 4.12 Abandoned Works

#### Environmental character

Abandoned Works is an interrupted highway project partly reclaimed by weather and vegetation: incomplete drainage, gravel beds, stacked or scattered pipe, rebar cages, cut earth, temporary barriers, erosion-control fabric, broken pallets, and machinery fragments that no longer form a functioning worksite.

The abandonment must be old enough for animal ecology to have developed but recent enough that the human intention remains legible.

#### Corridor value

This unusual biome offers a dense field of things built for purposes never completed. It supports unstable shelter, material abundance, blocked routes, hidden voids, sharp boundaries, and evidence of sudden human disappearance from one local project—not from the world.

#### Species-relative readings

- Mouse sees prefabricated chambers and joinable material separated by unsafe open work ground.
- Rabbit sees earth cuts, fabric-covered banks, pipe shadows, and pockets of returning vegetation.
- Squirrel sees rebar, fencing, posts, stacked pipe, and temporary structures as a precarious artificial network.

#### Visual cautions

- Do not imply a globally post-apocalyptic setting.
- Avoid an implausibly intact active construction site with no humans present.
- Do not make salvage abundance erase the environmental danger or incompleteness.

## 5. Founding roster as paired instruction

Each species receives two founding-biome relationships with contrasting expressions of the same colony grammar.

| Species | Founding biome A | Founding biome B | Contrast being tested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mouse | Culvert Garden | Concrete Trench | One dominant infrastructural organ versus continuous hardened edges |
| Rabbit | Rootbank Meadow | Bramble Hollow | Open ground made safe versus concealment made communal |
| Squirrel | Wooded Median | Rock Cut | Living canopy network versus routes across exposed separation |

This pairing allows the art to test whether JOIN, GATHER, and CONNECT survive a change of material language. If both members of a pair produce essentially the same settlement with different scenery, the species grammar has not been expressed strongly enough.

## 6. Studio visual-development brief

### 6.1 What the first images should establish

Before showing a colony, each biome should be recognizable as an inhabitable piece of highway country. An uninhabited terrain image should already communicate:

- the roads and their relationship to the median;
- the Reach's width, longitudinal direction, and boundaries;
- its defining ground, water, vegetation, or infrastructure;
- animal-scale paths, obstructions, refuge, and exposure;
- several plausible Places without preselecting all of them;
- sufficient open or navigable player space for later construction.

The image should not be a beautiful background waiting for buildings. It should be a spatial problem whose affordances can be read.

### 6.2 Suggested founding studies

For each of the six designated founding relationships, develop at least these comparative views from a stable base terrain:

1. **Before founding:** the territory without a colony, showing why the founders might recognize its potential.
2. **Early colony:** a small settlement whose species grammar is already visible without filling the Reach.
3. **Developed colony:** growth that strengthens the same grammar rather than replacing it with generic ornament.

The base terrain should remain materially recognizable across the sequence. Development may sculpt, clear, reinforce, drain, bridge, inhabit, or tend the ground, but should not silently substitute a more convenient landscape.

### 6.3 Composition requirements

- Prefer a 4:3 landscape plate for territory and colony studies.
- Show enough of both highway carriageways, barriers, or corridor context to prevent generic fantasy-land drift.
- Keep animal scale consistent.
- Leave meaningful walkable or navigable space around structures.
- Avoid perfectly symmetrical settlement layouts.
- Show structures embedded in, attached to, or negotiating the terrain rather than sitting on one flat presentation plane.
- Preserve at least several undecided buildable areas so the scene remains a player space.
- Use recurring fixed landmarks to make growth comparisons intelligible.

### 6.4 Species tests for generated colonies

Ask of every Mouse image:

- What physically joins the settlement?
- Can protected circulation be followed through it?
- Is growth thickening one inhabited body, or merely adding cottages?

Ask of every Rabbit image:

- Where is the shared safe ground?
- Which households face or participate in it?
- Where are the alternative refuge paths and the possibility of a daughter court?

Ask of every Squirrel image:

- What are the distinct nodes?
- Which routes connect them?
- Is there an alternate path or visible future loop?
- Would removing one bridge reveal a meaningful network weakness?

### 6.5 Corridor images for the other species

A founding-biome image should not become the only visual definition of that biome. Studio should eventually test at least one founding biome as an uncolonized corridor Reach viewed by a different species.

The terrain should remain unchanged. What changes is attention:

- foreground emphasis;
- visible route opportunities;
- perceived shelter and exposure;
- landmarks selected by the observing party;
- the narrative or interface language used to describe the Reach.

This is the strongest visual proof that MEDIAN has one world interpreted by three civilizations rather than three species-themed map sets.

## 7. Questions intentionally left open

This Source does not yet settle:

- whether twelve is the final publication count;
- the exact generation or selection method for founding Reaches;
- whether the player chooses among several suitable starts or receives one through the Founding Escape;
- the frequency or adjacency rules for unusual biomes;
- final biome effects on supplies, weather, Pressure, discovery, or travel;
- whether any non-founding Reach can later host a second permanent colony;
- the final relationship between biome identity and named local terrain features;
- final terminology for any procedural feature system previously called Anchor Points.

Visual development should clarify the spatial proposal before these questions are converted into rules.

---

## 8. Working summary

The proposed roster preserves one shared highway world while guaranteeing that the opening colony can express its species from the first act of settlement.

- Mouse begins where terrain can become one inhabited body.
- Rabbit begins where refuge can become shared safe ground.
- Squirrel begins where separated nodes can become a resilient network.

Every other species may later walk through those same places unchanged and understand them differently.

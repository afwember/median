# **0. Executive Handoff**

<!--@1.1-->
## **0.1 Primary conclusion**

<!--@1.1¶1-->
The reviewed field contains many meaningful neighbors but no direct twin. MEDIAN occupies an intersection already populated by animal colony builders, small-creature survival games, home-and-expedition loops, emergent citizen simulations, persistent character-history systems, highway-crossing games, and road-ecology fiction. Its defensible originality lies in binding those elements into one coherent proposition: a permanent, low-population animal civilization built inside the active median of a living highway, where competent stewardship creates sanctuary and every departure risks a named citizen.

## **0.2 What v0.5.0 changes about the comparison**

<!--@1.2¶1-->
The earlier comparison draft could defend MEDIAN primarily through milieu: wet asphalt, live traffic, scrap culture, and a highway median treated as homeland. Version 0.5.0 adds a second and stronger line of defense: the project now possesses a distinctive complete architecture, not merely a distinctive premise.

<!--@1.2¶2-->
  - Home and Away operate at different simulation scales: the Colony grows in Dwell; Citizens grow when hazarded Away.

<!--@1.2¶3-->
  - Dwell is organized through Places, Practices, Roles, Readiness, and species-specific spatial verbs rather than factory output grids.

<!--@1.2¶4-->
  - A well-run colony can reach Quiet Equilibrium. Stability is an achieved plateau, not a temporary pause in compulsory entropy.

<!--@1.2¶5-->
  - MEET is a universal situational register used for bounded choices at Home or Away.

<!--@1.2¶6-->
  - EMBODY is a globally available Home register through which sanctuary is felt at animal scale.

<!--@1.2¶7-->
  - Mouse JOIN, Rabbit GATHER, and Squirrel CONNECT translate ecological bodily preferences into visibly different settlement grammars.

<!--@1.2¶8-->
  - Campaign Memory preserves consequences without turning emotional intimacy into a buff economy.

# **1. Canonical Market Identity**

<!--@2.1-->
## **1.1 The primary manifestation**

<!--@2.1¶1-->
The desktop/console computer game remains the canonical full-spectrum manifestation developed by the main Concept Sourcebook. It is the only form expected to express all five Registers as distinct visual and control paradigms, the complete Dwell construction grammar, continuous corridor geography, direct Crossing action, persistent Citizen records, Campaign Memory, and Home-only EMBODY experiences.

<!--@2.1¶2-->
This document does not attempt to size a production budget, forecast sales, or identify a publisher category. It defines the conceptual shelf on which the work would be understood and the distinctions the design must keep legible.

<!--@2.2-->
## **1.2 Recommended genre language**

<!--@2.2¶1-->
| **Level**                | **Preferred language**                                   | **Reason**                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary label**        | Ecological colony-and-expedition strategy game           | Accurately foregrounds the Colony and the Home/Away rhythm without promising a conventional city builder or action RPG.                                     |
| **Secondary label**      | Narrative colony simulation                              | Signals named citizens, consequences, relationships, and Campaign Memory.                                                                                   |
| **Milieu label**         | Highway-median / highway-riparian animal civilization    | Names the setting’s unique imaginative geography and road-ecology basis.                                                                                    |
| **Emotional promise**    | Sanctuary, exposure, consequence, and return             | States the dramatic cycle more accurately than “cozy” or “survival” alone.                                                                                  |
| **Avoid as lead**        | Cozy animal city-builder                                 | Too broad, understates danger, and invites comparison primarily through cute screenshots.                                                                   |
| **Avoid as lead**        | RimWorld with mice / Watership Down meets Age of Empires | Useful private shorthand, but reductive and likely to misstate both mechanics and tone when used publicly.                                                  |
| **Authored design term** | Attachment-first base builder                            | Names the v0.5.0 priority: base systems are judged by how well they create attachment to one inhabited colony, not only by throughput or survival pressure. |

<!--@2.3-->
## **1.3 Audience intersection**

<!--@2.3¶1-->
MEDIAN is not designed for a single existing audience silo. Its likely readers or players arrive through overlapping interests:

<!--@2.3¶2-->
  - colony and settlement strategy, especially players who value legible growth but are fatigued by industrial production-chain optimization;

<!--@2.3¶3-->
  - emergent or procedural character stories, persistent wounds, relationships, memorials, and low-population attachment;

<!--@2.3¶4-->
  - animal-scale fiction that treats animals as culturally intelligent without erasing bodily vulnerability;

<!--@2.3¶5-->
  - ecological and environmental storytelling grounded in active human infrastructure rather than post-human ruin;

<!--@2.3¶6-->
  - tabletop and sourcebook readers who enjoy systems presented as complete cultural objects, even apart from software implementation.

<!--@2.4-->
## **1.4 The Concept Sourcebook’s actual product position**

<!--@2.4¶1-->

Its marketplace is therefore adjacent to RPG sourcebooks, videogame art books, fictional field guides, and independently published design objects. # **2. The v0.5.0 Distinctiveness Stack**

<!--@3¶1-->
MEDIAN should not rely on one supposedly unprecedented mechanic. Its position is cumulative. Each layer is individually comparable; the stack is the proposition.

<!--@3¶2-->
| **Distinctive layer**                     | **v0.5.0 expression**                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Active median homeland**             | The colony lives permanently between operating carriageways. Traffic is not a one-off obstacle or vanished ruin; it is weather, boundary, soundscape, resource current, and civilizational fact.         |
| **2. One emotionally central colony**     | The Home Median is not a disposable level or mobile base. It is the place whose accumulated physical and social history gives every expedition meaning.                                                  |
| **3. Low population; every name counted** | Citizens are individually apportioned at Home and individually hazarded Away. The roster is intimate enough that death, injury, retirement, and absence remain comprehensible.                           |
| **4. Two simulation scales**              | Dwell treats Citizens as equal shares of colony commitment; Away resolves their individual capacities and consequences.                                                                                  |
| **5. Species spatial identity**           | Mouse JOIN, Rabbit GATHER, and Squirrel CONNECT make settlement form, domestic feeling, and vulnerability species-specific.                                                                              |
| **6. Successful stillness**               | A well-run colony reaches real equilibrium. Good management creates time to observe, remember, and Embody rather than merely slowing decay.                                                              |
| **7. Universal MEET**                     | Discrete consequential situations use one staged choice grammar at Home and Away, from heavy winds to strangers and resource discoveries.                                                                |
| **8. Home-only EMBODY**                   | The player can participate in or accompany bounded animal-scale moments only within sanctuary. Calm Away is still Away.                                                                                  |
| **9. Non-industrial material culture**    | Citizens build from ecology and human residue without advancing toward steam factories, medieval kingdoms, or generalized technology trees.                                                              |
| **10. Campaign Memory**                   | Places and Citizens become records of what happened. Memory is not merely generated flavor and does not need to be converted into a stat bonus.                                                          |
| **11. Corridor civilization**             | Longitudinal travel, daughter colonies, margins, crossings, and the distant Metropolis imply a civilization shaped by linear infrastructure.                                                             |
| **12. Road ecology beneath the fiction**  | The setting’s paradox—barrier, corridor, refuge, hazard, edge habitat—is anchored in a real field of ecological study.                                                                                   |
| **13. Attachment-first base building**    | Construction, readiness, memory, and embodiment are selected to make one colony and its named Citizens increasingly specific and irreplaceable. Attachment is a system objective, not incidental flavor. |

<!--@3.1-->
## **2.1 The strongest new market distinction: sanctuary is playable**

<!--@3.1¶1-->
Many comparable games contain a base that is safer than the field. Version 0.5.0 makes a stronger claim: sanctuary is an achieved systemic condition with its own positive register. Dwell proves that the colony is safe; EMBODY proves that safety means something. This is more specific than a day/night shelter cycle, a morale bar, a first-person inspection camera, or a cozy idle state.

<!--@2.2#2-->
## 2.2 The attachment-first base builder

<!--@2.2#2¶1-->
“Attachment-first base builder” is an authored MEDIAN design and positioning term, not an established industry genre. It describes a base-building game in which construction, staffing, resources, pacing, and consequence are organized first around attachment to a particular inhabited place and its named residents. The phrase “sentimental base builder” remains useful as evocative internal shorthand, but attachment-first is the more exact public and analytical term: it names a design priority without implying reduced mechanical rigor, saccharine tone, or manufactured pathos.

<!--@2.2#2¶2-->
The proposition is an inversion of familiar base-building priorities. In a production-first game, attachment may emerge after the player has optimized a successful machine. In a survival-first game, attachment may emerge because the player has kept a shelter and its inhabitants alive under relentless pressure. MEDIAN contains both production and survival concerns, but subordinates them to a different end: the base must become a place the player knows, trusts, remembers, and wants to return to.

<!--@2.2.1-->
### 2.2.1 Three base-building orientations

<!--@2.2.1¶1-->
These orientations are diagnostic lenses rather than exclusive genre boxes. A single game may use all three. The distinction is which purpose governs the economy, pacing, interface, and treatment of citizens when priorities conflict.

<!--@2.2.1¶2-->
| **Orientation**      | **What the base primarily is**                                                              | **What successful play protects**                                                                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Production-first** | A productive machine whose spaces and citizens convert inputs into throughput.              | Efficiency, expansion, improved output, and increasing command over the economy.                           |
| **Survival-first**   | A defensive apparatus that holds entropy, scarcity, and disaster at bay.                    | Continued existence, recovery, and the postponement or overcoming of collapse.                             |
| **Attachment-first** | A particular inhabited place whose systems make named lives legible and worth returning to. | Stable ordinary life, accumulated memory, and specific people and Places the player does not want to lose. |

<!--@2.2.1¶3-->
Age of Empires provides a formative production-first benchmark; This War of Mine provides a severe survival-first benchmark; RimWorld demonstrates how attachment can emerge powerfully from individually tracked simulation. MEDIAN’s v0.5.0 claim is narrower and more deliberate: attachment is not merely hoped-for player behavior after the economy succeeds. It is the criterion by which the economy and its success state are designed.

<!--@2.2.2-->
### 2.2.2 How v0.5.0 makes attachment systemic

<!--@2.2.2¶1-->
The redesign does not attempt to create care through prose, portrait art, or charming animation alone. It reorganizes the game so that the colony becomes emotionally available through its mechanics:

<!--@2.2.2¶2-->
  - One permanent Home Median serves as the continuing social protagonist. Expeditions acquire meaning because a particular place and its accumulated history remain behind.

<!--@2.2.2¶3-->
  - The population remains small enough that every name is counted. Citizens are equal units of civic commitment at Home, preventing the Dwell economy from ranking friends primarily by labor efficiency.

<!--@2.2.2¶4-->
  - Home and Away operate at different simulation scales. Dwell grows the Colony; Away hazards and develops individual Citizens. Taking a Citizen away creates a comprehensible absence rather than an abstract labor penalty.

<!--@2.2.2¶5-->
  - Places, Practices, and Roles replace the logic of isolated production factories. A building matters because someone lives, works, gathers, recovers, teaches, or remembers there.

<!--@2.2.2¶6-->
  - Mouse JOIN, Rabbit GATHER, and Squirrel CONNECT cause home itself to mean something different to each species. Spatial planning produces identity, vulnerability, and feeling rather than a reskinned common build order.

<!--@2.2.2¶7-->
  - Quiet Equilibrium is an achievable plateau. Good management creates attention and leisure instead of merely slowing a universal decay clock.

<!--@2.2.2¶8-->
  - Role shortfalls become situated MEET circumstances rather than continuous numerical leakage. A weakness eventually becomes a threatened roof, exposed store, late warning, or isolated route that the player can understand and remember.

<!--@2.2.2¶9-->
  - Home-only EMBODY converts successful stewardship into direct animal-scale experience: Practice, Flow, Presence, Tenderness, and repose within the place the player helped make safe.

<!--@2.2.2¶10-->
  - Campaign Memory allows Citizens and Places to retain consequences without requiring every emotional fact to become a buff, productivity modifier, or collectible reward.

<!--@2.2.3-->
### 2.2.3 Mechanical rigor is the prerequisite for sentiment

<!--@2.2.3¶1-->
Attachment-first does not mean mechanics-light. The emotional headspace MEDIAN seeks does not open until the colony’s logical needs are legible and satisfiable. Players must understand why the colony is stable, what each Role commitment costs, what an expansion adds to Load, and what is sacrificed when Citizens leave on an Expedition. Sentiment unsupported by credible systems feels ornamental; systems that never release the player from triage leave no attention available for sentiment.

<!--@2.2.3¶2-->
The desired rhythm is therefore competence → stability → attention → attachment → chosen ambition or accepted risk. Stability must be achievable but not automatic. The player should periodically restore it after weather, growth, absence, or consequence, then be permitted to inhabit the result. The Home Loop is successful when a quiet visit feels earned rather than empty.

<!--@2.2.4-->
### 2.2.4 Design protections

<!--@2.2.4¶1-->
  - Do not use universal compulsory decay to keep the player continuously busy. Ordinary Role coverage must be capable of handling ordinary life.

<!--@2.2.4¶2-->
  - Do not turn Home assignment into an aptitude-ranking puzzle. Individual distinction belongs primarily to Away risk, consequence, and growth.

<!--@2.2.4¶3-->
  - Do not make EMBODY, Campaign Memory, or intimate Citizen scenes mandatory sources of economic advantage. Emotional attention must remain freely given.

<!--@2.2.4¶4-->
  - Do not treat quiet as wasted simulation time. Ordinary life is the positive output of a functioning colony.

<!--@2.2.4¶5-->
  - Do not let expansion erase the old settlement’s relevance. New wings, courts, routes, and daughter colonies should accumulate history rather than replace an obsolete tier.

<!--@2.2.4¶6-->
  - Do not scale the roster beyond emotional comprehension. If the player can no longer understand whose absence, wound, retirement, or death changed the colony, the attachment-first promise has failed.

<!--@2.2.4¶7-->
  - Do not reduce loss to generic efficiency damage. Important losses should attach to named Citizens, Places, relationships, and remembered circumstances.

<!--@2.2.5-->
### 2.2.5 Positioning use

<!--@2.2.5¶1-->
“Attachment-first base builder” should function as a critical and sourcebook term beneath the primary market label “ecological colony-and-expedition strategy game.” It explains why MEDIAN may resemble several familiar genres while selecting different success conditions. “Sentimental base builder” may appear in reflective design prose if sentiment is defined in its older, stronger sense: value accrued through association, memory, care, absence, and loss. It should not be used as unexplained store-page shorthand.

<!--@2.2.5¶2-->
MEDIAN does not ask players to care instead of thinking. It asks careful thought to make a place coherent enough to care about, so later decisions carry greater consequence.

<!--@2.2.6-->
### 2.2.6 Attachment-first acceptance test

<!--@2.2.6¶1-->
  - The player can identify who inhabits or is associated with important Places, not merely what each structure produces.

<!--@2.2.6¶2-->
  - A well-supported colony can remain quiet long enough for the player to observe it without fearing hidden deterioration.

<!--@2.2.6¶3-->
  - Removing a Citizen for an Expedition changes the Home posture in a clear, narratively comprehensible way.

<!--@2.2.6¶4-->
  - Expansion adds social and spatial history rather than only more capacity.

<!--@2.2.6¶5-->
  - After a MEET outcome, the player can describe what happened to the colony in situated terms rather than only reciting changed statistics.

<!--@2.2.6¶6-->
  - EMBODY and Campaign Memory reveal consequences and ordinary life without becoming optimization obligations.

<!--@2.2.6¶7-->
  - The loss or transformation of a Place or Citizen feels specific because the game has previously allowed that specificity to accumulate.

<!--@2.2.6¶8-->
Attachment-first is not a softer base builder; it changes the hierarchy of purpose: production supports stability, stability supports attention, and attention makes the colony irreplaceable.

# **3. Comparative Method**

<!--@4.1-->
## **3.1 Five comparison classes**

<!--@4.1¶1-->
| **Class**                        | **Definition**                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Current marketplace neighbor** | A game likely to be compared by screenshot, store tags, premise, or overlapping audience.                                       |
| **Structural ancestor**          | A work whose loop or simulation architecture illuminates MEDIAN even when setting and genre differ.                             |
| **Milieu relative**              | A work that shares roads, animal-scale human infrastructure, edge ecology, migration, or traffic danger.                        |
| **Tabletop precedent**           | A roleplaying or card design that shares community survival, animal vulnerability, persistent campaigns, or physical geography. |
| **Personal influence**           | A work that directly shaped the creator’s taste, emotional objective, visual language, or formative idea of play.               |

<!--@4.2-->
## **3.2 Comparison rules**

<!--@4.2¶1-->
  - Overlap does not imply derivation. Several works can independently occupy the same genre intersection.

<!--@4.2¶2-->
  - Difference should be described neutrally and specifically, not as a claim that MEDIAN is superior.

<!--@4.2¶3-->
  - Current development projects must be treated as moving targets. Announced features can change before release.

<!--@4.2¶4-->
  - Aesthetic shelf-mates such as Root or Everdell are worth acknowledging, but they should not be treated as mechanical twins merely because they feature woodland animals.

<!--@4.2¶5-->
  - The relevant question is not “What game is MEDIAN like?” but “Which expectation will this comparison create, and which MEDIAN principle must remain visible to prevent misunderstanding?”

<!--@5.4.2¶1-->

<!--@5.4.2¶2-->

<!--@5.4.2¶3-->
  - Its sanctuary is not merely interior coziness; it is the achieved condition enabling EMBODY and ordinary communal life.

<!--@5.5.2¶2-->

# **5. Strong Structural Ancestors**

<!--@6.1-->
## **5.1 This War of Mine — sanctuary, exposure, and return**

<!--@6.1¶1-->
This War of Mine establishes the clearest predecessor for the broad Home/Away rhythm: named civilians maintain a shelter during the day, while one leaves at night to scavenge dangerous locations and return with resources and moral consequences. \[12\]

<!--@6.1¶2-->
MEDIAN inherits the structural power of a home that becomes meaningful because leaving it is dangerous. Version 0.5.0 diverges by rejecting permanent desperation as the only dramatic engine. Its colony can establish civic continuity, expand, teach, remember, and attain successful stillness. The road remains lethal, but the game’s destination is not merely another night survived.

<!--@6.2-->
## **5.2 RimWorld — attachment through systemic specificity**

<!--@6.2¶1-->
RimWorld is the strongest precedent for attachment to a small roster through simulated needs, psychology, wounds, chronic conditions, relationships, environmental events, and travel. Its official description explicitly frames the game as an AI-driven story generator. \[13\]

<!--@6.2¶2-->
MEDIAN adopts the conviction that injuries, relationships, and odd individual histories create attachment. It rejects RimWorld’s unrestricted simulation breadth, body-part granularity, enormous possibility space, and combat centrality. MEDIAN is authored around a smaller dramatic grammar: Dwell, Travel, Risk, Meet, Embody, and a deliberate Campaign Record.

<!--@6.3-->
## **5.3 Wildermyth — citizens becoming histories**

<!--@6.3¶1-->
Wildermyth follows characters across careers, relationships, transformations, maiming, age, sacrifice, retirement, memory, and reappearance through Legacy. Its heroes become personal myths rather than disposable tactical units. \[14\]

<!--@6.3¶2-->
MEDIAN’s Tales, Distinctions, After-names, wounds, memorials, and Campaign Memory occupy related territory. The difference is scale and social subject: Wildermyth’s center is a heroic tactical party; MEDIAN’s citizens become histories because they belong to a colony that continues after any one of them leaves or dies.

<!--@6.4-->
## **5.4 Why these ancestors matter more than many animal games**

<!--@6.4¶1-->
The strongest mechanical lineage is not necessarily found in games with mice, rabbits, or squirrels. MEDIAN’s identity depends on how it alternates collective stewardship and individual hazard, how a small roster becomes emotionally legible, and how consequences become memory. These three non-animal works clarify those structures more directly than most woodland-animal titles.

# **6. Milieu Relatives and Kinetic Ancestors**

<!--@7.1-->
## **6.1 The Animals of Farthing Wood — migration through human danger**

<!--@7.1¶1-->
The Animals of Farthing Wood remains the strongest narrative relative for a mixed community of ordinary animals interpreting roads, predators, weather, and human development as lethal terrain. The motorway is a migration ordeal rather than a homeland, which defines the decisive inversion: MEDIAN begins where Farthing Wood’s refugees would never choose to remain.

<!--@7.2-->
## **6.2 A Search for Safe Passage — animal council meets road ecology**

<!--@7.2¶1-->
A Search for Safe Passage depicts animals forming a council to address a “Human Highway” dividing their range and combines fiction with educational material on wildlife movement and crossing design. \[26\]

<!--@7.2¶2-->
Its relevance is conceptual rather than market-threatening: animal society and real crossing ecology coexist, but the work is a conservation fable about repairing passage. MEDIAN is about the culture that forms when fragmentation itself becomes the inherited geography of home.

<!--@7.3-->
## **6.3 Over the Hedge — edge economy and human residue**

<!--@7.3¶1-->
Over the Hedge centers a woodland community whose habitat has been divided from suburban abundance by a newly built hedge, and whose economy is redirected toward human food and waste. \[25\]

<!--@7.3¶2-->
It is the strongest popular edge-economy relative: active humans are dangerous, incomprehensible, and materially generous. MEDIAN differs by replacing suburban consumption comedy with road ecology, pooled subsistence, longitudinal settlement, and serious consequences.

<!--@7.4-->
## **6.4 Frogger and Crossy Road — the isolated kinetic ancestor**

<!--@7.4¶1-->
Frogger formalized the road and river as moving hazard fields; Crossy Road modernized the same premise as an endless arcade hopper across freeways, railroads, and rivers. \[23\]\[24\]

<!--@7.4¶2-->
These games isolate the instant of Crossing and discard what comes before and after. MEDIAN keeps the bodily clarity of that danger but situates it within preparation, cargo, relationships, species movement, Exposure, rescue, consequence, and return. Crossing should remain brief enough to be frightening; the larger design exists to make the life on either side matter.

<!--@7.5-->
## **6.5 Endling, Way to the Woods, and NUTS — scale, care, and observation**

<!--@7.5¶1-->
| **Work**             | **Useful relation**                                                                                                                      | **Difference**                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Endling**          | Animal-scale survival through damaged human environments; shelter, planning, named young, teaching, and emotionally consequential care.  | More apocalyptic, accusatory, protagonist-family centered, and migration-driven. \[21\]                                           |
| **Way to the Woods** | A deer and fawn traverse abandoned roads and urban infrastructure to return home, producing strong scale contrast and vulnerable beauty. | Humanity and traffic are absent; infrastructure is ruin rather than continuing weather. The game remains unreleased. \[22\]       |
| **NUTS**             | Observation, maps, surveillance stations, and the gradual discovery of squirrel routes through a threatened environment.                 | The player is a human researcher; MEDIAN turns naturalist knowledge into the animals’ own cultural and strategic literacy. \[20\] |

# **7. Tabletop and Physical-Game Precedents**

<!--@8.1-->
## **7.1 The Warren — unavoidable TTRPG comparator**

<!--@8.1¶1-->
The Warren describes itself as a tabletop roleplaying game about intelligent rabbits making the best of a world of hazards, predators, seasons, and other rabbits; it explicitly centers survival and community, supports a two-hour session or campaign play, and follows changing generations. \[15\]

<!--@8.1¶2-->
A MEDIAN TTRPG must therefore distinguish itself through the Colony as shared campaign character, multiple core species, highway corridor ecology, Crossing as a special ritual, scrap material culture, Home/Away alternation, and the persistent Record. The Warren proves that the emotional territory is playable; it also makes vague “Watership Down RPG” positioning insufficient.

<!--@8.2-->
## **7.2 Bunnies & Burrows — historical predecessor**

<!--@8.2¶1-->
Bunnies & Burrows, first published in 1976 and returned to print in a modern edition, established intelligent rabbits confronting real predators, traps, and natural hazards, with problem solving emphasized over direct combat. \[16\]

<!--@8.2¶2-->
Its significance is historical and philosophical rather than a direct contemporary product threat. MEDIAN belongs to a long tabletop tradition of using animal vulnerability to change the meaning of danger and heroism.

<!--@8.3-->
## **7.3 Mouse Guard RPG — missions, territory, and institutional heroism**

<!--@8.3¶1-->
The Mouse Guard Roleplaying Game forms patrols and sends them on missions into the Territories, supported by extensive setting material and a formal animal society. \[17\]

<!--@8.3¶2-->
MEDIAN shares serious animal-scale adventure, weather and predator threats, mission structure, and the appeal of a rich sourcebook. It diverges from the Guard’s medieval institutions, swords, heroic public duty, and patrol-centered identity. MEDIAN’s citizens are first householders and neighbors; the colony’s needs create expeditions rather than a standing order assigning missions.

<!--@8.4-->
## **7.4 Earthborne Rangers — the campaign-card formal precedent**

<!--@8.4¶1-->
Earthborne Rangers is a cooperative customizable adventure card game in which a deck reflects personal history, then accumulates improved equipment, refined skills, and memories while the player explores an open world whose cards activate as a living environment. \[18\]

<!--@8.4¶2-->
It validates the idea that MEDIAN’s physical manifestation should be cooperative, campaign-based, environmentally responsive, and centered on persistent personal history rather than competitive rarity. MEDIAN’s fixed-content anti-consumerist doctrine and corridor tableau remain important differences.

<!--@8.5-->
## **7.5 Aftermath — the closest home-and-expedition board-game structure**

<!--@8.5¶1-->
Aftermath sends small-animal “providers” from a colony into abandoned human-scale environments on story missions, then returns resources and information to improve a home colony that faces hardship while they are away. \[19\]

<!--@8.5¶2-->
This is a strong formal precedent for colony consequences and miniature human environments. MEDIAN distinguishes itself through active humanity and traffic, species-specific spatial civilizations, lower combat emphasis, and a Home system whose reward is functioning sanctuary rather than solely survival infrastructure.

<!--@9.1.1¶3-->

<!--@9.1.1¶4-->

<!--@9.1.1¶5-->
  - Danger is serious without requiring gore or constant combat spectacle.

<!--@9.1.1¶6-->

<!--@9.1.2¶1-->

<!--@9.1.2¶2-->
  - The project broadens from one species to three core spatial civilizations and a guest ecology.

<!--@9.1.2¶3-->
  - The road is not a temporary ordeal on the way to pastoral refuge; it is the inherited geography of civilization.

<!--@9.1.2¶4-->

# **9. Road Ecology and the Highway-Riparian Position**

<!--@10.1-->
## **9.1 The real field beneath the setting**

<!--@10.1¶1-->
Road ecology is the scientific study of how roads, road systems, and traffic affect natural systems. The UC Davis Road Ecology Center identifies barrier and mortality effects, pollution, traffic noise and light, hydrologic and geomorphic change, invasive species, and other ecosystem modifications as part of the field. \[30\]

<!--@10.1¶2-->
This matters commercially and artistically because MEDIAN’s premise is not arbitrary surrealism. The road can simultaneously operate as lethal surface, movement barrier, edge habitat, corridor, disturbance zone, pollution source, and altered hydrologic system. The fiction intensifies a real ecological contradiction.

<!--@10.2-->
## **9.2 Verified MEDIAN-like findings**

<!--@10.2¶1-->
A 2022 wood-mouse study found that mice avoided crossing the road, frequently used verges, and often moved parallel to the road. This closely supports MEDIAN’s longitudinal-versus-transverse geography. \[31\]

<!--@10.2¶2-->
Field research has documented small mammals foraging in vegetated highway medians reached through wildlife passages, showing that medians can function as more than empty separators. \[32\]

<!--@10.2¶3-->
Federal wildlife-crossing guidance explicitly recognizes canopy crossings for tree squirrels and other arboreal or semi-arboreal mammals, validating infrastructure-as-canopy logic. \[33\]

<!--@10.2¶4-->
Roadside effects are contingent. The European-rabbit motorway study found reduced predator and hunting pressure near the road but did not find maximum rabbit abundance nearest the motorway. \[35\]

<!--@10.3-->
## **9.3 Road ecology versus highway-riparian**

<!--@10.3¶1-->
“Highway-riparian” is not presented as an established scientific term. It is MEDIAN’s authored cultural and imaginative geography: traffic flows become currents; shoulders and sound walls become banks; medians become islands; exits and interchanges become tributaries; runoff behaves like flood; human residue arrives as drift; settlements orient longitudinally along a channel they cannot control.

<!--@10.3¶2-->
The distinction should remain explicit:

<!--@10.3¶3-->
| **Term**             | **Use**                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Road ecology**     | The real scientific foundation: fragmentation, mortality, movement, edge effects, noise, light, pollution, hydrology, and mitigation. |
| **Highway-riparian** | MEDIAN’s civilizational metaphor: the lived cultural system that animals might build beside and within those flows.                   |

<!--@10.4-->
## **9.4 Nonfiction companion**

<!--@10.4¶1-->
Ben Goldfarb’s Crossings: How Road Ecology Is Shaping the Future of Our Planet is the strongest broad nonfiction companion identified in this research. It treats roads as forces of death, disruption, fragmented movement, noise, pollution, and ecological redesign while also documenting mitigation such as wildlife bridges and tunnels. \[34\]

It occupies a crowded set of shelves in a combination the reviewed field does not currently duplicate: colony strategy without industrial destiny; animal vulnerability without solitary survival; road ecology without post-human ruin; named character history without heroic-party primacy; and sanctuary not as a pause between systems, but as the system’s reason for existing.</em></td>

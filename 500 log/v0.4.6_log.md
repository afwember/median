# MEDIAN v0.4.6 — Log

Opened during the v0.4.5 assembly pass. **Nothing here is decided.** These are questions and observations that surfaced while writing sixty-four settled decisions into canon, logged rather than solved so that assembly did not become another design thread.

Numbered **V6-**.

## Calls made during assembly

*Not open questions — decisions taken to finish the pass, recorded so they can be overturned cheaply if they were the wrong ones.*

### V6-1 — "body-unit" is set lowercase

V5-1 heads its entry *The Body-Unit*, capitalized. V5-59's scope table lists *Body Unit* in the **plain** column, unhyphenated, as a designer-facing term. Two settled decisions, two spellings.

**Resolved as lowercase hyphenated — `body-unit` — throughout.** The hyphen-compound convention requires a **capitalized** first element, so a lowercase hyphen reads unambiguously as ordinary English compounding rather than as an in-world term. *Body-Unit* capitalized would have claimed to be a word the animals say, which it is not.

Appendix A and Section 6.4 both carry it in this form.

### V6-2 — Appendix H is new, and stubbed

V5-56 requires an appendix table for the descriptive aptitude bands and does not say which appendix. The assembly brief allocates A through G and does not provide one.

**Added as Appendix H**, stubbed on the same pattern as E, F and G. If the bands belong somewhere else — folded into A, or into a future interface appendix — H is cheap to dissolve.

### V6-3 — the two renumberings cancel, so Sections 35–38 keep their numbers

The brief anticipates that dissolving Section 34 renumbers Section 35. It does not, because the same edition also inserts a section at 10.

Inserting Choice-Event Cards moves the old 11–33 up by one. Dissolving the old 34 moves everything after it back down by one. **The old 35–38 therefore keep the numbers they had**, the document still runs to thirty-eight sections, and the only sections whose numbers changed are the old 11 through 34.

Stated in Appendix C so nobody re-derives it.

### V6-4 — V5-6 applied, though the application map omits it

V5-6 (cooperative carrying uses combined party capacity) is settled and names §13 as its home, but does not appear in the brief's section map. Applied at Section 14 alongside the weight ladder, where it is the operative consequence of the ladder. Flagged in case the omission was deliberate.

### V6-5 — Section 21.4 retitled

The Group G triage flags §20.4's single-species callout as document-history in disguise. V5-64's applied tables do not include it, so it was still standing. Removed, and the subsection retitled **Guest accommodation and depiction**, since V5-22 had already taken the other half of its old subject away.

### V6-6 — Appendix A's Population-per-Upkeep line restated

Appendix A carried *"Population-per-Upkeep is not a separate stat"* — a negation surviving in a checklist after the body's version was restated in v0.4.4. Restated to match: *the stat frame is three dials.* A V4-1 cleanup the brief did not ask for.

## Open questions raised by the applied decisions

### V6-8 — Does the antagonist ratio keep 70/30 as its midpoint?

V5-15 makes the split vary with season, Road Work, biome and Reach lifecycle state. It does not say whether 70/30 remains the base value that those conditions modify, or whether the whole figure is now a tuning target with no fixed centre.

The body currently reads *"roughly 70% antagonist / 30% non-antagonist, varying with conditions."* That is a guess at the right shape. **The band it varies within is undefined**, which matters because the funnel's downstream probabilities — 30% amenable, then the animal's own check, then the slot cap — compound against it.

### V6-9 — How many Guests may join one party?

V5-4a fixes an Active Guest at one body-unit, and V5-4 fixes a Standard Party at three to five. A Standard Party with one Guest is therefore two citizens and a Guest, which is written into canon.

**Nothing states whether a second Guest may go.** At the range's top a rabbit party of five body-units could in principle field two or three Guests, and at four Active slots by max Tier that becomes a real option. Whether an all-Guest expedition is legal, whether a minimum of core citizens is required, and whether Guests displacing most of a party undercuts *"they do not remove the core citizens' need to take risks"* are all unaddressed.

### V6-10 — The Songbird's new job has nothing to do yet

V5-41 gives the Songbird the day **Sky-watch**, mobbing and alarm-calling, as the counter to an aerial threat. V4-12 leaves the threat itself unbuilt.

Until the design pass happens, the roster carries a Guest whose stated function answers a hazard that does not exist. The Owl is fine — its detection radius was always general — but the Songbird has traded ambient morale for a job with no object. **Either its morale effect should be retained alongside, or the entry should read as anticipating the threat rather than countering it.** Currently the document does the latter by implication and does not say so.

### V6-11 — Where does generated *name* production live now?

Section 23.1 previously routed the optional enhanced name layer through Section 34. With Section 34 dissolved, the reference now points at **Appendix G**, which V5-63 scopes to a **visual** method — a canonical image carried forward by image-to-image.

Text generation is not that. Either Appendix G widens to cover generated prose and names as well as portraits, or names need their own pointer. The Chronicle's generated prose has the same problem: its mandatory templated fallback is stated in Section 22, but the enhanced layer it falls back *from* no longer has a described home.

### V6-12 — Does carry load feed the Crossing?

V5-10 makes load a modifier in **encounter** resolution. Section 24.1 has always said that *"citizen speed, fear, wounds, load, and formation affect responsiveness"* in the Crossing action — but Crossing uses no Approaches, no Turns and no exposure, and is explicitly not an encounter.

So load acts in two places by two different mechanisms, one of them unspecified. Given that a party is heaviest on the **return** crossing, and that Return Logic's 10% forced manual re-crossing is exactly when that matters, this is likely to be load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

### V6-13 — The Metropolis is not a Reach, and V5-30 now says every Reach sits between carriageways

V5-30's correction is right and is stated. The Rest-Stop Metropolis is at a rest stop, in the margins of human infrastructure, and is not between carriageways.

No contradiction — it was never called a Reach — but Section 25.4 treats it as a Field territory like any other, and a reader coming from the corrected Section 7.3 may reasonably ask what the Metropolis sits between. **One clarifying sentence in Part VI would close it.** Not written, because it is not in any decision.

### V6-14 — Should the world primer be numbered?

V4-2 asks for a primer *before* Section 3, and the countdown structure — four registers, three species, two loops, one folklore — is why it cannot simply become Section 3.

It is currently an **unnumbered** section between 2 and 3, listed in the Contents without a number. That is the only unnumbered `##` in the body, and it will look like an error to anyone who does not know why it is there. Alternatives: fold it into Section 2 as 2.8, make it a front-matter panel like the origin brief, or leave it and accept the irregularity.

### V6-15 — Does a Held Reach's Staging Post change Return Logic?

V5-37 lets a colony run Margin Raids from a Held Reach with a Staging Post. Return Logic (Section 24.2) is written around returning to the **Home** Median.

A Margin Raid launched from an outpost returns to the outpost, not home — and then, separately, the party has to get home along the corridor. Whether that is one journey or two, whether Return Logic fires once or twice, and whether a party can overnight at an outpost are all unstated.

### V6-16 — What does an ongoing card do if its situation ends first?

V5-55 lets a card open, stay open, and close later, and pairs it with Road Work's persistence window. Road Work's window is **capped regardless of severity**, and the player can shorten it through active recovery work.

So a card tied to that window can outlive its cause. Whether it resolves early, resolves as written, or resolves differently is undefined — and it is the obvious first case, since Road Work is the pairing the decision names.

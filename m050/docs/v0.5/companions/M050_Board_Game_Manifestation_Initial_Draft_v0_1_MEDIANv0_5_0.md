# 0\. Draft Summary

<!--@1¶1-->
What this proposed manifestation adds to the v0.5.0 architecture

<!--@1¶2-->
  - Adds a fifth canonical form: a cooperative spatial board game centered on one complete colony story in a single evening.

<!--@1¶3-->
  - Separates the board game from the existing cooperative fixed-content card game. The board game’s native strength is tactile construction, visible geography, Citizen standees, and species-specific topology; the card game remains the stronger persistent campaign and card-state expression.

<!--@1¶4-->
  - Makes JOIN, GATHER, and CONNECT physically different construction procedures rather than three printed bonuses applied to the same grid.

<!--@1¶5-->
  - Represents the Home/Away simulation split through dual-state Citizen records: equal colony capacity at Home, individual capability and Exposure Away.

<!--@1¶6-->
  - Preserves v0.5.0 Dwell, Readiness, Big and Small Weaknesses, MEET, Homecoming, Campaign Memory, and Home-only EMBODY without reproducing the computer game’s interface.

<!--@1¶7-->
  - Defines a fixed-content, anti-consumerist product with no randomized purchases, collectible miniatures, app dependency, campaign destruction, or deluxe-only rules.

<!--@1¶8-->
  - Targets 1–4 players and a complete 75–120 minute scenario, with optional short Chronicle campaigns rather than campaign dependence.

<!--@1.1-->
## Proposed insertion into the Computer, TTRPG, Card, and Mobile Manifestation Framework

<!--@1.1¶1-->
The board game should enter after the Cooperative Fixed-Content Card Game and before Standalone Mobile. It becomes Manifestation IV; Mobile shifts to Manifestation V. Comparative tables, product doctrine, future-work lists, and the closing count should be revised from four to five forms only after this draft is accepted.

# 1\. Canonical Definition and Medium Boundary

<!--@1.1#2-->
## 1.1 Canonical pitch

<!--@1.1#2¶1-->
The MEDIAN board game is a cooperative spatial stewardship game for 1–4 players. The group guides one small, named animal colony through a bounded seasonal pressure, a necessary outward venture, and an active return. Players place and orient Places, assign Citizen standees to colony Roles, create a species-correct Home, decide who can be risked Away, and resolve the consequences when the world reaches the Colony through MEET.

<!--@1.1#2¶2-->
A scenario should tell one complete story in 75–120 minutes. Campaign continuity is available, but the product must remain satisfying when played as a single evening rather than requiring long-form commitment.

<!--@1.2#2-->
## 1.2 Why this is a separate manifestation

<!--@1.2#2¶1-->
A board game is not merely the card game with a board under it. Its core verbs are spatial and tactile: place, attach, face, connect, occupy, remove, route, brace, and return. The player should understand the Colony by looking at an inhabited physical arrangement rather than primarily reading a hand, constructing a deck, or operating a card tableau.

<!--@1.2#2¶2-->
| **Form**                | **Primary physical subject**                   | **Native strength**                                                                                   | **Persistence emphasis**                        |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Board Game              | A built Home Median occupied by named standees | Spatial colony construction, visible absence, shared stewardship, one-evening dramatic completeness   | Optional short Chronicle; scenario-first        |
| Fixed-Content Card Game | A corridor tableau composed through cards      | Card-state persistence, Reach chains, personal stacks, campaign customization, Chronicle accumulation | Campaign-forward, while retaining scenario play |
| TTRPG                   | Spoken colony and Citizen Records              | Authored voice, folklore, grief, bonds, succession, and consequence                                   | Campaign as shared history                      |

<!--@1.3-->
## 1.3 Canonical role among the five forms

<!--@1.3¶1-->
The board game is the tactile colony manifestation. It should reveal two qualities with unusual clarity: first, that species identity is a way of arranging inhabited space; second, that sending a particular Citizen Away is a literal subtraction from Home. When Sedge leaves the Workshop, the standee and capacity disappear from the board. When Sedge returns wounded or distinguished, the same figure comes home changed.

<!--@1.4-->
## 1.4 What the board game must not become

<!--@1.4¶1-->
  - A point-salad efficiency contest in which Citizens are anonymous worker-placement pieces.

<!--@1.4¶2-->
  - A competitive engine builder in which separate player colonies consume the same corridor.

<!--@1.4¶3-->
  - A tactical skirmish game whose detailed combat overwhelms sanctuary, labor, and return.

<!--@1.4¶4-->
  - A deck-construction game whose board is mostly decorative.

<!--@1.4¶5-->
  - A campaign product that withholds emotional completion from a single session.

<!--@1.4¶6-->
  - A miniature-collecting line in which species, named Citizens, or gameplay power are sold through scarcity.

<!--@3-->
# 2\. Product Identity, Session Scope, and Outcomes

<!--@2.1-->
## 2.1 Canonical product identity

<!--@2.1¶1-->
A cooperative, fixed-content spatial board game with scenario-based play and optional linked Chronicles. The complete core product must support repeated play without randomized purchases, an app, destructive legacy requirements, or additional boxes needed to repair an intentionally incomplete experience.

<!--@2.1¶2-->
| **Parameter**   | **Initial specification**                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Players         | 1–4; best at 2–4. Solo uses the same shared Colony rather than a separate automa economy.                                              |
| Scenario length | 75–120 minutes, including setup and Homecoming.                                                                                        |
| Scenario scale  | Five to seven Dawns; one principal seasonal pressure; usually one major expedition and no more than one minor sortie.                  |
| Campaign option | Three to five linked scenarios with non-destructive persistence. Campaign play is an extension, not the price of emotional completion. |
| Victory model   | Narrative outcome bands and fulfilled Promises rather than victory points.                                                             |
| Product model   | Fixed content, complete box, optional fully disclosed expansions, no app dependency.                                                   |

<!--@2.2-->
## 2.2 Scenario frame

<!--@2.2¶1-->
Each scenario begins with six authored facts. Together they create a complete dramatic problem rather than a generic score chase.

<!--@2.2¶2-->
  - Site: the physical median, infrastructure, water, cover, and species topology available at setup.

<!--@2.2¶3-->
  - Promise: the thing the Colony has said it will accomplish or protect before the scenario ends.

<!--@2.2¶4-->
  - Season: the environmental cadence, active resources, and approaching change.

<!--@2.2¶5-->
  - Need: the reason an expedition or major Home project cannot simply be ignored.

<!--@2.2¶6-->
  - Pressure: the world condition likely to test current Readiness.

<!--@2.2¶7-->
  - Known Reaches: the limited Away geography from which the group chooses its route.

<!--@2.3-->
## 2.3 Outcome without score optimization

<!--@2.3¶1-->
At the final Dawn or scenario trigger, the group answers three questions: Did the Home remain inhabitable? Was the Promise kept? Who or what came back changed? The result produces one of three broad outcomes; no points are totaled.

<!--@2.3¶2-->
| **Outcome**  | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Promise Kept | The Colony remains and the scenario’s defining obligation is fulfilled. Consequences and losses still enter the Chronicle.                                  |
| Costly Home  | The Colony remains, but the Promise is incomplete or a lasting Condition, wound, loss, or displacement defines the ending.                                  |
| Dispersal    | The site can no longer be held. Surviving Citizens and their Chronicle may continue in a linked campaign elsewhere; defeat does not erase remembered lives. |

# 3\. Board Architecture and Species Construction

<!--@3.1-->
## 3.1 The table as a living corridor

<!--@3.1¶1-->
The board should preserve the highway median’s essential cross-section while keeping the Home physically central. Unlike the card game, the board game does not need a long Reach chain to justify its format. Its primary spatial achievement is the Colony itself.

<!--@3.1¶2-->
  - A central Home board or modular terrain frame contains Places, Role stations, Projects, Conditions, and Citizen standees.

<!--@3.1¶3-->
  - Compact Highway lane strips border one or both sides of Home and hold moving vehicle pieces during Crossing.

<!--@3.1¶4-->
  - Margin and Reach tiles extend only when an expedition is active, keeping the table focused on the Colony rather than on an always-open adventure map.

<!--@3.1¶5-->
  - Season, Pressure, Promise, and Readiness tracks remain adjacent to Home and never become a detached accounting dashboard.

<!--@3.1¶6-->
  - The Chronicle occupies a visible edge of the table so remembered history accumulates beside the physical settlement.

<!--@3.2-->
## 3.2 Three physical grammars

<!--@3.2¶1-->
The board game should exploit component geometry so strongly that an uninhabited layout can still be identified as Mouse, Rabbit, or Squirrel. Each species uses a different construction procedure rather than the same square tile with a different icon.

<!--@3.2¶2-->
| **Species** | **Construction components**                                                                | **Placement verb** | **Physical test**                                                                                                    | **Expansion rhythm**                          |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Mouse       | Interlocking room and wing tiles with marked door edges and shared foundations             | JOIN               | Each new Place belongs to one continuous inhabited body through a valid wall, passage, sleeve, or covered connection | Room → Wing → Connected Second House          |
| Rabbit      | Court centers, perimeter arcs, inward-facing Place wedges, and daughter-court frames       | GATHER             | Places recognize a shared social center while remaining within the court’s cover and circulation limit               | Complete Court → Bud Daughter Court → Connect |
| Squirrel    | Separated node platforms linked by route sticks, cords, bridges, branches, or cable pieces | CONNECT            | Every active node has a credible route to the Web; major nodes increasingly seek a second way home                   | Reach → Reinforce → Thicken into Loops        |

<!--@3.3-->
## 3.3 Spatial alignment as reduced burden

<!--@3.3¶1-->
Good species placement should not merely emit a generic percentage bonus. It should lower the particular Routine Load created by the Colony’s current shape. A well-joined Mouse wing shares shelter and circulation; a complete Rabbit court shares warning and care; a looped Squirrel Web shares access and evacuation. The board may express these benefits through covered load icons, completed circuits, or shortened Role requirements rather than through a universal “+5% alignment” marker.

<!--@3.4-->
## 3.4 Guests and mixed society

<!--@3.4¶1-->
Guest Citizens may occupy Places, contribute to Roles, and become emotionally central without rewriting the core species grammar. A crow may sit within a Rabbit court; a turtle may inhabit the lower reaches of a Squirrel Web. The host Colony still JOINs, GATHERs, or CONNECTs according to its founding culture unless a scenario explicitly depicts a hybrid settlement.

# 4\. Citizens, Council Structure, and Components

<!--@4.1-->
## 4.1 Core component families

<!--@4.1¶1-->
| **Component**                                     | **Function**                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Home terrain and species construction pieces      | Build the persistent visible Colony and encode JOIN, GATHER, or CONNECT through physical arrangement.                   |
| Named Citizen standees and records                | Occupy Places and Roles, leave visible vacancies when Away, and carry personal history.                                 |
| Role / Readiness board                            | Tracks equal Citizen capacity, contextual Load, surplus, and deficiency without individual Home aptitudes.              |
| Project strips and Citizen-Day markers            | Represent construction, repair, preparation, and other multi-day civic commitments.                                     |
| Highway lane strips, vehicles, and Traffic pulses | Create a brief readable commitment puzzle during Crossing.                                                              |
| Reach and Margin tiles                            | Provide compact Away geography, resource opportunities, and route choices.                                              |
| Pressure and Encounter material                   | Convert world conditions and Role shortfall into bounded MEET situations.                                               |
| Resource and Condition tokens                     | Represent Sustenance, Durable Stores, Flexible Scrap, structural strain, Resolve, and authored scenario needs.          |
| Chronicle slips, tags, or erasable inserts        | Preserve wounds, bonds, Distinctions, After-names, losses, Guests, and remembered events without destroying components. |
| Quiet Moment prompts                              | Translate Home-only EMBODY into optional tactile and narrated micro-rituals.                                            |

<!--@4.2-->
## 4.2 Dual-state Citizen records

<!--@4.2¶1-->
Each Citizen should possess a Home face and an Away face. This is not merely convenient component design; it physically expresses the v0.5.0 simulation split.

<!--@4.2¶2-->
| **Home face**                                                                                     | **Away face**                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Name, portrait, current Role, current Place, availability, and one equal unit of colony capacity. | Approach icons or broad aptitudes, Tool and Keepsake slots, Supply, wounds, Exposure space, Distinctions, and personal consequences. |
| No variable productivity coefficient. Twig is one Gardener or one Builder when assigned.          | Individual difference matters. Twig may be the better speaker, carrier, climber, observer, or rescuer.                               |
| The card remains beside Home while the standee occupies a Role or Place.                          | The record travels with the expedition party and returns changed.                                                                    |

<!--@4.3-->
## 4.3 Witnesses rather than owners

<!--@4.3¶1-->
To preserve attachment without dividing the Colony into private engines, each player becomes the Witness for one or more Citizens during the scenario. The Witness handles the record, reads the Citizen’s prompts, and makes the final decision when that Citizen voluntarily accepts personal Exposure. They do not own the Citizen, their Role output, or any private resource.

<!--@4.3¶2-->
  - Colony assignments, construction, supplies, and scenario strategy remain shared decisions.

<!--@4.3¶3-->
  - A Witness may advocate for a Citizen, speak a brief line for them, or refuse a voluntary personal hazard; the table cannot reduce them to communal equipment.

<!--@4.3¶4-->
  - Witnessing may pass between scenarios, after retirement, or when a Citizen’s life ends. Continuity remains with the Colony.

<!--@4.4-->
## 4.4 Rotating Council responsibilities

<!--@4.4¶1-->
Procedural responsibility should rotate so one experienced player cannot silently operate the entire game. Suggested phase leads are Steward of Home, Keeper of the Road, Pathfinder, and Keeper of Names. These are table duties, not asymmetrical powers, and combine naturally at lower player counts.

<!--@6-->
# 5\. Core Day and Scenario Loop

<!--@5.1-->
## 5.1 Scenario setup

<!--@5.1¶1-->
1\. Choose a species Colony, Site board, and scenario Promise.

<!--@5.1¶2-->
2\. Place the starting Places, Citizens, Role requirements, Stores, known Reaches, and current Conditions.

<!--@5.1¶3-->
3\. Set the Season and Pressure clocks, then assign each named Citizen a Witness.

<!--@5.1¶4-->
4\. Read the opening situation aloud. The board should already show why Home is worth protecting before it shows what threatens it.

<!--@5.2-->
## 5.2 One game Day

<!--@5.2¶1-->
1\. Dawn: advance Season or reveal world Pressure; adjudicate yesterday’s return, recovery, and ongoing Conditions.

<!--@5.2¶2-->
2\. Council: review Role margins, current Promise, known risks, and which Citizens are available. Discussion should end in a small number of actual commitments.

<!--@5.2¶3-->
3\. Dwell: assign or retask Citizens, advance Projects in Citizen-Days, build or improve at most one significant Place, and prepare shared Supplies.

<!--@5.2¶4-->
4\. Departure: choose an expedition party when the scenario Need demands it. Remove their standees from Home and flip their records to Away.

<!--@5.2¶5-->
5\. Travel and Crossing: move through a short Reach route, pay the cost of distance, and resolve any highway Crossing as a brief commitment sequence.

<!--@5.2¶6-->
6\. Meet: answer one bounded Home or Away situation through two materially different spends or refusal. Shared success and personal Exposure resolve separately where appropriate.

<!--@5.2¶7-->
7\. Homecoming: return Citizens, Guests, salvage, knowledge, injuries, and absence to specific Places and relationships. Homecoming is an active phase, not an inventory report.

<!--@5.2¶8-->
8\. Quiet and Chronicle: when the Colony reaches Quiet Equilibrium, the group may enter one optional EMBODY analogue and may preserve one event as Campaign Memory.

<!--@5.3-->
## 5.3 Pacing rule

<!--@5.3¶1-->
A scenario should last five to seven Dawns. No phase should expand merely because components permit it. Dwell should produce a few consequential posture decisions; Crossing should remain frightening because it is brief; MEET should present a sharp choice rather than a menu; Homecoming should receive enough time to make return emotionally real.

<!--@5.4-->
## 5.4 Construction in Citizen-Days

<!--@5.4¶1-->
Projects use integer Citizen-Days. Assigning one Builder to a two-day Project completes one step per Dawn; assigning two Builders completes two steps, but those two named Citizens are visibly absent from other Roles. Project strips should show the Place being made rather than a generic construction queue.

# 6\. Dwell, Readiness, Shortfall, and Home MEET

<!--@6.1-->
## 6.1 Capacity, Load, and Margin

<!--@6.1¶1-->
Every assigned Citizen provides one unit of their current Role. The board compares that Capacity against contextual Load created by population, Places, season, Projects, exposure, and species topology. Home Mode does not inspect Citizen aptitude.

<!--@6.1¶2-->
| **Margin state** | **Board meaning**                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Positive         | Routine responsibility is covered and spare Readiness is available for pressure, preparation, or voluntary ambition.                |
| Zero             | The Colony is competently maintained but brittle. Ordinary life proceeds; additional pressure may create an Encounter.              |
| Negative         | A responsibility is under-supported. The Colony is exposed to a category of trouble but does not automatically suffer daily damage. |

<!--@6.2-->
## 6.2 Successful stillness

<!--@6.2¶1-->
Adequate Role coverage establishes real equilibrium. Structures do not lose arbitrary condition merely because time passed. Food does not evaporate to force clicks. A well-run Colony can coast until season, ambition, absence, or an external circumstance changes the problem. That quiet is the space in which attachment, observation, and EMBODY become available.

<!--@6.3-->
## 6.3 Physical Shortfall Ledger

<!--@6.3¶1-->
The board game should make the v0.5.0 weakness system legible without turning it into constant arithmetic. Each deficient Role receives a Shortfall marker showing depth. Total deficiency advances an Encounter Pressure track or increases the chance that the next relevant world condition escalates into MEET. When an Encounter family is selected, only its relevant Roles are compared: the weakest becomes the Big Weakness; the second becomes the Small Weakness.

<!--@6.3¶2-->
The players may often infer their vulnerabilities, but the exact next manifestation remains uncertain because season, Site, and Encounter family still determine what form the shortfall takes.

<!--@6.4-->
## 6.4 Three-choice Encounter frame

<!--@6.4¶1-->
A Home Encounter should normally offer exactly three responses: spend one capacity or resource to protect the primary stake, spend a different capacity or resource to protect or redirect the secondary stake, or spend neither and suffer the compounded result.

<!--@6.4¶2-->
| **Heavy Winds example** | **Cost**                                               | **Result**                                                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brace the Upper Reach   | Spend Flexible Scrap and interrupt one Builder Project | Structural Condition holds; unattended stores suffer the lesser consequence.                      |
| Move Households Inward  | Commit emergency Labor and lose Resolve                | Citizens and stores are protected; the exposed Place gains structural strain.                     |
| Wait It Out             | Spend nothing                                          | The Big and Small Weaknesses both transact: major strain, lost stores, and possible displacement. |

<!--@6.5-->
## 6.5 No double charging

<!--@6.5¶1-->
A Role shortfall should not simultaneously increase Encounter frequency, impose automatic daily loss, worsen the Encounter, and then demand an unrelated repair tax. The board-game director should choose the dramatic transaction appropriate to the scenario. Continuing burden belongs to global Conditions or a clearly named Project, not to stacked punishment for the same deficiency.

<!--@8-->
# 7\. Expedition, Crossing, Away MEET, and Exposure

<!--@7.1-->
## 7.1 Departure as visible subtraction

<!--@7.1¶1-->
Selecting an expedition party is the board game’s clearest Home/Away decision. Each chosen standee leaves a Role, Place, or Project. The resulting empty slot and changed Margin remain visible while the party is Away. Players should never need a reminder that the expedition was paid for partly with domestic weakness.

<!--@7.2-->
## 7.2 Compact Travel

<!--@7.2¶1-->
Travel uses a short route of Reach and Margin tiles rather than a broad adventure board. Two to four route steps are sufficient to create distance, carrying limits, weather, and the temptation to press farther. The route should contain enough information to support planning but not become a separate tactical exploration game.

<!--@7.3-->
## 7.3 Crossing as a brief ritual

<!--@7.3¶1-->
The Highway lane strip holds vehicle pieces and a small visible queue of Traffic pulses. Before commitment, players may wait, reposition, prepare, or reduce cargo. Once a Citizen enters the road, every new pulse demands forward motion, an authored species action, or personal Exposure. Reversal should be rare and costly. A complete Crossing should normally last two to five minutes.

<!--@7.3¶2-->
  - Mouse reads cover, edge, and staged refuge; preparation can make a hostile open lane temporarily legible.

<!--@7.3¶3-->
  - Rabbit reads bursts between safety pockets; explosive movement may solve one lane while making the next commitment sharper.

<!--@7.3¶4-->
  - Squirrel reads vectors, anchors, and uninterrupted trajectory; a correct line rewards commitment more than evasion.

<!--@7.3¶5-->
These are tendencies expressed through small asymmetric rules, not three separate minigames.

<!--@7.4-->
## 7.4 Away MEET

<!--@7.4¶1-->
Encounter material presents a bounded circumstance and a limited set of Approaches such as Contest, Evade, Parley, Yield, or Withdraw. The party assembles a shared result from individual Away capabilities, Tools, Keepsakes, and Supplies. Success determines what the party accomplishes. It does not determine who comes home unchanged.

<!--@7.5-->
## 7.5 Personal Exposure

<!--@7.5¶1-->
Each Citizen resolves Exposure separately after taking responsibility, pressing the situation, carrying the critical object, rescuing another Citizen, or remaining too long Away. The exact probability device remains provisional. The preferred prototype is a small personal dice pool in which added Exposure increases access to both Distinction and Harm faces rather than merely subtracting health.

<!--@7.5¶2-->
  - Low Exposure usually produces no permanent change.

<!--@7.5¶3-->
  - More dice widen the possible outcome toward achievement, wound, Tharn, or combinations.

<!--@7.5¶4-->
  - The Witness decides whether their Citizen voluntarily takes additional Exposure for another.

<!--@7.5¶5-->
  - Tharn interrupts ordinary resolution and creates a relational question: who goes back, and what must they abandon to do it?

<!--@9-->
# 8\. Homecoming, Chronicle, and EMBODY

<!--@8.1-->
## 8.1 Return is an active board state change

<!--@8.1¶1-->
The returning party is not simply placed back into available worker slots. Homecoming should be resolved in a deliberate order: who arrives first, which Place receives the injured, where a Guest is welcomed, what salvage becomes communal, which knowledge changes a Practice, which Citizen cannot resume their former Role, and what the Colony now remembers.

<!--@8.1¶2-->
1\. Place returning standees at the Colony edge rather than immediately at their former stations.

<!--@8.1¶3-->
2\. Resolve wounds, Tharn, Guests, burdens, and any Citizen who did not return.

<!--@8.1¶4-->
3\. Assign recovered goods or knowledge to specific Places, Practices, or Projects.

<!--@8.1¶5-->
4\. Restore or deliberately leave vacant Home Roles.

<!--@8.1¶6-->
5\. Name one consequence that changes how the board is read before the next Dawn.

<!--@8.2-->
## 8.2 Chronicle and persistence

<!--@8.2¶1-->
At the end of a scenario, one or more Chronicle slips may be kept beside the Colony record. Persistent changes should use erasable sheets, sleeves, removable tags, or reprintable forms. The game should never require destruction of a singular Citizen card in order to prove that death mattered.

<!--@8.3-->
## 8.3 Board-game EMBODY analogue

<!--@8.3¶1-->
When the Colony reaches Quiet Equilibrium and no MEET requires resolution, the group may choose one available Citizen for a brief, non-scoring Life Moment. The board becomes a guided physical prompt rather than an economic puzzle. Participation is optional and grants no exclusive resource, recovery rate, or tactical bonus.

<!--@8.3¶2-->
| **Species / affect**             | **Example physical ritual**                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mouse - Small Work or Warmth     | Trace a continuous route through joined rooms, moving the standee from a familiar Practice to a warm interior Place; read or improvise one sensory detail encountered along the way. |
| Rabbit - Presence or Social Play | Move across the completed court to a friend, patch of sun, meal, or low mound; another Witness chooses how the nearby Citizen responds.                                              |
| Squirrel - Flow                  | Run the standee along a connected bridge route or full loop of the Web, pausing at one chosen lookout. The pleasure is that the route is known and safe.                             |

<!--@8.3¶3-->
A distinctive Life Moment may become Campaign Memory. Most need not. The purpose is to let the group attend to ordinary aliveness after the board’s logical demands have been satisfied.

<!--@10-->
# 9\. Board Game / Card Game Separation

<!--@9.1-->
## 9.1 Why both forms can remain canonical

<!--@9.1¶1-->
The existing card manifestation should not be absorbed into this proposal. The two physical games ask different questions of the player and should produce different tables.

<!--@9.1¶2-->
| **Dimension**             | **Board Game**                                                                                      | **Fixed-Content Card Game**                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary question          | Can we physically arrange and staff a Home that can withstand this bounded pressure?                | Can this persistent Colony and expedition network survive and change across a linked corridor campaign? |
| Primary manipulation      | Place tiles, node links, standees, route pieces, shared tracks, and a small number of support cards | Citizen, Place, Reach, Traffic, Encounter, Supply, Exposure, and Chronicle cards                        |
| Native species expression | Different component geometry and construction procedures                                            | Different card relationships, keywords, tableau rules, and campaign options                             |
| Session promise           | One complete colony story in 75–120 minutes                                                         | One complete scenario in 60–120 minutes within a stronger linked-campaign identity                      |
| Persistence               | Optional short Chronicle; board state normally resets between standalone scenarios                  | Persistent Citizens, wounds, bonds, Places, Reaches, personal stacks, and Chronicle                     |
| Emotional strength        | Visible habitation, visible absence, tactile return, and the physical beauty of a functioning Home  | A singular named card accumulating history across many sessions                                         |
| Main failure risk         | Becoming a Euro-style worker-placement optimization exercise                                        | Becoming a deck-optimization product in which cards replace Citizens                                    |

<!--@9.2-->
## 9.2 Shared material is allowed

<!--@9.2¶1-->
The board game may use cards for Encounters, Pressure, Traffic pulses, Citizen records, and quiet prompts. The distinction is not card count. It is where the game’s decisive state lives. In the board game, the decisive state is the spatial Colony and its occupied or vacant Places. In the card game, the decisive state is the evolving card tableau, personal stacks, and persistent campaign collection.

<!--@9.3-->
## 9.3 Scenario design emphasis

<!--@9.3¶1-->
Board-game scenarios should emphasize one Site, one species construction grammar, one major Promise, and one pressure arc. The card game is better suited to longer corridor revelation, accumulating Reaches, and broader campaign mutation. The TTRPG is better suited to sustained dialogue and contradictory folklore. This division prevents the board game from becoming an inferior compromise among the other forms.

# 10\. Ethical Product Doctrine, Prototype Package, and Acceptance Criteria

<!--@10.1-->
## 10.1 Anti-consumerist boundaries

<!--@10.1¶1-->
  - No randomized packs, blind components, rarity tiers, chase miniatures, speculative resale design, or limited-print gameplay power.

<!--@10.1¶2-->
  - No app required for setup, Traffic, scenario resolution, campaign storage, or solo play.

<!--@10.1¶3-->
  - No deluxe edition may contain exclusive rules, Citizens, species systems, or scenarios required for the complete design.

<!--@10.1¶4-->
  - Optional expansions must be fixed-content, fully described, compatible with the complete core game, and additive rather than corrective.

<!--@10.1¶5-->
  - Campaign recording must be non-destructive or supported by freely reproducible sheets.

<!--@10.1¶6-->
  - The game must not manipulate attachment by threatening neglect, absence penalties between sessions, or real-world time pressure.

<!--@10.2-->
## 10.2 Recommended initial paper prototype

<!--@10.2¶1-->
The first prototype should test the board game’s distinctive claim rather than attempt all three species or a complete product line.

<!--@10.2¶2-->
  - One species Home kit. Squirrel is the strongest demonstration of tactile routes; Mouse is the simplest demonstration of joined spatial construction. Prototype one first, then test the other before deciding which is the better teaching set.

<!--@10.2¶3-->
  - Eight named Citizens with dual Home/Away records and standees.

<!--@10.2¶4-->
  - Five Roles, one Role / Readiness board, and two short Project strips.

<!--@10.2¶5-->
  - Six Dawns, one Promise, one seasonal Pressure arc, and one principal expedition objective.

<!--@10.2¶6-->
  - One compact lane strip, six vehicle pieces, and eight Traffic pulse cards.

<!--@10.2¶7-->
  - Three Reach tiles, four Away Encounters, four Home Encounters, and one Homecoming frame.

<!--@10.2¶8-->
  - One species-specific Life Moment prompt and one Chronicle sheet.

<!--@10.2¶9-->
  - No campaign persistence beyond recording the final state after the first two tests.

<!--@10.3-->
## 10.3 Questions reserved for prototype

<!--@10.3¶1-->
  - Should total deficiency advance a deterministic Pressure track, alter an Encounter draw, or do both at different depths?

<!--@10.3¶2-->
  - Does personal Exposure work better as dice, a token bag, or a small deck when the board game already contains several component types?

<!--@10.3¶3-->
  - How many Citizens can remain individually memorable at the table before the board reads as labor inventory?

<!--@10.3¶4-->
  - Can all three species fit one complete core box without making setup, storage, and teaching hostile to the intended 75–120 minute experience?

<!--@10.3¶5-->
  - How much private Witness authority is necessary to prevent quarterbacking without fragmenting the shared Colony?

<!--@10.3¶6-->
  - Should linked play preserve the physical Home layout, preserve only Citizen and Chronicle records, or offer both levels?

<!--@10.4-->
## 10.4 Acceptance criteria

<!--@10.4¶1-->
  - A player can identify Mouse, Rabbit, or Squirrel from the unoccupied construction layout alone.

<!--@10.4¶2-->
  - Removing a Citizen for an expedition visibly changes Home posture and creates an understandable risk.

<!--@10.4¶3-->
  - Home assignments use equal Citizen capacity; individual differentiation becomes mechanically significant Away.

<!--@10.4¶4-->
  - A well-run Colony can reach real Quiet Equilibrium rather than merely slowing constant decay.

<!--@10.4¶5-->
  - Home shortfalls become situated MEET choices instead of continuous leakage and repair clicking.

<!--@10.4¶6-->
  - Crossing is readable, tense, and complete within five minutes.

<!--@10.4¶7-->
  - The scenario ends in 75–120 minutes with an emotionally complete Homecoming and no victory-point accounting.

<!--@10.4¶8-->
  - Players remember at least two Citizens by name and can say how they changed.

<!--@10.4¶9-->
  - EMBODY appears as optional non-instrumental aliveness, not a bonus action or recovery grind.

<!--@10.4¶10-->
  - The design cannot be mistaken for the fixed-content card game when both are described without artwork.

<!--@10.4¶11-->
  - The product remains complete, fixed-content, app-independent, and non-exploitative.

# Landscape Voice, Tolkien and Adams, and Landscape Orientation

**Artifact class:** Developmental Source
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 8, 2026
**Status:** Exploratory and non-authoritative
**Scope:** MEDIAN m051 and later development

This Source preserves a promising direction for MEDIAN's narrative presentation
and environment-led development. It is not a finished Spec, does not amend the
locked m050 architecture, and does not independently establish mechanics,
terminology, implementation requirements, or canon. Its propositions remain
subject to author review, development, reconciliation, testing, and promotion.

The companion atomic representation is derivative of this prose Source. The
prose document is the canonical home of the idea until a later development task
accepts, revises, or rejects its propositions.

# 1. Landscape Voice

## 1.1 The proposal

MEDIAN may employ a situated narratorial voice that renders authoritative game
events as they are experienced through bodies moving through a particular
place. The provisional name for this device is **Landscape Voice**.

Landscape Voice is not limited to static scenery. Landscape remains present
when the voice renders action because terrain, scale, weather, light, sound,
cover, infrastructure, and available movement shape what action is possible and
how it is experienced. The voice might open broadly after a successful Crossing
into a quiet midday Margin, or contract into fragments during a Mouse party's
flight from a failed contested encounter.

The governing distinction is:

> **The voice may change tempo and attention. It may not change truth.**

Landscape Voice should not invent a story independently of play. It should
recognize and render the story already produced by player action and persistent
world state.

## 1.2 Attachment-forward function

Landscape Voice has strong attachment-forward potential because it binds an
event to the particular Citizens, place, conditions, possessions, risks, and
history that produced it.

The player need not remember an abstract transaction such as completing a
Crossing or resolving a Node. The player may instead remember who stopped under
the reflector, who carried an injured companion, what the far Margin looked
like in the rain, what was left behind, or how Home received the returning
party. Mechanical consequence remains intact, but narrated experience gives it
a personal and spatial surface.

This can make ongoing player action into story without requiring a separately
authored plot to interrupt ordinary play. MEDIAN already produces narratively
meaningful facts:

- who went;
- why they went;
- what route they selected;
- what they carried;
- what they encountered;
- which Approach succeeded or failed;
- what was discovered, promised, lost, abandoned, or brought home;
- who was injured, frightened, separated, or protected;
- when the party turned back; and
- how the Colony received the result.

Landscape Voice may give those facts immediate literary form.

## 1.3 Relationship to the existing narrative architecture

The proposal should reuse the narrative transformation MEDIAN already requires
for the Record, Memories, Citizen Tales, Item Tales, and the Chronicle. It
should not establish an unrelated prose generator or a parallel narrative
authority.

A possible lifecycle is:

1. Player action and world resolution produce authoritative facts in **the
   Record**.
2. The existing narrative engine renders a bounded passage from those facts,
   established history, current sensory conditions, and the selected
   narratorial orientation.
3. The player experiences that passage during play.
4. The passage, its source Record references, and its relevant participants and
   location enter the existing **Memories** process.
5. Existing salience rules determine whether it remains an immediate memory,
   contributes to a Citizen Tale or Item Tale, or qualifies for civic treatment
   in the Chronicle.

The Record remains authoritative. Narration is an interpretation grounded in
the Record, not a replacement for it. A narrated passage should retain
provenance to the facts that generated it.

The exact passage first experienced by the player should normally be preserved
with those references. Later memory or Tale development may contextualize it,
but should not silently regenerate the same moment into a contradictory event.
Immediate narration does not make every moment Chronicle-worthy. Experience,
personal memory, and civic history remain different levels of salience.

## 1.4 Voice, pacing, and situated attention

Narratorial choices can guide the felt pace of play without falsifying its
state.

An observant passage may widen its attention across foreground, middle
distance, horizon, weather, and sound. A pressured passage may narrow to the
next edge, a companion ahead, the shadow overhead, or the route that has just
closed. Sentence length, perceptual breadth, rhythm, and selection of detail may
express whether time feels expansive, compressed, suspended, exhausted, or
dangerously immediate.

The narratorial orientation should be derived from actual conditions rather
than selected merely to manufacture excitement. A frenzied voice belongs where
the Record supports pursuit, exposure, separation, injury, panic, or urgent
movement. Quiet observation belongs where the world and the player's attention
have genuinely made room for it.

Species may influence selective attention without creating three incompatible
worlds or rigid prose templates:

- Rabbit attention may favor cover, exposed distance, shared ground, warning,
  and the continuity of familiar Places.
- Mouse attention may favor seams, edges, cavities, fine routes, enclosure, and
  small structural failures or opportunities.
- Squirrel attention may favor anchors, height, gaps, momentum, route
  continuity, and distributed refuge.

These are tendencies rather than cognitive limits. The physical world and the
resolved event remain one shared truth.

## 1.5 Information boundary

Landscape Voice may draw from:

- authoritative Record facts;
- known persistent world state;
- current weather, light, sound, terrain, and visibility;
- Party composition and condition;
- established Citizen and relationship history;
- established species attention;
- immediately perceivable material and movement; and
- consequences that have actually resolved.

It should not invent or disclose:

- hidden motives or undiscovered hazards;
- object knowledge the party has not earned;
- relationships or memories absent from state;
- outcomes that have not resolved;
- unsupported internal emotions;
- required mechanical instructions disguised as prose; or
- moral conclusions the player should be permitted to form.

A useful formulation is:

> **Landscape Voice is authoritative about situated perception and restrained
> about interpretation.**

## 1.6 Scarcity and presentation

Landscape Voice should be scarce enough that its appearance means the moment
deserves attention. Candidate thresholds include:

- emergence onto the far Margin after a Crossing;
- first arrival in a new Reach or terrain family;
- first sight of a major destination;
- reaching an overlook;
- entering a significant settlement;
- emerging from constricted infrastructure into open land;
- a major reversal or flight whose geography matters;
- dawn after an important journey;
- Homecoming after consequential absence; and
- return to a familiar place after material change.

It should not appear merely because the player crossed an ordinary screen or
map boundary.

The visual world should remain primary. A passage may enter unobtrusively near
the lower edge of the presentation, remain long enough to be read, and fade or
dismiss naturally through ordinary input. It should not impose a fixed
thirty-second wait, become a cutscene by default, or carry information required
to operate the game.

The game should remain mechanically intelligible when a passage is omitted.
The passage succeeds when it changes how the player apprehends a place or
action without becoming another control layer.

## 1.7 Conservation of System

Landscape Voice is strongest if it is a presentation route through existing
systems:

- the Record supplies truth;
- the existing narrative transformation supplies language;
- an existing spatial or dramatic transition supplies the moment;
- the existing Memories architecture receives the grounded passage; and
- existing salience rules determine later Tale or Chronicle use.

It should not become a sixth Register, a second Record, a separate memory
ledger, a runtime authority, or a new mandatory event class. It should deepen
existing transitions rather than manufacture a parallel workflow.

# 2. Tolkien and Adams

## 2.1 Critical lineage, not authority

J. R. R. Tolkien and Richard Adams offer a useful critical lens for MEDIAN.
Their work does not establish project authority, and this Source does not claim
that a specific line of direct influence has been biographically proven. The
value lies in recognizing a creative and perceptual method.

The shared insight may be described as:

> **Landscape is syntax.**

Terrain does not pause an event so that scenery can be described. Terrain
shapes perception, available choices, movement, and therefore event:

> terrain -> perception -> available choices -> movement -> consequence

A ridge determines what can be seen. A belt of trees conceals. A stream is
landmark, obstacle, resource, boundary, and route. A road must be understood
before it can be crossed. Description becomes causal geography.

## 2.2 Tolkien's painterly orientation

A useful interpretation of Tolkien's prose is that imagined landscape may be
composed visually before it is rendered verbally. A passage frequently begins
from a selected observation point and organizes foreground, middle distance,
horizon, path, obstruction, light, weather, verticals, and diagonals as a
picture.

This painterly composition can also produce navigable prose. Because the
underlying place is coherent, the resulting description gives the reader both
an image and a mental map.

For MEDIAN, the important inheritance is methodological rather than imitative:
choose where the observer stands, construct what can be seen from there, and
let the physical relationship among things govern the prose.

## 2.3 Adams's animal-height transformation

*Watership Down* applies similarly serious attention to landscape while moving
the observer close to the ground and placing perception inside prey-animal
life.

At that height:

- a hedge becomes wall, roof, concealment, and corridor;
- a ditch becomes valley and route;
- open grass becomes exposure;
- a road becomes a vast spatial boundary and intermittent disaster;
- a culvert becomes monumental infrastructure; and
- line of sight becomes a fundamental property of survival.

The landscape is not merely traversed. It is inhabited, read, feared, used,
remembered, and socially interpreted.

## 2.4 MEDIAN's extension

MEDIAN extends this orientation into landscapes made by humans for unrelated
purposes. Highway lanes, barriers, drainage, verges, sound walls, culverts,
shoulders, bridge abutments, signs, service areas, wrecks, and road work cease
to be background. At animal scale they become geography, ecology,
infrastructure, boundary, refuge, danger, and political territory.

The progression may be framed as:

- Tolkien asks what land means to travelers.
- Adams asks what land means to small animals.
- MEDIAN asks what systems emerge when small animals understand human
  infrastructure as terrain.

This is an interpretive genealogy, not a claim of ownership or a direction to
imitate either author's prose. MEDIAN should learn from their seriousness about
place while preserving its own vocabulary, systems, species, corridor, and
voice.

## 2.5 Behavioral character without allegorical assignment

A place may acquire a behavioral or emotional character through its physical
organization without being assigned a simple moral label. Elevation, drainage,
outlook, grazing, soil, access, exposure, enclosure, maintenance, and history
can make one place support refuge and another unease before any narrator tells
the player what to feel.

A Rest-Stop Metropolis, broad wooded Median, Jersey-barrier strip, interchange
island, and forest corridor should not differ only in artwork. Their physical
forms should make animal life and civilization behave differently.

Landscape Voice may reveal that character through selective detail, but should
not flatten terrain into safe/evil, reward/punishment, or biome stereotypes.

# 3. Landscape Orientation

## 3.1 Developmental method

Landscape Orientation is a proposed method for developing MEDIAN places,
environmental art, authored situations, narrative passages, and future Source
material.

For each significant location:

1. Establish the governing Specs and known world state.
2. Construct a concrete place rather than a generic terrain category.
3. Select an animal-scale observation point.
4. Establish the physical cross-section, grade, materials, vegetation,
   drainage, light, weather, sound, visibility, and human infrastructure.
5. Determine what is visible, concealed, reachable, traversable, useful,
   dangerous, familiar, or unknown from that point.
6. Consider how different bodies and species selectively attend to the same
   facts.
7. Derive candidate situations, narrative observations, and developmental
   propositions.
8. Check every candidate against governing Specs before it can acquire further
   authority.

The method may run upstream of prose and situation writing. It does not run
upstream of established project authority.

## 3.2 Art and canon boundary

A visually compelling plate may reveal overlooked affordances or development
needs. It may generate a Source proposition. It does not silently establish a
mechanic, terminology rule, ecological fact, or canonical exception.

The safe relationship is:

> governing Spec -> constructed landscape -> situated observation -> candidate
> Source proposition -> explicit review

This preserves visual discovery while preventing attractive images from
becoming accidental canon.

## 3.3 One world, several readings

Landscape Orientation supports MEDIAN's existing principle that species
identity arises through different ways of inhabiting one persistent world.

The same human object or terrain feature remains physically identical while
its salient affordance changes. A tree may be shared cover to Rabbit, a cavity
and seam system to Mouse, and a route anchor to Squirrel. The difference lies
in attention, body, purpose, history, and current need—not in three separate
realities.

This permits environmental art, narration, Field presentation, EMBODY scenes,
MEET composition, and Sourcebook material to reinforce one another without
reducing species to numerical modifiers or rigid literary caricatures.

## 3.4 Landscape as causal world state

The strongest MEDIAN landscapes should do more than look distinct. Their form
should change:

- what can be seen and known;
- where safety and exposure occur;
- how sound and weather travel;
- where water accumulates;
- what routes remain continuous;
- what kinds of refuge and settlement are possible;
- what can be carried or defended;
- where animals and human activity intersect; and
- what becomes memorable.

This causal orientation protects landscape from becoming decoration added
after systems design. It also protects systems design from becoming abstract
mechanics later covered with interchangeable scenery.

## 3.5 Development questions

This Source deliberately leaves the following unresolved:

- whether `Landscape Voice` is the eventual accepted name;
- which existing event thresholds invoke it;
- how narratorial orientation is selected from state;
- whether major authored locations receive species-conditioned variants;
- how much prose is authored, assembled deterministically, or generated under
  constraint;
- what exact passage and provenance enter Memories;
- how later Tale or Chronicle interpretation may transform a preserved passage;
- which vocabulary belongs to narrator, player, and animal understanding;
- how repetition is prevented across long campaigns; and
- how the presentation translates across desktop, tabletop, card, and mobile
  manifestations.

These are development questions, not permission to create new systems in
advance of need.

## 3.6 Evaluation test

A future implementation or prototype should satisfy this test:

> Remove the narration and the game still functions. Restore it and the player
> apprehends the place, action, and participating lives more deeply.

The strongest result would make MEDIAN's simulation and literature feel like
two views of the same event: the Record gives the event integrity, Landscape
Voice gives it experience, and Memory gives it duration.

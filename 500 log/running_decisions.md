# MEDIAN Phase 2 — Running Decisions

Style conventions and plate decisions as they lock. Maintained so the thread can be resumed or handed off without replaying the conversation.

**Convention:** decisions are numbered P2-1 upward, dated, and marked **LOCKED** or **WORKING**. A working decision is being acted on but may still move; a locked one requires a stated reversal to change.

---

## Pass-level

### P2-1 — One visual identity across the whole deck · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24*

Styling never varies with a plate's subject. No warm-for-colony / cold-for-crossing, no parchment-for-Field. One identity applied uniformly to all plates.

**Reverses:** the handoff's Stage 1 instruction to tag each plate with a "visual pipeline" drawn from two register-derived visual languages (Rendered-embodied / Illustrated-surveyed). That instruction is styling-varies-by-content and is retired.

**What this rule actually forbids — clarified 2026-07-24.** The target is **mimicry**: a plate adopting the visual language of the mode it documents, so the Encounter plate is styled to look like an Encounter screen. That is the false note. It is *not* a restriction on tonal or palette range.

**Dark night scenes are fully available.** Illustration content varies as the world varies — night crossings, headlights, the Metropolis after dark, Night Velocity. What stays constant is the **chassis**: ground, grid, header treatment, callout forms, typography, caption voice. Set 2 already models this correctly, pairing a dark photographic header band with a warm body page.

**Also does not cover:** within-plate coding. Colour-coding parallel items inside one plate is ordinary information design and remains available.

**Consequence:** the surviving distinction between the registers is *subject matter*. A Field Mode plate depicts a naturalist's survey plate as an illustrated object; it is not rendered as one.

### P2-2 — Plates are illustrative, not depictions of UI · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24*

A plate is a drawn document about the game, not a frame of the game and not a screenshot. Where an interface genuinely is the subject, it appears as an **annotated inset specimen** inside an illustrative plate — never as the plate's own surface.

**Consequence:** four plates from the old Plan are reconceived — Colony Dashboard, Expedition Launcher, Field Mode, Expedition Rating & Debrief. Two legacy plates retire outright on this rule: Set 2's phone-UI Gameplay Overview and v0.3's MEDIAN OS Expedition Control.

### P2-3 — The deck has its own identity · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24*

The infographic deck is its own artifact. It is not obliged to look like the game's registers, the GDD, or any prior deck.

### P2-4 — Plates are named, not numbered · **WORKING**
*2026-07-24*

Numbering is deferred until text is live and layered, at which point numbers can be added without rework. Plates are referenced by name in the interim.

### P2-5 — Production tags replace pipeline tags · **WORKING**
*2026-07-24*

Each plate carries a production tag describing what must be *made*, not how it is styled: **NEW** (original illustration required) · **HARVEST** (usable drawing exists, recompose and correct) · **DIAGRAM** (no scene — objects, specimens, structure) · **INSET** (carries an annotated interface specimen). Tags combine.

### P2-6 — One merged production plan · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24*

`MEDIAN_Phase2_Production_Plan.md` supersedes both `MEDIAN_v0.4_Infographic_Plan.md` and `MEDIAN_v0.4_Needed_Artwork.md`. Infographic plates and concept art are two tracks in one document, sharing pipeline tags and open-item flags.

### P2-7 — Output format · **AMENDED by P2-41**
*2026-07-24, inherited from GDD §33.1 and Appendix D*

Layered, editable `.pptx`. Text never flattened. Generated illustration inside a plate is a raster layer; text, labels, callouts, and shape elements sit over it live.

*Amended the same day — see P2-41. Plates on settled canon ship with generated text flat; plates touching open Appendix D items retain a stripped base for later recompositing. Body-text pages stay layered in all cases.*

## Answers from the Stage 1 question pass

*2026-07-24. Nine of the ten logged questions were answered by the designer in one pass. Several are canon-grade and are marked for the next GDD revision — see "Feeds the next GDD pass" below.*

### P2-13 — The deck's reader and register · **LOCKED**

**A mix of a game design document and an RPG core sourcebook** — the kind worth reading cover to cover as a teenager. This is why the graphic push is heavy.

This is the single most consequential styling decision made so far and it constrains all of Stage 2c: caption voice, text density, how much in-world material a plate carries, and how tables are treated. An RPG core book decorates its information rather than stripping it; it sets in-world excerpts apart from body text, opens sections with full-bleed art, and uses margins. That is the target.

**Consequence — designer-facing content recedes over iterations.** Sections aimed solely at designers get progressively less space as the deck matures. The plates most affected are Exposure & Outcomes and Contest/Recruitment, which the old Plan explicitly conceived as showing raw probabilities "for the designer." Under P2-13 the funnel is explained to a reader; the percentages recede to where a curious reader would want them rather than leading.

### P2-14 — No people, anywhere · **LOCKED**

No human figures in any plate or any concept art.

**This is tighter than canon.** §33.2 permits human bodies in exactly one case — Road Work and Founding-Escape-class construction threats, where "their presence *is* the point." Phase 2 declines that allowance. Road Work and the Founding Escape are depicted through machinery alone.

The artistic case for going tighter: machinery without an operator reads as indifferent force rather than intent, which is what §10.2 already requires of it — evaded, never negotiated with, never fought. There is no one to appeal to. Vehicles remain visible constantly and traffic is never off-screen; this rule was only ever about bodies.

*Flagged for the GDD pass: whether §33.2's exception should simply close.*

### P2-15 — Founding Escape naming · **WORKING**

**"The Founding Escape" stands as the in-fiction name.** It sits acceptably in a folk register that §22.2 tunes homely and plain rather than ornate.

**The lost home is the "Ancestral Warren / Den / Burrow,"** species-specific per campaign. This is a correct use of the species terminology rule — §15.1 and §31 bar Warren/Den/Burrow only from *generic* use, and a rabbit campaign's ancestral home being a Warren is exactly the intended usage.

### P2-16 — Contested encounters show a roughly equal opposing party · **LOCKED**

Depict an opposing group of comparable apparent mass to the player's party. Do not compose a shot that invites counting, and do not caption a number. Mass, not count — §25.3's rule expressed as a staging convention rather than a diagram.

### P2-17 — Species signatures read as spatial multiplicity · **WORKING**

Warren Flow gets a visual specification even though the mechanic is still open: **a mouse Burrow shows one entrance; a rabbit Warren shows half a dozen at colony scale, plus at least one remote escape tunnel visible in the Field layer.** Multiplicity of exits is the rabbit's readable signature.

The escape tunnel surfacing in Field view is canon-supported: §3.5 requires the Colony and Field presentations of the same ground to *be* the same place, so colony structure extending into the Field layer is correct rather than a liberty.

*Open, and worth confirming:* whether this generalises — rabbit exits, squirrel caches, mouse relay stations all reading as countable spatial multiplicity, each species' signature legible as "how many of this thing, and how far apart." If it holds, the three signature systems share one visual grammar.

### P2-18 — The Metropolis · **LOCKED**

**Species mix:** raccoons, plus a good mix of core species and Guests. Core species appearing at the Metropolis creates no conflict — the no-cross-recruitment rule (§4) governs the *colony's* roster, not who exists in the world.

**The plaza name is procedural** — a different name each campaign, generated through the folk-name grammar like any other place. `"Exit 17 Pine Ridge"` originated as model self-plagiarism rather than authorship, but it has de facto continuity across at least four existing images and is retained where useful.

**Plate convention:** because the name is generated, no plate may present one as *the* name. Any name shown is captioned as a generated example.

### P2-19 — The Guest House · **LOCKED**

One structure family — the **Guest House** — whose **skin and build location vary by species.** Birds get a nest in a tree; a Mink's sits at water; a Mole's goes underground. Placement is part of the expression, not incidental.

This resolves the sixteen-habitats problem as one family with variants, and it ties Guest accommodation to the spatial logic already in canon: Anchor Points (§8.2) and the principle that where a structure sits matters (§28.2). §15.1 already lists "Guest accommodation or integration space" as a family, so this refines an existing family rather than adding one.

*Follow-on: §15.2 requires every upgradeable family to carry a named two-to-four-rung ladder, so the Guest House needs one in Appendix E.*

### P2-20 — The Crossing plate is a survey of the mode · **LOCKED**

Crossing graphics wait on the system being designed. The plate covers what the mode is *for* — staging, reading traffic, commitment, the First Law as literal mechanics, the accessibility commitments, the 70/20/10 return, and the fact that it is not an encounter — without asserting moment-to-moment play.

### P2-21 — Exposure is plateable either way · **WORKING**

Exposure may end up surfaced at Encounter round decision points, or stay a background quantity the player feels only through the effects of their decisions. **Both are plateable**, because the deck exists to increase understanding of the game rather than to function as a player's handbook. The plate proceeds; the game-side question stays open for design.

### P2-22 — Traffic as Weather needs depicting, not designing · **LOCKED**

Confirmed settled in §9.3. Wind Draft, the River's Spume with its four manifestations, and Litter are canon and complete. Art only.

### P2-24 — Spot illustrations are a distinct asset class · **LOCKED**

The GSS vignettes are **not** promoted to standalone full-page plates. They become **small illustrations beside body text on ordinary GDD pages** — which is what that deck was reaching for and got backwards.

**The original error was compositing, not scale.** Setting dense body copy *on top of* a dark illustrated field fails on legibility, which the Analysis correctly identified. Its remedy — make them full-page plates — was the wrong conclusion from the right diagnosis. A sourcebook puts spot art *beside* text, not under it.

**Consequence:** the pass now has three asset classes rather than two — plates, key art, and spot illustrations. The spot class has no backlog yet and needs one; most plates generate usable offcuts, so it should be built as production runs.

**This reframes Stage 2b.** If the deck is a book with body-text pages carrying spot art, then "plates standing alone" and "plates embedded in the GDD" stop being two uses to reconcile — they are one book with two page types. Orientation should be decided on that basis.

# Stage 2a — The Anthropomorphism Line · **SETTLED**

*2026-07-24. Gates every plate showing a citizen, which is most of them. This section is the specification an illustrator works from.*

**The governing frame.** §1.1 already drew the line before this conversation: *Mouse Guard* is an art reference for "small-animal material culture and landscape-as-existential-force, in a modern register — **not a source of medieval trappings.**" A cloak and a belt are material culture. A sword is a medieval trapping.

### P2-27 — Dress marks leaving home · **LOCKED**

**At home, citizens are bare.** Away, they wear a cloak, a belt, and carry a Tool. Kitting up happens at the Staging Post before a crossing.

This puts §1's Sanctuary → Exposure → Return rhythm on the character's body: an animal's dress says whether it is home or away before anything else does. It also protects §33.1's "not a costume drama" — a cloak means something precisely because most citizens most of the time are not wearing one.

**Two permanent exceptions, worn at home and away:**

- **The Keepsake** (§19) — personal, permanent, non-transferable, travels automatically. This is the citizen's always-on identifier: a bead, a bit of ribbon, a bottlecap on a cord.
- **Adaptive equipment** (§18.1) — a splint, a harness, a wheeled carry-frame. Produced by the Crafter, offsets a specific impairment, never chosen or swapped. **A maimed veteran is visibly maimed at home**, which is the entire point of Pillar 2.5. It must never read as armor or as a gadget.

**Consequences worth building on:**

- **The Staging Post gains a concrete second function.** §15.1 already lists it as a structure family at the highway edge; it is now also where cloaks and Tools live and where a party dresses. A strong art subject and a natural home for §19's standing Tool assignments.
- **The Founding Escape reads correctly.** The trio flee with nothing — adolescents caught at an outlying storage room. They arrive at the new median bare. The colony's first cloaks are made later, which is a progression rather than a costume.
- **Tier shows in dress quality without industrializing.** Tier I is torn bag strip; Tier IV is selected, cleaned, well-stitched scrap. This is §15.2's building-ladder principle — grandeur through scrap-mastery, never machinery — applied to citizens.
- **Young citizens are bare.** They do not expedition, so nothing follows.

### P2-28 — Colour comes from the roadside · **LOCKED**

Cloak and fabric colour is **found, not dyed**: high-vis orange, safety-vest yellow, sign green and white, tarp blue, burlap tan, plastic white, retread black.

*Mouse Guard* solves single-species identity with dyed cloaks, and MEDIAN needs that device — §4 puts one species in every colony and §2.2 demands citizens be visually distinguishable. But §13's material culture is scavenged, and scavenged fabric has no dye pot behind it. The highway margin happens to have an unusually strong palette, so a citizen in torn tarp and another in faded vest mesh are told apart *and* the setting is stated in the same stroke.

This is the decision that makes the result unmistakably MEDIAN rather than the reference.

*Watch item for 2c:* bright synthetics can fight the warm storybook tone §33.1 asks of the Colony register. Since dress is an away-only signal (P2-27), the collision is smaller than it looks — but weathering levels are a real 2c dial.

### P2-29 — Upright to work, four-legged to move · **LOCKED**

Citizens sit up to use their forepaws and drop to four legs to travel. Naturalistic for all three species, which genuinely do both.

**Full bipedalism would cost the Crossing.** §3.4 wants that register compressed and overwhelming; an animal sprinting four lanes low to the asphalt is far more vulnerable than a little person running. It would also push toward the miniature-humans framing §1.2 excludes by name.

**Where posture legibility concentrates:** the Encounter register, which is close, character-scale, and where §3.3 puts the art budget — and the Colony register. Field is top-down and surveyed, so posture barely reads there. Prioritise accordingly.

### P2-30 — No role-specific dress, anywhere · **LOCKED**

A per-Role visual is too much for the game and is not adopted. **Role reads from activity and tool** — in the deck's illustrations and in the game alike.

This is already how canon works: §12 says assignments always produce visible action in the colony, and §15.3 gives every building an always-visible functional icon. A Healer is a citizen working at a building marked Healing. Nothing needs to be worn, and cloaks stay free to carry individual identity rather than job function.

### P2-31 — The hand item: grip over object · **WORKING**

*Proposed, not contested. Flagged working until tested on a plate.*

A mouse holding a needle looks like a mouse holding a rapier. This is the standard failure mode and it cannot be avoided by banning nouns, since §13's flagship Tool is literally a pull-tab and a needle. Two rules do the work instead:

1. **Held at the balance, carried, or slung — never one-ended and point-outward.** Posture reads before the object does. Zeke in the reference scans as a guard rather than a fighter almost entirely because his arms are crossed and the blade is sheathed.
2. **Prefer objects whose function is legibly work.** A needle **with thread on it** is a sewing tool; a bare needle is a sword. A coil of wire, a hook, a pry-bar, a carrying frame, a lantern, a bundle.

**This resolves the opossum image's staff.** Held as a walking or probing tool it is fine; held as a quarterstaff it is not. That party is away, so its cloaks are correct — the only remaining fault in that image is the three core species.

### P2-32 — What is never drawn · **LOCKED**

Derived from §19, §33.1, and Appendix A rather than chosen here:

- **No weapons.** No sword, spear, pike, shield, or bow. The slot is named **Tool** and never Weapon, explicitly to prevent this drift.
- **No armor.** Including **bracers** and **baldrics** — a cross-body strap exists to carry a weapon and reads martial even when empty. If a belt is worn it is a *carrying* belt: pouches, a coil, a hook.
- **No forged metal.** Scrap is assembled and lashed, never smelted. Existing concept art with a copper-smelting forge is drift on record.
- **No helmets, no uniforms, no livery.**
- **No human figures anywhere** (P2-14).

### P2-33 — Expression sits in ears and posture first · **WORKING**

Rodent facial musculature is limited, and pushing a face past it tips into cartoon — against §33.1's grounded stylized realism. Ears are enormously expressive on all three species and cost nothing. Body posture carries the rest.

This matters mechanically, not just tonally: §2.3 requires fear to be visible in the world as "a frightened posture," and §33.3 requires tharn to read through more than a UI icon.

### P2-34 — Guests wear less, or nothing · **WORKING**

Guests are full equal-status citizens (§20), but they do not need cloaks to be distinguishable — they are already a different species from everyone else in the colony, which is the entire problem cloaks exist to solve. A crow, a snake, and a fox are not going to be confused with each other or with the core species.

Guests therefore carry at most a single signature object. Several cannot wear anything at all; a snake has nowhere to put a belt.

*May want GDD confirmation, since Guests are equal-status citizens and this treats them differently in one visible respect.*

# Stage 2b — Format · **SETTLED**

*2026-07-24.*

### P2-39 — REVERSES P2-35 and P2-37: landscape 16:9, desktop-first · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24, later the same day.*

**The book is read on a desktop screen and is not printed.** That single fact overturns the portrait decision, which rested on three arguments that all fail under it:

- *"Landscape forces unreadable body-text measure."* **Wrong, and my error.** That assumed a single column. Three columns at 16:9 give roughly 55 characters each; two columns at Letter landscape give 60–65. Both are textbook measure.
- *"Print convention favours portrait."* Moot if never printed.
- *"A sourcebook is a portrait book."* Sentiment, not function.

**Screen area is the binding constraint.** A portrait page on a 16:9 monitor renders at about 58% of the width, or forces scroll-per-page — which destroys the one thing a plate is for, being taken in at once. These plates are dense and need the full screen.

**Three things this buys back:**

- **Spreads disappear entirely.** No pagination constraint, no facing-page problem, no `.pptx` workaround. The ~5 panoramic plates are simply pages.
- **The world's horizontal geometry stops fighting the page** — cross-section, corridor, highway, median.
- **The legacy landscape library is already 16:9.** The v0.3 deck, the Concept deck and the GSS deck are all 1672 × 941 = exactly 1.78:1. They re-use at native aspect with no reflow, which materially raises the value of everything tagged HARVEST.

**Settled:** 16:9 · three-column text grid · no spreads · PowerPoint's native default slide size, so the tool stops being fought.

**Superseded below.** P2-35 and P2-37 are retained for the record only.

### ~~P2-35 — Portrait standard, spreads for panoramic plates~~ · **SUPERSEDED by P2-39**

Portrait throughout, with a defined **double-width spread** for the handful of genuinely panoramic subjects.

**Why portrait, given a horizontal world.** A landscape page with one text column runs past 140 characters to the line and forces three columns; portrait gives one comfortable column or an easy two. And the horizontal objection is answered by the Set 2 Highway Ecology plate, which is portrait and carries a **full-width landscape hero band** — the hero can be landscape inside a portrait page, losing nothing and gaining vertical run beneath it. The v0.3 Margin plate, at identical area, fits only two panel zones below its hero.

**Portrait also suits enumeration**, and MEDIAN enumerates constantly: eight biomes, eight Roles, seven Active Guests, nine Ambient, four Tiers, five Approaches, four registers, three species, four seasons, five daily beats, four Ratings, five Laws, five expedition categories, four harm rungs.

**Confirmed spreads:** the Cross-Section · the Corridor Chain · Field Mode.
**Candidates, to settle when designed:** the Four Registers · the Rest-Stop Metropolis · Road Work.

*The Four Registers is a late addition to the candidate list and a strong one: four parallel columns — verb, register, attention, tempo — run naturally across a wide surface, and it is the one plate that has to make four things feel genuinely distinct at a glance. It is also in Part I, which means the spread format gets proven inside the first slice rather than waiting for Part II.*

*The legacy landscape majority — 35 pages against 15 — is misleading. Key art crops to any shape, the GSS vignettes are small and croppable, and v0.3 contributes diagram structure rather than pages. The one deck that is genuinely a source of layouts is Set 2, and it is portrait.*

### P2-36 — One book · **LOCKED**

The GDD **becomes** the sourcebook. Body text, spot illustrations, and full plates are one artifact; the 44-plate list is that book's illustration programme rather than a separate deck.

**The GDD already anticipated this.** §34 says of the IMAGEGEN blocks: *"In a future full GDD these blocks become sidebars. They are marked inline here so the optional layer is never mistaken for core specification."* One book keeps that promise.

**Consequences:**

- **Plate order is book order** — the GDD's eight parts. The Production Plan is already sequenced this way.
- **Plate placement becomes a real decision.** Sourcebook convention is a full-page plate at a section opening and spot art inline. Needs settling in 2c.
- **Two page types, not one.** The identity must hold at full-plate density *and* at sparse body-text-with-marginal-art density. The sparse page is the harder test and belongs in the first production batch.
- **Page furniture enters scope** — running heads, folios, section openers, sidebar treatment, table styling, pull-quotes. A standalone deck would not have needed any of it. This meaningfully enlarges 2c.
- **Appendices E and F are holes in the book.** Per-family upgrade ladders and folk place-name component banks are both *"Deferred. To be authored."* They are authoring dependencies, not art tasks, and the book cannot be finished without them.
- **Spreads constrain pagination.** A spread only works if it falls across facing pages, so the panoramic plates need planned *positions*, not just content. Note the practical wrinkle: `.pptx` has no concept of facing pages, so a spread is either a single 17×11 slide or two abutting slides designed as one — worth deciding before the Cross-Section is built.

**Scope, stated plainly.** The GDD is roughly 32,000 words. At sourcebook density that is ~40 text pages, plus 49 plate sides once spreads are counted, plus front matter and appendices — call it **~100 pages**. The handoff expects the GDD to roughly double as artwork lands, which would put the finished book nearer **130–140 pages.** That is a real book, and worth knowing before the first plate.

Ratio 0.77. Standard RPG sourcebook trim, prints without fuss, comfortable for a two-column text page with marginal art. **Spreads are 17 × 11.**

### P2-40 — Generated text garbles only where it is unspecified · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24.*

Evidence from two prompt runs: every string a prompt supplies **verbatim** renders legibly; every label the model is left to invent turns to gibberish. The v0.2 species prompt specified all five descriptor fields and all three blurbs, and all of it came out clean. The EXPEDITION prompt specified its phase headings — those rendered — and left the gear callouts and sensor list unspecified, which produced *WNPEN LOAP LINEVG* and *CRTRACTION DREK HIGH*.

**This does not relax P2-7.** Live editable text is still required by §33.1 and Appendix D, and generated type is neither editable, nor consistent across 44 plates, nor typographically ours.

**But it changes the method.** Specify every string in Pass 1 anyway, because a plate generated with real copy is sized for real copy, looks finished, and is therefore judgeable — and it can then be stripped to a scaffolded base plate that still carries ruled lines where each line of type belongs. That stripped plate is what goes into PowerPoint.

**Corollary — re-aspecting.** Image-to-image re-aspect works on plates with **distinct** panels and a dominant hero. It fails on plates with **parallel repeated** structure, where the model cannot track which content belongs to which column and duplicates or swaps them. Generate parallel-structure plates at target aspect directly.

### P2-41 — AMENDS P2-7: flat for reading, stripped base retained by risk · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24.*

P2-7 required layered editable text on everything, from §33.1 and Appendix D. That rule was written when generated text was assumed to be unusable. P2-40 disproved the assumption: specified strings render cleanly, and they render *better integrated* than an overlay would — sharing the paper, the light and the aging, where live type sits flat on top of it.

**So the typographic case for layering is gone.** What remains is the ability to change a word later without regenerating the picture — and regeneration does not reproduce the same picture (P2-40, corollary).

**The exposure is forward revision, not backwards creep.** An earlier draft of this entry cited the day's canon findings as evidence of drift risk. That was wrong and is withdrawn: every one of those findings came from auditing legacy assets or from deliberately running deprecated prompts as style tests. Nothing produced under the current discipline has regressed. The real exposure is that the GDD is still developing — Appendices E and F are unwritten, and seventeen Appendix D items will resolve into copy.

**Therefore, risk-weighted rather than uniform:**

|---|---|---|
| **Settled canon** | Three Species · Four Registers · Cross-Section · Biome Codex · Economy · Roles · Two Loops · Laws & Sayings · Seasons · The Records · Tiers · Scale Language | **Ship flat.** No base retained. §4's stat frame is locked integers; these are unlikely to change a word |

### P2-42 — State the downstream use, not just the constraint · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24.*

Adding *"(text will be recomposited offline as editable vector type)"* to the strip instruction improves compliance, because it tells the model **why** — which lets it make the right call in cases the instruction does not explicitly cover. A bare constraint has no such generalisation.

Applies to every prompt in the pass: say what the asset is *for* downstream, not only what it must look like.

### P2-43 — Upscaling preserves art and destroys type · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24, from the Three Species production run.*

A high-res pass with a strong exactness instruction — *"be as EXACT as possible about all the art details, do not reinterpret anything"* — **fixed the composition drift** seen in the earlier upscale: bolt holes, caches and layouts all survived intact. That wording works and should be reused.

**But it corrupted every text string.** *Coordinated hanling · Strong rescoe · Dump and collapse · Dispereal · Thelt and spollage · Chospest to feed · Small wids · Squirreb.* Most dangerous of all, **"Low load" became "Low food"** — a plausible-looking wrong fact rather than obvious nonsense, and the kind of error that ships. The aspect also drifted from 16:9 to 3:2.

**The rule: ship flat OR upscale, never both.**

For a desktop-read book this does not bite — native output is already 2600 px wide, which is 1:1 on a 2560 monitor. Upscaling is only needed for print, and if print is ever wanted, strip first and set type over the upscaled art.

**Any upscale gets a full canon re-check**, including the text, and an aspect check.

### P2-44 — The strip pass destroys text zones *above* illustrations · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-24, after two consecutive failures on the same zone.*

The text-strip pass reliably **preserves** text zones sitting *below* or *beside* an illustration, and reliably **destroys** zones sitting *above* one — the illustration expands upward into the vacated space. This survived both a tightened instruction and the addition of the downstream-use sentence (P2-42), so it is architectural rather than a wording problem.

**Two ways to live with it:**

- **Design plates with text below and beside artwork only**, never above; or
- **Accept it and rebuild the top zones in PowerPoint**, which is trivial — they are empty rectangles.

Descriptor panels below illustrations and table grids both survive the strip cleanly, so the device kit established by the Three Species plate is already strip-safe apart from its column headings and blurbs.

### P2-45 — Editorial method: certainty levels, claims, regret, four voices · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-25. Adopted from an outside review of the concept-book pivot.*

Four devices governing how a plate is *written*, distinct from how it looks. Specified in full in the Production Plan §3.5.

- **Levels of certainty** — fully / structurally / evocatively specified, assigned deliberately. **This replaces "flagged as open" as the book's way of handling Appendix D.** Seventeen open items become seventeen resolution choices; Crossing is not unfinished, it is evocatively specified.
- **The claim test** — every plate carries a one-line thesis before it gets a layout. *"Winter is not a debuff"* rather than *"Seasons."* Canon supplies most of them.
- **The regret test** — *can a sensible player genuinely regret either available choice?* Applied as a check that the tradeoff is visible and not merely the capability.
- **Four voices** — authoritative, in-world, player-facing, designer. The designer voice stays rare; too much of it breaks the spell.

**Explicitly rejected from the same source:** *"where a mechanic is unsolved, choose a plausible answer and move forward."* That is precisely how the five legacy decks improvised content that did not survive contact with canon. The certainty levels give the honest version — decisive about what is settled, evocative about what is not, and **never a number invented to fill a hole.** Gaps still go to the questions log.

*Also open, for 2c:* whether running prose uses reader vocabulary ("vehicles," "culvert") or colony vocabulary ("Roaring Iron," "the Rivers of Thunder"), and where the friction between the two is deliberate.

### P2-46 — Worked examples are a third content track · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-25.*

The book needs a recurring **example of play** — the device that turns an encyclopedia into a game, and the thing the pass had no way of producing. Five to write, listed in the Production Plan §3.6, with **one expedition from Launcher to Record entry** as the flagship.

### P2-48 — Prompts never name a file · **LOCKED**
*2026-07-25, from a broken run.*

Every reference inside a prompt is to **"the image attached to this message,"** with filenames explicitly disregarded. Naming a specific plate broke a generation: the file had since been renamed, the model went hunting for a name that no longer existed, and the correct attached image was ignored.

**This extends to the notes at the top of each prompt file.** Those are written for the human, but they are read by the model along with everything else when the file is dragged in — so a note reading *"attach the Three Species plate"* becomes an instruction the model will try and fail to follow. Notes now say *"attach the current reference plate as the first image."*

The general form: **a prompt may only refer to things the model can actually see.** Filenames, folder paths and document names are all invisible to it.

### P2-38 — Folklore is the book's connective tissue · **WORKING**

*Proposed, following from P2-36.*

§6 already places the Laws and Sayings on loading screens, the Community Board, and at the Story Circle — short, memorable, tonal, and repeated. In a sourcebook those are **section epigraphs and pull-quotes**: the Laws open the sections whose systems they teach, and the Sayings carry the tonal register between them.

This costs nothing, it uses material that already exists and is already canon, and it means the book's connective tissue is in-world voice rather than editorial apparatus — which is exactly the register P2-13 asks for. It also gives the folklore a fourth exposure surface beyond the three §6.1 lists.

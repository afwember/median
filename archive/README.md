# Archive

Superseded material, kept for history.

---

## ⚠ Nothing in this folder is canon

Current canon is `canon/MEDIAN_GDD_v0.4.3.md` and nothing else. Every file here has been replaced, and several contain material canon now explicitly rejects.

This warning is not decorative. The project has already lost time twice to superseded material being read as current — once from legacy artwork asserting rejected mechanics, once from a decision living in a log while a plan file still stated its opposite. **An archive of old canon editions is the most dangerous version of that failure**, because a superseded GDD looks exactly like the real one. If you are pointing an AI tool at a file, check the version in the filename before you attach it.

## What goes where

**`gdd/`** — prior editions of the design document, plus the supporting papers that were absorbed into one and no longer stand alone.

**`threads/`** — design conversation transcripts. The primary sources. Where an appendix says something was "considered and not adopted," this is where the considering happened, usually in more depth than the summary of it.

## Why keep any of it

Three reasons, in order of how often they actually matter.

**The reasoning outlives the decision.** Appendix B records that *Beasts*, *Growler*, *Shells* and *Dragons* were each considered as names for Roaring Iron and rejected — *Dragons* because the imagery was excellent but the noun was borrowed from another genre. That kind of reasoning is what stops a good idea being re-proposed every six months, and the threads hold far more of it than any appendix can.

**Rejected ideas come back around.** Several things cut from early versions returned later in better form. Traffic-as-weather was a vague notion in v0.2 and is now three named hazards in §9.3. The archive is where you check whether an idea was rejected on its merits or merely rejected too early.

**A concept book gets to show its work.** Part IX exists because a sourcebook is more interesting when it feels observed rather than invented. The same is true of the project itself: a book with a visible development history is a more interesting object than one that arrives fully formed.

# MEDIAN Phase 2 — Questions Raised by the Art Pass

Design gaps the artwork exposes. **Logged, not solved by the art pass.** This document feeds a future GDD pass; Phase 2 does not resolve anything here inline.

**Convention:** Q-1 upward, with the plate that raised it and what specifically cannot be drawn without an answer. Marked **OPEN**, **ANSWERED** (with the answer and where it lands), or **NOT A GAP** (investigated, canon already covers it).

**Status:** all ten Stage 1 questions are closed. Answers are recorded as decisions P2-13 through P2-22 in `MEDIAN_Phase2_running_decisions.md`; five are canon-grade and are queued for the next GDD revision.

## Raised at Stage 1 — all closed

### Q-1 — The Founding Escape has no name, and a plate needs to title it · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: The Founding Escape plate · GDD §6.3, §35.1, Appendix D*

**"The Founding Escape" stands as the in-fiction name**, on the grounds that it is not far from in-world already — which holds up against §22.2's homely-and-plain register. **The lost home is the "Ancestral Warren / Den / Burrow,"** species-specific per campaign, which is a correct rather than generic use of the species terminology.

→ P2-15. Lands in §6.3 and §35.1; closes the Appendix D "Unnamed in fiction" item.

### Q-2 — What does a Presence look like when it is never counted? · **ANSWERED — and now closed in canon**

*Settled by V5-12 in the v0.4.5 pass: adversary numbers are free to depict. The artwork may show whatever number makes a compelling picture, and the fiction may name one. The count was always flavour, and flavour is the illustrator's to choose.*

*Original answer and reasoning below.*
*Raised by: The Encounter Frame plate · GDD §25.3*

**Contested encounter plates show a roughly equal opposing party** — comparable apparent mass to the player's party, composed so as not to invite counting, never captioned with a number. Mass, not count, expressed as a staging convention rather than a diagram.

→ P2-16. Art convention; no GDD change needed.

### Q-3 — Exposure never displays as a number, so how is it shown at all? · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: Exposure & Outcomes plate · GDD §25.5, §25.8*

**Plateable either way.** Exposure may end up surfaced at Encounter round decision points, or remain a background quantity the player feels only through the effects of their decisions. The plate proceeds regardless, because the deck exists to increase understanding of the game rather than to serve as a player's handbook.

→ P2-21. The plate is unblocked. **The game-side question remains genuinely open for design** and is not resolved by this answer.

### Q-4 — Warren Flow is unspecified and the rabbit plates depend on it · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: The Three Species plate, rabbit warren cutaway · GDD §4.2, Appendix D*

**Multiplicity of exits is the rabbit's readable signature.** A mouse Burrow shows one entrance; a rabbit Warren shows half a dozen at colony scale, plus at least one remote escape tunnel visible in the Field layer. The tunnel surfacing in Field view is canon-supported — §3.5 requires the Colony and Field presentations of the same ground to be the same place.

→ P2-17. Gives Warren Flow a visual specification even though the mechanic itself stays open in Appendix D.

**Follow-on raised by the answer, and worth confirming:** whether this generalises to all three signature systems — rabbit exits, squirrel caches, mouse relay stations, each reading as countable spatial multiplicity. If it holds, the three share one visual grammar and the species plate gets much stronger.

### Q-5 — The Metropolis plaza name and species mix are deferred to this phase · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: The Rest-Stop Metropolis plate · GDD §29.4, Appendix D*

**Species:** raccoons, plus a good mix of core species and Guests. Core species at the Metropolis create no conflict — no-cross-recruitment governs the colony's roster, not who exists in the world.

**The plaza name is procedural**, generated fresh each campaign through the folk-name grammar. `"Exit 17 Pine Ridge"` was model self-plagiarism rather than authorship, but has de facto continuity across four existing images and is retained where useful.

→ P2-18. Closes two Appendix D Phase-2 deferrals. **Plate convention:** no plate presents a generated name as *the* name; any name shown is captioned as an example.

### Q-6 — Guest accommodation is a charm feature with no specification · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: Active Guests, Ambient Guests, Structures & Upgrade Ladders · GDD §15.1, §20.4*

**One family — the Guest House — whose skin and build location vary by species.** Birds get a nest in a tree; the Mink's sits at water; the Mole's goes underground. Placement is part of the expression.

Sixteen Guests therefore need one family with variants rather than sixteen structures, and Guest accommodation inherits the spatial logic already in canon — Anchor Points, and the principle that where a structure sits matters.

→ P2-19. Refines the existing §15.1 family. **Follow-on:** §15.2 requires every upgradeable family to carry a named two-to-four-rung ladder, so the Guest House needs one in Appendix E.

### Q-7 — Crossing is the signature mechanic and the least-specified system · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: The Crossing plate · GDD §23.1, Appendix D*

**The plate is a survey of the mode.** Exact crossing graphics wait until the system is designed. The plate covers what the mode is for — staging, reading traffic, commitment, the First Law as literal mechanics, accessibility commitments, the 70/20/10 return, and its exclusion from the encounter model — without asserting moment-to-moment play.

→ P2-20. The underlying system stays open in Appendix D; the plate no longer waits on it.

### Q-8 — Does the deck have a reader? · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: the whole pass · no GDD section*

**A mix of a game design document and an RPG core sourcebook** — the kind worth reading cover to cover as a teenager. This is the reason for the heavy graphic push. As the deck iterates, sections aimed solely at designers get progressively less space.

→ P2-13. The most consequential answer of the ten: it sets caption voice, text density, in-world material, and table treatment for all of Stage 2c, and it demotes the designer-facing probability content on the Exposure and Contest plates.

### Q-9 — Is "the Giants" depictable? · **ANSWERED**
*Raised by: Road Work, The Founding Escape, The Giants vocabulary · GDD §33.2*

**No people, anywhere.** No human figures in any plate or any concept art.

**This is tighter than canon.** §33.2 permits human bodies in exactly one case — Road Work and Founding-Escape-class threats, where "their presence *is* the point." Phase 2 declines the allowance; those moments are carried by machinery alone. The artistic case: machinery without an operator reads as indifferent force rather than intent, which is what §10.2 already demands of it. Vehicles are unaffected and remain constantly visible.

→ P2-14. **Flagged for the GDD pass: whether §33.2's exception should simply close.**

### Q-10 — Do the traffic-as-weather hazards still need a design pass? · **NOT A GAP**
*Confirmed by the designer.* Traffic as Weather is designed and needs only depicting. §9.3's Wind Draft, the River's Spume with four manifestations, and Litter are canon and complete; the Analysis's earlier candidate menu is superseded.

→ P2-22.

## Carried forward — open going into Stage 2

Neither is a blocker, but both were surfaced *by* the answers above rather than resolved by them.

### Q-11 — Does the multiplicity grammar cover all three species? · **OPEN**
Q-4's answer gives the rabbit a strong visual signature. Whether squirrel caches and mouse relay stations read the same way — countable, spatial, "how many and how far apart" — would unify the three signature systems under one visual grammar. Worth a decision at Stage 2a or 2b, since it affects every species plate and the three Priority 1 colony images.

### Q-13 — A tenth Ambient Guest: the monk parakeet · **OPEN — design, not art**
*Raised 2026-07-25, incidentally, by an outside reviewer inventing a "feral parakeet faction" without knowing the roster.*

**The joke has a real animal behind it.** Monk parakeets have maintained established feral colonies in New York City for decades — famously at Green-Wood in Brooklyn, and in the understructure of the West Side Highway. They are the only parrot that builds its own nest: large communal stick structures, characteristically raised **on human infrastructure** — utility poles, substations, floodlight rigs.

That is an unusually exact fit for MEDIAN's stated working principle of real ecology as inspiration. It is a loud, conspicuous, highly social bird that colonizes exactly the kind of overlooked human structure the game is about, and it would bring a genuinely different register to a roster that currently has one bird at night and one songbird.

**Why it is logged rather than adopted.** §20.2's Ambient roster of nine is settled canon, and adding to it is a design decision, not an art one. Two things make it worth raising anyway: Appendix D already flags **the Turtle's effect as the weakest on the roster** — "a flat trickle duplicates what an outbuilding already does, and it deserves something with more character" — so there is an open slot-shaped problem. And a communal nest-builder raises an obvious candidate effect around **structure, shelter or construction** that nothing else on the roster covers.

*For a future GDD pass. Not to be drawn until it is canon.*

### Q-12 — Exposure's player-facing expression, in the game · **OPEN**
Q-3 unblocked the plate without settling the design. §25.5 and §25.8 leave exposure derived, undisplayed, and non-persistent; whether it surfaces at round decision points remains a real design question with real consequences for the Encounter frame. Belongs to the design thread, not to Phase 2.

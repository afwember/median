# GROUP A — THE NUMBERS

## V5-1 — The Body-Unit: two mice to one rabbit · **SETTLED**
*§4.1. No stat changes. All published values were correct.*

**The Body-Unit is the aggregate quantum:** one rabbit, one squirrel, or **two mice**. Capacity, party size and party score are counted in body-units.

| | Carry | Food | Body-unit | Unit carry | Unit food |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rabbit** | 10 | 10 | 1 animal | 10 | 10 |
| **Squirrel** | 20 | 10 | 1 animal | 20 | 10 |
| **Wood Mouse** | 6 | 5 | **2 animals** | **12** | 10 |

**Food is the exact quantum. Carry carries a deliberate premium.**

Every body-unit eats exactly 10 — two mice consume precisely one rabbit's ration. Carry then runs **10 : 12 : 20**: rabbit nominal, mouse-pair a fifth better, squirrel double.

**The mouse's 20% edge is payment for risk.** A mouse Standard Party puts **six citizens in the field where a rabbit party puts three** — twice the bodies exposed to wounds, tharn and death. The premium is what that buys. Without it, playing mice would be strictly worse: same cargo, double the casualties.

**This confirms rather than corrects §4.1.** The mouse's carry-per-food is 1.2 against the rabbit's 1.0, so *"Density — many small bodies, efficient per food"* is accurate as written. **An earlier draft of this entry proposed changing mouse carry from 6 to 5 to make the quantum exact; that was wrong** and would have deleted the species' actual advantage while "fixing" a number that was already right.

## V5-2 — Aggregate like half-animals, suffer like whole ones · **SETTLED**
*§4.4, §25.5. The governing principle behind the Body-Unit.*

The quantum applies to **capacity**, never to **consequence**.

| Resolves at the **body-unit** | Resolves at the **individual animal** |
|---|---|
| Carry capacity | Wounds and maiming |
| Party size and composition | Tharn |
| Party Score in an encounter | Exposure |
| Cooperative lifting | Death |
| Food upkeep | Bonds, Hearths, Records |

**What this produces.** A rabbit colony loses citizens rarely, and each loss is a whole body-unit — a blow. A mouse colony loses more often, and each loss is half a unit — attrition. **Comparable capability lost across a campaign; entirely different grief.**

This gives §4.4's Safety in Numbers a cause at last. A mouse party spreads exposure across twice the bodies, so each individual's exposure runs lower while the number exposed runs higher — *"most of the group makes it, and occasionally one doesn't."* That was asserted; it is now arithmetic.

It also shapes loot: a Standard item carried by two mice is dropped or split if either goes down, where a rabbit carrying the same item either brings it home or does not. **Mice are more resilient in aggregate and more fragile per item.**

## V5-3 — Items live at ×10 resolution, on a five-tier weight ladder · **SETTLED**
*§13. Resolves the deferred item-weight thresholds in Appendix D.*

**The bug, and why it happened.** §4.1's scale exists for one reason: the real ratios are Rabbit 1 : Squirrel 2 : Mouse 0.6, and 0.6 is not an integer. Canon says as much — *"the stat frame is scaled so that no species carries a fractional value."* Carry 10 was never "ten items"; it is one unit of capacity at ×10 resolution. §13 then pinned items to the **pre**-multiplied scale with *one slot equals one unit of value*, leaving stats and items an order of magnitude apart. A three-squirrel party could carry sixty things.

| Tier | Weight | Examples | Who lifts it alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trifle** | 1 | a seed, a crumb, a bead, a gravel chip | anyone |
| **Small** | 5 | an acorn, a bottle cap, a pull-tab, a berry cluster | one mouse |
| **Standard** | 10 | a grass bundle, a large mushroom, a coil of wire | a rabbit — or **two mice, at 12** |
| **Heavy** | 20 | a bread crust, a folded fabric scrap | a squirrel, or two rabbits |
| **Cooperative** | 40+ | beyond any single body-unit | two squirrels · four rabbits · seven mice |

**The basic Relay Network operation is a pair of mice sharing one Standard item** — a single mouse cannot lift it at 6, a pair manages at 12 with a little to spare. Exactly the "short, visible handoff" §4.4 describes, arrived at by arithmetic rather than assertion.

**Hauls become nameable.** A Standard Party brings home roughly 6 Smalls as mice or rabbits, 12 as squirrels — instead of sixty. Those can be written into a Record entry.

## V5-4 — Party size is 3–5 body-units, for every species · **SETTLED**
*§27. Not previously quantified anywhere.*

| Species | Standard Party | Range | Carry at standard | Carry at max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rabbit** | 3 animals | 3–5 | **30** | 50 |
| **Squirrel** | 3 animals | 3–5 | **60** | 100 |
| **Wood Mouse** | 6 animals | 6–10 | **36** | 60 |

**One rule underneath: three to five body-units.** It reads as 3–5 animals for rabbits and squirrels, 6–10 for mice. A squirrel Standard Party carries **exactly double** a rabbit's; a mouse Standard Party carries **20% more** than a rabbit's, paid for by having twice as many citizens at risk.

**"Standard Party" is the default the Launcher offers** and the size the game is tuned around. The range is available; the standard is the shape.

**Proportional commitment stays near-constant.** Against working ceilings of ~15 for rabbits and squirrels and ~25 for mice, a Standard Party is roughly **20% / 20% / 24%** of the colony. Sending a party *feels* the same across all three species while the numbers look nothing alike. *(Those ceilings are estimates, not canon — see V5-7.)*

**Consequences:**

- **Tharn's "who rescues, not whether"** (§18.2) requires a third body still holding the line. Three body-units is the floor for that choice to exist.

## V5-4a — A Guest is one Body-Unit, and displaces two mice · **SETTLED**
*§20.1, §27.*

An Active Guest occupies **one full body-unit** — so it displaces one rabbit, one squirrel, or **two mice.**

| Standard Party with a Guest | Composition |
|---|---|
| Rabbit | 2 rabbits + 1 Guest |
| Squirrel | 2 squirrels + 1 Guest |
| **Wood Mouse** | **4 mice + 1 Guest** |

**This resolves an asymmetry cleanly.** Counted per animal, a Guest would have been 33% of a rabbit party and only 17% of a mouse party — meaning Guests mattered less to mice for no reason anyone chose. Counted in body-units, a Guest is **exactly one-third of every Standard Party**, whatever the species. The found-family beat weighs the same in all three campaigns.

**It also gives the §20 note its baseline.** Active Guests are to carry and contribute like core species — and a body-unit is the unit of that contribution, so **a Guest's nominal carry is 10**, matching a rabbit. Larger Guests may exceed it and smaller ones fall short; the roster pass in Group E tunes individual values against that baseline rather than inventing them.

*Note that a Guest replacing two mice trades 12 carry for a nominal 10.* A mouse party pays slightly more capacity for a Guest than a rabbit party does — which is correct, since Guests bring options rather than cargo, and the mouse party's whole premise is cargo through numbers.

## V5-5 — One expedition at a time · **SETTLED**
*§9.0, §27.*

A single expedition runs at once, colony-wide. One expedition per citizen per day remains but is no longer the binding constraint.

Each departure becomes an event rather than a dispatch. The Launcher's question — *"who do I trust with this?"* — stays weighty because there is no second party to fall back on. The day acquires a shape: you send a party and you wait. §5.3 already describes the loop in the singular throughout, and this protects Pillar 2.1 — the colony is the heart, and expeditions are its dangerous counterpart, not a logistics network to optimise.

## V5-6 — Cooperative carrying uses combined party capacity · **SETTLED**
*§13.*

The party's carry totals. Anything within the total comes home, with heavy items visibly shared in the art and the fiction. No per-item pairing bookkeeping.

This is what makes collective strength real: seven mice together lift what no mouse can, which is the species' whole thesis rendered as a rule.

## V5-7 — Advancement costs move to ×10 · **SETTLED**
*§31.1.*

Tier advancement resource costs scale with the stat frame, and should be **genuinely expensive** — a visible drop in the stores, felt rather than noted.

This sits against §31.1's caution to *"avoid arbitrary giant resource sacrifices,"* and both hold: significant **and legible**. The player should see what it took and understand why. Exact values remain deferred numerics.

## V5-8 — The population study is deferred, with a brief · **DEFERRED**
*§10.1, §11, §17.3, §17.4.*

Whether there are one or two Nesting Seasons per year is not settled in isolation. It belongs to a dedicated pass covering the whole curve.

**What that pass must cover:**

- **Ceilings per species.** §11 puts mice at 1.5–2× rabbit and squirrel head-count; the ~15 and ~25 used above are working estimates, not canon.
- **Wanderer arrival rate**, and whether it scales with Tier — already open in Appendix D.
- **Nesting Season frequency and yield.** §10.1 currently implies spring always, autumn sometimes.
- **Expected death rate**, which V4-11 establishes should be low.
- **Role staffing pressure.** Eight standing Roles need bodies, and those bodies cannot be on expedition.
- **Outpost stationing draw**, which removes citizens from home without raising the ceiling.
- **Party size as a fraction of the colony** — now ~20–24%, worth holding roughly constant.
- **The curve against four Tiers** — how a founding trio reaches a full colony across a campaign.
- **Whether the mouse ceiling should be an exact multiple of the rabbit's**, given the body-unit. §11's "one-and-a-half to two times" may want to become exactly two, so that colonies compare cleanly in body-units as parties now do.

**Growth arrives in body-units, not individuals.** *(Flagged 2026-07-26; to be settled by this pass.)* Where a rabbit or squirrel colony gains **one** citizen, a mouse colony gains **two** — a Nesting Season yielding a pair, a wanderer arriving as a bonded pair or a sibling pair, a Hearth producing two rather than one.

The whole frame now runs in body-units — carry, party size, food, Guest slots. Growth is the one place it does not, and leaving it in individuals would make the mouse curve take twice as long to reach a comparable colony while every other system already treats two mice as one. **Fixing this is what makes §11's density claim true in practice** rather than only at the ceiling: mice do not merely *end up* more numerous, they *become* more numerous at the same rate everyone else does, counted properly.

It also gives Nesting Season a species-specific texture worth writing: rabbits and squirrels name one new citizen, mice name two.

*Consequences for the pass: whether wanderers arrive in pairs or singly (and if singly, whether that makes them proportionally rarer for mice), whether a mouse Hearth's pair are always littermates, and whether deaths should correspondingly be counted in pairs — they should not, per V5-2, since consequence never quantizes.*

# GROUP B — THE ENCOUNTER

## V5-9 — Every contested encounter carries a decision · **SETTLED**
*§25.1, §25.8. Inverts the published "common case."*

**Contested encounters run two or three rounds, with one or two Turns.** The one-round, no-Turn encounter is eliminated: an encounter where the player chooses an Approach and then only watches is not a decision, it is an animation.

| Rounds | Turns | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | **1** | **The common case.** Approach, a round, a real decision, resolution |
| **3** | **2** | Still close after Round 2. Rare. The epic case |

**The self-selecting length mechanism survives intact** — closeness still decides whether an encounter runs to three. Only the floor moves, from one round to two.

**The exception, and the only one: uncontested nodes.** A quiet node still opens the Encounter frame and is still described — §25 is emphatic that "a quiet, uncontested node still gets its moment," and that rhythm holds. But it **may resolve immediately**, without a Turn. *The moment is the description, not the decision.*

**Canon edits required.** §25.1's table and its characterization of one round as "the common case." §25.8's guardrail *"At most two Turns per encounter. Most encounters have none"* — the second sentence is now false and inverts to *most contested encounters have one.* The three-round ceiling is untouched.

## V5-10 — Load makes an expedition end itself · **SETTLED**
*§23.1, §25.4, §25.8. Two notes that turn out to be one mechanism.*

**Carry load becomes a modifier in encounter resolution**, alongside the terrain, weather and maiming modifiers §25.4 already defines. A laden party is a slower, clumsier, more exposed party.

**This gives an expedition a natural arc.** Early nodes are met light and capable; later nodes are met heavy and vulnerable. The party gets richer and worse at the same time, and every additional node is a real gamble rather than a free draw.

**It also answers §25.8's own warning about node-to-node creep** without adding a limiter. Nothing caps the number of nodes; the cargo does it. And it hands §23.2's *"the player chooses when to head home"* an actual decision — go home heavy and safe, or press on and get worse at surviving what you find.

No new system: an existing modifier slot gains an existing quantity.

## V5-11 — Tharn seizes the Turn · **SETTLED**
*§18.2, §25.4. Four notes resolved by one mechanism.*

**Tharn is a bombshell.** When it fires the encounter stops being about the party. The frame centres on that citizen, by name, and **the next Turn is forced and scoped: it must address the tharn.**

That requires no new machinery. The Turn already exists between rounds; tharn simply seizes it. This is also the answer to *"tharn needs a citizen-level focus in the encounter cycle"* — the group-mass model (§25.3) holds everywhere except here, and tharn is the single event that pulls one animal out of the mass mid-encounter. **The exception is the drama.**

**The choice is who rescues, not whether.** The forced Turn presents the party members who could go, and the player picks one. Each carries a different cost — a bonded friend, the strongest, the one already carrying the most. Exposure and Bonds are both existing systems and both do the work.

**Tharn cannot fire on a final round.** Rescue requires a subsequent Turn, and no Turn follows the last round. So tharn occurs only on Round 1 of a longer encounter, or Round 2 of a three-round one — **which means it can only happen in an encounter that was already going long, and therefore already close and tense.** The dramaturgy falls out of the timing rule.

**Losing a citizen to untended tharn should not occur under normal play.** Abandonment remains possible and remains painful, but it is the consequence of a deliberate refusal or a genuinely impossible position — never of inattention.

**Repercussions are small, lasting and specific.** §18.2 already provides "a persistent fear memory tied to a trigger"; the trigger is now **the species that caused it.** A citizen who went tharn before weasels is worse in the presence of weasels afterward. Cheap, characterful, and it makes a Record entry read like a life.

## V5-12 — Adversary numbers are free to depict · **SETTLED**
*§25.3. Closes an open question from the art log.*

The adversary remains a **Presence** and is never counted in the rules. But **the artwork may depict any number of animals that makes a compelling picture**, and the fiction may name one — *"a pair of weasels hold the culvert"* — exactly as §25.3 already permits.

**This closes Q-2 in `art_raised_questions.md`**, which asked what a Presence looks like when it is never counted. The answer: paint what reads best. The number was always flavour, and flavour is the illustrator's to choose.

## V5-13 — Guests and antagonists are named from the first encounter · **SETTLED**
*§20, §26.1, §26.2. Mostly a confirmation; one real extension.*

Already canon for Guests: *"A joining Guest receives a Given Name — and carries it from the first encounter, before any decision to recruit. The player meets a named animal, not a candidate."*

**The extension: recurring antagonists are named too.** §26.2's worked example — *"the same animal that drove a party off a node last season can be sitting at the Story Circle this one"* — only lands if the player can tell it is the same animal. **Recognition requires a name**, and the name has to have been there when it was an enemy.

## V5-14 — A Distinction is named for the encounter that earned it · **SETTLED**
*§18.3, §22.1.*

Distinctions derive their character and their wording from the circumstance that produced them, rather than being drawn from a generic list.

**This reaches further than it looks.** §22.1 grants a citizen's **After-name** with their first Distinction — so the After-name is now derived from a specific remembered event. A citizen is not named "the Swift"; they are named for the night they did the thing.

## V5-15 — Antagonist ratio varies with conditions · **SETTLED**
*§26.1. Closes an Appendix D open item.*

The 70/30 antagonist split is no longer fixed. It varies with season, recent Road Work, biome, and a Reach's lifecycle state — the last already implied by §28.4, where outbuilding investment makes a claimed Reach calmer.

§26.1 currently flags exactly this as open; the note settles it in the affirmative.

## V5-16 — Change Approach is a Turn action, and it costs · **SETTLED**
*§25.2, §25.4. Adds a fifth Turn option.*

The player may **change Approach at a Turn** — abandoning a Contest to Parley, breaking off a Parley to Evade.

**Two constraints keep it from being a free retry:**

- **Only Approaches that were available at entry.** §25.2's information rule stands: the player saw which Approaches were on the table and which were not. Weasels holding a culvert still do not Parley, and switching cannot conjure an option that never existed.
- **Switching forfeits momentum.** The new Approach's Presence applies fresh, and **exposure does not reset** — everything the party has already spent staying in it, they have still spent. A reversal, not a rewind.

**Why this is the strongest addition in the group.** §25.2 opens with *"An encounter is not automatically a fight."* Until now that was true only at the moment of entry — one choice, then commitment. It is now true **inside** the encounter, which is where it actually matters dramatically: the fight that goes badly and becomes a negotiation is a scene, and the system could not previously produce one.

It also gives the Turn real weight. Its existing options — Supply, Rescue, Press, Withdraw — are adjustments. This one is a reversal, and it makes the Turn a genuine decision point rather than a menu of small effects.

## V5-17 — Maiming is ameliorated, never compensated · **SETTLED — canon holds**
*§18.1, §25.5. The proposal is declined; the reasoning is recorded so it need not be re-argued.*

**Adaptive equipment reduces a maiming's penalty and never grants a capability the citizen did not have.** §18.1 stands unchanged: *"can ameliorate — not erase."*

**Declined: a maiming granting a dramaturgically related boost** — losing an eye and gaining sharper hearing. Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is the supercrip trope**, in which disability is a trade that comes out even. §18.1 rejects it by name — *"The game must never imply that disability automatically grants genius or supernatural compensation"* — and Appendix B records the v0.1 compensating-trait line as superseded. That is a considered position, not an oversight.
2. **It inverts Pillar 2.5.** "Consequence without disposability" holds because a maimed citizen remains valuable *as themselves*, through experience and relationships and the role they grow into. If the injury hands back a stat, the citizen becomes valuable **because** of the loss, and the loss stops being one.
3. **It collapses the exposure symmetry.** §25.5 makes Distinction and Maiming the two tails of one distribution, driven by the same thing. A Maiming that carries a boost is not a bad tail, and §18.3's shared driver stops meaning anything.

**What the proposal was reaching for already exists, with the causality the right way round.** §18.1: some veterans *"become calm expedition anchors who reduce tharn risk in less-experienced companions."* That is a real mechanical benefit attached to a maimed citizen — **earned by having survived, not granted by the wound.** A citizen who was maimed necessarily lived through something, and the anchor effect comes from the living-through.

*If veterans should be more rewarding, the lever is to expand what experience buys — not to make injury pay.*

# GROUP C — ANTHROPOMORPHISM AND ART

*Reverses Decisions 86 and 93, set two days earlier. The reasoning for the reversal is recorded in full; the earlier reasoning is left in the log rather than deleted.*

## V5-18 — The line is material, not quantity · **SETTLED**
*§33.1. The governing reframe; the rest of Group C follows from it.*

**Banned: Metal Medievalism.** Swords, shields, chainmail, plate, forged helms, anything that reads as made by a smith. **Permitted: everything improvised.** Leaf helmets, cloth-scrap cloaks, twine belts, bark and shell, pouches, staffs.

**Why this is the better test.** Decision 86 asked *how much* a citizen wears — a slider, endlessly arguable, and impossible to state as a rule. This asks *what it is made of*, which is a category with a clean edge.

**And MEDIAN already had this rule everywhere except dress.** §13: scrap is assembled and lashed, never smelted. Appendix B rejects the copper-smelting forge on record. §19 names the equipment slot **Tool** and never Weapon for the same reason. The material test governed buildings, crafting and equipment from the beginning — dress was the one place it was never applied, and applying it resolves the question that was left open in §33.1 for four versions.

**Staffs are permitted, governed by grip.** A staff carried, leaned on, or used to probe is a tool. A staff held as a quarterstaff is not. The existing rule stands: *held at the balance, carried, or slung — never one-ended and point-outward.*

## V5-19 — Citizens wear their gear always · **REVERSES DECISION 86**
*§33.1.*

**Dress no longer marks leaving home.** Citizens carry their cloak, belt, pouch and kit at home as much as away.

**The reason the earlier rule failed.** Decision 86 made dress the visible marker of the Sanctuary–Exposure rhythm, which was elegant. But it left citizens bare in the Colony register — where the player spends most of their time, and where §2.2 requires them to be *"named, visually distinguishable, and historically tracked."* A colony of identical bare rabbits is exactly what that pillar forbids. **Outfit is a substantial part of how a player holds a citizen in mind**, and stripping it at home stripped it where it mattered most.

**The rhythm does not need the body to carry it.** §1 already states that Sanctuary, Exposure and Return are *"enacted by the game's presentation"* — the Crossing is compressed and loud, the Colony warm and slow. The registers were always doing this work; dress was a redundant second signal, bought at the cost of the thing the pillar actually asks for.

**Retained from Decision 86:** the Keepsake as a permanent worn identifier, and adaptive equipment worn always. Those were right.

## V5-20 — Four-legged in the Crossing; the image governs elsewhere · **AMENDS DECISION 86**
*§33.1, §3.4.*

**The Crossing is explicitly four-legged, without exception.** An animal low to the asphalt is far more vulnerable than a small person running, and §3.4 wants that register compressed and overwhelming. This is the part of Decision 86 worth keeping absolutely.

**Everywhere else, posture serves the picture.** Bipedal where it makes a better image, four-legged where that does. The earlier rule — upright to work, four-legged to travel — was defensible naturalism that cost compositions for no gameplay reason.

## V5-21 — Role reads from kit, not livery · **SETTLED**
*§33.1, §12, Pillar 2.3. Resolves a tension flagged and left open in the notes.*

**No uniform, badge, cord or livery marks a Role.** But a citizen's **carried kit plausibly reflects their work** — a Crafter's pouch of oddments, a Healer's bundled moss, a Builder's lashings, a Watchkeeper carrying nothing at all because their work is to watch.

**This resolves the tension the notes identified.** "Role is never worn" did cut against *Ledger and Legible*, which asks that important state be visible in the world first. Activity and place carry most of it — §12 makes assignments produce visible work, §15.3 gives every building a functional icon — but a citizen standing still, listed in the Roster, or out on expedition showed nothing.

**Kit closes that gap without livery**, and it only became available once V5-19 permitted gear at home. Role becomes legible through **material culture** rather than through uniform, which is the same move the whole setting is built on.

## V5-22 — Guests are anthropomorphized identically · **REVERSES DECISION 93**
*§20.4.*

Guests wear what core citizens wear, to exactly the same degree. The distinction is removed entirely.

**Decision 93 rested on a premise V5-19 dissolved.** Its argument was that Guests need no cloth to be *distinguishable*, being already a different species. True — but under V5-19 cloth is not about distinguishability, it is about **identity**. A named crow with a particular satchel is a character; a generic crow is set dressing. Guests are full, equal-status named citizens (§20), and depicting them with less was the one visible respect in which the document treated them as lesser.

## V5-23 — Part IV opens on the living, not on tharn · **SETTLED**
*Book contents.*

Part IV is *"who lives there, what happens to them, how they are remembered."* Opening it on **Going Tharn** — the worst thing that can befall a citizen — is off-register for a part about their lives.

**New opener: a group portrait.** Several named citizens together, in the manner of a sourcebook introducing its cast. That states what the part is about before a word of it is read.

**Tharn moves inside, to §18.2**, where it belongs and where it still hits hard.

## V5-24 — The biomes become a section, not a plate · **SETTLED**
*§8.1.*

The eight biomes are given **three or four per page, each with a large rendering beside its text, half-page each, alternating sides** — rather than compressed into a single codex plate.

This is how field guides actually work, it gives each biome room to be a *place* rather than an entry, and it turns the most atmosphere-rich material in the document into two or three generous pages instead of one crowded one.

**Consequence for the book plan:** "Biome Codex" ceases to be one plate and becomes a multi-page section, with the eight biome renderings promoted from supporting artwork to the section's substance.

## V5-25 — Night is a trade, and highway nights are lit · **SETTLED**
*§9.1, §9.2.*

**Night must not be a penalty box.** These are animals; night is prime operating time, and a design that quietly discourages it fights its own fiction.

**Night is a genuine trade, not a tax.** Against it: extreme vehicle speed, reduced visibility, owls, higher tharn risk. For it: **far fewer vehicles, no hawks, a quieter Margin, and less human activity.** The Fifth Law already frames this exactly — *"Hawks own the day, owls own the night"* — as an exchange of predator rather than an addition of one.

**And the art must not render night as darkness.** A highway at night is one of the most *lit* environments there is — sodium vapour, headlight wash, brake-light red, reflective signage, and all of it doubled in wet asphalt. **A bright night is the truthful night**, and it is also the one that makes night play look inviting rather than punitive.

## V5-26 — Audio becomes the sound of the world · **SETTLED**
*§33.3.*

**§33.3 is not deleted; it is re-registered.** Its production direction goes — *"audio is systemic,"* directional-audio implementation, accessibility specification. Its **sensory content stays**, rewritten as description of what the place is like to live in.

Traffic density and approach learnable by ear. Low-frequency vibration from something large before it is visible. Tharn as narrowed sound, breath and pulse. Road Work's persistence window as a changed soundscape. The River's Spume as a continuous ambient bed shifting with season and traffic.

All of that is **worldbuilding**, and a sourcebook that describes a place should describe how it sounds. What it should not do is specify an implementation for a thing no one is implementing.

**This is a general principle worth applying beyond §33.3: production specification becomes sensory description.** Other sections may need the same treatment.

# GROUP D — THE WORLD

## V5-27 — The Edge Effect is retired as a named concept · **SETTLED**
*§2, §7.4, §8.1, §9.3. Three notes; one error underneath them.*

**The claim was half true, and stating it as one gradient is what broke it.**

§9.3 asserts that *"the strip nearest the highway is simultaneously the most resource-rich ground in the world and the most hazardous, and both qualities fall off together."* Real road verges are salt-damaged, compacted, mown and polluted at the pavement; plant richness peaks **away** from it. As an account of sustenance, the gradient runs backwards.

But the Margin's richness in canon was never botanical. §7.4 lists *"windblown seeds, insects, roadkill traces, litter, packaging, tire fragments, and other human detritus"* — **all of it edge-concentrated**, because litter is thrown from cars, roadkill is on the road, and tire fragments come from tires.

**What survives, precisely:**

- **Hazard scales with proximity to the road**, one-directionally. Wind Draft and the River's Spume are genuinely worse near the pavement, so Traffic-as-Weather's proximity scaling (§9.3) is untouched.
- **Richness does not scale. It changes character.** The Margin is rich at both edges, in different goods.

**The name goes.** "Edge Effect" was doing philosophical work — §2 already demotes it to *"a loosely-binding working principle"* — while asserting a unified gradient that does not exist. Once the claim is corrected, the term is carrying a false generalisation and the truth is stateable without it.

## V5-28 — The Margin has two edges and two economies · **SETTLED**
*§7.4, §9.3. Replaces what the Edge Effect was doing.*

| | **Road edge** | **Wall edge** |
|---|---|---|
| **Goods** | Rigid and Flexible Scrap, roadkill, litter, packaging, tire fragments | Sustenance — sheltered growth, seed drift banked against the wall, undisturbed soil, insects |
| **Hazard** | Lethal. Wind Draft and the Spume at full strength | Low. Quiet, still, out of the wind |
| **Why** | Everything the road sheds lands here | Nothing disturbs it — no mowing, no salt, no traffic |

**No litter reaches the wall.** Debris is thrown or blown from the carriageway and settles near it; the far edge stays clean, which is precisely why it grows.

**This is a better decision than the gradient was.** The old question — *how close to the road dare I go* — had one answer on a slider. The new one is *which edge am I working today, and for what*, and the answer changes with what the colony currently needs. A colony short of scrap and a colony short of food want opposite ends of the same strip.

**Each Reach has a Margin on both sides**, stated early in §7.4. The two share ground type but differ in personality, hazard level and resource pool — so a Reach offers four distinct working grounds, not one.

## V5-29 — Reach lifecycle: Unknown · Walked · Held · **SETTLED**
*§7.5, §28.1.*

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Unknown** | A blank on the corridor map. Never scouted |
| **Walked** | Scouted and visitable through Field Mode. Nothing persists; it reverts to wild between visits |
| **Held** | Outposted. A genuine extension of the colony, fog cleared permanently |

Three plain words in the homely register §22.2 requires, each naming a *relationship to the place* rather than a status. **Walked** is what a Median Scout literally does.

## V5-30 — Geography phrasing corrections · **SETTLED**
*§7.3.*

**The Highway borders every Median Reach, not only the Home Median.** The section's phrasing implies otherwise throughout and needs correcting — every Reach in the corridor sits between carriageways, which is what makes it a Reach.

**"Longitudinal" is correct and stays.** The note asks whether it inverts standard geographic usage — longitude running north–south. It does, but geography is the wrong field: **highway engineering uses longitudinal for along-the-axis and transverse for across it**, and that is the vocabulary this entire world is built from. The usage is not merely defensible, it is the right one for the subject.

## V5-31 — Crossovers bound the Reaches, and their danger is exposure · **SETTLED**
*§7.3. Answers the note's "discuss."*

**Service crossovers and U-turn areas mark the boundary between one Reach and the next** — a diegetic bound, so a party knows it has left one Reach because it crossed one.

**The note's objection is correct: they carry almost no traffic.** Maintenance and police, rarely. As a vehicle gauntlet they would be feeble.

**So their hazard is not vehicles — it is exposure.** Open pavement, no cover, long sightlines, nowhere to freeze that is not visible. The crossover is the one place in the corridor where a party is *seen* rather than *struck*, which makes it structurally different from a transverse Crossing rather than a weaker copy of one.

This also fits §7.3's existing claim that longitudinal travel uses *"abstracted travel beats and corridor hazards rather than a single lane-by-lane gauntlet."* The crossover is the punctuation between beats.

*And it is the natural home for an aerial threat if that system is ever built — open ground with no cover is exactly where a hawk matters.*

## V5-32 — An Anomaly reaches the Margin · **SETTLED**
*§9.2.*

The Anomaly traffic cycle stops being only a rewritten pattern and becomes an **opportunity**. A crash means stopped traffic — a crossing window that does not otherwise exist — and **spilled cargo in the Margin**, a windfall of goods no ordinary node produces.

This gives the rarest cycle a reason to be anticipated rather than merely survived, and it connects directly to §36.3's suggestion that world-event set-pieces reskin existing registers rather than adding systems: a pileup is an Anomaly with a Field consequence.

## V5-33 — Four seasons, four Spumes · **SETTLED**
*§9.3, §10.1.*

The River's Spume currently has Heat (summer), Cold (winter), Exhaust (Rush cycle) and Oil Leak (Anomaly) — leaving spring and autumn without a texture.

- **Spring — Chemical.** Salt runoff from winter, herbicide on the verges, fertiliser drift from adjacent land. Sharp, bitter, and it taints what grows.
- **Autumn — Dust.** Dry debris, chaff, leaf litter and grit lifted by passing traffic. **Reduces visibility**, which no other Spume does.

*Note a small reversal: §9.3 currently records Chemical Runoff as considered and "not adopted as a separate named hazard." It returns not as a hazard of its own but as a Spume texture, which was always its right home.*

## V5-34 — Road Work is local; its disruption is corridor-wide · **SETTLED**
*§10.2.*

**The physical work strikes one Reach.** Municipal crews work a stretch; that is what they are.

**But the card-pool modifier is corridor-wide.** §10.2 already makes Road Work tilt the Choice-Event deck toward hazard and raise event frequency; that effect now reaches every Reach, so **nowhere is insulated.** A colony cannot wait out a Road Work window by working elsewhere.

*And a resurfacing programme may strike several Reaches in sequence — the rarer, heavier case.*

## V5-35 — Choice-Event Cards come before Seasons and Road Work · **SETTLED**
*§10.3 → its own section.*

Cards are decoupled from §10 and given their own section, **positioned before Seasons and Road Work** rather than appended after them.

They are a whole-game mechanic, running in both Colony and Field, and both Seasons and Road Work reference them as an existing mechanism — Road Work modifies the card pool, and seasonal events arrive as cards. §5.1's domestic loop already names *"a pending Choice-Event Card"* several sections before the reader learns what one is.

## V5-36 — Home ground carries contest, falling with Tier · **SETTLED**
*§24.4. Closes an Appendix D open item.*

Contest is possible on the Home Reach's Field layer, and **its likelihood falls with each Colony Tier** — early foraging at home carries real risk; a mature colony's ground is genuinely safe.

This makes Tier advancement *felt at home* rather than only in range and capability, which is the one place §31's list of Tier benefits does not currently reach.

**One exception, flagged forward:** an aerial threat would **not** diminish with Tier. Walls, lookouts and investment shape every ground-level danger; nothing a colony builds affects what comes from above. That asymmetry is the strongest argument yet for the bird system in V4-12.

## V5-37 — Outposts gain a Staging Post, and with it Margin Raids · **SETTLED**
*§27.1, §28.2.*

This gives outposts an active purpose beyond passive trickle and fog-clearing, and it completes a thought §27.1 half-states — that a Margin beside a wild Reach can be raided but *"lacks Home efficiencies."* Now it can have them, if the colony builds for it.

## V5-38 — Three Outpost Roles · **SETTLED**
*§28.3.*

Home Colony Roles do not fit an outpost — there is no Nursery, no Story Circle, no Infirmary. A small dedicated set replaces them, one per thing an outpost actually does:

- **Keeper** — maintains the central building; improves the passive trickle.
- **Watcher** — works the visibility and predator-reduction outbuildings; lowers contest.
- **Forager** — works the local ground.

§28.3 currently implies every stationed citizen is a Forager by default. Three Roles make stationing a decision rather than a placement.

## V5-39 — Special Journeys formalised, and marked as extensible · **SETTLED**
*§27.1.*

Two examples are given proper treatment, both **gated to Tier II or above**:

- **The Rest-Stop Metropolis** — the primary destination and the campaign's victory arc.
- **The Ancestral Warren, Den or Burrow** — the lost home, revisitable as a ruin. §7.6 should state this where the place is introduced, not only in §27.1.

**And the category is explicitly an extensibility hook.** The book says outright that further story-forward destinations belong here, which makes Special Journey the named place where the world can grow without disturbing anything else.

## V5-40 — Selective expansion is intended · **SETTLED**
*§8.2.*

The note observes that Anchor Points imply a strategy of **skipping Reaches to hold out for one that suits the species**, and asks what that implies.

**It implies the system is working.** §8.2 makes Anchors *"a bonus layer, never a gate"* — so skipping is optional rather than required — and §28.4 already endorses the behaviour, rewarding *"exploration for a good, wide strip worth committing to rather than settling the first tile the party reaches."*

The strategy self-regulates: a colony that reaches far for good ground holds a thin, long chain with distant outposts and a longer journey home. Reach is paid for in exposure.

Worth stating positively in §8.2 so it reads as a choice the design offers rather than a loophole a player found.

## V5-41 — The sky is answered by a bird, not a building · **SETTLED**
*§20.2, §24.4, and the standing bird question in V4-12.*

**The in-system counter to aerial threat is an Ambient Guest.** Nothing the colony builds affects what comes from above — but something it *befriends* does.

**This resolves the asymmetry V5-36 exposed.** Ground-level danger falls with Colony Tier because walls, lookouts and outbuildings are investments a colony can make. Aerial danger cannot be built against. So it falls by a different currency entirely: **not construction, but relationship.** The one threat immune to everything the colony makes is answered by the one system that isn't making anything.

**The roster already contains the answer, matched to the Law.** The Fifth Law splits the sky by time — *"Hawks own the day, owls own the night"* — and §20.2 already holds a pair that splits the same way:

- **Owl** — *"increases passive perimeter predator-detection radius, night-weighted."* Already written, already passive, already never an escort. A resident raptor holding territory is real behaviour, and the effect needs no change.
- **Songbird** — currently ambient morale. Songbirds **mob raptors and give alarm calls**, which is among the most conspicuous behaviours in real bird ecology. It is the natural day-watch counterpart, and it would give the roster's least distinct member a genuine job.

**WHOOT was the answer before the question existed.** §20.2's template character — a barred owl with a permanently weak wing, who can no longer hunt on the move and has become a stationary watcher — was written to warm up a rule about passivity. He turns out to be the fully-formed embodiment of the colony's only defence against the sky.

**Why this is the right shape, not just a convenient one:**

- **No new system.** Pillar 2.6 satisfied exactly: an existing slot, an existing effect, an existing character.
- **The Ambient tier gains real stakes.** §20.2 currently calls Ambient Guests *"uniform, downside-free safe investments"* — pleasant and low-consequence. Being the sole counter to an entire threat category makes the tier strategically weighty for the first time.
- **It costs a capped slot.** Four Ambient slots at maximum Tier, so sky-safety competes directly with Songbird morale, Bee yield, Toad preservation. A real choice rather than a free fix.
- **It extends across the corridor for free.** §20.2 already allows one Ambient Guest per outpost central building — so an outpost with an Owl is watched and one without is not, with no additional rule.
- **And it is the found-family thesis at its sharpest.** §20 says the Guest system is where that beat lives. The colony's answer to the thing it cannot fight is to take one of them in.

**Consequence for V4-12.** The bird design pass no longer needs to invent a defence — it needs only to specify the threat. That halves the work and removes the risk of the mitigation being bolted on afterward.

**Special Artifacts may be carried by a citizen, occupying the Tool slot.**

**§13 and §25.7 currently disagree.** §13 places Artifacts *"at the colony level, distinct from the three citizen equipment slots."* But §25.7 says an Artifact *"may grant an extra Turn"* **in an encounter** — which requires somebody out there to be holding it. # GROUP F — STRUCTURE AND VOICE

## V5-59 — The hyphen-compound: a language without a conlang · **SETTLED**
*Whole document. The largest voice decision in the pass.*

**In-world compound nouns take a capitalized first element and a hyphen:** *After-name*, *Far-hole*, *Gap-waiting*, *Sky-watch*, *Hearth-kin*, *Road-edge*, *Wall-edge*.

**What the convention means.** It marks a concept that is **one word in the animals' own tongue** and needs two in English. The hyphen preserves the unity the translation breaks — Tolkien's *Elf-friend*, *Ring-bearer*, *Barrow-wight*. It is a translator's mark, not decoration.

**Why it is worth more to MEDIAN than to most settings.** §1.1 forbids borrowing *Watership Down*'s invented words, and §6 requires every scrap of folklore to be original — but nothing in the document has established that these animals have a **language**, only that they have sayings. This asserts one, at zero cost: no invented vocabulary, no glossary of unpronounceable syllables, nothing to memorise. And it lands squarely in the register §22.2 already demands — *"homely and plain, the 'The Hill / Bywater' end of Tolkien rather than the elvish end."* Hyphen-compounds are Anglo-Saxon in feel, which is exactly right.

**Canon already contains the proof.** §22.1: *"The designer-facing term for this field is the agnomen; **After-name** is what the game says, because MEDIAN's folk register is deliberately homely and plain."* The convention is already operating, already justified, and already paired with its plain designer counterpart.

**The scope rule: hyphenate what the animals say; leave plain what the book says.**

| In-world — hyphenate | Designer or reader term — plain |
|---|---|
| After-name · Far-hole · Road-edge | agnomen · Median Reach · Body Unit |
| Sky-watch · Hearth-kin · Gap-waiting | Warren Flow · Cache Network · Choice-Event Card |

**Two constraints, or it becomes twee:**

- **Compound nouns only.** *Roaring Iron* and *the Rivers of Thunder* are adjective-and-noun phrases and stay as they are; forcing them into compounds would damage both.
- **Capital-lowercase**, following the pattern: *Elf-friend*, not *Elf-Friend*. **After-name** is already correct.

**It is a generative device, not merely orthographic.** The convention *produces* vocabulary. The rabbit's distant escape exit — Warren Flow's readable signature, per Decision 90 — becomes the **Far-hole**. The Margin's two economies become the **Road-edge** and the **Wall-edge**. The Owl's function becomes the **Sky-watch**. Each is a word the animals would plainly have, arrived at without invention.

**And it opens a door on the tharn question (V4-18).** A MEDIAN word for the freeze can now be built rather than borrowed — *Stone-still*, *Fear-lock*, *Going-stone* — in a register the document has already established. That question stays deferred, but it is no longer stuck for want of a form.

*An appendix glossary of in-world compounds will be wanted eventually.*

## V5-60 — Three story colonies, one per species · **SETTLED**
*Confirms V4-7. Load-bearing across the worked examples, Part IX, the Founding Escape, and the named Guests.*

Three named colonies, one of each core species, with named citizens, recurring throughout the book. Every worked example, Record excerpt, caption and story beat draws on one of them rather than inventing a fresh anonymous instance.

**They demonstrate the naming system by existing.** §22.3 gives the player three registers to choose between at founding; the three story colonies take one each, so the reader sees all three working before the mechanic is explained:

| Colony | Naming register | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Rabbit** | **Tribute** — named for the Ancestral Warren | It carries the lush Founding Escape, and that story is about what was lost |
| **Squirrel** | **Aspirational** — hope, height, horizon | Canopy-dwellers who build upward and reach |
| **Wood Mouse** | **Grounded** — biome-realistic, plain | The species of density and practicality, named for its ground |

*Names themselves are the designer's to author; the register assignment is the structural claim.*

**They also supply the named Guests** the §20 note asks for — further characters in WHOOT's spirit, each woven into one colony's history rather than floating free.

## V5-61 — The book opens on the Founding Escape · **SETTLED**
*§35.1, and confirms V4-8.*

**The book opens with narrative, before any system material** — the Founding Escape, told in full, from inside the **rabbit** colony.

**One piece of writing does two jobs.** It is both the book's opening and §35.1's narrative treatment, and it establishes register and stakes before the reader is asked to hold a single rule.

**Why the rabbit gets the lush version.** It is the default first species, it carries the largest artwork gap in the project, and Part III already opens on the rabbit warren. Concentrating the fullest narrative treatment on the least-illustrated species closes both holes with one effort.

**The squirrel and mouse Foundings are short summaries**, referenced later rather than told at length. Three full versions would be repetition; three *acknowledged* versions establish that every campaign begins this way.

## V5-63 — The generative layer becomes a thread, not a section · **SETTLED**
*§34, dissolved into three placements. Applies the principle established in V5-26.*

**§34 stops being a section and becomes a property of the book**, in three parts.

### 1. An early sidebar

Placed in the front matter, before Part I. One short panel establishing what the optional visual layer is, and carrying §34's guarantee at full strength:

> **The base game must be complete, coherent and emotionally effective without any of it. Nothing in this layer is ever load-bearing.**

It appears early so that a reader meeting the first distributed sidebar already knows what it is and that skipping it costs nothing.

### 2. Distributed sidebars at the moments themselves

Small marked panels through the body, each naming a concrete instance rather than describing a category:

> **CREATED VISUAL — "Sharpnose goes Tharn"**

**Roughly nine, at the points §34 already identifies**, now placed where those moments actually occur rather than clustered in one section: a permanent change to a citizen's portrait (§18.1) · a Distinction earned, which is also the After-name moment (§18.3, §22.1) · tharn (§18.2) · Guest recruitment (§26.2) · the Rating and debrief (§21.3) · a Tale's illustration (§21.2) · Tier advancement (§31) · outpost completion (§28) · catastrophic Road Work (§10.2).

**The examples use citizens of the three story colonies** (V5-60), which makes the generative thread another strand tying those colonies through the book rather than a parallel apparatus. *Sharpnose* is already a name canon uses in §21.1's worked event-feed example.

**The sidebars are visually a distinct class** — one treatment, recognisable at a glance, so the whole optional layer can be seen and skipped as a unit. This is precisely what §34 anticipated when it noted the IMAGEGEN blocks would become sidebars.

*Small flag: "CREATED VISUAL" is slightly ambiguous, since every visual in the book is created. Worth a moment's thought at typesetting — "GENERATED" states it plainly, though it dates faster.*

### 3. A procedural appendix

**New — Appendix G.** The method described **procedurally rather than programmatically.**

The core of it: **a continually updated canonical image, carried forward by image-to-image.** A citizen has one canonical portrait; a permanent change is applied *to that image* rather than generated afresh; the result becomes the new canonical reference and the next change is applied to it. Identity persists because the image is never re-invented, only amended. §21.2's *"one record, two outputs"* is the same claim from the text side.

**What belongs here:** the reference-and-amend cycle. Why continuity of species anatomy, orientation, existing scars and physical quirks matters more than fidelity to any single prompt. How a structured description and a reference image work together. What degrades across repeated passes, and why text degrades faster than art.

**What does not:** API calls, model names, vendors, cost, latency, authentication, service continuity. Those are implementation, they age badly, and they were the least interesting thing in §34.

**This appendix is documentation rather than speculation.** The method is in active use on this project — the plate pipeline, the reference-plus-annotation approach, and the findings already logged in `running_decisions.md` about specified strings, upscale drift and text corruption are all the same practice. It should be written from what actually happens, not from what might.

**Structural consequence:** §34 as a numbered section disappears, which renumbers §35 in Part VII. The guarantee that the layer is never load-bearing must survive that dissolution intact — it moves to the early sidebar, and it is the one thing that must not be shortened.

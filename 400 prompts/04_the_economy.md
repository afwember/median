# The Economy — the sceneless plate

**Tests:** whether the identity carries a page with no landscape, no animals and no scene — specimens on paper. Roughly a third of the book's plates are this kind.
**Prepend the house-style block, and attach the current reference plate as the first image.**

---

> **Core subject:** a widescreen sourcebook plate presenting the colony's economy as **specimens laid out and labelled on the page** — a collector's tray, not a landscape. There is no scene, no horizon and no animal anywhere on this plate. Every object is painted individually against the cream paper, in watercolour and ink, lit softly from one side and casting a faint shadow onto the page as though set down on it.
>
> A thin header bar across the top reads "THE ECONOMY" in a clean serif, with "MEDIAN · PART III · SECTION 13" at the right-hand end.
>
> **FOUR BORDERED PANELS** across the page, separated by hairline rules, each with a small blank rounded badge in deep sign-green at its upper left, a heading, and its specimens arranged in a loose grid with fine leader lines running to small labels.
>
> **Panel 1 — "SUSTENANCE · PERISHABLE"** · *"Decays. Abundant in spring and summer."*
> Blackberries, tender green shoots, a beetle, a small brown mushroom, a windfall apple fragment, a torn corner of a food wrapper with crumbs.
>
> **Panel 2 — "SUSTENANCE · DURABLE"** · *"Stockpiles. The backbone of autumn and winter."*
> Acorns, a hazelnut, dried seed heads, a bundle of dried grass tied with fibre, dried mushroom slices, a dried crust.
>
> **Panel 3 — "FLEXIBLE SCRAP"** · *"Binds, weaves, lashes, seals."*
> A coil of stripped copper wire, a length of packaging twine, a strip of burlap, a fragment of blue plastic bag, a cut zip-tie, a perished rubber band, a shoelace end, a tangle of fishing line.
>
> **Panel 4 — "RIGID SCRAP"** · *"Braces, shields, surfaces, reinforces."*
> Aluminium pull-tabs, two bottle caps, a sea-smoothed glass pebble, a bread clip, a popsicle stick, a fragment of tyre tread, a small hex nut, a shard of glazed ceramic, gravel chips.
>
> **THE ARTIFACT PANEL.** Below the four, set slightly apart and bordered more heavily than the rest, a narrower panel headed "SPECIAL ARTIFACTS" with the line *"Rare, singular, and never generic. Each one solves a specific problem."* It holds three objects painted with noticeably more care than the specimens above: **the pull-tab-and-needle multitool**, lashed together with fine thread and clearly the flagship object of the colony's material culture; a small brass key; and a intact glass marble.
>
> **THE RULE BAND.** Running the full width along the bottom, a single bordered panel divided into three compartments by thin vertical rules:
>
> - `ONE CARRY SLOT EQUALS ONE UNIT OF VALUE — large items scale in proportion, and some require two animals`
> - `WINDFALLS ARE THE EXCEPTION — rare finds worth more than their bulk`
> - `THERE IS NO CURRENCY — nothing here is money, and nothing converts to it`
>
> A narrow footer bar across the bottom reads "OBSERVE. PLAN. ADAPT. TEACH THE YOUNG. THE MEDIAN REMEMBERS."
>
> Every object must be believable as something a small animal could actually find beside a highway, painted at a scale where its real texture shows — the burr on a cut zip-tie, the crimp of a bottle cap, the fibre of twine.
>
> Render every text string exactly as given, in US spelling, and invent no additional labels.

## What to check

- **Does a page with no scene still feel like this book**, or does it go flat and catalogue-ish?
- **Do the objects sit on the paper** — soft shadow, correct scale relationship — or float?
- **Does the specimen-tray device hold** for the other sceneless plates: the Eight Roles, the Construction Queue, Exposure and Outcomes?
- The Set 1 legacy deck has the best existing scrap-object art in the project. Worth comparing the result against it before rebuilding elements.

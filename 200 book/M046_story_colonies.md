# The Three Story Colonies — second sketch

**A design document, not reader-facing prose.** Nothing here is settled. Notes from the first read are applied; items marked **PROVISIONAL** are my recommended answer to something you flagged for discussion, applied so it can be judged in place rather than in the abstract.

## Two terminology notes

**Character is designer-facing. Citizen is what the game says.** Sharpnose is a *Citizen* to a player who has embodied New Elderbank. To us she is a *Character*: authored content that recurs, carries examples, and has a fate we decide. Same split canon already runs at §23.1 — *the designer-facing term is the agnomen; After-name is what the game says.*

**A colony's name is the Home Median's After-name.** Not "two names for two things" — one place, with a Given-equivalent and an earned second name. The corridor generates the Reach's folk name; the founding earns the After-name; both persist and both are used, exactly as with a citizen. This is why the player authors exactly one name in the whole game: founding is the one event significant enough to confer an After-name that the player caused.

> **Ashcross**, which the corridor has always called it, and **New Elderbank**, which it earned by being lived in.

## The three

| Species | Reach (given) | Colony (After-name) | Biome | Register | Lost home |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rabbit** | Ashcross | **New Elderbank** | Wooded Median | **Tribute** | the Ancestral Warren, **Elderbank** |
| **Squirrel** | Elmwater | **Morningside** | Creek Split | **Aspirational** | the Ancestral Den, **Nine Beeches** |
| **Wood Mouse** | Pipe End | **Rushbottom** | Culvert Garden | **Grounded** | the Ancestral Burrow, **Chaff End** |

Each colony is cast at **seven body-units of core species, two Active Guests and two Ambient Guests** — a mid-Tier-III shape. Seven rabbits, seven squirrels, fourteen mice.

**All three foundings share one structure.** §35.1 is universal: a mature ancestral home, the Laws being drilled in the nursery, the founding party elsewhere at a peripheral store because of a free-afternoon choice the player makes, then the machines, then an escape played through all four registers. **What differs is how much warning each colony had, and therefore what it remembers.** Elderbank had none. Nine Beeches had a night. Chaff End had most of a season, and thought it was winning.

**All three flee with three**, so §35.1 stands unamended. For rabbits and squirrels that is three body-units and a Standard Party on day one. For mice it is **one and a half** — and Rushbottom goes out anyway, under strength, the moment Husk makes four. Two body-units is legal for everyone; it is simply worse, and it is a party that has given up the rescue before it leaves.

# I · NEW ELDERBANK

*Rabbit. Tribute. Seven citizens, four Guests.*

Six of the seven are already in canon. **Bramble, Twig, Sharpnose, Nutmeg, Daisy** and **Fennel** appear in §18.1's bond examples and §22.1's event feed, with three bonds already written:

> *Bramble and Twig — best friends* · *Sharpnose and Nutmeg — partners in crime* · *Daisy and Fennel — inseparable since the culvert*

## The founding trio

Adolescents on the last morning at Elderbank — not the children being drilled in the nursery, which is why they were out at the peripheral store when the machines came.

**Bramble.** The one who said what to do next, and kept saying it for four days. Not the bravest; the one who would not stop moving. Became the colony's first Teacher because somebody had to.
*Keepsake:* a knotted bootlace, used at Elderbank to measure tunnel runs.

**Twig.** The smallest, and the one who remembers the old warren best — can describe rooms the others cannot picture, including some that may never have existed. Of Bramble's Hearth. Daisy was born of it.
*Keepsake:* a scrap of blue tarp, carried out in the teeth.

**Shadepatch, called Sharpnose.** **PROVISIONAL** — *Sharpnose is an After-name, and this is how the book teaches the naming system.*

Given the name Shadepatch at Elderbank. Was mockingly reciting the Second Law — *the mowing is fire* — to needle the others about having to memorise it, and then it was true. Has not recited anything since — Law or Saying, at the Circle or anywhere else.

Her After-name was earned in the colony's first real encounter — a four-round contest against a feral cat holding a fallen log, the rarest length there is: she went at the log itself, burst the rotten face of it open with her nose, and the cat left. The Distinction was granted for that, and the After-name with it. She has been Sharpnose ever since, and nobody uses Shadepatch.

**She survives the campaign.** Sharpnose is a star character and does not die in this book.
*Keepsake:* none. Refused to carry anything out.

*Why this works:* the six canon names are flora and plain nouns, and *Sharpnose* is the one that does not fit the palette. Making it an After-name explains the anomaly, demonstrates the mechanic on a citizen the reader will meet repeatedly, and gives canon's existing example set a worked instance of its own naming system. **Note:** this is now the four-round case, which canon opens deliberately for encounters that stay close three times running. Your original instinct was right and the cap was the thing that had to move.

## The four who came after

**Nutmeg.** Arrived years into the campaign, and was one of the children being drilled in the nursery on the last morning — which the trio had believed impossible. Carries Laws the colony has never heard, with total conviction and no way to prove a word of it. Some are accepted; some are not. Bonded to Sharpnose: the one who mocked the lesson and the one who was actually listening.

**Daisy.** The first citizen born at New Elderbank, of Bramble and Twig's Hearth. Has never seen Elderbank and does not miss it, which is its own kind of loss.

**Fennel.** Found in a flooded culvert on a Margin run, alone, pinned under a shifted slab for most of a day before the party reached him. He lost the use of a hind leg and kept the shape of it — canon already marks a created visual, *Fennel, after the culvert* (§19.1), and this is the change it depicts. He has never given an account of how he came to be there that two citizens remember the same way. Daisy was on the party that brought him in.

**Teasel.** Lost a forefoot to a Road Work onset in her third year and expeditions still, on routes chosen for her. The colony's calm anchor: parties with Teasel in them go tharn less, and canon says why — it is earned by having survived, not granted by the wound (§19.1). She is the book's worked demonstration of Pillar 2.5.

## The four tiers

**Tier I — Scavenger Camp.** Three adolescents in a scrape under the ash. Bramble measures everything with the bootlace. The colony is named at the first civic build — Twig proposes it, and nobody argues. The first crossing to the Margin takes nine days to work up to. Sharpnose earns her name at the log.

**Tier II — Fortified Settlement.** The warren has four ways out and Bramble is digging a fifth. Daisy is born; the Nursery is the first structure built for someone who does not exist yet. WHOOT arrives and stops being a threat halfway through the second night. Teasel loses the foot.

**Tier III — Independent Colony.** Two Reaches held. Nutmeg arrives, and the Story Circle stops being a place where the Laws are recited and becomes a place where they are added to. Fennel is brought in from the culvert. The colony can feed itself through a winter without a single Margin raid, and raids anyway.

**Tier IV — Sovereign Network.** A chain of outposts and a warren with more exits than anyone can hold in their head at once — including the **Far-hole**, which surfaces in a hedge at the far end of Ashcross almost at the service crossover, and which four citizens have ever used. Bramble is old and still Teacher.

## From the Chronicle

> **The First Day.** *They came to the ash at evening, and they were three, and they had been three for four days. Bramble said: the air smells good here. And they stopped.*

**First Saying:** *The air smells good here.* Said at New Elderbank whenever anything is settled — a building finished, an argument ended, a citizen come home.

**Places that earned a name.**

- **Sharpnose's Retreat** — the fallen log, and the cover the party fell back to and held during the encounter that named her. **PROVISIONAL** rename from canon's *Sharpnose's Rest*, which reads as a grave. One night confers a citizen's After-name and a place's, which is §23.2's *one naming grammar, two scales* demonstrated in a single event.
- **The Fifth Hole** — Bramble's fifth bolt-hole, dug in a season when nobody could see the need for it, and the reason the colony lived through one particular Road Work.
- **Nutmeg's Crossing** — the stretch of Highway she came over, alone, in the dark.

# II · MORNINGSIDE

*Squirrel. Aspirational. Seven citizens, four Guests.*

A den in nine beeches, taken over two mornings for an interchange widening. The work began, stopped at dark, and came back — so Nine Beeches had one night in which to believe it had time. Three young squirrels were out at a peripheral cache on the second morning, having chosen the afternoon before over the nursery lesson.

## The founding trio

**Rowan.** Named the colony. Kept saying *there will be somewhere better than this* through eleven days of moving, and was right, and has never entirely been forgiven for it. Runs the Cache Network by instinct and cannot explain any of it.
*Keepsake:* a beech leaf, pressed flat and gone brown.

**Cobb.** The heaviest hauler and the least willing to leave. Built the first bridge across the near channel and has rebuilt it eleven times. Believes, quietly, that Rowan's reaching is how a colony loses things.
*Keepsake:* a bent brass screw, worked out of the guardrail at Nine Beeches on the last morning.

**Gale.** The fastest, and the only one who went back to look. Came back and would not say what was there. Scouts further than anyone sanctions.
*Keepsake:* a length of fishing line, found on the road on the second day and kept because it was the first thing the new world gave them.

## The four who came after

**Tassel.** Walked in at the second winter from a Reach that was perfectly habitable, for reasons she has always given cheerfully and never the same way twice. The colony's Caretaker and its best judge of when a cache has gone bad. Not every citizen who arrives alone arrives damaged.

**Wick.** Born of Rowan and Cobb's Hearth in the third spring; the first citizen of Morningside who has never had to be told that a tree can be cut down.

**Vane.** Crafter, and the one who reads weather off the creek and the canopy well enough that Morningside plans around her. Made every fastening on Cobb's bridge.

**Larch.** A wanderer from a squirrel colony three Reaches down that failed — flooded out, and not all of them got clear. Brought the news that other colonies exist, and that they end. Morningside has scouted differently since.

## Guests

| | Guest | Tier | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slip** | mink | Active | Opens rescue from water, culverts and drainage. Prey species will not Parley with her. |
| **Churr** | raccoon | Active | Opens sealed human containers. Draws other scavengers, raising contest at nearby nodes. Met three times as an antagonist first. |
| **Reel** | songbird | Ambient | Morale at home, and the day Sky-watch. |
| **Ballast** | turtle | Ambient | Strongbox — a portion of the stores survives any single loss event. |

*Slip's complication is amended:* the first draft said she was *worth almost nothing away from water*, which prices a citizen rather than describing one. **This is a canon fix too** — §21.1's roster gives the Mink "little value away from water," and that is a scope limit dressed as a drawback. Every other Guest's *closes* column names an actual complication. Hers should too.

## The four tiers

**Tier I — Scavenger Camp.** Three squirrels in a drey lashed into the fork of the largest elm, with every acorn they own in one place because there is nowhere else to put it. Rowan names the colony at the first civic build and half the colony — which is one squirrel — thinks the name is too much. The near channel is uncrossable and the far bank is visibly better.

**Tier II — Fortified Settlement.** The first bridge. Caches split four ways on Cobb's insistence, which matters the first time the creek rises. Slip is met at the near channel, and is a problem.

**Tier III — Independent Colony.** Caches across three Reaches, and a real argument about whether that is a network or an overextension. Gale reaches an abandoned weigh station further down the corridor than anyone has been, and comes back describing something nobody believes. Slip joins. Wick is born, and Morningside starts thinking in generations.

**Tier IV — Sovereign Network.** The canopy runs unbroken over two Reaches and the caches are spread wide enough that no single loss can cripple them. Rowan is proved right in a way that is hard to celebrate.

## From the Chronicle

> **On the Naming.** *Rowan stood at the sign and said: we will call it for the light. And Cobb said: call it for the ground. And it is called for the light.*

**Defining Saying:** *There will be somewhere better than this.* Said with warmth and said with an edge, depending who is saying it.

**Places that earned a name.** **Cobb's Bridge**, rebuilt eleven times and then twelve · **The Far Bank**, which is not quite a place name — it is what Morningside calls whatever it has not reached yet, and it moves · **Nine Beeches**, the lost den, because squirrels count what is gone.

# III · RUSHBOTTOM

*Wood mouse. Grounded. Fourteen citizens, four Guests.*

Chaff End was under a verge that was taken for a lane addition and a retention basin. The survey marks went in early and the work came on for most of a season; the mice kept moving deeper until there was no deeper, and they were good at it, and they believed they were winning. Then one morning the cut reached the Burrow, and behind the cut came the basin, and nothing has grown on that ground since. **Three** were out at the far store when it happened, and they came out of the pipe at the end of it.

> **CANON — caught here, not by the markup.** The first draft destroyed Chaff End with *drainage work*, which canon lists as a **Road Work** event (§11.2) — cyclical, survivable, and explicitly *not* the Founding Escape (§35.1: *the Founding Escape is not an instance of the Mowing*). A Founding Escape must be a singular permanent annihilation where nothing grows back. A lane addition with a retention basin is that, and it keeps the water character that sends the survivors to a Culvert Garden.

**The founding is three, and completed to six.** Three flee, which for mice is an awkward half of a body-unit. Husk is already at Pipe End when they arrive. Weft and Warp come in as a separated pair inside the first season. Six animals, three body-units — and only at six can Rushbottom field a party at all.

## The three who came out of the pipe

**Barley.** Counted. Sixty-one were under Chaff End and four came out, and Barley has written all sixty-one. Rushbottom's Records are the most complete on the corridor because of one mouse's refusal to let a number be approximate.
*Keepsake:* a bottle cap, carried out because it was already being carried and there was no moment to put it down.

**Thimble.** Made the first Relay work — stationed herself at the narrowest point of the pipe and passed everything through for three days without being asked. Rushbottom's whole logistics culture is a copy of that week.
*Keepsake:* a thimble. She was named for it long before she carried it, and nobody at Rushbottom finds this remarkable.

**Pip.** The smallest, the loudest, and the reason the other three kept going. Went back into the pipe twice for citizens who turned out not to be there.
*Keepsake:* a length of red twine, retied so often it is now three lengths knotted.

## Husk, who was already there

**Husk.** Was living in the root void at Pipe End when the three came out of the pipe — had been there a season, alone, from somewhere he has never satisfactorily explained, and made room without being asked. **Rushbottom is the only colony of the three that moved into a place that was already occupied**, and both occupants stayed: Husk in the root void and Sump in the culvert mouth. Neither was recruited. Neither left.

## Weft and Warp — the separated pair

**PROVISIONAL** — *the mouse wanderer mechanic, demonstrated at the founding.*

**Weft** arrived alone at the end of the first season and said there had been someone with him. **Warp** was found eleven days later, two Reaches down, by a party that went looking because of what Weft said.

They are not siblings and were not travelling together when Chaff End went; they met on the road. They are inseparable now, and Rushbottom treats a single mouse arriving as an unfinished event.

## Guests

| | Guest | Tier | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sump** | toad | Ambient | Reduces spoilage; steadies the wet ground. Was in the culvert mouth before the mice arrived and made no objection to company. |
| **Flit** | bat | Ambient | Protects structures from decay of every kind, which a Culvert Garden badly needs. |
| **Rasp** | snake | Active | Parley by intimidation and display. Movement penalty in winter. |
| **Dart** | weasel | Active | Contest against small predators; high carry and fight. Prey species will not Parley with him — **and Rushbottom is a colony of prey species.** |

*Dart is deliberately provocative.* A mouse colony recruiting a weasel is the found-family thesis at maximum pressure, and canon supports it exactly: every recruited Guest is a full, equal-status named citizen. It is also the best available test of whether that rule means anything. Flag if it is a step too far.

## Why every citizen is named

**PROVISIONAL** — *adopting your reading, because it gives the species a psychology.*

The other two colonies name their citizens because the game does. Rushbottom names them because **not being counted is the mouse fear.** A species that survives by numbers is a species that can be lost inside its own numbers, and the Long Count exists against that.

This also supplies something canon has deferred: tharn's per-species flavour (Appendix D). Rabbit tharn is the freeze in the open. Mouse tharn is **the certainty of not being found** — and the rescue that resolves it is somebody arriving and using your name.

## The four tiers

**Tier I — Scavenger Camp.** Three mice in a root void that already had a mouse in it, running a relay because no single mouse can shift the loads a new colony runs on — a Standard item takes two (§14). Barley starts the count. With Husk they are four animals and two body-units, and they go out on that: short runs, small hauls, and no third body to send if anyone freezes. When one does freeze, the other carries her out and the run is over — which is how Rushbottom's first Trusted Friend bond gets made, in the worst hour of its first season. The colony is named at the first civic build, for the rushes. Weft arrives; the colony goes looking for Warp on the word of a stranger, and finds him. The first structure built is not a shelter but a store.

**Tier II — Fortified Settlement.** Tuck and Seam are born and Rushbottom goes from six to eight in a night. The micro-tunnels reach the culvert mouth and Sump is discovered to have been there the whole time. First flood. The store holds.

**Tier III — Independent Colony.** The densest colony of the three, and the one that can account for every citizen, every store and every path without consulting anyone's memory. Bran arrives with intelligence nobody asked for and everybody uses. Rushbottom scouts further than a colony its size has business scouting, because it can afford the time.

**Tier IV — Sovereign Network.** A relay chain running the length of four Reaches without a single citizen carrying anything far — which requires either the **Under-run** or a standing complement of **Carriers**, and Rushbottom has both. It is the colony that would arrive anywhere with a complete list of everyone who had ever lived there.

## From the Chronicle

> **The Count.** *There were sixty-one under Chaff End. Three came out of the pipe. Barley has written all sixty-one, and reads them at the Circle each spring, and it takes a long time, and nobody leaves.*

**Defining Saying:** *Small hands, short road.* The Relay stated as a proverb, and the colony's philosophy: no one carries anything far.

**Names the colony keeps** — *deliberately not the same heading as the other two, because Rushbottom keeps names rather than places.* **The Pipe**, the specific culvert they came out of, and the colony's memorial · **Pip's Turn**, the point inside it where Pip went back the second time · **The Long Count**, the annual reading of the sixty-one · and the sixty-one themselves, which are names and nothing else now.

# MEDIAN v0.5 — Baseline Disposition Ledger
## Checkpoint: Material Economy, Civic Production, Supplies, Tools, and Cultural Continuity

**Checkpoint date:** 30 July 2026  
**Status:** Authoritative working ledger for later specification authoring  
**Scope:** Adopted baseline rulings through BSA-11A, plus supersessions, deferred-development flags, and non-STATE commentary

---

## Document Doctrine

This ledger preserves adopted rulings at specification-level detail. It is not the final polished STATE specification, but it is the controlling working record for subsequent synthesis.

Where a later ruling contradicts an earlier one, the later ruling controls. Provisional names, numerical values, and unresolved authoring details are marked explicitly.


# BSA-01 — Laws and Sayings

**Adopted in full.**

Laws remain MEDIAN’s functional, world-facing tutorialization layer.

Sayings remain a separate, non-instructional form of cultural expression.

Existing Laws must be revalidated and, where necessary, rewritten after the v0.5 architecture and baseline decisions settle.

Laws teach durable truths about the world and its systems. They do not teach transient UI procedures, exact tuning values, obsolete mechanics, or instructions whose truth depends on one manifestation of the game.


# BSA-02 — Deep Laws and Species Dialect

**Adopted.**

Deep Laws are stable semantic truths about MEDIAN’s world, culture, and mechanics.

Each Core civilization expresses those truths through its own phrasing and spatial imagination:

- **Mouse:** joining, enclosure, adjacency, passage, and life made safe through connected interiority;
- **Rabbit:** company, court, frontage, assembly, and collective movement;
- **Squirrel:** connection, routes, redundancy, height, and the danger of isolated nodes.

Guests may interpret, translate, misunderstand, dispute, or reframe Laws for character and humor.

Guest interpretations do not create separate mechanics.

> Mechanical truth remains stable under phrasing variation.


# BSA-03 — Literacy and Writing

**Adopted with scope constraint.**

Animals in MEDIAN can read and write.

Written names, Records, Laws, memorials, signs, Chronicle material, and other textual culture may exist.

Literacy is narrative permission. It is not a stat, skill, Role, progression track, or source of species differentiation.

There are no separate species writing systems required by the baseline architecture.

Oral culture remains important.

The game gives no explicit metaphysical explanation for animal literacy.


# BSA-04 — Chronicle and Citizen’s Tale

**Adopted.**

The **Chronicle** is the Colony’s selective civic history. It records founding, transformation, and only momentous events at the scale of the whole civilization.

Every named Citizen has a **Tale**.

A Citizen’s Tale is comprehensive but concise biography at the scale of one life. It is more detailed than the Chronicle without becoming an exhaustive transcript.

A Tale may include origin, Hearth and family, relationships, Role history, wounds, Maiming, fears, Distinctions, Keepsakes, After-names, ordinary Places and routines, aging, death, and legacy.

Every Expedition involving that Citizen is recounted concisely in a character-history style: what occurred, what the Citizen did, and what changed for them.

A Tale may contain short invented interstitial moments drawn from established state. These small scenes may show ordinary life, habits, relationships, or transitions that were not separately simulated.

Interstitial material may not invent new mechanics, major unseen events, relationship reversals, wounds, deaths, or consequential choices the player should have been allowed to witness.

The three layers remain distinct:

- **Campaign Memory:** persistent source facts and remembered material;
- **Chronicle:** selective civic history;
- **Tale:** comprehensive but concise personal biography.


# BSA-04A/B — Prior-Life Tales for Arriving Citizens

**Adopted.**

Every named Citizen enters play with a life before the player first sees them.

Non-campaign-born arrivals—including Wanderers, Guests, rescued or displaced outsiders, and recruited neighbors—possess a concise **Prior-Life Tale**.

On first meaningful inspection, the player may know the broad outline of origin or former community, family, Hearth and important relationships, prior work and ordinary life, journey or displacement, important losses, fears and competencies, Keepsake provenance, wounds, Maiming, Distinction or After-name, and the immediate route by which this Citizen reached the Colony.

Not every category is mandatory. The purpose is a credible prior life, not a questionnaire.

Information materially relevant to hospitality, relationship, residence, or citizenship must not be concealed merely to create a mechanical surprise.

Further detail may emerge through trust, EMBODY, relationships, return visits, meetings with figures from the Citizen’s past, or later events that place old experience in a new light.

Later revelation may deepen, contextualize, or correct the initial account. It should not routinely replace the Citizen with a wholly contradictory secret identity.

Social status may develop continuously:

> stranger → recurring visitor → Wanderer → resident Guest → Citizen

The Tale remains continuous throughout.

Recruitment or citizenship does not create personhood.

Founders possess their shared pre-campaign and Founding Escape biography. Citizens born during the campaign begin their Tale at birth.

> **A Guest may not yet belong to the Colony, but they already belong to their own life.**


# BSA-05 — Lasting Citizen Change

## Fear Memory

Fear Memory belongs to the Exposure framework. It is not a separate condition track or meter.

It is a persistent, context-tagged memory.

When a related danger recurs, Fear Memory may accelerate Exposure, alter how change manifests, or make tharn more likely.

Later safe experience, successful return, relationships, or EMBODY may soften or transform its mechanical force.

The history remains in the Citizen’s Tale even when the immediate mechanical pressure ebbs.

Fear Memory is not a permanent universal penalty.

## Distinction

Distinction is the positive functional tail of Exposure, balancing Maiming.

It is not a generic achievement or universal stat increase.

A Distinction is lasting, narrow, grounded in a specific event or pattern of conduct, situational, and recognizably part of that Citizen’s history.

> Maiming changes what a Citizen cannot easily do.  
> Distinction changes what a Citizen can unusually do.

Exact effects remain tunable.

## After-name

After-name is core.

It is a rare public recognition of identity arising from a Citizen’s history.

It may arise from Distinction, Maiming, repeated conduct, relationship, rescue, failure, or another defining experience.

An After-name is not automatic with every Distinction.

It is socially used in Chronicle, Tale, interface, and dialogue.

It need not add another stat effect.

## Keepsake

A Keepsake is one of three mechanically active personal item categories:

- **Keepsake:** persistent emotional anchor;
- **Tool:** persistent practical capability;
- **Supply:** bounded consumable preparation.

Every Keepsake has provenance.

The standard measurable effect is:

> **Once between Homecomings, a Citizen may draw on their Keepsake to soften one increase of Exposure.**

The exact numerical unit of “soften” may be tuned.

The effect is bounded to one use, personal, refreshed through return Home, not refreshed per MEET, and not part of a magical catalog of bespoke bonuses.

A Keepsake may help with a relevant Fear Memory, but it does so through the same bounded effect.


# BSA-06 — Minimal Personal Inventory

**Adopted.**

Every Expedition-capable Citizen has exactly three mechanically active personal item slots:

1. one active Keepsake;
2. one Tool;
3. one Supply.

These are functional channels, not literal anatomical attachment points.

The intended physical tendencies are:

- **Keepsake:** worn or kept close;
- **Tool:** reusable hand item or working implement;
- **Supply:** prepared consumable or small bundle.

A Tool may include a sack or other Carry implement.

One item occupies each slot.

Citizens may possess additional fictional belongings that are not mechanically tracked.

Cargo remains separate at party scale and is governed through Carry and Burden.

Tools grant permissions or narrow contextual effects. They do not provide generic stat inflation.

Citizens may change active items at Home, subject to availability and provenance.

Guests use the same inventory architecture. Compatibility and manifestation handle morphology rather than creating separate Guest inventory systems.


# BSA-06A — Item Sources, Production, and Acquisition

**Adopted.**

The Colony prepares Supplies, deliberately makes Tools, and acquires Keepsakes through lived history.

These must not collapse into one generalized crafting-and-loot economy.

## Supplies

Supplies are prepared through ordinary civic work.

They consume appropriate Sustenance and/or Scrap. They are not produced from nothing.

Routine preparation is abstracted. The player does not queue every pouch or bundle.

Common Supplies emerge through ordinary activity. Unusual Supplies may require a Practice, model, relationship, unusual material, or Project.

## Tools

Tools are not produced incidentally.

A Tool is made deliberately through the Construction Queue by a Crafter.

Tool-making consumes Scrap, time, Crafter Capacity, and, where relevant, an appropriate Place or Practice.

Repair and adaptation may require tasked Crafting. Routine maintenance may remain abstracted.

## Node recovery

Tools and Supplies may occasionally be found through Node exploration or MEET.

Found objects must be plausible in context.

A recovered object may be immediately usable, returned as Cargo, repaired, adapted, or studied.

Tools should be rarer and more memorable than Supplies.

Supply finds may provide immediate relief or introduce a Supply form the Colony later learns to prepare.

MEDIAN is not a randomized loot game.

## Rest-Stop Metropolis

The Rest-Stop Metropolis may eventually barter finished Tools, specialized Supplies, replacement items, unfamiliar materials, adaptation work, and knowledge that allows reproduction at Home.

Barter occurs through goods, relationships, obligations, and information—not universal coin.

The Metropolis does not invalidate Home Crafting.

## Keepsakes

Roughly half of arriving Citizens should enter the playable story with an existing Keepsake.

This is a generation target, not a visible die roll or exact quota.

The founding group should include at least one Citizen with a Keepsake and at least one with an empty Keepsake slot.

Every arriving Keepsake has provenance.

A Citizen with an empty slot may later acquire a Keepsake through Expedition, MEET, relationship, gift, rescue, death or separation, ceremony, Place, recovered object, Guest relationship, or a quiet EMBODY event.

Keepsake acquisition is an explicit small story event.

An existing possession may become a Keepsake because meaning accrues to it.

Keepsakes are not crafted buffs, trade commodities, interchangeable inventory stock, automatic rewards, or objects assigned solely for optimization.

Transfer, inheritance, loss, return, and memorial placement are consequential.

The Metropolis may provide an object that later becomes a Keepsake. It cannot sell mechanically active Keepsakes as finished sentimental commodities.

A Citizen has one active Keepsake. Other sentimental objects may exist without mechanical activation.

> **The Colony prepares Supplies, chooses to make Tools, and lives long enough for objects to become Keepsakes.**


# BSA-07 — Resource and Bodily Logistics

## Regular resource stocks

The Colony uses four ordinary resource stocks:

- **Perishable Sustenance**
- **Durable Sustenance**
- **Flexible Scrap**
- **Rigid Scrap**

The design contains two forms of nourishment and two forms of recovered matter.

Flexible and Rigid Scrap are broad civic stocks, not component taxonomies.

Flexible Scrap includes wire, twine, cloth, rubber, line, and other matter suited to binding, wrapping, weaving, lashing, or sealing.

Rigid Scrap includes pull-tabs, wood fragments, caps, ceramic, hardware, and other matter suited to bracing, shielding, surfacing, or reinforcement.

Finds may retain narrative specificity while Away, then aggregate into the appropriate Colony stock.

There are no routine metal, wood, stone, fabric, or plastic subcategories.

Singular objects retain provenance instead of dissolving automatically into generic stock.

## Recipe tendencies

- Supplies usually use Flexible Scrap and may use Sustenance.
- Tools usually use Rigid Scrap and often some Flexible Scrap.
- A sack or other Carry Tool may be Flexible-only.
- Residence and enclosure Projects tend toward Flexible Scrap.
- Bracing, surfacing, defensive, and load-bearing Projects tend toward Rigid Scrap.
- Major civic works and Tier-defining Projects commonly require both.

Recipes should use small whole-number requirements.

## Body-unit economy

Bodily logistics operate through body-units.

- One Rabbit = one body-unit.
- One Squirrel = one body-unit.
- Two Mice = one body-unit.

Body-units govern Sustenance, Carry, cargo burden, cooperative lifting, and party scale.

Individuals govern Exposure, wounds, Maiming, death, equipment, relationships, identity, Tale, Distinction, After-name, and other personal consequences.

Six Mice remain six named Citizens with six personal states, but constitute three body-units for logistics.

## Nomenclature amendment

“Wood mouse,” “Wood Mouse,” “Wood Mice,” and “wood-mouse” are deprecated in active v0.5 canon except in historical quotation.

The species name is **Mouse** singular and **Mice** plural.

Use “Mouse civilization” and “two Mice constitute one body-unit.”

This is a nomenclature ruling, not a mechanical or ecological change.

## Sustenance

The Colony consumes:

> **1 Sustenance per body-unit per Dawn.**

Ordinary meals and Sustenance accounting belong to DWELL.

There is no universal Away ration system, hunger meter, meal deduction, individual food upkeep, or multi-day arithmetic food tax.

Eating, resting, and incidental food may appear as texture in Tale, EMBODY, TRAVEL, Stopover, and MEET.

Food may still be Cargo, aid, barter, rescue provision, an authored Supply, or an objective.

Citizens supported by the Colony continue to count toward ordinary Sustenance demand while Away. Departure cannot reduce Colony food demand as an exploit.

Perishable Sustenance is normally consumed before Durable Sustenance.

Durable Sustenance remains vital for winter, disruption, preservation, and civic reserves. It is not primarily Away ration arithmetic.

### Core logistical table

| Civilization | Citizens per body-unit | Carry per body-unit | Sustenance per Dawn |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rabbit | 1 | 10 | 1 |
| Mouse | 2 | 12 | 1 |
| Squirrel | 1 | 20 | 1 |

## Healing

There is no separate medicinal resource stock.

Where treatment has a material cost, Healing uses ordinary Sustenance.

A Medical or Remedy Supply may use Sustenance and Flexible Scrap.

Splints and adaptive devices may use Rigid Scrap.

Routine recovery is governed primarily by time, Healing coverage, Patient Load, and suitable Places.

Material cost is reserved for significant treatment rather than charged every convalescent Dawn.

## Nesting

There is no Nursery Matter stock.

Nesting requires a suitable Residence-and-Care Place, civic margin, Caretaking and Teaching support, and **10 reserved Sustenance per expected new body-unit**.

Therefore one Rabbit young requires 10, one Squirrel young requires 10, and two Mouse littermates require 10 together.

This is a preparedness commitment, not a recipe that manufactures offspring.

New young then contribute to ordinary body-unit consumption.

## Expedition party scale

Ordinary Expeditions contain **2–3 body-units**.

Three body-units is the standard ordinary maximum.

This produces 2–3 Rabbits, 4–6 Mice, or 2–3 Squirrels before Guest and mixed-party adjustments.

Larger parties occur only as authored exceptions, such as evacuation, migration, major hauling, or a Grand Caravan.

Larger parties are not unlocked as a permanent cap increase.


# BSA-08 — Special Artifacts

**Adopted.**

A Special Artifact is a singular named Colony object.

It is not generic stock or a currency such as `Artifact ×3`.

Every Special Artifact has a concrete physical identity, provenance, a persistent location or custodian, and a limited set of credible uses.

It remains identifiable until it is used, exchanged, transformed, lost, or memorialized.

A Special Artifact may enable a major Project, provide an irreplaceable component for a Tool or adaptive device, establish a Practice, satisfy a relationship or barter requirement, reveal knowledge, support a Tier-defining civic achievement, or carry durable historical meaning.

An Artifact may later become a Tool or Keepsake through an explicit transformation, but it begins as a separate category.

> **A Special Artifact is a singular recovered object whose identity and provenance matter mechanically and which does not dissolve into Scrap.**


# BSA-08A — Place of Pride

**Adopted.**

Every Colony Gathering Place contains one **Place of Pride**: a prominent communal position displaying one eligible Special Artifact.

The Colony may possess multiple Artifacts, but only one is displayed and active through the Place of Pride.

The displayed Artifact provides a stated **Civic Effect**.

This is not magic. The effect arises through repeated story, shared lesson, accessible knowledge, affirmed relationship, confidence, or civic identity.

A Civic Effect must be narrow, legible, and grounded in the Artifact’s provenance.

It may support or protect a Role under defined conditions, make a Practice available, preserve knowledge, alter a Home MEET option, support hospitality or diplomacy, reduce a specific recurring disruption, or provide another closely related permission.

It may not provide broad productivity percentages, universal morale, generic Colony stat increases, stackable auras, or build-combination optimization.

Not every Artifact is eligible for the Place of Pride.

An Artifact record may identify several possible uses: Place of Pride, Project, study leading to a Practice, Tool or adaptive device, MEET, barter or diplomacy, ceremonial return, or undedicated custody.

Practical, dangerous, secret, fragile, or culturally inappropriate objects may be ineligible for display.

The player may change the displayed Artifact during DWELL, outside crisis or unresolved MEET.

The new Civic Effect begins on the following Dawn.

Changing the display has no additional resource cost and requires no permanent Role assignment. The Dawn boundary prevents rapid swapping.

The previous Artifact returns to named Colony custody.

Because the Artifact is real, it may be removed, studied, damaged, stolen, surrendered, returned, transformed, or traded. Removing it ends its Civic Effect.

A Keepsake is personal. The Place of Pride is communal.

An active Keepsake may not be casually socketed into the Place of Pride. It may become a Colony Artifact only through inheritance, voluntary gifting, retirement, civic dedication, or another authored transition.

A memorial display is distinct. A memorial object placed at the civic center indicates continuing active significance, not merely storage of the dead.

The Place of Pride exists from Tier I, even if its physical form is only a ledge, stone, root, branch, or another improvised position.

It may become more protected or ceremonial as the Colony develops.

There is exactly one Place of Pride and one active displayed Artifact.


# BSA-08B — Gathering Place

**Adopted.**

Every Colony possesses a **Gathering Place**: the principal cultural and ceremonial Place where Citizens assemble to interpret their shared life.

Gathering Place is the universal functional term.

It does not prescribe a circular plan, open ground, a single elevation, or another species-specific spatial form.

The Gathering Place supports the teaching and interpretation of Laws, Sayings and storytelling, civic ceremonies, After-name recognition, Maturity and arrival observances, memorial practices, the recounting of Chronicle material, Council-centered cultural events, and the Place of Pride.

Each Core civilization gives the Gathering Place its own architectural form and in-world name.

A Rabbit Gathering Place may plausibly be a court or Story Circle.

Mouse and Squirrel expressions must arise from their own spatial identities rather than reproducing the Rabbit arrangement underground or aloft.

Species-specific names remain for a later Place-nomenclature pass.

Until then:

- **Gathering Place** is the canonical functional label.
- **Story Circle** may remain a Rabbit-specific expression.
- **Circle** is deprecated as the universal term.

A Gathering Place may be enclosed or open, centralized or distributed around a shared focus, level or vertically arranged, intimate or capable of receiving the larger Colony, purpose-built or gradually recognized through repeated use.

> **It is the place where the Colony gathers to say what its life means.**


# BSA-09 — Functional Recipe Classes

**Adopted.**

Tools and Supplies are organized into a small number of canonical functional classes.

Each class defines mechanical function, the circumstances in which it matters, whether it is persistent or consumed, and a simple resource recipe.

Individual objects are manifestations of a class, not separate mechanical recipes.

Species-appropriate objects may share one class and one recipe while differing in form, material, scale, and cultural expression.

## Recipe grammar

Personal items use only these recipe shapes:

- **2 of one resource**
- **1 + 1 of two resources**
- **2 + 1 of two resources**

No Tool or Supply recipe uses more than two resource types.

The `2 + 1` form is reserved for greater capability, broader application, or more demanding construction. It is not the automatic default.

Personal-item recipes do not use long ingredient lists, fractional quantities, generic Crafting points, Special Artifacts as interchangeable ingredients, or species-specific cost tables.

## Tier-defining Projects

Tier advancement is not purchased directly with resources.

However, a defining civic Project supporting advancement must consume a **significant quantity of both Flexible and Rigid Scrap**.

Its material commitment must be unmistakably larger than a personal Tool or Supply recipe.

A Tool costs a handful of material. A Tier-defining Project represents a Colony’s sustained recovery and commitment of both forms of Scrap.

Project completion is only one advancement proof. Material expenditure cannot substitute for population stability, Equilibrium, institutional coverage, corridor capability, or other required evidence of civic maturity.


# BSA-09A — Sustenance Typing and Winter Cultivation

## Supply recipes

The default Sustenance ingredient in a Supply recipe is **generic Sustenance**.

Either Perishable or Durable Sustenance may satisfy it.

A Supply may explicitly require Perishable Sustenance where freshness or living material is essential, or Durable Sustenance where preservation and stability are essential.

Typed Sustenance is exceptional and mechanically justified. It is not used merely to make recipes appear different.

This prevents winter from arbitrarily disabling ordinary Supply preparation.

## Ordinary winter Gardening

An ordinary Garden produces no regular Perishable Sustenance in winter.

Gardeners remain active during the dormant season by protecting roots, bulbs, seeds, and viable planting stock; maintaining drainage, wind shelter, mulch, and coverings; managing compost and decaying organic matter; inspecting stored planting material; gathering limited naturally available roots, shoots, fungi, or bark where credible; and preparing the Garden for renewed production.

Covered Gardening Capacity preserves the Garden’s condition and next-season readiness.

A neglected Garden may enter spring damaged, slow to resume, or below normal production until restored.

## Winter Cultivation

**Winter Cultivation** is a learned Colony Practice concerning protected roots and shoots, fungi, deep soil, compost warmth, sheltered growth, and careful winter harvesting.

Possessing the Practice does not directly generate food.

The Colony must also adapt a Garden through a related Project and cover that Place with Gardener Capacity.

The full expression is:

> **Winter Cultivation Practice + adapted Garden Place + Gardener coverage = one-quarter normal Perishable Sustenance production in winter.**

Fractional production is resolved through whole-unit cadence.

A Garden with a normal rate of 1 Perishable Sustenance per Dawn produces 1 every 4 Dawns in winter.

Several Gardens may aggregate their cadence, but the interface does not display fractional stock.

Durable Sustenance remains the foundation of winter survival.

## Sources of Winter Cultivation

Winter Cultivation must have at least one self-sufficient Colony route and may have several external routes.

A Colony that maintains a Garden through a full dormant season may receive a Gardener-centered Home MEET concerning what survived, sprouted, remained warm, or could be protected.

Resolving that developmental MEET may establish Winter Cultivation.

The Practice may be learned earlier through an appropriate Node, another Colony or animal community, the Rest-Stop Metropolis, a recovered Special Artifact, a Tale or Record, or a knowledgeable Wanderer or Guest.

External routes change timing and story. They do not alter the resulting shared Practice.

A Resident Guest may help the Colony discover or apply Winter Cultivation, but no Guest is its exclusive source.

A Guest contribution should remain situated, attributable, bounded, and nonessential.

A Guest might open a developmental Home MEET, identify a suitable site, reduce a Project requirement, or permit one bounded emergency winter harvest.

Groundhog’s Seasonal Telegraph and the Bumblebee Household’s Carry the Bloom are not silently expanded into permanent winter-production multipliers.

## Terminology correction

“Winter Garden Affordance” is deprecated as the formal name.

- **Resident Affordance** remains a precise Guest term.
- **Winter Cultivation** is a Practice.
- The physical adaptation is a Garden Project.
- The reduced winter yield is the resulting Place function.


# BSA-09B — Place Development Through MEET

**Adopted.**

Constructed Places may acquire additional functions through developmental Home MEETs.

A developmental MEET occurs when sustained use, seasonal experience, civic need, or consequence makes a new possibility credible.

The reusable sequence is:

> **Use and world experience → developmental threshold → Home MEET → learned Practice or civic decision → Project, where physical adaptation is required → new Place function**

Places do not have generic levels such as Garden I / II / III, Workshop Level 2, upgrade percentages, or abstract improvement slots.

A Place develops because something specific has become true there.

A developmental threshold is a world-state condition, not an experience bar.

Possible thresholds include maintaining a Place through its first winter, repeatedly using it under pressure, receiving a particular kind of Patient or Guest, suffering and repairing a characteristic failure, completing a relevant Expedition, acquiring a Practice, Artifact, or external relationship, reaching a population or civic-use condition, or accumulating enough lived experience that a new use becomes evident.

The interface may indicate unresolved potential or that Citizens are noticing something. It should not display `Garden Upgrade: 74%`.

Crossing a threshold does not silently add a bonus.

The Home MEET identifies what Citizens observed, who understands or disputes it, what the Colony might do with the knowledge, what competing need or cost exists, and what commitment would make the possibility real.

The player may pursue the new function, choose among distinct adaptations, postpone, or decline.

A postponed developmental MEET may recur when circumstances support it. It is not automatically lost because the player deferred once.

A developmental MEET may establish knowledge without completing physical work.

- A **Practice** is what the Colony now knows how to do.
- A **Project** is the physical or civic work needed to embody that knowledge.
- A **Place function** is what becomes sustainably possible after completion.

Some developments require no construction. Others require a Project.

Only developments that materially change how the Colony lives, responds, or understands a Place deserve this treatment.

A major Place might gain only one or two historically meaningful new functions across an entire campaign.

> **A Place does not level up. The Colony lives with it long enough to discover what else it may become.**


# Deferred Development Flag — Studying an Appropriate Node

**Not yet STATE canon. Flagged for later development.**

Later work must determine what makes a Node appropriate to a particular discovery; whether STUDY is a Node MEET choice, a repeated relationship with the Node, or both; what observations persist between visits; whether named Citizens, Tools, seasons, or existing Practices affect what can be learned; how study produces a developmental Home MEET rather than directly unlocking a function; whether study can reveal a Practice, identify a Project opportunity, or provide a named prerequisite material; and how to prevent STUDY from becoming a generic research button or technology-tree substitute.

Working doctrine:

> **A Node may show the Colony that something is possible. The Colony must still interpret that knowledge at Home and choose whether to embody it.**


# BSA-09C — Projects, Tools, and the Construction Queue

**Adopted.**

The Construction Queue is the shared execution layer for deliberate work that produces a defined, persistent result.

It is no longer exclusive to Builders.

Every Queue entry has a defined result, a related Role, a material requirement where appropriate, and a required quantity of committed Role labor.

While a Citizen services the Construction Queue, their Role Capacity is committed to that entry.

That Capacity does not simultaneously provide the Role’s ordinary passive function.

If several Citizens provide the same Role, only committed Capacity is suspended. Uncommitted Capacity continues ordinary coverage.

> **A Citizen may maintain the Colony’s ordinary function or deliberately change what the Colony can do. They do not perform both with the same civic share.**

A Project is enacted through the Construction Queue by its related Role.

The related Role is determined by the Project’s principal civic function, not simply by whether material is moved.

A Gardener therefore enacts a Winter Cultivation adaptation even when the Project consumes Scrap.

A developmental MEET does not instantly upgrade a Place.

The full sequence is:

> **World experience → developmental threshold → Home MEET → Practice or decision → Construction Queue entry → completed Place function**

Tools are produced through the same Construction Queue.

Each Tool class has a simple Scrap recipe. A Tool entry is serviced by a Crafter. Committed Crafter Capacity does not provide ordinary passive Crafting. Completion creates one persistent Tool.

Tool-making is a deliberate opportunity cost.

Supplies do not use the Construction Queue. They are produced through Provisioning.

Ordinary maintenance belongs to passive Role function.

A problem enters the Construction Queue only when it requires a defined intervention with a persistent result, including substantial rebuilding, Place adaptation, establishment of a new function, Tool production, or major civic work.

> **Roles sustain what the Colony already has. The Construction Queue commits those Roles to making something newly true.**


# BSA-09C.1 — Construction Queue Concurrency

**Adopted: one active entry per related Role.**

The Colony may progress several Construction Queue entries simultaneously, but only one active entry for each Role.

A Builder Project, Gardener Project, and Crafter Tool may therefore progress at the same time.

Two Crafter entries cannot progress simultaneously merely because both exist in the Queue.

Additional Citizens assigned to a Role may add Citizen-Days to that Role’s active entry, preserve ordinary Role coverage, or remain fully on ordinary Practice.

The true limit is available named-Citizen Capacity and the loss of ordinary coverage, not an abstract Colony-wide queue-slot statistic.


# BSA-09D — Provisioning

**Adopted.**

**Provisioning** is the shared passive-transformation system through which available Role Capacity converts existing stocks into prepared stocks or consumable items without using the Construction Queue.

Provisioning is routine, conditional, ongoing, and player-directed.

It never creates resources from nothing.

## Provisioning Roles

### Caretaker — Preservation

Caretakers may transform:

> **Perishable Sustenance → Durable Sustenance**

This represents drying, protecting, sorting, wrapping, caching, and other culturally appropriate preservation.

Preservation is limited by available Perishable Sustenance, Caretaker Capacity not committed to a Project, suitable Stores or household Places, known preservation Practices, and player instructions.

### Crafter — Supply Preparation

Crafters may transform Sustenance and Scrap into Supply items according to known class recipes.

Supply Preparation is limited by available ingredients, Crafter Capacity not committed to a Tool or Project, a suitable Workshop or preparation Place, known Supply recipes, and player-set targets and reserves.

A Crafter servicing the Construction Queue provides no Provisioning with that committed civic share.

## Player control

Provisioning does not automatically consume every eligible resource.

Each Provisioning process may be:

- **Prepare:** operate while inputs and Capacity permit;
- **Hold:** suspend transformation while retaining the Role assignment;
- **Unavailable:** lacking the required Capacity, Place, material, or Practice.

Holding Provisioning does not make the Citizen idle. The Role continues its broader Standing Practice but does not convert stock.

## Targets and reserves

Each Provisioning output may have a player-set target.

Provisioning stops when the target is met and resumes when stock falls below it.

The target means:

> **Keep the Colony prepared to this degree, provided doing so does not violate its reserves.**

The player may reserve quantities of any regular stock.

Reserved resources remain in Stores but cannot be consumed by Provisioning.

Reserves may protect material for a Tool, Place-development Project, winter, Nesting, an anticipated MEET, or a Tier-defining Project.

Provisioning may use only unreserved stock.

A known or planned Project may receive a direct reservation of some or all required material.

Reserved material remains visible but is protected from Provisioning, unrelated Queue entries, and ordinary discretionary spending.

It is not consumed until the Project reaches the appropriate commitment point.

The player may release a reservation.

An emergency MEET may explicitly allow the Colony to break a reservation, but the threatened Project must be named clearly.

## Role priority

Available Role Capacity follows this priority:

1. Capacity committed to an active Construction Queue entry;
2. Capacity required to cover ordinary Role Load;
3. remaining Capacity available for Provisioning.

Routine transformation does not silently outrank civic stability.

|---|---|---|
| Supply | Provisioning | Routine readiness |
| Tool | Construction Queue, Crafter | Deliberate durable capability |
| Project | Construction Queue, related Role | Persistent civic or Place change |
| Routine Role function | Passive Role Capacity | Daily maintenance and support |

> **Provisioning keeps prepared things ready. The Construction Queue makes lasting things real.**


# Developmental Appendix Note — Mechanical Resolution Grammar

**Designer commentary only. Not STATE material.**

MEDIAN’s preference for small named classes, bounded permissions, fixed slots, integer costs, concise adjudication, and explicit opportunity cost resembles tabletop card-game resolution.

This is an observation about design method, not an in-world rule or mandatory presentation layer.

> **Tabletop clarity beneath; lived civilization above.**

The mechanical layer should remain compact enough to state clearly.

Citizens, Places, provenance, MEET, Chronicle, and Tale prevent that clarity from flattening the world into tokens and effects.


# BSA-10 — Cross-Functional Supply Classes

**Adopted.**

Supplies use a small set of physical-function classes.

They do not map one-to-one onto the five MEET Turn verbs.

Each Supply class has a concrete physical logic, broad utility, a fixed simple recipe, and species- or circumstance-specific manifestations.

The class remains stable while the represented object varies.

## Working class set

| Supply class | Physical logic | Working recipe |
|---|---|---|
| **Binding** | Tie, secure, tether, wrap, patch, restrain | 2 Flexible Scrap |
| **Remedy** | Soothe, stabilize, clean, revive, protect | 1 Sustenance + 1 Flexible Scrap |
| **Distraction** | Lure, occupy, misdirect, bait, mask | 2 Sustenance |
| **Device** | Wedge, mark, signal, brace, trigger, probe | 1 Flexible Scrap + 1 Rigid Scrap |

The names and exact recipes remain subject to the dedicated Supply pass.

The governing decision is settled:

> **Supplies describe prepared things and what those things can do—not prepaid copies of Turn verbs.**


# BSA-10A — General Utility Doctrine for Supplies

**Adopted.**

A Supply class represents a broad physical capability, not an encounter key and not a consumable copy of one MEET Turn.

With three Supply slots and many possible spend windows across an Expedition, the intended pressure is:

> **Do we spend this now?**

—not:

> **Did we guess the one encounter where this item works?**

Each Supply should be potentially useful in many circumstances.

Its physical verbs may support different Turns depending on context.

For representative Expeditions, each Supply class should plausibly apply to more than half of the meaningful spend opportunities; most spend windows should admit at least two Supply classes; and no ordinary route, Node family, season, or encounter category should make a class routinely useless.

This is an authoring and testing target, not a visible percentage or universal guarantee.

Applicable does not mean optimal.

A Supply normally strengthens a valid Turn, softens cost or Exposure, protects an additional subject or stake, extends the action’s reach, or creates a credible alternate approach.

Core Turn agency is not generally item-gated.

An object intended for one specific lock, creature, Node, or scripted circumstance is not an ordinary Supply. It should instead be represented as Node-specific Cargo, an objective object, a Special Artifact, a relationship gift, or a specialized Tool.

> **A player who launches with any reasonable combination of three Supplies should repeatedly see credible opportunities to use all three. The difficulty should be deciding which opportunities deserve them.**


# BSA-10B — Supply Synergy in the Turn Interface

**Adopted with complexity correction.**

When a carried Supply is clearly relevant to a Turn, the Turn option displays a small inline Supply indicator.

Example:

> **PARLEY — Offer help to the wounded raccoon**  
> `Remedy available`

The player may resolve the Turn normally or spend the indicated Supply.

Selecting the indicator reveals the concise contextual effect, which Supply will be consumed, and confirmation.

The interface does not introduce a separate inventory phase, compatibility submenu, list of every theoretically plausible Supply, hidden experimentation, or “best choice” signaling.

Supply applicability is visible because remembering an implicit compatibility matrix is not meaningful difficulty.

The strategic question is whether the present benefit justifies consuming one of the Expedition’s few prepared Supplies.

Authors surface only the clearest and most consequential synergies.

Broad fictional applicability does not require every imaginable use to become a player-facing option.

> **The interface shows where a Supply can help. It does not tell the player whether to spend it.**


# BSA-10C — Contextual Supply Effects

**Adopted.**

A relevant Supply provides one contextual effect in the Turn where it is offered.

The player decides only:

> **Spend the Supply here, or preserve it.**

The interface does not ask the player to select a second Supply mode.

The same Supply remains dimensional because different encounters apply its physical character differently.

The encounter supplies the one clear contextual effect.

The earlier proposal for two player-selected modes per Supply is superseded.


# Superseded Note — RENDER as a Guest-Only Permission

An earlier draft treated RENDER primarily as a Red Fox Guest Permission.

That formulation is superseded.

RENDER is a full Tool class under BSA-11.

A Guest Permission may access the same functional grammar without an ordinary carried Tool where the Guest’s body and ecology justify it.


# BSA-11 — Core Tool Classes

**Adopted.**

The canonical Tool vocabulary contains five functional classes:

| Tool class | Primary function |
|---|---|
| **Carry** | Increase or reorganize personal and party Carry |
| **Reach** | Extend contact, control distance, probe, hook, retrieve, or operate across a gap |
| **Cut** | Sever, pierce, scrape, open, divide, or free |
| **Brace** | Support, block, wedge, shield, stabilize, reinforce, or hold |
| **Render** | Process suitable recovered matter into a usable form at a Node |

Each Tool is a persistent personal item occupying the Citizen’s single Tool slot.

The class defines its mechanical permission.

Physical manifestation remains species-specific, material-specific, and provenance-specific.

Different objects are not separate Tool types unless their function genuinely differs.

## Render Tools

A Render Tool permits suitable material at a Node to be processed in the field.

Where the Node explicitly supports it, Render may convert part of a Node yield into the indicated Supply class, prepare recovered matter for safer transport, separate usable material from a hazardous or burdensome whole, or expose a more useful form of the Node’s existing yield.

Render does not create open-ended Away crafting.

It cannot produce arbitrary Supplies, create Tools, Keepsakes, or Special Artifacts, bypass authored Node limits, create additional inventory slots, or operate without credible material to process.

The Node indicates the available Render result.

Example:

> **RENDER available: Convert part of this windfall into 1 Distraction Supply.**

The resulting Supply occupies an empty or already-spent Supply slot.

## Guest relationship

A Guest Permission may grant access to Render without carrying a conventional Render Tool when the capability follows directly from the Guest’s body, behavior, and ecological knowledge.

The Red Fox’s Carcass Claim is the principal example.

The Guest is not operating a separate crafting system.

The Guest accesses the same Render grammar through a distinctive living capability.


# BSA-11A — Latent Martial Utility of Tools

**Adopted.**

MEDIAN has no Weapon class.

The Colony does not manufacture, classify, equip, or celebrate objects whose principal civic purpose is injuring another animal.

Nevertheless, the physical functions of most Tools have obvious utility when a Conflicted MEET becomes violent.

The following Tool classes possess latent martial utility:

- Reach;
- Cut;
- Brace;
- Render.

Carry does not possess this property by default.

## Application

During a Conflicted MEET, a relevant Tool may provide one slight contextual advantage to an approach that materially resembles a fight.

Examples:

- Reach may maintain distance or control an approach.
- Cut may threaten, sever, pierce, or free under pressure.
- Brace may block, hold ground, deflect force, or protect another Citizen.
- Render may apply processing implements or anatomical knowledge against resistant living danger.

The Tool’s relevance appears as a compact inline indicator on the relevant Turn.

Example:

> **PRESS — Force the attacker away**  
> `Reach Tool applies`

There is no separate combat inventory step or attack-mode selection.

## Scale

Latent martial utility is slight, contextual, persistent rather than consumed, non-stacking within one Turn, and resolved through the ordinary MEET system.

It may soften Exposure, improve immediate effect, help protect another subject, preserve distance, or reduce escalation.

It does not create attack ratings, damage values, weapon reach statistics, armor penetration, combat techniques, or a separate injury-resolution system.

The encounter authors the one contextual benefit.

## Weapon prohibition

The prohibition on depicted weapons remains fully in force.

MEDIAN does not depict dedicated swords, spears, clubs, or manufactured armaments; armories or weapon stores; weapon recipes; combat loadouts; armed-unit silhouettes; weapon progression; or heroic visual language centered on being armed.

A Tool must remain visibly and mechanically credible in its ordinary working purpose.

A civic cutting implement may be used desperately in violence.

It must not be redesigned until its real primary identity is a knife or sword while retaining “Cut Tool” as a euphemistic label.

A Brace Tool may protect a Citizen.

It must not become a standardized battle shield detached from ordinary bracing, structural, carrying, or rescue use.

## Carry exception

Carry already has substantial logistical value and gains no inherent martial advantage.

A particular Conflicted MEET may recognize a creative use of a sack, sling, drag-cloth, or carried burden.

That remains an authored contextual interaction, not a universal property of Carry.

> **Tools are made for work, not violence. When violence reaches the Colony, Citizens may defend themselves with the implements of ordinary life.**

Violence does not acquire its own item economy, professional identity, or progression layer.


# Complexity-Drift Watch

The following warning signs are active for subsequent design work:

- solving edge cases before defining the baseline interaction;
- adding menus to express dimensionality;
- exposing every theoretically plausible interaction;
- turning authoring flexibility into player-facing procedure;
- creating a second decision after a decision that was already sufficient;
- introducing a new meter, submenu, click, or exception when an existing mechanic can carry the result.

Working check:

> **Does this require another click, submenu, meter, or exception?**

A “yes” is not an automatic rejection, but requires explicit justification.

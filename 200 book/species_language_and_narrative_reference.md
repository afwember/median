# Species Language and Narrative Reference

**Status:** Non-authoritative future reference

**Purpose:** Preserve useful narrative ideas from an external brainstorming
conversation after removing conflicts, unsupported claims, and unnecessary
procedural expansion.

**Authority:** This note is not a specification, ruling, source for atomic
extraction, or independent carrier of game canon. Current MEDIAN specifications,
governance, accepted evidence, and future reconciliation remain authoritative.

## Governing idea

Species voice should arise from selective attention, not accents, intelligence
rankings, rigid syntax, or invented subsystems.

The same persistent world can be described differently because each species
notices the features most relevant to its way of inhabiting that world:

- **Rabbit — GATHER:** what gathers life and makes shared ground safe.
- **Mouse — JOIN:** what joins, opens, encloses, or fails at fine scale.
- **Squirrel — CONNECT:** what connects points across height, distance, and
  motion.

This is an authoring lens. It creates no new mechanic, Register, taxonomy, or
species-exclusive rule.

## Translation without a constructed language

Animal language can be implied through English by controlling:

- what receives a name;
- which distinctions become compact recurring terms;
- characteristic metaphors and comparisons;
- selective Hyphen-bound compounds;
- what speakers assume rather than explain; and
- which aspect of a shared object each species identifies first.

Hyphen-bound compounds remain governed by the Authorial Grammar. They represent
intentional translated lexical units and should remain rare cultural anchors,
not decorative compounds distributed through ordinary prose.

## Shared object, species-specific attention

An object does not change identity when another species encounters it. The
salient affordance changes.

For example, one tree might be experienced as:

- **Rabbit:** shelter edge, shared warning ground, forage boundary, or familiar
  cover;
- **Mouse:** textured wall, cavity system, seed trap, seam, or protected
  micro-route;
- **Squirrel:** launch geometry, branch network, cache structure, route anchor,
  or refuge connection.

This technique is especially useful for narrative prose, species-specific
inspection text, EMBODY scenes, infographics, environmental art direction, and
cross-species dialogue.

## Rabbit narrative lens

MEDIAN Rabbits combine principally above-ground physical life with dense social
continuity. Their sense of spatial depth should therefore arise through cover
rather than a universal subterranean-warren model.

Useful Rabbit contrasts include:

- dense / open;
- covered / exposed;
- held / scattered;
- familiar / unwatched; and
- enclosed by living growth / visible to the sky.

Rabbit language and narration may attend to shared ground, synchronized
warning, known cover, communal memory, and the continuity of familiar Places.
It should not erase the agency of individual named Citizens through compulsory
collective grammar.

## Mouse narrative lens

Mouse speed should appear as precision and predictive spatial processing, not
frantic speech, forgetfulness, or simple-mindedness.

A Mouse may notice fine structural facts while a larger animal is still
identifying the broad event. In prose, the Mouse can appear to have already
compared several gaps, surfaces, routes, and failure points before selecting an
action. Concision can suggest completed reasoning rather than limited thought.

Do not generalize this into supernatural foresight, automatic success, a
species-wide habit of interrupting others, or freedom from long memory. Mouse
civilization remains capable of history, Records, planning, civic continuity,
and sustained attachment.

## Squirrel narrative lens

The useful Squirrel emphasis is three-dimensional relation:

- trajectory and momentum;
- anchors and gaps;
- flex, stability, and structural failure;
- distributed security;
- route memory; and
- the connection between separated Places.

Avoid reducing Squirrels to manic, selfish, transactional hoarders. Cache
practice and spatial vigilance may shape particular stories, but they do not
override equal civic standing or define Squirrel personhood. Ground use remains
part of Squirrel ecology rather than a universal abyss or phobia.

## Naming implications

Names often become evocative through accumulated use rather than beginning as
poetic inventions. Ordinary inhabitants may name Places through location,
appearance, use, material, or a remembered incident.

The emerging Core Species tendencies are:

- **Rabbit names:** shared living ground, verge plants, cover, familiar natural
  forms, and modest communal vocabulary;
- **Mouse names:** fine materials, grain and stores, textiles, seams, tools,
  enclosure, and joined parts; and
- **Squirrel names:** routes, anchors, motion, weather, hanging or turning
  forms, fastening, and vertical structure.

These are tendencies rather than closed name generators. Given Names,
After-names, Colony names, Reach names, and Place names remain distinct naming
questions unless later reconciliation explicitly relates them.

## Human objects and changing familiarity

Perceptual description can make first contact with an unfamiliar human object
feel non-human without denying the animals intelligence:

1. an unfamiliar object is initially described through sensed material,
   movement, sound, danger, or affordance;
2. repeated encounters establish a shared term; and
3. the term becomes ordinary vocabulary without reenacting discovery every
   time.

MEDIAN animals inhabit a human-made corridor and maintain knowledge. They
should not remain permanently unable to understand common fences, drains,
vehicles, reflectors, or structures. Constant circumlocution would make the
prose laborious and the civilizations implausibly ignorant.

## Ideas specifically rejected or constrained

The following brainstorming directions should not be carried forward as MEDIAN
rules:

- radical species-wide numerical limitations;
- Mouse confinement to a short present or inability to sustain history;
- Squirrel personhood defined by possession, transaction, mania, or solitary
  territoriality;
- Rabbit collective grammar that suppresses individual Citizens;
- function titles, nest ranks, honorific castes, or Owsla-like social tiers;
- universal kennings for ordinary human objects;
- routine decorative hyphenation;
- species accents or rigid sentence templates;
- hidden cognitive rankings among Core Species;
- thumping, interruption, cache possession, or predictive pathing promoted into
  new subsystems merely because they are evocative; and
- external literary or linguistic claims treated as authority without reliable
  verification.

## Recommended future use

At the narrative and Augments phase, use these lenses to derive multiple
species-grounded presentations from already reconciled content. They may inform
prose, dialogue, environmental captions, illustration briefs, and infographic
comparisons. They should not independently determine mechanics or reopen
settled specifications.

The preferred application is conservative: one canonical event, Place, or
object; several truthful species-specific readings; no additional system.

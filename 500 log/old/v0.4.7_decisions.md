# MEDIAN v0.4.7 — Running Decisions

Canon revisions arising from the **Registers, Views, and Encounter Mode** thread. Numbered **V7-**.
Source: `600 archive/threads/` · visual prototypes at `300 art/047 encounters` (thirteen plates, two of them layout guides).

**Status:** proposed, nothing settled. Marked **· SETTLED** where the entry is a recommendation ready to be taken as written, **· OPEN** where it needs a ruling before it can be applied.

**The thread's single most valuable output** is that it relieves a strain nobody had named. §2.7 states that no fifth Register may be added and that every presentation need must resolve into Colony, Field, Encounter or Crossing. The document then carries five presentation needs that resolve into none of them — the Expedition Launcher (§28.2), Choice-Event Cards (§10), the Report and Share debrief (§22.3), Base Defense (§27.4) and Road Work onset (§11.2). The pillar was being honoured in letter and quietly broken in practice. **The Register/View distinction repairs all five at once and adds nothing.**

# GROUP A — THE REGISTER CONTRACT

## V7-1 — A Register is a mode of attention, not a geographic zone · **SETTLED**
*§3, §3.1–3.5. Restates rather than changes; the current wording permits the wrong reading.*

A **Register** is MEDIAN's complete visual and interactive contract for one kind of player attention. It fixes camera and framing, scale, treatment of time, navigation, available controls, information hierarchy, how citizens are represented, and the class of decision being asked for.

**A Register is therefore not determined by location.** The Home Median is usually Colony, but Launch and Homecoming happen there in Encounter. A Margin is usually Field, but a raccoon at a berry bush opens Encounter on the same ground.

§3's table already implies this — its column is *Attention*, not *Place* — but §7 through §29 are organised geographically, and the document nowhere states that the two axes are independent. **State it once, in §3, and the rest of the document stops being ambiguous.**

| Location | Registers it can appear through |
|---|---|
| Home Median | Colony · Encounter for domestic events, Launch, Homecoming |
| Highway lanes | Crossing |
| Margin or Reach | Field · Encounter when something is directly engaged |
| Field node | Field before entry · Encounter during interaction |
| Outpost | Field · Encounter · limited Colony functions |
| Rest-Stop Metropolis | Field navigation layer · multiple Encounter venues |
| Colony structure | Colony observation · Encounter when it becomes consequential |

**The Register changes because the player's relationship to the place changes.** In Field, a berry bush is navigable geography. In Encounter, a raccoon claims it.

## V7-2 — A View is a manifestation within a Register · **SETTLED**
*New term. §3, §33. The mechanism that keeps §2.7 honest.*

A **Register** is the mode. A **View** is a particular presentation constructed inside it.

Colony Overview View · Crossing View · Field Reach View · Contested Resource Encounter View · Colony Event Encounter View · Recruitment Encounter View · **Expedition Launch Encounter View** · **Homecoming Encounter View** · Rest-Stop Market Encounter View.

**This is the whole repair.** Launch is not a fifth Register; it is an Encounter View. Homecoming is not a management mode; it is an Encounter View. A colony event is not a Choice Card mode; it is an Encounter View arising from Colony state. Nothing new is added and four Registers hold.

*Naming note:* **View** is designer-facing. The game says nothing; the player only ever sees the screen.

## V7-3 — Encounter's verb becomes **Interact** · **SETTLED**
*§3, §3.3, §26. Corrects a contradiction that already exists in the document.*

§3's table gives Encounter the verb **Meet**. §26's opening sentence says the frame is the same *"whether the result is a windfall, a negotiation, or an ambush."* **Those cannot both be true.** A windfall meets nobody, a sealed cooler meets nobody, and an uncontested wildflower field meets nobody.

**Meet is replaced by Interact**, which is deliberately broad and covers the cases canon already has: a resource claimed by a raccoon, an unclaimed field, a guest considering recruitment, a predator, a failing drain, road work shaking a web, an expedition being received home, a broker at the Metropolis.

> **An Encounter is a fixed, bounded scene in which particular citizens interact directly with a person, place, object, need, opportunity or danger, and the result persists in the world.**

Its defining quality is **specificity**.

## V7-4 — Field's verb becomes **Explore** · **PROVISIONAL**
*§3, §3.2, §25.*

Proposed alongside V7-3. It is a weaker case. §3's attention column reads *"Reading territory — where things are, what my route is,"* and **reading** is the more accurate verb; *Explore* is the better marketing word. §25.4 also makes everyday home foraging a Field run, and *Explore* over-promises novelty for a walk to the food store.

**Recommendation: take Interact, hold Explore.** If Explore is taken, §25.4's home-ground case needs a sentence saying that the same Register can be routine.

## V7-5 — The transition rule · **SETTLED**
*§3, §10, §25, §26.*

> **Native Registers reveal situations. Encounter makes them directly interactive.**

Colony play reveals that a structure is failing, that two households have competing needs, that a guest wants a permanent place. The player enters Encounter to address it, and returns to Colony with the outcome visible as changed world state — a structure reinforced, labour diverted, rooms closed, a household displaced, a citizen injured, an event in the Record.

Field play reveals or approaches a resource, a stranger, a predator, shelter, a venue. The player enters Encounter to interact with it, and Field resumes with changed cargo, condition, relationships, knowledge, route information, party composition, time and risk.

## V7-6 — The threshold rule: information stays native · **SETTLED**
*§10, §26. Protects the weight of the transition.*

Not every alert becomes an Encounter. Routine state remains in the Register that produced it — pantry level changed, rain started, a work assignment finished, a routine repair consumed material, a route is slower.

A situation earns Encounter when it carries some combination of **a genuine choice · named or socially meaningful participation · competing needs · uncertain outcome · opportunity or danger · persistent consequence · a reason to stop surveying and attend.**

> **Information remains native. Consequential interaction becomes Encounter.**

**The fixed camera is itself the signal:** this moment deserves attention and may be remembered. Spend it too often and it stops meaning anything — the same restraint §10 already applies to cards (*"these are events, not nags"*).

# GROUP B — WHAT ENCOUNTER NOW HOLDS

## V7-7 — Choice-Event Card is the content object; Encounter is its manifestation · **SETTLED**
*§10. Strengthens rather than replaces.*

The **Choice Event** is the authored packet. The **Encounter** is how the player meets it. A card no longer necessarily appears as a literal card laid over Colony or Field.

A packet carries: trigger conditions · encounter classification · origin Register · scene location · required backdrop · relevant world objects · initiating group · counterpart or environmental condition · stakes · available approaches · requirements and costs · group outcomes · individual consequences · duration and escalation · Chronicle language · return-state changes.

Runtime: **TRIGGER → SITUATION OPENS → ENCOUNTER VIEW → INTERACTION → RESOLUTION → WORLD-STATE CHANGE → RECORD.**

§10's existing lifecycle — open, stay open, close, feed the Record — survives intact and is now the same lifecycle an Encounter has.

## V7-8 — An Encounter is a bounded *scene*, not a bounded *resolution* · **SETTLED**
*§10, §26. Repairs a contradiction inside the thread itself.*

The thread defines an Encounter as bounded and then permits it to be left unresolved — enter the drain failure, learn that two Rigid Scrap are needed, leave, gather, return. **Both are wanted. The word doing the work is *scene*.**

- **Open** — the situation becomes known and is physically marked in the settlement.
- **Remain open** — it persists and may escalate.
- **Close** — action, inaction or elapsed time resolves it.
- **Record** — the result enters colony memory when significant.

**Scoped to Colony-origin Encounters.** A contested node in the field is not left open and returned to later; the party is standing in it. §26's four-round cap is untouched.

## V7-9 — Participant representation scales with encounter class · **SETTLED**
*§26, §33. The most useful thing the thread produces after V7-2.*

A Colony Encounter should not present three named portraits merely because expedition scenes do.

| Scale | The subject is | Named citizens appear? |
|---|---|---|
| **Whole-colony** | available labour, materials, structural resilience, threatened rooms, time before escalation | No |
| **Standing role** | one role-holder the colony has entrusted | The role-holder |
| **Household or group** | a family, room, nursery or work group | Those affected |
| **Personal incident** | a citizen trapped, injured, responsible or uniquely qualified | Yes — as character-specific as an expedition |

> **Named citizens appear because the situation makes them relevant, not because the interface requires a fixed party lineup.**

Expedition Encounters keep the named strip without argument: those exact citizens chose to leave, their skills set the options, their equipment is specific, and individual consequence attaches to them.

## V7-10 — Encounter composition is a partition, not two hostile parties · **SETTLED**
*§26.2, §26.3, §34.*

The left/right partition reads as combat by default and should be stated not to be. The two sides are **the elements being brought into consequential relationship**.

Party ↔ raccoon claiming berries · rabbits ↔ rat band at a wreck · party ↔ potential recruit · party ↔ broker · squirrels ↔ hawk and available cover · **colony labour and materials ↔ heavy winds** · **colony web ↔ road work** · **returning expedition ↔ receiving colony**.

A resource, a damaged structure, a weather condition or a social need occupies the counterpart position as readily as another animal. This is the visual expression of §26.2's *"an encounter is not automatically a fight."*

## V7-11 — Expedition Launch is an Encounter View · **SETTLED**
*§28.2, §33.4. Reframes; adds no system.*

The Launcher is **not unit deployment.** Citizens are not inventory transferred from a base into a mission.

> **Citizens choose to leave their home in order to sustain or grow it. They leave because they belong.**

The colony generates a need — food, materials, knowledge, medicine, contact, a safer route, an outpost, a guest relationship. Citizens may volunteer, decline, request different preparation, insist on accompanying someone, refuse a route because of prior trauma, or stay because of obligations at home.

This is compatible with §28.2 as written — *"the question should feel like who do I trust with this"* — and gives it a scene. §28.2's information prohibitions are unchanged: no contest disclosure, no odds, exactly one equipment decision per citizen.

**The colony is visible behind them. The party faces outward.**

## V7-12 — Homecoming is an Encounter View — **and it is new scope** · **OPEN**
*§22.3, §24.2, §5.1. The one place the thread proposes gameplay rather than framing.*

Canon ends an expedition on a **Rating** and a **Report and Share** debrief (§22.3) — a screen, not a scene. §5.1's step six says the domestic loop *"absorbs the expedition's gains and consequences into the physical town,"* and nothing currently makes that playable.

The thread makes reception an active Encounter at the same threshold as Launch, with real decisions: where injured citizens receive care · whether they go home, to a healing place, or to observation · where food, scrap, tools and artifacts are placed · whether resources are consumed or reserved · where a guest stays · how knowledge updates maps · who rests · whether the run earned a Distinction or an After-name · how losses enter the Chronicle.

The emotional order is stated well and is worth keeping verbatim:

> **recognition → relief → assessment → care → allocation → remembrance** — not *crossing complete → inventory screen.*

**Take it, but log it as scope.** This is a new decision surface, not a reframing, and it should be costed as one rather than admitted quietly under V7-2. The mirrored composition — outward at Launch, inward at Homecoming, same camera axis, an empty position signifying absence — is the cheapest strong image in the proposal.

**Ruling needed:** does Homecoming's allocation replace the automatic absorption in §5.1, or sit on top of it for significant returns only? A Homecoming scene after every Margin Raid will wear out faster than the Encounter transition can afford (V7-6).

## V7-13 — The expedition circle, restated · **SETTLED**
*§5.3, §24, §25, §26.*

> COLONY — a need emerges through domestic life
> **ENCOUNTER · LAUNCH** — named citizens choose to form an expedition
> CROSSING — the party accepts bodily exposure
> FIELD — the party explores the Reach
> **ENCOUNTER(S)** — the party interacts with resources, beings, opportunities, dangers
> FIELD — the party navigates home carrying what occurred
> CROSSING — the changed party attempts the final passage
> **ENCOUNTER · HOMECOMING** — the colony receives citizens, goods, injuries, guests, knowledge and absences
> COLONY — domestic life resumes in a materially and historically altered home

Launch and Homecoming are **matched brackets**, and the reciprocity is the point: at Launch, citizens commit themselves to the colony's future; at Homecoming, the colony accepts responsibility for what that commitment produced and cost.

# GROUP C — WHAT THIS BREAKS, AND THE RULINGS THAT REPAIR IT

*The prototypes surfaced four collisions with settled canon. Three have clean repairs; one needs a ruling.*

## V7-14 — The five Approaches survive as taxonomy; the option strip carries surface labels · **SETTLED**
*§26.2, §26.3. The most consequential ruling in this log.*

The prototypes show option strips that are not the five Approaches — *Trade* alongside Contest and Parley; *Freeze Low, Break for Cover, Scatter, Shield Supplies, Yield Ground* against a hawk; *Reinforce Drain, Move Stores, Evacuate Nursery, Call Neighbours, Wait It Out* against rising water. **The writing is better than the canonical labels.** §26.2 nonetheless folds trickery into Parley on the explicit grounds that a separate Approach *"would only fragment the set."*

**Resolution: keep five, and make the surface labels legal.**

**The five Approaches are the resolution taxonomy.** The Encounter View shows a per-encounter **surface label** for each available Approach, authored in the Choice Event packet, mapped to exactly one of the five.

| Surface label | Resolves as |
|---|---|
| Freeze Low · Break for Cover · Scatter | **Evade** |
| Trade · Offer Food · Ask Skill · Ask Directions | **Parley** |
| Shield Supplies · Yield Ground · Let Them Take It | **Yield** |
| Brace the Span · Reinforce Drain · Contest | **Contest** |
| Wait It Out · Stand Down · Leave Undisturbed | **Withdraw** |

**Nothing downstream changes.** §26.3's Presence-per-Approach still applies, because the label maps to an Approach before Presence is read. §26.4's resolution is untouched. What the player learns is the situation's own vocabulary rather than a menu of five nouns repeated three hundred times.

**Two constraints hold.** Withdraw and Yield are never removed (§26.2), so **every strip carries a label mapping to each of them**, however it is worded. And the mapping is authored, never inferred — a packet with an unmapped label is a content error.

> **Note:** *Evade* does not appear by name anywhere in the prototype set, and appears as a surface label in only one. Whether that is authoring drift or a real signal that Evade is rarely available at resource nodes is worth checking against §27.1.

## V7-15 — Road Work's five-option scene is Telegraph or Persistence, never Onset · **SETTLED**
*§11.2, §26.9. A direct contradiction, cheaply repaired.*

§11.2: Onset is *"a group-layer resolution for any citizens caught present — a single round, no Approach, no Turn. Citizens are exposed by circumstance rather than by choice."* §26.9 repeats it. The prototype offers five options during Road Work.

**The scene shown is not the Onset.** It is the **Telegraph** — the colony reading survey marks, vibration and dust and deciding what to secure — or the **Persistence** window, where the player's counterplay is the repair system.

**Ruled: Road Work supports Encounter Views at Telegraph and Persistence, and none at Onset.** Onset stays choiceless, which is the whole of its horror and the reason §34.2 keeps the machinery unoperated. Any Road Work Encounter View must name its phase in the header.

The same test applies to every environmental Colony Encounter: **if the player is being offered a choice, the event has not struck yet, or it is still running.** The moment of the blow is never an Encounter.

## V7-16 — No citizen numerals, and no new group meters · **SETTLED**
*§17, §20, §33.3, Appendix D, Pillar 2.3.*

The prototypes show Health and Spirit as bar meters on every participant strip, and the Metropolis plate shows explicit numerators — *Health 32/32, Spirit 24/24.*

§17: *"Exact values remain hidden. Players see descriptive bands, clear consequences, and history rather than raw ratings."* §20: *"A number is only shown where the underlying stat is already public — colony-level carry and capacity figures, never a citizen's hidden aptitude."* §33.3 and Appendix D say the same. `200 book/Contents.md` briefs the Character Record plate as *descriptive bands, never pips.*

**Ruled, in three parts:**

1. **Citizen condition displays as a descriptive band** (Appendix D), never as a bar and never as a numeral.
2. **Colony-scale quantities may display as figures**, because they are already public under Pillar 2.3 — available labour, Flexible Scrap, threatened households, builder capacity, queue position. The Colony Encounter strips are correct in kind and wrong only where they show citizen state.
3. **Every group meter must be declared as display or as state.** *Party Unity, Community Resolve, Party Alarm, Party Calm, Negotiation Posture, Harvest Outlook* appear across the prototypes. §26 has exactly one group quantity — Party Score against Presence — and it is hidden. **Any of these that is a new tracked number dies under §2.6.** Any that is a descriptive readout of existing state may live, named as such.

## V7-17 — Spirit is refused · **SETTLED**
*§2.6, §19.1, §19.2.*

**Spirit** appears as a second per-citizen meter throughout the prototypes. It is a new stat, and canon already refuses it twice: §2.6 — *do not add a new stat when an existing system can express the same decision* — and §19.2 — ***"Fear is a core system, not a cosmetic morale debuff."***

Everything Spirit is reaching for already exists: the **harm ladder**'s Shaken rung, **fear memories** tied to a species or trigger, **grief** from a Hearth loss, and the **stress threshold** that fires tharn. Those are conditions and histories, not a bar that fills.

**Ruled: no Spirit.** Where a prototype shows one, the citizen carries a named condition instead — *Shaken*, *carrying a fear of the road*, *grieving* — which reads better and costs nothing.

## V7-18 — Positioning language in Encounter · **OPEN**
*§26.8. Needs a ruling before the class is authored further.*

§26.8 is a canon prohibition: **no positioning, facing, turn order, initiative or targeting.** *"The party is one body on the front end; individuality lives on the back end only."*

The surprise-predator prototype labels *Sparse Cover, Low Bramble, Shadow Pass, Open Grass* on the scene and offers *Scatter* and *Break for Cover*. Read one way this is flavour on a single group Evade roll. Read the other way it is tactical geography, and the picture currently teaches the second reading.

**Two legal answers, and the choice matters:**

- **(a) Cosmetic.** Cover labels are scene description with no mechanical referent; Scatter and Break for Cover both resolve as Evade at the same Presence. §26.8 is untouched. *Recommended.*
- **(b) Terrain modifies Presence.** Cover becomes a per-Approach modifier authored in the packet. This does **not** breach §26.8 — it adds no positioning the player controls — but it does add a term to §26.4's resolution and needs writing there.

What is not legal is the player choosing *where* a citizen stands.

## V7-19 — Base Defense is a Colony Encounter View · **SETTLED**
*§27.4, §26.9.*

§27.4 already says Base Defense *"resolves through the full encounter frame with the colony as the party."* Under V7-2 that sentence has a name: **Base Defense is a Colony-origin Encounter View**, and it is the existing precedent for everything in V7-9. It should be authored alongside Heavy Winds and Road Work rather than treated as a separate subsystem.

# GROUP D — THE PRINCIPLE WORTH PROMOTING

## V7-20 — Facts belong to world state, not to a Register · **SETTLED — proposed for Part I**
*§2.6, Pillar 2.6. The thread's best sentence, and it does not currently exist in canon.*

> **Important facts belong to world state, not to a Register. A Register displays or acts upon those facts; it never creates a parallel version of them.**

One citizen record. One wound. One relationship. One equipment object. One cargo state. One route. One resource inventory. One expedition record. One settlement history.

**Different Registers expose different consequences of the same fact.** One injured leg produces reduced work capacity in Colony, slower movement in Field, a narrower window in Crossing, altered options in Encounter, and a need for care at Homecoming. One piece of cargo is selected at Launch, burdensome in Crossing, carried through Field, offered or protected in Encounter, assigned at Homecoming, and visible in Colony storage afterward.

**This is a stronger statement of Pillar 2.6 than Pillar 2.6 makes**, and it is the sentence that makes four visually ambitious Registers affordable. It belongs in Part I as a clause of 2.6, not in a thread.

Its operational form, for every proposed feature:

> **Is this a new system, or a new situation produced by existing systems in another Register?**

| Good expansion | Risky expansion |
|---|---|
| Winter changes Crossing windows and Field travel | A separate surgery minigame |
| A relationship changes who volunteers at Launch | Unique dialogue rules per Guest species |
| An injury changes Homecoming care | A new inventory system used only at Homecoming |
| Road Work creates a Colony Encounter through existing structure, labour and event systems | Separate combat engines for Field and Encounter |
| A Special Artifact creates new Encounter options | Different wound systems in each Register |

## V7-21 — Encounter backdrops derive from known Field and Colony locations · **SETTLED**
*§34, §36. Promote from production suggestion to canon.*

Four Registers are mechanically coherent and visually expensive, and Encounter is the expensive one — it wants large quantities of detailed fixed scenes. **Mechanical restraint does not automatically produce production restraint.**

The mitigation that matters: **an Encounter backdrop is a rendering of a place the player already knows from Field or Colony**, re-lit and re-populated by time, weather, season and state. This is §3.5's spatial-fidelity constraint doing double duty — it was written so the player's mental map holds, and it also means the Encounter class draws on a library that already had to exist.

The rest of the bounding is per-Register and worth stating: constrained camera increments in Colony · lane-and-window movement in Crossing · node- or route-linked traversal in Field · fixed scenes with pose variants and modular UI in Encounter.

> **The Registers should feel categorically different without each becoming a feature-complete standalone genre.**

# OPEN QUESTIONS

### V7-22 — The whole prototype set is one beat, and it is not the hard one

All eleven realized plates show the **Situation** beat: the scene described, the Approaches shown, nobody having acted yet. **Not one shows a Turn.** No close round, no exposure resolving, no aftermath.

That is half of §26.1, and it is the half the fixed camera makes difficult. §19.2 requires that when a citizen goes tharn, *"the frame centres on them by name, and the next Turn is forced and scoped to the tharn: it presents the party members who could go, and the player chooses one."* A fixed wide scene with a six-slot option strip and a three-portrait footer is not obviously able to do that.

**The chassis is proven for the entry choice and untested for the thing the entry choice exists to produce.** Before any further Encounter content is authored, one encounter should be drawn in three states — Approach, Turn, and a tharn forcing *who goes back for her* — on the same backdrop.

### V7-23 — Can a Colony Encounter produce a Distinction?

A latent contradiction that Colony Encounters make load-bearing.

§19.3 scopes Distinctions **entirely within the expedition-only rule**: *"a citizen who never expeditions again earns no further Distinctions."* §26.5 says individual resolution fires *"where genuine danger is present — a contested node, Base Defense, Road Work's onset."* **Two of those three are at home.**

So canon already resolves individual consequence at the colony, and already forbids the good tail there. A citizen who goes into a flooding culvert to move the winter stores is at high exposure by every definition §26.5 gives, and cannot be recognised for it.

Three possible rulings, and the third is probably right:

1. Hold the line — Distinctions are expedition-only, and domestic courage is recorded in the Chronicle without a mechanical mark.
2. Open it — high exposure grants both tails wherever it occurs, and §19.3's expedition clause is deleted.
3. **Split it** — Colony Encounters can produce **Maiming and fear memories** (they already do, via Road Work onset) but not **Distinctions**, and the asymmetry is stated rather than accidental. Harsh, thematically defensible, and it keeps the After-name earned in the field.

### V7-24 — Does every Encounter earn a Rating?

§22.3's four Ratings — Legendary, Successful, Hard-Earned, Failed — are derived from the encounter model and written into the Chronicle and every participant's Tale. **They are written as expedition grades.** Whether a Heavy Winds Encounter, a Launch, or a Homecoming earns one is undefined, and grading a Homecoming feels wrong on its face.

Provisional answer: **Ratings attach to the expedition, not to each Encounter within it**, and Colony Encounters write a Chronicle entry without a grade. Needs confirming, because §22.3 currently reads as though every resolved encounter produces one.

### V7-25 — How often is Homecoming allowed to fire?

See V7-12. If Launch and Homecoming bracket every Margin Raid, the bracket becomes the routine and V7-6's threshold rule is violated by the two Views that most depend on weight.

### V7-26 — Does an outpost support Colony Register?

V7-1's table lists outposts as *"Field · Encounter · potentially limited Colony functions."* §29.2 keeps an outpost to a light footprint drawn from a dedicated set, never the Home catalogue, and §29.3 stations citizens there. Whether a stationed citizen's outpost is ever *dwelt in* — Colony Register, at reduced scope — or only ever visited, is unresolved and affects what §29 has to render.

# DOWNSTREAM — the prototype plates

`300 art/047 encounters`, thirteen plates. **The chassis is sound and the plates are cheap to remake once the rulings above are settled** — one layout, swapped content. Logged here so nothing is lost, not as a work order.

**Canon breaks, by ruling:**

| | Plates affected | Ruling |
|---|---|---|

| Explicit numerals — *Health 32/32, Spirit 24/24* | Rest-Stop Metropolis | V7-16 |

| *Trade* shown as a sixth Approach | Contested Resource ×2, Metropolis | V7-14 |

| Five options offered during Road Work | Road Work | V7-15 — relabel as Telegraph |
| Cover labels and Scatter / Break for Cover | Bird Threat | V7-18 — awaiting ruling |

**Roster drift.** The colonies are right — Rushbottom for the mice, Morningside for the squirrels, and Bramble / Twig / Sharpnose for the rabbits is exactly `story_colonies.md`. The names inside them are not. **Pinecap, Needle, Willow, Sedge, Reed, Ash, Pinehook, Mossglove, Bristle, Rivet, Cinder, Hook, Mossback** are not in any seeded bank. **Cobb and Pip are correct.** And **Bramble is used for a rabbit and a mouse on different plates** — a collision that has to go whichever way the rest is fixed. V5-63 requires every worked example to draw on a story colony; these should be re-cast from the banks in `story_colonies.md` rather than invented again.

**What the plates get right, and should be preserved through any remake:**

- **The advisor footer speaks in a named citizen's voice** — *Sedge advises: Brace ladders first. A fall from the upper reach could cost more than timber.* · *Reed says: Bring them in. Count what returned. Then count what it cost.* This is the single best thing in the set. §26 has no such device and should acquire one. It is V7-9 made audible: the advice comes from the animal the colony trusted with that work.
- **Colony Encounters swap the portrait strip for a colony-state strip** — the clearest possible demonstration of V7-9, and it is already drawn.
- **Launch and Homecoming share a camera axis**, party facing out then in, formation broken on return. V7-12's argument in one comparison.
- **Six participants and six options on the Uncontested Resource plate** — the strip scales to a mouse party without redesign, which is the §4.4 body-unit asymmetry surviving contact with the interface.
- **Environmental labels are anchored into the scene** rather than collected into a legend, which is Pillar 2.3's flavour-plus-function pattern applied to a rendered image.

**Not yet drawn, and needed:** a Turn state · a tharn state · Base Defense · an Encounter left open and returned to.

# GROUP E — THE TWIN MODES, THE GRAMMARS, AND THE SEQUENCE

## V7-27 — The Twin Modes: Home and Away · **SETTLED**
*§1, §3, §5, §24. A layer above the four Registers.*

MEDIAN has **two Modes** and, inside them, the four Registers.

| | **HOME** | **AWAY** |
|---|---|---|
| Registers | Colony · *Encounter* | Crossing · Field · *Encounter* |
| Temperature | warm, close, lit | cold, wide, loud |
| Time | slow, lingerable | committed |
| Risk | rare, telegraphed, present-tense | constant, ambient, immediate |
| What accumulates | belonging, history, names | knowledge, material, scars |

**Encounter is the edge of the coin** — the one Register facing both ways, which is why Launch and Homecoming live inside it.

**The test is not which Register is on screen. It is: has anyone crossed?** A low-stakes forage on the Home Median's own Field layer (§25.4) is still Home; the party has not left. That is precisely what §5.2 wants — *"the first real expedition reads as the same activity, across the road."*

**Why these words.** §24.1 already writes it: *"The standard transverse sequence runs **Home → Away**."* Plain, symmetrical, one syllable each, and — unlike Register, Presence, Approach and body-unit — this is a term the animals themselves would use. *Three are away* is a sentence a colony says with its stomach; *Exploration mode* is a sentence a menu says.

**Rejected: Domestic / Exploration.** *Exploration* over-claims. §29.1 clears a Reach's fog permanently once an outpost completes, so most of the late campaign's away-play involves no exploring at all. The pair is also not parallel — a quality against an activity.

***Inward* and *outward* remain available as the descriptive gloss** where the attention framing is wanted, which is the framing §3 uses for Registers.

## V7-28 — Two Encounter grammars · **SETTLED**
*§26.1, §26.2, §26.3, §27.1. Supersedes the "third state" proposed under V7-15.*

Canon half-states this already: §26.1's beat table gives **Uncontested** its own row with **no rounds and no Turns** — the frame opens, the node is described, *"it may resolve at once."* The Approach ladder was never meant to run there.

> **The test: is there a Presence — something that must be overcome, got around, bought off, or left?**

| | **CONTESTED** | **UNCONTESTED** |
|---|---|---|
| Presence | yes — animal **or environmental** | none |
| The question | *how do we meet this?* | *what do we take?* |
| Options | the fixed five, greyed (V7-29) | **Take · Study · Mark for Return · Leave Undisturbed** |
| Never removable | Yield · Withdraw | Leave Undisturbed |
| Structure | 1–4 rounds, Turns, Presence per Approach | resolves at once, or in one round |
| Exposure | the full model (V7-23) | none |
| Covers | rats at a wreck · raccoon at a bush · hawk · weasels in a culvert · **Heavy Winds** · **Road Work** · Base Defense · Ledger the Broker · a Guest candidate | wildflower field · an unclaimed cache · a windfall |

**Environmental hazards are contested, and canon already said so.** §11.2 describes Road Work's machinery as *"a distinct environmental-hazard antagonist type — something to evade or shelter from, never negotiated with or fought."* Two of the five verbs, in one sentence, and the word *antagonist*.

A greyed Parley does not disqualify an encounter from being contested. §26.2 gives the precedent directly: *"Weasels holding a culvert do not Parley."*

**Ledger and a Guest candidate are contested**, not uncontested. He trades on his own floor; she can say no, and §27.2 gates recruitment on *"the animal's own check."* Both are Parley-shaped with Contest and Evade grey — which makes §30.2's promise literal: *"a player who has spent forty hours choosing Contest and Evade arrives somewhere that rewards neither."*

**Uncontested is therefore the quiet half of the Field**, not a broad second system — exactly as §27.1 implies, since whether a node is contested at all varies by biome, outpost status, season and roll.

**The uncontested spine has real stakes** because carry is finite (§14) and nodes are renewable (§7.4). Seeds *or* stems, not both. A field stripped comes back slower. **Mark for Return** is a scent mark — animal behaviour and route memory — and **Leave Undisturbed** stops being a null option and becomes husbandry.

## V7-29 — The option strip: the fixed five · **SETTLED**
*§26.2, §26.3. Replaces V7-14 entirely.*

**The strip is always the five Approaches, always in canonical order, greyed when unavailable.**

> **CONTEST · EVADE · PARLEY · YIELD · WITHDRAW**

That order is §26.2's own, and it is an exposure gradient running from commitment to disengagement. It puts the two that can never be removed at the right-hand end, so **the exits are always visible and always in the same place** — which matters most in an ambush.

**Each box carries its base name plus a short line describing what that Approach *is here*.** The name teaches the taxonomy; the line supplies the situation.

**Grey, never hide.** Greying teaches the world in a way hiding cannot:

> **PARLEY** — *A hawk will not be spoken with.*
> **PARLEY** — *The wind is not listening.*
> **CONTEST** — *There is nothing here to fight.*
> **EVADE** — *There is no slipping past what is already being watched.*

That last one explains the plates: **Evade is available when the Presence is in your way or coming at you, and not when it is sitting on the thing you want.** You cannot sneak a wrecked car out from under four rats. The art was right; the strip explains it.

**And it makes Guests legible for the first time.** §21.1's premise — *"Guests are option-openers, not stat sticks"* — is currently invisible. Under a fixed strip, recruiting Rasp **lights Parley in encounters where it was grey**, and the raccoon lights Contest at a sealed container. Affinities light existing slots; they never add one.

**The editorial rule for the description line: say what the action *is*, never how likely it is to work.** §26.2's information rule holds — availability is shown, difficulty is not.

| Legal | Not legal |
|---|---|
| *Break for the bramble line. Quick, and loud.* | *Likely to work.* |
| *Give the rat band the wire and keep the rest.* | *Safe option.* |

**Trade folds into Parley**, on the identical argument §26.2 used for trickery: offering value to move someone's position is a social act, and *"a separate Approach for it would only fragment the set."*

**Five slots, not six.** The layout guide's sixth will always fill, and whatever fills it becomes a de facto sixth Approach. *Trade* is Parley. *Demand* is Parley. *Observe* is a Turn, or nothing.

**The strip audits the content.** Applied to the existing plates it immediately caught: *Secure Coverings* and *Reinforce Ladders* are both **Contest**, and merge · *Call Community Work* is not an Approach but a **Press**, and belongs on the Turn · *Shield Supplies* is a **Turn** action and may want a permanent place in §26.4's set · Heavy Winds had **no Yield at all**, and *let the awnings go and save the stores* is the best decision on that card.

**Within the weather-hazard class, Contest availability is the severity dial.** Heavy Winds: Contest lit — you can hold this. Road Work Onset: Contest grey — you cannot. One difference, and the player knows how bad it is before reading a word.

**Road Work Onset's strip**, for the record:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **CONTEST** | grey | *Nothing here can be held against it.* |
| **EVADE** | lit | *Get clear of the cut. Now, and by the shortest way.* |
| **PARLEY** | grey | *There is no one to speak to.* |
| **YIELD** | lit | *Leave the stores. Leave the tools. Take the young and go.* |
| **WITHDRAW** | lit | *Abandon the ground entirely.* |

**Three lit boxes and none of them a victory.** The horror is not an empty strip; it is a full one on which every option is a choice of what to lose. The greyed Parley is §34.2's argument in five words — *an operator invites appeal; the absence of one is the horror.*

**Still live:** §11.2 and §26.9 both read *"a single round, no Approach, no Turn"* for Onset. An Onset with a five-box strip supersedes that clause.

## V7-30 — The Staging Post is improvised ground, not a building · **SETTLED**
*§16.1, §20, §24.1, §28.1, §29.2, §32.*

Its three jobs come apart.

**The mechanical gate goes.** §28.1 and §29.2 make a Staging Post the permit for a Held Reach to run Margin Raids. That is a checkbox, not a decision — raiding is what an outpost is for. §29.4 already puts the real investment in the outbuildings.

**The Tool storage does not need it.** §20's operative rule is that Tools are a standing colony-level assignment, never picked at the Launcher. Tools live in colony stock.

**The threshold stays, as ground.**

> **A Staging Post is improvised and impermanent** — the ground at a Reach's edge, lightly prepared in species-specific ways where a party arrives to cross. Every colony has one because every colony has an edge. Every Held Reach has one for the same reason.

If the eventual Crossing mechanic has a preparation step, **it happens here**, on both sides.

**Species flavour survives at no cost** — a scrape at the hedge line, a launch branch, a pipe mouth. Three names for the same ground.

**The Margin-side post reads worse than the home-side one.** The Margin is never buildable under any circumstance (§7.4), and on the return the party is tired and laden. Same vocabulary, worse execution.

**Edits:** remove from §16.1's structure families · remove the outpost permit from §28.1 and §29.2 · remove from §32's Tier I capability list · rewrite §20's Tools line. **Keep §20's sentence** — *"where citizens stop being at home"* — which is the best thing about the concept.

## V7-31 — The expedition sequence · **SETTLED**
*§3.5, §7.1, §24, §25.4, §28. Makes V7-13's circle concrete.*

The first thing that shows all four Registers and both Modes in one unbroken chain, with no load screen anywhere (§3.5).

> **COLONY** → **LAUNCH** *(Encounter — Mode shifts to Away)* → **FIELD** *(Home Median)* → **CROSSING** → **FIELD** *(Margin)* → *nodes and cards* → **return** → **HOMECOMING** *(Encounter — Mode shifts to Home)* → **COLONY**

**Outbound, in full.** Launch resolves not back into Colony but into **Field Mode on the Home Median** — an overhead map of the player's own Reach. The party moves as **one body** (§26.3) up the strip, **passing its own sustenance nodes**, and steers to an edge. Reaching it, the viewport becomes **Crossing**, showing the improvised post. Prep, then the crossing, then **Field Mode on the Margin**.

**On the Margin**, the party moves freely, entering nodes by choice and meeting Choice Cards unbidden (V7-36). The player leaves by steering back to the edge — which is where §24.2's *"the player chooses when to head home"* actually happens.

**The return resolves on §24.2's existing distribution, now with viewport behaviour:**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **70%** | straight to **Homecoming** | the crossing elided — §24.2's *"short automatic or cinematic crossing that respects the challenge already completed"* |
| **20%** | **Field Mode on the Home Median** | across automatically, but something changed on the way. The party walks in, and Homecoming follows |
| **10%** | **Crossing**, at the Margin-side post | manual re-crossing, laden |

**The asymmetry is deliberate: outbound is played, the return is usually elided.** Ritual going out, relief coming in.

**Four things that fall out of the shape:**

- **Which edge is a decision.** §7.1 permits terrain, lane count, elevation and vegetation to differ on each side, so left and right are a real choice made before any risk is taken.
- **The 20% case produces its own dilemma.** The party walks back past the sustenance nodes it ignored that morning, now laden and possibly hurt. §14's carry limit says they probably cannot stop. *The Median feeds you; it will not make you*, standing in front of them.
- **The 20% swing must break positive sometimes.** §24.2 says it can go either way; a bonus find, not only a wound.
- **The day should visibly drain.** Staying longer hauls more and crosses home worse — Evening Rush's fumes and erratic traffic, or Night Velocity's speed and raised tharn risk (§9.2). The decision should be legible from the light.

**The outbound walk is not dead time at Tier I.** §25.4: contest on the Home Reach's Field layer *"falls with each Colony Tier."* Early on the walk to the edge can produce its own card or node; later it is a quiet stroll past your own ground, which is a good way to feel the colony maturing.

*The map object is **the party**. §28.2 and §25.1 already use the word throughout, and it should be drawn as one clustered body rather than three tokens, per §26.3.*

## V7-32 — Colony has two states: Open and Called · **SETTLED**
*§3.1, §10, §33.1.*

**Open** is the default. No clock, no queue, no pressure. Build, arrange, assign, watch, read the Records. The player can sit here for days and the game does not tap them on the shoulder — which is §3.1's existing promise, *"the only register the player can linger in without cost,"* finally funded.

**Called** is a card landing. The camera fixes, the frame closes, the situation is dealt with, and the player is returned to Open.

**The card is the only thing that ever seizes the Colony camera.** Not a Work finishing, not stores dipping, not a citizen arriving at work. Those are things the player notices. A card is a thing that stops them — which is the same signal the fixed Encounter camera carries everywhere else.

**The parallel is exact and worth stating:** in Field you traverse freely until you reach something; in Colony you potter freely until something reaches you. Same rhythm, inverted.

## V7-33 — The home never takes from you while you are looking away · **SETTLED**
*§1, §2.5, §3.1, §19.2, §27.4.*

> **MEDIAN never takes a named animal from you off-screen.**

This is already the principle behind §19.2's tharn clause — *"losing a citizen to untended tharn happens through deliberate abandonment or an impossible position, not through inattention."* Extended to the whole domestic layer.

**While the player is Away, the colony may:** advance its Works · resolve small things itself, in character, and report them warmly · **gain** things — a wanderer walks in, a kit is born · suffer inconvenience — a Work slows, a seal fails, Perishables spoil.

**While the player is Away, the colony may not:** lose a citizen or a Guest · take a Maiming · suffer a structural catastrophe.

**The mechanism is the open Encounter (V7-8).** A home crisis that could hurt someone **opens while the player is away and waits.** The colony holds the line. The player returns to a **situation, not an outcome** — escalated, more expensive than it would have been that morning, and nobody hurt yet. Then they choose, and any loss is theirs.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** §2.4 requires leaving in order to advance. A colony that gets hurt because the player left teaches them not to leave, and breaks the sanctuary/exposure/return rhythm by making sanctuary a third kind of exposure.

**And safety is not the same as warmth.** The card pool must lean warm — arrivals, occasions, windfalls, the first hard frost — or home becomes a place things happen *to*. Heavy Winds should be memorable **because most cards were not that.**

## V7-34 — Launching hands the day over · **SETTLED**
*§3, §9.1, §24.*

**Colony register closes at Launch and does not reopen until Homecoming.** The player is Away.

**Consequence, and it is the best thing the rule produces: while the player is away, the colony makes its own decisions.** A Work that goes wrong does not wait — whoever is there handles it, in character, and the player learns at Homecoming what they did.

That gives the animals left behind agency, makes leaving cost something other than bodies, and means a colony with a steady Builder and an experienced Healer genuinely fares better in the player's absence. Bounded by V7-33: they resolve it, and it does not cost a life.

## V7-35 — Ambient Guests are the warning economy · **SETTLED**
*§10, §21.2, §27.3.*

§10 forbids numeric countdowns and requires every telegraph to be *"qualitative and diegetic — a smell, a sound, a changed sky, a Guest's unease."* Under V7-32 that becomes the Ambient roster's principal job, and gives §21.2's nine animals a strategic identity they currently lack.

| Buy **warning** | Buy **capability** |
|---|---|
| **Groundhog** — approaching cards, generally | **Bee** — yield from foraged plants |
| **Songbird** — the day sky | **Firefly** — safe night activity |
| **Owl** — the night sky | **Turtle** — stores survive a single loss |
| **Toad** — wet ground, water coming | **Bat** — structures resist decay |
| **Mole** — soil and structure | |

**Four slots at maximum Tier against nine animals** (§27.3). So housing a Guest asks a real question: *how much surprise am I willing to live with?* A colony with a Groundhog and a Toad sees most things coming and forages worse. A colony with a Bee and a Firefly is richer, brighter, and gets hit blind.

**And the warning is an animal, not a notification.** Sump comes up out of the culvert mouth and sits on the high stone. Reel will not settle. Whistle is out of his burrow at the wrong hour.

## V7-36 — Two ways into an Encounter · **SETTLED**
*§10, §25.1, §26.*

| | **You go to it** | **It comes to you** |
|---|---|---|
| **Colony** | a Work called · a Place attended to | a **Colony card** |
| **Field** | a **node** entered | a **Field card** |
| **Crossing** | — | — |

Crossing stays outside it, per §24.1: it is an action mode with no Approaches, no Turns and no exposure.

**Field cards open the Encounter frame** rather than floating over the map. §10 already reserves the space — *"occasional random-encounter cards appear during traversal… Litter is the flagship Field card"* — and its guardrail holds: **nodes are the meal, cards are the weather.** A run averaging three cards has stopped being a place the player reads.

**Four kinds:** **Opportunity** (Litter — risk and reward are the same object, always declinable) · **Condition** (the Spume turning, wind getting up — no counterpart, changed terms) · **Ambush** (the hawk) · **Arrival** (a stranger between nodes — §18.4's expedition-encounter route for wanderers, and the kind that keeps the Field deck from being all threat).

**The card carries the terrain, and terrain sets the price, never the menu.** The party did not choose to be in open grass; the card found them there. Per V7-18, that modifies each Approach's chance of success and never the player's ability to choose it. The same Hawk card landing in the bramble line is a one-round event — which is the correct reward for having taken the wall-edge route.

**Which is how §7.4's two economies finally bite.** Road-edge is rich and keeps interrupting; wall-edge is quiet and slow. Not a route to optimise — a kind of day to choose.

# GROUP F — RANGE

## V7-37 — Home is a state, not a place · **SETTLED**
*§2.1, §3.1, §21.2, §29. Closes V7-26.*

**The Home Median carries the Home state fully. A Held Reach carries it partially. Nothing else carries it at all.**

An earlier reading treated an outpost as a node visited in Field, which was wrong twice over: §29.2 permits **a full Hearth to relocate** to a two-slot outpost, and §21.2 describes Ambient Guests as *"visible residents with routines and relationships."* Canon already assumes somebody **lives** there, and a node has no residents.

**V7-26 answered: yes, an outpost supports Colony register, at reduced scope.**

| | **Home Median** | **Held Reach** |
|---|---|---|
| Colony register, Open state | full | **yes, small** |
| Practices | all seven | **Store · Watch · Rest · Care** |
| Works | all four kinds, many hands | tiny — Keeping and Tending only, one or two hands. **Making requires a visiting party** *(v0.5)* |
| Species grammar | operating | **never** |
| Cards | the full domestic deck | a thin deck, telegraphed by the outpost's Guest |
| Story Circle · Records · Nursery | yes | **no** |

**Care is the addition worth arguing for.** §29.2 currently offers resource-trickle and predator-reduction outbuildings, which is thin and mercenary. A waystation's real job is **shelter and mending** — a party hurt three Reaches out wants somewhere to stop rather than a forced march home. Care at a low rung is what makes an outpost an island rather than a depot.

**And the Ambient Guest slot makes sense again**, because it warns the animals who live there (V7-35). Whether the frontier is observant or productive becomes a real question at a place the player cannot watch.

### Why an outpost still never becomes a replacement town

§2.1 and §29's opening both insist the Home Median stays the centre. Three things hold that line, and none is an arbitrary cap:

- **The species grammar cannot operate.** One or two citizens cannot cluster, ring a green, or loop. *(v0.5, but the population cap that causes it is canon today.)*
- **No Nursery, no Records, no Circle.** It cannot grow people and it cannot accumulate history.
- **Population still counts against the ceiling** (§29.3, unchanged). Every animal out there is one not at home.

> **An outpost gives you the roof. It never gives you the village.**

### The register it has that the Home Median does not

**An outpost is cozy and lonely at the same time.** A lit room with two animals in it, in a Concrete Trench four Reaches from everyone they know, with a groundhog at the perimeter. Warm, and not a community. That is a specific and unusual feeling, and it is the reason to build outposts as places rather than as flags.

**The biome does the work for free.** §7.5 lets adjacent Reaches differ sharply, so every outpost is a lit room in a different kind of place — and §15 already gives each biome its own wear: the Culvert Garden rots timber, the Concrete Trench bakes and cracks, the Thin Grass Ribbon scours. **Every outpost maintains differently because of where it is.**

### Species asymmetry at outpost scale · **v0.5**

Each species meets the frontier differently, and it falls out of the domestic grammar rather than needing rules:

- **Mouse** — an outpost is *a room that touches nothing*, the one thing a mouse colony never builds. And mouse tharn is **the certainty of not being found**. A stationed mouse lives inside the species' specific fear.
- **Rabbit** — a home fronting onto nothing. Rabbits draw strength from Company and a stationed rabbit has none, which is why §29.2's **relocating Hearth** is the rabbit answer.
- **Squirrel** — barely a change. *A node with one span to it* is already their word for an outpost.

> **Flag: squirrels are the risk case for §29's "never a replacement town."** Theirs is the only grammar that scales down gracefully, so a squirrel campaign is the one where the network can quietly become the thing and the home becomes a node among nodes. Morningside's Tier IV — *"the canopy runs unbroken over two Reaches"* — is that state, and story_colonies already names the feeling: *Rowan is proved right in a way that is hard to celebrate.*

## V7-38 — The leg is the unit of Away focus · **SETTLED**
*Amends V7-34. §9.1, §24, §29.*

> **Focus stays with the party while they are in motion or exposed. When they stop somewhere safe, focus can return to the colony. The citizens stay where they are.**

- A **Margin Raid is one leg** — out, across, work the Margin, back. Focus never leaves, and V7-34 holds exactly as written.
- A **Special Journey is many legs.** At each safe stop the day ends, attention returns to the colony, Works advance and cards land. Tomorrow the player picks the party up three Reaches out, still there, still carrying what they carried.

**V7-34's clause becomes:** *Colony register closes when the party is in motion or exposed, and reopens when they are at rest somewhere safe — home, or a Held Reach.*

**Both are Home-state, so V7-33 covers both.** Nothing is taken from the resting party while the player is at the colony, and nothing is taken from the colony while the player is out on the chain. An incident in either place **opens and waits**.

### The Stopover

**Arriving at an outpost is a reduced Homecoming.** Same grammar — uncontested, put things down, tend what is hurt — at much smaller scale, with no allocation decisions and no ceremony. The party arrives, the load goes into the store, somebody lights something, the day ends.

One system, two sizes. A Stopover is not a bridge between Modes; only Homecoming is.

## V7-39 — Outposts are what make range possible · **SETTLED**
*§28.1, §29, §31.1.*

**Without a safe stop, a day cannot end away from home.** Range is therefore capped at what a party can do and undo between dawn and dusk — one Reach out, perhaps two.

> **The corridor is only as long as your chain of places to stop.**

That is arithmetic rather than a gate, and it converts §31.1's *"reached only after a chain of outposts and relationships makes the journey survivable"* from a statement of intent into a mechanism.

**Three functions, all of them refuge, and none of them extraction:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **End the day** | the primary function, and the one everything else follows from |
| **Mend the hurt** | Care at low rung; stop rather than push home wounded |
| **Hold the load** | spoils cached here and collected on a later run |

**The third is the one previously missed.** Carry is finite — §28.2 gives party totals of 30 / 60 / 36. If a party can put goods down at Reach 4 and collect them later, **the corridor becomes a relay**, which at Rushbottom is the colony's own philosophy operating at corridor scale. *Small hands, short road.*

### Held ground is fast ground

The obvious risk is that six legs each way is a slog. **The answer is already canon and is better than a fast-travel affordance.**

§29.4: contest falls as the player invests, and *"a developed one is a place where the corridor has learned not to bother you."* So a leg through a well-held Reach is quick and quiet; a leg through wild ground is a gauntlet.

> **The player builds their own fast travel by holding ground.**

The chain to the Metropolis shortens in felt time exactly as it is earned. And story_colonies' standing OPEN item on **longitudinal transit** — a high-Tier building linking a Reach into a continuous run, permitting Relay and Cache auto-connection — is precisely the Tier IV expression of this, and now has a reason to exist.

### Decay has a floor

§15's decay against one pair of hands means outposts degrade between visits. That is the right pressure, but it needs a floor or players stop building them and the victory arc dies with them.

> **The claim is permanent. The investment is not.**

An outpost never decays below §29.4's own baseline — *"a flag in the soil and little more."* It walks back to bare, losing trickle and contest reduction, and never loses the Reach. Recovery is a Work; abandonment is not a failure state.

### An outpost incident arrives at home as a card

Per V7-33, a stationed citizen is a named animal and cannot be lost off-screen. So an outpost crisis does not resolve out there unseen. **Word comes**, as a Choice Card landing in Colony's Called state (V7-32), and answering it costs an expedition.

> **Every Reach you hold is one more place that can send for you.**

## V7-40 — §24.2 governs the crossing, not the journey home · **SETTLED**
*§24.1, §24.2, §26.4, §31.2.*

Return Logic's **70 / 20 / 10** is scoped to the **transverse crossing** — which is where canon already put it, inside §24 Crossing. It has never governed the journey back down the corridor.

**Longitudinal return is played, Reach by Reach.** Which means:

> **The haul is carried home.**

Six Reaches, laden, through whatever ground has not been made safe, under §26.4's *"a laden party is slower and more exposed"* and §24.1's *"load bears on the return as well as on encounters."*

**This reframes the Metropolis run entirely.** It is not *go there, trade, return*. It is *get there, acquire, and then begin the second half* — and the second half is harder, because you are heavy.

**And it makes the Grand Caravan the ordinary loop at full stretch** rather than a bespoke climax mode. §36.3 asks that rare set-pieces *"reskin the existing Field and Encounter registers"* rather than become new systems. The caravan qualifies without being designed for it.

## V7-41 — The shape of the corridor · **SETTLED**
*§7.5, §7.6, §28.1, §31. New canon; the corridor has never been dimensioned.*

| | Distance | Days each way | Stops needed | Minimum outposts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Ancestral home** | **3 Reaches down** | 2 | 1 | **one** |
| **The Rest-Stop Metropolis** | **6 Reaches up** | 3 | 2 | **two** |

**Working figure: a party clears two Reaches in a day.** Prototype variable, but it is what makes the staging above come out, and it should be tuned as one number rather than per-destination.

This matches story_colonies, which places the Metropolis *"five or six Reaches down the chain in one direction."*

**The teaching structure falls out of the numbers.** The ancestral home is the journey that teaches what an outpost is *for* — one stop, gated at Tier II, achievable with a single investment. **The Metropolis is the journey that requires a chain**, and by the time a player attempts it they already understand why.

**Neither is terminal.** Reaches 7, 8, 9 and beyond above; 4, 5, 6 and beyond below. The corridor is unbounded in both directions. This keeps the chain from reading as a level with two ends, and gives §31.2's *"continue indefinitely"* somewhere to go after the caravan.

### Up is the future; down is the past

Free, from the numbers alone, and worth stating as canon:

**Down the chain is the ruin of what was lost** — a permanent hazardous construction zone where nothing grows (§7.6, §35.1), three Reaches away, revisitable from Tier II. **Up the chain is the only city in the world.**

> **A colony can go back and look at what it lost before it can reach what it might become.**

**Naming, deferred.** §6.3 establishes that the animals hold regional-variant pairs for the things they care about most, and the two directions of the corridor will have folk names. They will not be neutral ones.

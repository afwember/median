## 1. What the device actually is

**The panel is not a new content class. It is the Records, printed.**

This matters more than it sounds. §22.2 already requires that every **CREATED VISUAL** carry a non-generative fallback, and states the fallback for the Tale in mandatory terms: *"templated prose from recorded events, no model involved."* The mockup is that fallback, designed rather than described. It introduces no new system, no new marker family that has to be justified against Pillar 2.6, and no in-world object that does not already exist — a citizen's **Tale** (§22.2) is where the moment of the After-name is written *automatically*, by canon, at §23.1.

So the argument for the whole programme is one sentence: **the book is showing the reader a page of the game's own Records, and the mechanic card beside it is the Almanac's half of the same event.** Literary view left, summary view right. That is §22's two-register split, laid out as a spread.

Two consequences follow, and both are worth taking:

- **The panel gets two headers, not one.** The mockup's kicker reads **THE TALE** — citizen scale, possessive, *Sharpnose's Tale*. Colony-scale beats take **THE CHRONICLE** — definite article, in-world name *The Remembering*. §22's styling convention already sets the grammar; the panel just obeys it. Four of the fifteen below are Chronicle panels.
- **Panels and CREATED VISUALs should be paired, not parallel.** Eight CREATED VISUALs stand in v0.4.6 (§11.2, §19.2 ×2, §22.2 ×2, §27.2, §29.4, §32). Six of them name a moment a panel below also covers. Where they coincide, the panel is the prose and the CREATED VISUAL is the image of the same beat — which is exactly the relationship canon already specifies, and it means the illustration programme does not grow.

### The mockup is canon-clean

Checked line by line against v0.4.6. Four rounds is the ceiling (§26.1, §26.8) and the rarest length; the prose's three interventions read correctly as the three Turns a four-round encounter carries. *First Distinction → After-name* is §19.3 verbatim. *Field only* is the expedition-only rule. *High exposure opens both tails* is §26.5's governing principle stated without shifting it. *Most citizens never earn one* is §23.1. The cat is a Presence and is never counted (§26.3). Nothing needs changing.

### Panel anatomy — the spec the fifteen are written to

Derived from the mockup, fixed so the class is consistent.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Running head** | `MEDIAN: <subject>` — the mechanic, not the citizen. *Distinction and After-names*, not *Sharpnose*. |
| **Kicker** | `THE TALE` or `THE CHRONICLE`. Nothing else. |
| **Headline** | The event, in the folk register. Homely and plain (§23.2). *Sharpnose at the fallen log.* Five to seven words. |
| **Prose** | Two paragraphs, **150–200 words**, past tense, in-world voice. Paragraph one is the situation and the pressure; paragraph two is the act and its consequence. The mechanic's key term lands **bolded, once, in the final sentence** and nowhere else. |
| **Card head** | Portrait, **GIVEN NAME** in caps, and where relevant an italic second line — *Born Shadepatch*, *Found at Pipe End*, *Of Rowan and Cobb's Hearth*. |
| **The rule box** | One gold band. The mechanic as a formula, not a sentence: `FIRST DISTINCTION → AFTER-NAME`. If it does not fit an arrow, it is the wrong mechanic for a panel. |
| **The four points** | Numbered 1–4, bold headline plus one or two lines. **Point 4 is always the constraint or the cost** — *Most citizens never earn one*. The device must never read as a reward brochure. |
| **Voice** | Prose is **in-world**; card is **authoritative** (Production_Plan §3.5). Never designer voice inside a panel. The panel is the one place the two registers touch, and the seam is the gutter. |

### Placement rule

A panel sits **at the section that defines the mechanic**, never at the section that merely refers to it. Where two sections share a mechanic — §19.3 and §23.1 both carry the After-name — the panel prints once, at the definition, and the other site carries a one-line callback with a folio reference. The mockup therefore belongs at **§19.3**, and §23.1 gets *"Shadepatch became Sharpnose at a fallen log in the colony's first season — see p. ●●."*

**Restraint clause.** Fifteen panels across 2,328 lines is roughly one every fifty pages of set text. That is the intended frequency and it is deliberately below the rate at which the device would become wallpaper. Three reserves are listed at §5; nothing else is promoted without cutting one.

## 3. The panels

### 1 · Front matter, The Founding Escape — *"What the cut reached"*
**Rushbottom · THE CHRONICLE · Barley, Thimble, Pip**

`ROAD WORK ≠ FOUNDING ESCAPE`

**Why here.** §6.3 and the front matter both insist the Founding Escape is *categorically not* an instance of the Mowing, and the distinction is load-bearing across §11.2, §35.1 and the whole campaign arc. It is also the single hardest thing in the document to feel rather than parse, because the reader has not yet met a Mowing to contrast it against. Chaff End is the answer: the mice survived Road Work at Road Work's own scale, for most of a season, and *were good at it* — which is precisely why the moment it stopped being Road Work is legible.

**The scene.** The survey marks went in early. **NEW** — Barley had counted them: forty-one stakes along the verge, and a colony that could name the number felt it had the measure of the thing. Chaff End went deeper, and deeper again, and read the machines the way the Second Law says to read them — fire passes, the burned ground feeds. It had a season of being right. Then the cut came through the Burrow and did not stop at the Burrow, and behind the cut came the basin, and the water stood where the nursery had been and did not go down. Three were at the far store. They came out of the pipe at the end of it and there was nothing to go back to — not damaged ground, not burned ground, **ground that would not come back**.

**Card.** 1 · **ROAD WORK PASSES** — cyclical, evadable, survivable; the Margin returns. 2 · **THE FOUNDING ESCAPE DOES NOT** — singular, permanent, and nothing grows. 3 · **THE MACHINES ARE THE SAME** — which is why Road Work frightens a colony that has seen one. 4 · **YOU ARE NEVER SHOWN IT** — dust, noise, distance; the loss is known by absence (§34.2).

> **Depends on flag C** (§4). The panel names a number and cannot be written until the sixty-one are settled.

### 2 · §6.1 The Laws of the Median — *"The morning Shadepatch was funny"*
**New Elderbank · THE TALE · Shadepatch, Bramble, Twig**

`WHAT THREE ADOLESCENTS REMEMBERED → THE COLONY'S LAW`

**Why here.** §6.1 makes two claims a systems paragraph cannot carry: that folklore is the tutorialization layer, and that this colony's version of it is *wrong in places* because the only survivors were the ones not in the lesson. The second claim pays off three times downstream — the Teacher assignment, the Metropolis's contradictions, and Nutmeg. It needs a face at the front of the book.

**The scene.** Three adolescents at a peripheral store on the last morning at Elderbank, sent out of the way. Shadepatch is doing the Teacher: the flat delivery, the pause before the second half, *the mowing is fire* — and Twig is laughing hard enough to have to sit down. **NEW** — Bramble is the one who tells her to stop, not because it is disrespectful but because he cannot remember the Fourth Law and wants to hear it done properly one more time. She does the Fourth badly and on purpose. Then the ground moves.

Closing line: she has not recited anything since, at the Circle or anywhere else — and what New Elderbank teaches its young about the Fourth Law is **what Bramble could reconstruct from a joke.**

**Card.** 1 · **FIVE LAWS, FIVE SYSTEMS** — each teaches a real mechanic. 2 · **THE TEACHER PASSES THEM ON** — the Role reduces colony-wide fear because the Laws are survival instruction. 3 · **NO SEPARATE TUTORIAL VOICE** — the first Road Work surfaces the Second Law; the first owl surfaces the Fifth. 4 · **YOURS HAVE HOLES IN THEM** — no adult survived. The Metropolis will one day correct you.

*This panel plants Shadepatch thirteen sections before panel 10 collects her. The reader meets the name, meets the anomaly, and gets the answer at §19.3 — which is the book's one long-range setup and worth protecting in layout.*

### 3 · §11.2 Road Work — *"The fifth hole"*
**New Elderbank · THE CHRONICLE · Bramble, Teasel**

`TELEGRAPH → ONSET → PERSISTENCE`

**Why here.** Road Work is a three-phase system, a card-pool modifier, and a thematic echo all at once, and §11.2 currently teaches all three in exposition. The Fifth Hole is canon's own answer (story_colonies: *dug in a season when nobody could see the need for it, and the reason the colony lived through one particular Road Work*) and it converts the section's abstract counterplay into a decision somebody made two years early and was mocked for.

**The scene.** Two beats, separated by a season break inside the panel. **Telegraph:** Bramble digging a fifth way out with four already open, and Twig asking what it is *for*. **NEW** — his answer is that the warren has four exits and they all come up inside the same forty feet, which is one accident, not four. **Onset:** the machinery comes at the Ashcross end and the four are on the wrong side of it. Teasel is in the open when it starts and does not get all the way back — a single round, no Approach offered, nobody choosing anything (§11.2, §26.9). **Persistence:** a changed soundscape, restricted runs, and the colony living out of the Fifth Hole for the length of the window.

**Card.** 1 · **NO COUNTDOWN, EVER** — survey marks, disturbed ground, a changed smell. Nothing tells you when. 2 · **ONSET IS NOT AN ENCOUNTER** — one round, no Approach, no Turn. Exposure by circumstance, not by choice. 3 · **THE WINDOW IS CAPPED** — severity is intensity, never length. Repair work shortens it. 4 · **THE MARGIN IS WIPED, AND COMES BACK** — the Second Law is describing this, and is correct.

*Teasel's forefoot is lost in this panel and valued in panel 8. Do not restate the injury there; the reader has seen it.*

### 4 · §14 Resources and the economy — *"Three days at the narrow place"*
**Rushbottom · THE TALE · Thimble, Pip, Husk**

`ONE STANDARD ITEM = TWO MICE`

**Why here.** §14's weight ladder and §4.4's body-units are the document's most arithmetic passage, and the one place a reader can decide the game is a spreadsheet. The Relay is the fix: the numbers *are* a culture. Rushbottom's whole logistics practice is a copy of one week, and that week is a scene.

**The scene.** The first season, four animals and two body-units, and a haul at the far end of the pipe that no single mouse can shift. Thimble stations herself at the narrowest point without being asked and stays there for three days, passing everything through. **NEW** — the thing that finally comes through is a coil of wire, a Standard at ten, and it takes Pip at one end and Husk at the other; halfway along Pip's grip goes and the coil drops and rolls back the way it came, and they do it again. Twice more. Nobody proposes a better method because there is not one — six carried alone is not enough and twelve carried together is.

Closing: *Small hands, short road* is not a philosophy Rushbottom arrived at. It is a description of that week, said afterwards.

**Card.** 1 · **ONE RABBIT, ONE SQUIRREL, TWO MICE** — one body-unit. Every body-unit eats ten. 2 · **CARRY RUNS 10 : 12 : 20** — a mouse pair beats a rabbit and loses to a squirrel. 3 · **IT SPLITS OR IT DROPS** — a Standard carried by two mice goes down if either does. 4 · **BODY-UNITS NEVER RESOLVE HARM** — wounds, tharn and death are always the individual animal. A rabbit colony loses a whole unit; a mouse colony loses half of one.

### 5 · §15 Construction and the Queue — *"The eleventh bridge"*
**Morningside · THE TALE · Cobb, Vane, Rowan**

`RESOURCES SUNK → DAYS FIXED → NOTHING HURRIES IT`

**Why here.** The Queue's rules are all prohibitions — no refund, no interruption, no speed-up, and the building goes offline while you work on it — and prohibitions read as arbitrary until somebody is standing in one. Cobb's Bridge is canon's own recurring object and the only structure in the corpus with a number attached to its failures.

**The scene.** The creek takes the bridge in autumn and Cobb starts the eleventh the next morning. **NEW** — Vane, who made every fastening on the tenth, will not sign off on the same span and wants four days for a different anchorage. Cobb wants three. The argument is not about materials; the materials go in on day one either way and do not come back out. They take the four. For four days the near channel is uncrossable and the far-bank caches are simply gone — not lost, unreachable, which Rowan finds worse. Nothing shortens it. On the fifth morning it is there, and Cobb walks it before he holds it, and it holds.

Closing: he has rebuilt it eleven times and expects to rebuild it a twelfth, and says so, out loud, at the Circle, which is not the same as thinking the bridge was a mistake — a distinction **Rowan has never once granted him.**

**Card.** 1 · **SPENT AT THE START, AND SUNK** — no refund, no partial recovery, no mid-build loss. 2 · **NOTHING DERAILS IT** — not Road Work, not a card, not an event. 3 · **IT GOES OFFLINE WHILE YOU BUILD IT** — the real cost of an upgrade is doing without the thing. 4 · **THERE IS NO SPEED-UP** — none exists anywhere in MEDIAN. Certainty lives here so risk can live in the field.

### 6 · §18.1 Bonds and Hearths — *"What Rowan and Cobb agreed on"*
**Morningside · THE TALE · Rowan, Cobb, Wick**

`SHARED EXPERIENCE → TRUSTED FRIEND → HEARTH`

**Why here.** §18.1 and §18.2 need a case where the bond is not a reward. Morningside's founding pair disagree about the colony's central question — reach against resilience — and are a Hearth anyway, which demonstrates that bonds are formed by events and not by compatibility, and sets up §18.2's deployability cost with something the player would actually regret losing.

**The scene.** Two moments and a consequence. The first: the eleven days of moving, and Cobb carrying Rowan's share on the ninth without discussion — **Trusted Friend**, formed the way §18.1 says, by surviving something together. The second: **NEW** — the night the near channel rose over the first bridge with both of them on the far side and no way back until light, spent under a guardrail, having the argument they have always had and getting no further with it. **Hearth**, from a second such experience. Wick is born in the third spring, of that Hearth, and is the first squirrel at Morningside who has never had to be told that a tree can be cut down.

Closing: the colony's most settled pair is also the pair it can no longer send anywhere separately.

**Card.** 1 · **TWO TIERS, ONE PATH** — a shared significant experience makes a Trusted Friend; another makes a Hearth. 2 · **NAMED, NEVER SCORED** — bonds are displayed as description. There is no affinity meter. 3 · **A HEARTH IS A FOUND FAMILY** — core species only; Trusted Friend is the tier that crosses to Guests. 4 · **AND IT COSTS YOU RANGE** — a Hearth cannot be split for solo stationing. Your most settled citizens are your least mobile.

> **Depends on flag F** (§4). Cobb's fate is open, and this panel is the reason to close it.

### 7 · §18.4 Wanderers — *"Nutmeg's Crossing"*
**New Elderbank · THE TALE · Nutmeg, Bramble**

`ARRIVES ALONE → ORDINARY CITIZEN, NO SLOT`

**Why here.** §18.4 already names the flagship case in the abstract — a wanderer who is another survivor of the Founding Escape, carrying a Law the trio remembered wrong — and then leaves it as a design possibility. Nutmeg is that case, standing, and the panel is the payoff for a piece of setup the book made in Part I.

**The scene.** Years into the campaign. She comes over the Highway alone, in the dark, which nobody at New Elderbank does and nobody thinks is possible — the Fifth Law is on her side and nothing else is. She is one of the children who were in the nursery at Elderbank on the last morning. **NEW** — the first thing she says that anybody writes down is the Fourth Law, correctly, all of it, in the form the Teacher used; and Bramble, who has been teaching it for eleven years, hears where the joke went in. He does not stop teaching. Some of what she brings is accepted and some is not, and the arguments are still running.

Closing: she costs no Guest slot, no amenability check, and one lighter residence cost, and she is a citizen of New Elderbank from the hour she arrives — **and everything the colony believed about being the only ones is wrong.**

**Card.** 1 · **THE PRIMARY EARLY GROWTH PATH** — faster than Nesting Season, and less weighted. 2 · **AN ORDINARY CITIZEN** — full standing, a Given Name, a Record from the moment of arrival. No Guest slot. 3 · **THREE WAYS IN** — an expedition encounter, a rescue, or a stranger at the median's edge as a Choice-Event Card. 4 · **EVERY ONE HAS A REASON** — the backstory is written into the Tale on arrival. That is what stops them reading as spawned units.

### 8 · §19.1 The harm ladder — *"The route they chose for Teasel"*
**New Elderbank · THE TALE · Teasel, Daisy**

`MAIMED ≠ SPENT`

**Why here.** Pillar 2.5 is the design's most-stated and least-demonstrated claim, and §19.1's own sentence — *some veterans, precisely because they have survived, become calm expedition anchors* — is the exact thing a paragraph cannot prove. story_colonies designates Teasel the book's worked demonstration. This is where it lands.

**The scene.** A Margin raid two years after the Road Work. **NEW** — the party is Teasel, Daisy, and one more, and the route was chosen the night before at the Staging Post: the wall-edge the long way round, because the road-edge scramble is not a thing Teasel does now and everybody says so out loud without it being a discussion. Daisy is young and it is her fourth run. At the node something moves in the grass and Daisy's ears go flat and stay flat, and Teasel — who is the slowest animal on the strip and cannot brace on that side — says her name and then says what it is. It is nothing. They come home with a full carry and no Rating worth writing down.

Closing: the adaptive brace on her foreleg was made by a Crafter out of a bread clip and twine, and it gives back **some** of what the machine took, and it will never give back the rest. *The mending is not the making.*

**Card.** 1 · **FOUR RUNGS** — Shaken, Wounded, Maimed, Death. Three of them before the fourth. 2 · **VISIBLE AND PERMANENT** — a maimed citizen is drawn maimed, and stays that way. 3 · **ADAPTIVE EQUIPMENT REDUCES; IT NEVER GRANTS** — it offsets a specific impairment and gives no capability she did not have. 4 · **THE CALM IS EARNED, NOT ISSUED** — the wound confers nothing. Having survived it does.

### 9 · §19.2 Fear and going tharn — *"Nobody is coming"*
**Rushbottom · THE TALE · Barley, Pip**

`EXPOSURE > THRESHOLD → THARN → FORCED WITHDRAWAL`

**Why here.** §19.2 carries two rules that need one scene: tharn's per-species flavour, which Appendix D defers and story_colonies supplies (**mouse tharn is the certainty of not being found**, and the rescue that resolves it is somebody arriving and using your name), and **Forced Withdrawal**, the rule for when there is no third body to send. Rushbottom's first season is both simultaneously — four animals, two body-units, going out anyway.

**The scene.** The worst hour of the first season. Barley and Pip are out on a two-unit run because two is what Rushbottom has, and Barley freezes at a node with a hawk's shadow somewhere above the culvert line. **NEW** — she does not run and she does not call. What she is certain of, absolutely and immediately, is that nobody knows she is here, and that the count of Rushbottom will be two and nobody will ever write her down. Her exposure climbs each round and keeps climbing. There is no third body: the Turn that would ask *who goes back for her* has nobody to offer, so it is not asked. Pip comes anyway, because there is no one else to be the party. He says **Barley** — the whole name, twice — and then gets her out, and the node is unresolved and the run is **Failed** and both of them come home.

Closing: Rushbottom's first Trusted Friend bond was made that hour, and the Long Count was started the following week, and it is not a coincidence.

**Card.** 1 · **ONE TRIGGER** — exposure exceeds the citizen's stress threshold, inside an encounter. Load and isolation feed it without rules of their own. 2 · **IT KEEPS RISING** — the tharn citizen drops cargo, stops contributing, and climbs each round until somebody resolves it. 3 · **THE NEXT TURN IS FORCED** — the game asks *who goes*, not *whether*. 4 · **UNLESS THERE IS NOBODY** — with no third body the run simply ends, Failed, and everyone comes home. *We do not leave what we can carry.*

> §19.2's **CREATED VISUAL — "Sharpnose goes tharn"** stands, unmoved, on the same spread. Rabbit tharn is the freeze in the open; mouse tharn is this. One section, two species, one trigger — which is exactly what §19.2 claims and has never shown. Recommend the image run opposite the panel rather than on it.

### 11 · §22.1 The Colony Record — *"The Long Count"*
**Rushbottom · THE CHRONICLE · Barley**

`NAMED EVENTS → ALMANAC → CHRONICLE`

**Why here.** §22 asserts that the Records are a first-class system and *not a resource* — no Knowledge currency, no Story currency, nothing farmable — and that the event feed names individuals as its atomic data spine. A reader who has played anything else will assume the Records are a menu. The Long Count is the argument that they are not: it produces nothing, it is read aloud once a year, and it takes a long time.

**The scene.** Spring at the Circle. Barley reads the sixty-one. **NEW** — she reads them in the order they lived at Chaff End rather than alphabetically, which means the reading walks the old Burrow chamber by chamber and takes the shape of a place none of the listeners has ever seen; the mice born at Rushbottom know the route through a home that no longer exists and could not tell you how. It takes most of the evening. Nobody leaves. The Almanac's line for the day says *Barley read the Long Count*, and the Chronicle's says something else, and both are true.

Closing: **the sixty-one are names and nothing else now**, and the Records are the only place they are anything at all.

**Card.** 1 · **TWO VIEWS, TWO SCALES** — *The Almanac* and *The Chronicle* for the colony; a *Likeness* and a *Tale* for each citizen. 2 · **THE FEED NAMES INDIVIDUALS, ALWAYS** — *"Nutmeg and Daisy healed Sharpnose,"* never *"two citizens performed healing."* 3 · **IT WRITES ITSELF** — the player touches three things: the colony's name, a Greatest Triumph, a Next Goal. 4 · **AND IT IS NOT A RESOURCE** — no currency, no bonus, nothing to farm. A Tale is closed when a citizen dies. It is never erased.

### 12 · §24.1 The crossing action — *"Gale went first"*
**Morningside · THE TALE · Gale**

`WAIT FOR THE GAP → THE GAP IS GIVEN, NOT OWED`

**Why here.** The Crossing is the signature mechanic and, by §24.1's own admission, the least-specified system in the document — *evocatively specified*, per Contents. That is the argument for a panel rather than against one: prose is the register the mode is currently written in, and a panel commits nothing mechanical while making the mode's *feeling* canon. Squirrels are the right species because §4.2 says they carry the greatest exposure when forced to ground, so the mode's cost is highest for them.

**The scene.** Elmwater, autumn, the Dust on the Spume and visibility down the corridor short. **NEW** — Gale is the fastest animal at Morningside and has crossed more than anyone and is the only one who has ever gone back to look at Nine Beeches, and none of that is worth anything at the kerb. Two gaps come and she takes neither. Cobb, behind her, laden, would have taken the first. The third gap is worse than the second and she takes it, because the thing she has learned that the others have not is that gaps do not improve in a queue, and the wind off the last vehicle rolls her at the centre line and she is on the lane island rather than across it, four-legged and low, waiting again.

Closing: she scouts further than anyone sanctions and she has never once lost an animal on the road, and the colony has decided those are two separate facts about her. They are not.

**Card.** 1 · **AN ACTION MODE, NOT AN ENCOUNTER** — no Approaches, no Turns, no exposure. Its own design. 2 · **THE ROAD IS READ, NOT ROLLED** — density, vehicle class, headlights, sound, wind shear, micro-cover. 3 · **LOAD BEARS ON IT** — and bears harder on the way home. *The heavier the haul, the longer the road home.* 4 · **THE FIRST LAW IS THE SPEC** — *wait for the gap; the gap is given, not owed.* Folklore, tutorial text and an accurate description of the mechanics, at once.

### 13 · §26.4 Resolution and the Turn — *"Slip did not wait"*
**Morningside · THE TALE · Slip, Gale, Vane**

`ROUND LANDS CLOSE → ONE TURN → NEXT ROUND`

**Why here.** The Turn is the encounter model's whole player-facing surface and §26.4 teaches it as a five-row table. It also needs to demonstrate a **Guest complication that names something the Guest does** — the fix story_colonies applies to the Mink, and canon's own governing rule for the roster. Slip is both in one beat.

**The scene.** A water node at the Creek Split, contested, Round 1 close. **NEW** — the party has Gale, Vane and Slip, and the Approach was Evade because Vane read the channel and said it would hold. It did not hold; Vane is in the water and pinned under the near lip, and the Turn opens, and the player does not get it. Slip is already gone. She does not wait to be sent — that is what she is, and it is on her roster entry, and Morningside recruited her knowing it. She has Vane out before the second round resolves. Slip's own exposure goes up, because she is the one who went in, and the Turn the player would have spent on a Supply or a Press is spent for them.

Closing: the round after, the party could have Parleyed with what was holding the channel, and could not, because **prey species will not Parley with a mink** — and Morningside knew that too.

**Card.** 1 · **A CLOSE ROUND BUYS A TURN** — clean rounds end the encounter. Four rounds is the ceiling, no exceptions. 2 · **FIVE THINGS YOU CAN DO** — play a Supply, commit a Rescue, change Approach, Press, or Withdraw. Once. 3 · **RESCUE MOVES EXPOSURE** — it raises the rescuer and lowers the rescued. The Launcher's question is *who do I trust with this*; the Turn is where it is answered. 4 · **A GUEST'S COMPLICATION IS SOMETHING THEY DO** — never an absence of benefit. No Guest is merely weak in the wrong place.

### 14 · §27.2 The recruitment funnel — *"Churr, three times"*
**Morningside · THE CHRONICLE · Churr, Rowan, Tassel**

`ANTAGONIST → NON-ANTAGONIST → AMENABLE → RESIDENCE COST`

**Why here.** §27.2 names the raccoon as the canonical worked example and §21.3 supplies the principle — *antagonist status is a position an animal currently occupies, not always a fixed nature* — but the whole thing only lands if the reader can tell it is the same animal, which §V5-63 correctly says requires a name that was there when he was the enemy. Three meetings is a story and one paragraph is not.

**The scene.** Three dated Chronicle entries in one panel. **First:** Churr takes a node off a Morningside party in the second summer and Rowan writes the name down because Rowan writes everything down. **Second:** eighteen months later, a sealed cooler at the wall-edge and Churr already at it, and neither party contests — a non-antagonist contact resolving as mutual avoidance, and both sides leaving with something. **Third: NEW** — the winter Tassel judges a whole cache gone bad and Morningside carries it out to the far bank rather than eat it, and Churr is there, and it is not a negotiation so much as a thing that had already been decided by both of them some time earlier. He comes in the following spring. The residence cost is paid in Durable stores the colony can spare and a raised box built against the trunk, which is the Guest House in one of its faces.

Closing: **the same animal that drove a party off a node three winters ago sits at the Story Circle now, under the same name.**

**Card.** 1 · **CONTEST IS REVEALED AT THE NODE** — never at the Launcher. 2 · **THE FUNNEL** — contested, then 70/30 antagonist, then 30% amenable, then the animal's own check and a residence cost. 3 · **SLOTS CAP IT** — four Active at max Tier against a roster of seven. A full roster means passing on a good recruit. 4 · **AND A GUEST IS A CITIZEN** — Record, bonds, Keepsake, Distinctions, After-name. Not a unit, not a subordinate.

### 15 · §30.2 The Metropolis — *"What they say at the plaza"*
**Rushbottom · THE TALE · Bran, Nook**

`SAME ENGINE → VENUES AS NODES → PARLEY`

**Why here.** §30.2 makes the boldest claim in Part VI — that a player who has spent forty hours choosing Contest and Evade arrives somewhere that rewards neither — and §30.3 promises that the colony's certainties turn out to be local. Rushbottom carries it because a mouse colony arriving at the corridor's one city with a complete written list of everyone who has ever lived there is the funniest and saddest version of the encounter, and because Bran is canon's most trusted Median Scout and Nook is the wanderer who arrived alone with no other.

**The scene.** The tavern is a node and entering it opens the Encounter frame. **NEW** — Bran's Approach set has Contest greyed and Evade pointless and Parley wide open, and the animal he is talking to is a raccoon behind a plank, who is polite, and unhurried, and does not much care where Rushbottom is. What Bran is trying to buy is a name: the folk name of a Reach four down the chain that Rushbottom has walked and cannot find anyone who has heard of. What he gets instead is the Second Law, said wrong — *the mowing is fire, and fire is owed* — which is not how Rushbottom has ever said it and is said here by three different animals the same way.

Closing: Nook reads the sixty-one to nobody in particular before they leave, out of habit, and a mouse at the next table waits until she is finished and then asks her to say one of them again. **NEW** — Rushbottom has never once considered that its own certainties might be the small ones.

**Card.** 1 · **NO NEW REGISTER** — a Field territory, surveyed like any other, with venues as nodes. 2 · **IT LEANS ON PARLEY** — the best proof in the game that an encounter is not automatically a fight. 3 · **ITS LAWS CONTRADICT YOURS** — a bigger, older settlement remembers the world differently, and some of the contradictions are corrections. 4 · **AND IT IS NOT YOUR NEW TOWN** — the Metropolis is a destination. The Home Median stays the heart.

## 4. Continuity flags — things to rule on before writing

Seven, in descending order of how much they block.

> **A · BLOCKING — §19.3's epigraph contradicts its own rule and the mockup.**
> The GDD prints: *"Sharpnose's Tale, entry the ninth: for what she did at the culvert the night the water rose, the Circle names her **Sharpnose Flood-wise**, and the Distinction and the name are written as one line."* Three problems. It sits directly under *"the first one is the one that names you,"* and it depicts a name changing at a later Distinction. It sites the naming at a culvert flood; story_colonies sites it at a fallen log against a cat. And *Flood-wise* is a compound in a register story_colonies deliberately keeps plain.
> **PROVISIONAL fix:** keep the entry, keep the flood, and flip its lesson. *"Sharpnose's Tale, entry the ninth: for what she did at the culvert the night the water rose, the Circle grants a second Distinction, and does not give her a second name."* The epigraph then teaches the harder half of the rule — that later Distinctions accrue without churning the name — which is the half the body text states and nothing currently demonstrates. It also stops competing with the panel four inches above it.

> **B · BLOCKING — *Sharpnose's Rest* or *Sharpnose's Retreat*.**
> §23.2 prints **Sharpnose's Rest** as its worked example of a history-conferred place name. story_colonies proposes **Sharpnose's Retreat**, on the grounds that *Rest* reads as a grave and Sharpnose survives the campaign. story_colonies is right and the panel at §19.3 plus the callback at §23.2 both depend on the answer. Recommend adopting *Retreat*, and noting in §23.2 that one night conferred a citizen's After-name and a place's — which is §23.2's *one naming grammar, two scales* demonstrated in a single event, and currently unstated.

> **C · BLOCKING — the sixty-one, and how many came out.**
> story_colonies is internally inconsistent. The opening and the Chronicle both say **three** came out of the pipe and *"the founding is three, and completed to six"*; Barley's entry says *"Sixty-one were under Chaff End and **four** came out,"* and Pip's says he was *"the reason the other **three** kept going."* Panels 1, 9 and 11 all name the number and none can be written until it is settled. Recommend **three out of the pipe, Husk already at Pipe End** — which preserves §35.1's *all three flee with three*, makes Husk's arrival the thing that gets Rushbottom to four, and reads Barley's "four" as her counting Husk in, which is a characterful reason for the discrepancy rather than an error. If that reading is adopted it should be stated, because it is a nice one.

> **D · Teasel's maiming has no site in canon.**
> story_colonies says a Road Work onset in her third year. Panel 3 places it at Ashcross during the Fifth Hole event and panel 8 depends on it having happened. Nothing contradicts, but nothing confirms either. Confirm or move.

> **E · Cobb.**
> Open in story_colonies, and now load-bearing: panels 5, 6 and 12 all use him, and panel 6 is a Hearth demonstration that becomes a grief demonstration if he dies. If Cobb dies, Part IX's *The Winter That Was Hard* is where it should happen, and panel 6 should be written knowing it. Recommend closing this before the Morningside panels are drafted.

> **F · Guest name styling.**
> Canon sets **WHOOT** in capitals; story_colonies sets the other eleven plain and flags the inconsistency. Fifteen panels will print Guest names repeatedly. Recommend WHOOT stays capitalised as an inherited proper form and everything else stays plain, with a one-line note in Appendix C. Any answer is fine; an unmade decision is not.

> **G · CREATED VISUAL count.**
> The change guide specifies nine; v0.4.6 carries eight (§11.2, §19.2 ×2, §22.2 ×2, §27.2, §29.4, §32). Since six of them now pair with panels, the discrepancy should be reconciled in the same pass rather than separately.

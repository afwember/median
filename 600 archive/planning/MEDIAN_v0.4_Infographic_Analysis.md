# MEDIAN — Infographic Analysis: What Merits Consideration for v0.4

**Purpose:** A harvest pass over three generations of infographics, flagging anything compelling enough to consider adopting into canon. This is an *analysis*, not a canon edit — nothing here changes v0.4 unless you choose to fold it in. Phase 2 (remaking the art itself) is out of scope; this is only about ideas.

**Note on status:** Items below are graded as **[FITS]** (clean fit, fills a real gap), **[OPEN-ITEM]** (a candidate answer to one of the five parked tuning questions), **[FLAVOR]** (worldbuilding/naming worth harvesting), **[ART]** (visual-language reference, mostly for Phase 2), or **[DRIFT]** (do not adopt — logged so the record is explicit).

---

## The three sets at a glance

**v0.1 Set 1 (photoreal miniature-diorama).** Strongest *folkloric* register of the three. Loose on mechanics, but full of Watership-Down-flavored worldbuilding — "Laws of the Median," folk names for places, the human world as "The Giants." A few citizen/roster inaccuracies (rats and raccoons as inhabitants). Best mined for tone and diegetic surfaces.

**v0.1 Set 2 (illustrated storybook).** The most *on-model* set and by far the richest source of usable ideas. Made from the v0.1 doc, it independently lands on much of what became v0.4 canon (Anchor-Point-style placement bonuses, the four-state durability ladder, the crossing loop). Its citizen dossiers, expedition-rating, colony-record, and event-card mockups are the standout material across all three sets.

**v0.3 (ChatGPT from the Gemini v0.2 thread).** Largely the *rejected drift* — the tactical-extraction / military-spec register the decision record explicitly overturned (casualty %, squad loadouts, pheromone routing, canopy bypass, subterranean conduits, industrialization, TEEN/MATURE). Loose on accuracy exactly as you flagged. **But** buried inside the militarized skin are a few genuinely useful things: concrete Traffic-as-Weather hazard types, the scale-differential art device, and the real-ecology "edge effect" gradient. Worth reading past the chrome for those.

## A. Compelling — fits cleanly, fills a real gap  [FITS]

1. **Citizen dossier cards with personal "Possessions."** *(Set 2, Citizens page; echoed by v0.3's gear cards.)* Named citizens shown with a one-line personal motto, species·role, a short earned-history blurb, and **one or two signature keepsake items** (Nutmeg's Lucky Clover, Twig's Bone Wrench, Daisy's Daisy Charm). The **Possessions/keepsake layer is not in v0.4** and is a low-cost, high-warmth characterization device that directly serves the "durable identity" and "stories over statistics" pillars. Recommend considering it as a small addition to the citizen dossier (§8.1). *Caveat:* the cards display stats as filled star-pips; keep v0.4's descriptive bands instead of exposing pip counts, to preserve hidden-value suspense.

2. **"Expedition Rating" — a post-mission grade.** *(Set 2, Expeditions page.)* **Legendary / Successful / Hard-Earned / Failed**, each with a flavor line. This is a clean diegetic wrapper over the *group-level* resolution (§9.7), and "Hard-Earned = goal met but heavy losses" is a perfect frame for the "costly victory" the independent-resolution rule already produces. Recommend as the presentation layer for expedition outcomes.

3. **A "Report & Share" debrief beat.** *(Set 2, Expeditions page.)* Returning parties report *what we found / dangers / new maps / who fell*, yielding a "sharing bonus" that speeds colony growth and unlocks options. Concretizes v0.4's "record the story" step (§4.3 step 8) and gives the intelligence/rumor system and the **Teacher** role something tangible to feed. Recommend.

4. **Choice-based telegraphed-event cards.** *(Set 2 phone mockup, "Heavy Rain.")* An incoming event with **costed response options** — *Reinforce Drainage (−wood) / Move Supplies (−time) / Do Nothing (risk it)*. A strong, legible model for seasonal/weather events and for the Road Work counterplay already in canon (§5.4 active-recovery). *Caveat:* the mockup shows a numeric countdown; keep Road Work's telegraph **non-numeric** per Decision 32 — a countdown is fine for ordinary weather, not for Road Work.

5. **Colony Record / chronicle panel.** *(Set 2, Citizens page; Set 2 dashboard.)* A persistent almanac: *Founded, Founding Members, Current Citizens, Largest Threat, Greatest Triumph, Next Goal.* Surfaces named milestones and gives the legacy/continuation arc (§13.3) a home. Recommend as the colony's chronicle/legacy surface.

6. **Named relationship pairs.** *(Set 2, Citizens page.)* Explicit bonded pairs ("Bramble & Twig — best friends"; "Scab & Whoot — risk and watchfulness"). A simple diegetic display of the relationship system the dossier already implies. Recommend for the dossier/relationship UI.

7. **A physical "Community Board."** *(Set 1, Acorn Hollow render: "Mowing Alert Tomorrow / Food Share at the Culvert / New Scouts Report In.")* An in-world notice board as the colony's task/alert surface — state made visible *in the world* rather than in a menu, which is exactly the "ledger and legible" pillar. Recommend.

8. **A "Recent Events" log that names individuals.** *(Set 2 dashboard: "Nutmeg and Daisy healed a sick citizen"; "a young scout returned from first patrol.")* A rolling, in-fiction activity log referencing citizens by name. Cheap, warm, reinforces the named-cast pillar. Recommend.

## B. Candidates for the five open items  [OPEN-ITEM]

9. **Concrete Traffic-as-Weather hazard types** *(v0.3 Margin spec + Highway Ecology).* This is the single most useful thing the v0.3 set offers. The parked "Traffic-as-Weather manifestation" question (Decision 58 / §20) has a ready-made candidate menu here, and all three are believable-for-animals and scale by proximity to the road:
   - **Highway Draft** — vehicle wind-shear gusts that strip cover and flatten pioneer flora (strongest at the edge).
   - **Road Spray & Runoff** — de-icing salt and hydrocarbon drift that poisons soil and degrades gear.
   - **Thermal Plume** — asphalt heat radiation that accelerates decay and raises heat exposure.
   Plus, from Set 2: **Chemical Runoff** and **Invasive Plants** as slower environmental hazards. Recommend banking these as the candidate set when you spec the Field-Mode encounter-state pass. *(These stay parked per Decision 58 — this is a menu for that future pass, not a decision now.)*

10. **A construction-project queue with slots + progress bars.** *(Set 2 dashboard / crafting mockup: "Project Slots 4/5," % bars, Speed Up.)* A natural surface for the **Establish-Outpost construction clock** (open item) and for build timing generally. Useful structural reference for how that clock and its counterplay would actually read to the player.

## C. Worldbuilding, folklore & naming worth harvesting  [FLAVOR]

11. **The folkloric register — "The Laws of the Median."** *(Set 1.)* "Cars are death — cross only with care / The mowing is fire — flee or be cut down / Water is life — guard it well / Share the bounty; hoard and you starve / Watch the sky — owls own the night, hawks own the day." This is precisely the Watership-Down lapine-lore voice the game is reaching for. Recommend as onboarding culture, loading-screen aphorisms, and Teacher/elder flavor.

12. **Dual literal + folk naming for Reaches and Field-Mode nodes.** *(Set 1: "Mowing Zone / The Burning," "Rest Stop Field / The Plenty," "North Interchange / The Moss Gate." Set 2: named nodes — The Ditches, Old Fence Line, The Stony Patch, The Berry Thicket, Twin Oaks, The Overpass.)* Giving each Median Reach and each notable node a folk name alongside its plain descriptor gives the corridor a mythic, memorable geography and strengthens Field-Mode legibility (§3.5, §3.7, §9.3). Strong, recommend.

13. **Diegetic vocabulary for the human world.** *(Set 1 / Set 2.)* "**The Giants**" (humans/vehicles), "**rivers of thunder / roaring rivers**" (the highways), "**the Mowing**" (a Road Work instance). Evocative in-world language. Recommend adopting the vocabulary. *Caveat:* "The Giants" implies visible humans; keep it as *spoken folklore*, consistent with the art rule that human bodies on-screen are usually tonally wrong (§15.2).

14. **"A Note on Names."** *(Set 2.)* "Names are earned, gifted, or chosen. They are a promise: 'I see you. You matter. You belong.'" A culture note that motivates the naming / low-population pillar better than a mechanics paragraph could. Recommend as manual/flavor text.

15. **Player-named colony on a physical welcome sign.** *(Set 1 / Set 2, "Acorn Hollow — Welcome.")* Small, warm, on-tone; the colony's name appears as a built object in the world.

16. **Per-building upgrade-name ladders.** *(Set 2, Buildings page: Lookout Post → High Lookout → Signal Tower → Guardian Spire.)* Evocative names per upgrade tier, good flavor for the construction/tier systems.

17. **Culture mottos / taglines.** "Observe. Plan. Adapt. Teach the young. The Median remembers." · "Stories over statistics." · "Small is grand." "Stories over statistics" in particular crystallizes the ledger-and-restraint ethos in three words. *Caveat:* quotes attributed to "**Fiver**" and a guest literally named "**Kehaar**" borrow Watership Down's actual characters — keep the *homage*, but use original in-world names to avoid leaning on named IP.

## D. Visual-language references (mostly Phase 2, banked now)  [ART]

18. **Scale-differential visualizations.** *(v0.3: truck/rabbit/mouse at true metric scale; "soda can → fortress," "drainage pipe → conduit," "tire tread → hazard field" object-to-terrain transforms; scale/weight labels on citizen cards.)* Directly serves the §15.2 scale-language goal. The "everyday object shown at true scale, becoming monumental terrain" device is excellent and on-tone (a soda can *is* a fortress). Best single art idea to carry forward from v0.3.

19. **Improvised material culture.** *(v0.3 gear cards; Set 2 buildings.)* Gear and structures built from specific, believable debris — coaxial-cable harness, sewing-needle multitool, soda-can plating, bicycle-spoke, reflector-plastic goggles, bottle-cap lantern. On-tone with the *Mouse Guard* material-culture reference and the scrap economy (§6.3), **as long as it stays non-weaponized and non-industrial**. A rich reference bank for building/equipment art.

20. **Colony built *into* wrecked human objects.** *(v0.3 engine-block warren; Set 1 rusted barrels/cans as structures.)* Nesting inside an abandoned truck, a soda can, a culvert — the AoE-scrap aesthetic. Strong, on-tone visual motif worth making a deliberate art pillar.

21. **Diorama tier-progression panorama.** *(v0.3 tiers page.)* Camp → warren → dense hub → connected network as one continuous visual escalation. Good reference for *showing* tier growth — **provided Tiers 3–4 are de-industrialized** (a network of lit nodes across the median, not "Industrial Hub / Sovereign Empire / fuel depots").

22. **Real-ecology explainer + the "Edge Effect / Quiet Zone" gradient.** *(Set 2, Highway Ecology.)* Root network, food web, microclimates, movement corridors — grounds the "real ecology as inspiration" working principle and is genuinely charming. The standout is the **Edge Effect (rich but risky) vs Quiet Zone (deeper = safer)** gradient: a real ecological principle that elegantly grounds the whole risk/reward geography *and* the Traffic-as-Weather proximity scaling (§2.5). Worth adopting as the stated organizing logic for space within a Reach.

23. **Intra-median sub-zones.** *(Set 2, "Lay of the Land": North Woodland / Central Thicket / Open Meadow / South Drainage.)* Internal variation within a *single* median. Could enrich Anchor Points and Field-Mode texture (a Reach isn't uniform end-to-end). Worth considering as intra-Reach flavor.

## E. Drift to NOT adopt — logged for the record  [DRIFT]

The v0.3 set is mostly the material the decision record already overturned. Cataloguing it so the "why not" is explicit:

- **The whole "MEDIAN OS" tactical-military framing** — estimated casualty %, threat indices, squad loadouts, "HOLD TO CONFIRM // LAUNCH EXPEDITION," predictive casualty models. Violates Decisions 1, 2, 4, and especially 17 (never show exact odds). It's also a useful *negative* reference: the exact wrong way to present the Expedition Launcher.
- **Pheromone routing / "Pheromone Router Beacon"** (Mouse) — rejected outright (Decision 8).
- **"Bypasses ground-level traffic entirely" canopy network** (Squirrel) and **subterranean culvert conduits linking medians and margins** — both violate the no-bypass rule: every species must engage the crossing (Decision 10; v0.1 §4.3).
- **Rats and raccoons as citizens** — "Grist" the rat chem-tinker, "Raccoon Workshop," raccoon "tinkerers." Rat is antagonist-fauna only (Decision 15); raccoons are not a core species. (Note: this is the "Grist" misdepiction the record already flagged.)
- **Multi-species colonies** — the dashboard's "Rabbits 18 / Squirrels 12 / Wood Mice 7 / Other Allies" roster. Violates single-species colonies (Decision 23).
- **Industrialization** — "Industrial Hub," "Sovereign Empire," a FUEL/ENERGY resource, "Research Tree," electronics as loot, "Trap Grid," pikes/shields/spears, TEEN/MATURE rating. Violate Decisions 1, 2, 22, the electricity exclusion, and the base-builder-not-combat framing.
- **High population + military hierarchy** — pop caps of 87–200+, "Queens / Alphas / Guard Squads." Violate the low-population, know-every-name ceiling (Decision 7) and the standing-Role model (Decisions 48–50).
- **Bottle-cap "Currency"** (Set 1) — violates the no-standalone-currency rule (Decision 18).
- **Collapsed/expanded economies** — a single "Materials" stat, a separate "Water" stat, "Metals / Plastics / Electronics" loot classes. Contradict the Sustenance(Perishable/Durable) + Flexible + Rigid model (Decisions 18–20).
- **Fixed sound-wall height ("6.5m")** — canon makes wall height flavor-only, never a fixed figure (Decision 25).
- **Roster mismatches** — Deer Mouse, Otter, Pigeon as guests; Snake filed as Ambient. Contradict the settled rosters (Decisions 12–14).
- **"Smoke Pouch" crossing tool** (Set 2) — animals deploying smoke screens reads as tech-fantasy; off-tone.

## Two borderline items (your call, not a recommendation)

- **A "Reputation" stat** *(Set 1 / Set 2 HUDs)* and **"Knowledge" as a tracked resource / "Visitors Helped" counter** *(Set 2).* There are narrative hooks for faction standing and intelligence (the Metropolis, rumors, trade). But adding tracked currencies cuts against the restraint pillar (2.7), which deliberately avoids new stats when an existing system can carry the decision. Flagged as optional, not recommended — mentioning only because they recur across sets.
- **"WHOOT," the barred owl with a permanently weak wing who becomes a stationary watcher** *(Set 2 citizen card).* Not a system, but a lovely embodiment of two canon ideas at once — the Owl as an Ambient Guest (Decision 14) and "valuing a maimed citizen" (§8.4). Worth keeping as a template character when you write flavor.

---

## Bottom line

If you adopt nothing else, the highest-value, lowest-risk pickups are: **citizen Possessions/keepsakes (A1), the Expedition Rating grade (A2), the Report & Share debrief (A3), the Colony Record chronicle (A5), the "Laws of the Median" folkloric voice + dual place-naming (C11–C13),** and — for the parked open item — **the three concrete Traffic-as-Weather hazard types (B9).** All of these fit v0.4 as it stands without reopening a single settled decision.

## G. Blue Text-Backgrounds — the obsolescence question  [ART]

You flagged that these may be "stylistically obsolete." Breaking that into its parts:

32. **The obsolete layer: the navy-blueprint field + rusted-rivet frame.** This chrome *is* the v0.3/v0.2 technical-military register — blueprint grid, metal plates, corner screws, faint radar reticles. If the project commits to the v0.1 Set 2 feel, this frame is the piece that clashes and should be retired. Agreed: **as a style, the blue-blueprint frame is obsolete under a Set-2 direction.**

33. **The reusable layer: the corner vignettes.** The diorama illustrations tucked into each frame are warm, photoreal-storybook, and fully on-tone — the *same* family as the Concept Artwork deck, not the chrome around them. Several are individually valuable: the **salvage-sort workshop** ("Salvage / Sort / Save"), the **winter food-store cutaway**, and especially the **crow handing a pull-tab to mice in a den** — which is a *Guest-interaction/trade moment* rendered narratively (the "Kehaar" beat in a single image). These illustrations can be re-cropped and reused against a warmer treatment; they are not obsolete. **Retire the frames, keep the pictures.**

34. **An independent reason to retire them as *text* backgrounds: legibility.** Setting style aside, a dark, busy, illustrated field behind dense GDD body text is a readability problem — low contrast and visual competition with the prose. Even a Set-2-styled full-bleed background would fight the text. The better pattern is to let these images be **full-page plates or section openers**, with clean, high-contrast pages for the actual body copy. So: retire the blue backgrounds *as text backgrounds* regardless of the style question; promote the good vignettes to standalone plates.

## H. Direction — "v0.3 layout choices" + "v0.1 Set 2 feel"

Your stated intent — preserve some v0.3 *layout* choices while moving to the Set 2 *feel* — is coherent and worth stating precisely, because "layout" and "feel" are separable and the v0.3 set conflates them. A working split for the v0.4 remake pass:

**Preserve from v0.3 (structure / layout):**
- The **information-dense single-poster layout** — one topic per landscape sheet, a strong title bar, modular labelled panels.
- **Callout labels pinned to a hero image** (the "annotated diorama" device) — used well, this is the cross-section and colony-dashboard format.
- **Cross-section / cutaway diagrams** (corridor cross-section, multi-level burrow, the workshop cutaway) — genuinely useful and worth keeping as a recurring format.
- **True-scale reference bars and object-to-terrain scale devices** (§15.2 support).
- **The corner/edge vignette-plus-open-field** page structure — *as a layout*, it's fine; it's the *styling* of that field that changes.

**Replace from v0.3 (styling / feel), moving toward Set 2:**
- Swap the **navy-blueprint + rusted-military-HUD chrome** for the **warm, tactile, storybook-real** treatment of Set 2 / the Concept Artwork deck (parchment, wood, lantern-light, leaf motifs, hand-lettered signage).
- Drop the **militarized vocabulary and framing** wholesale (OS terminals, threat indices, "asset/operative," targeting reticles, casualty math) — this is also where the content drift lived, so replacing the skin and correcting the canon go together.
- Prefer **diegetic in-world surfaces** (a carved duties board, a story-circle sign, a colony record etched on bark) over **abstract dashboard chrome** wherever a panel can plausibly be an object in the world — this is the Set 2 move and it doubles as "ledger and legible."

Net: **v0.3 gave good bones and bad skin.** The remake keeps the bones (dense, modular, annotated, cross-sectioned, to-scale) and reskins them in the Set 2 register. The blue text-backgrounds are the clearest casualty of that shift — frames retired, best vignettes promoted to plates.

# Addendum 2 — Three "Rogue" Images (unfiled)

Three loose images that never made it into a deck. One of them matters a lot.

## I1. "The Turn of the Seasons" poster — this is the target look realized  [FITS / ART]

This is the most important single asset in the whole review: a finished-looking seasons infographic that **already is the synthesis this document has been recommending** — v0.3's dense, modular, annotated-poster *layout* rendered in the v0.1-Set-2 *feel* (warm parchment, painted seasonal panels, hand-lettered headers, leaf-and-acorn iconography, diegetic "Acorn Hollow / Exit 17 Pine Ridge" signage). If you were looking for proof that "v0.3 bones + Set 2 skin" is achievable in one sheet, this is it. **Treat it as the reference template for the v0.4 remake pass.**

It's also remarkably on-canon in content:
- The **Core Philosophy** sidebar ("Small is grand / Every life has a name / The world is beautiful and dangerous / Build. Gather. Survive. / Stories over statistics") and the **"Observe. Plan. Adapt. Teach the Young. The Median Remembers."** footer are exactly the culture-voice pickups (C17) landing perfectly.
- The five columns' **Perishable-vs-Durable key resources** (Tender Greens/Berries/Insects/Rainwater → Acorns/Nuts/Mushrooms/Wood → Stored Food/Bark) track the seasonal Sustenance fork (§5.3, §6.3).
- **"Colony Lessons" (Observe / Plan / Adapt / Teach / Remember)** and **"The Median Provides / Demands Respect / Your Legacy"** are clean, on-tone framings of the pillars and the legacy arc.

**One canon caveat to fix in the remake:** the poster treats **"Mowing — The Great Danger" as a fifth season** and places it as a fixed spoke on the Seasonal Rhythm wheel. Per Decision 32, Road Work/Mowing is *decoupled from the season calendar* (only a marginally higher chance in summer) and *arrives without numeric warning* — so it should not read as a scheduled fifth season on a cyclic wheel. The fix is small and keeps everything good about the panel: present Mowing as a **recurring event that can strike in any season** (its own callout box, not a wheel spoke). Pleasingly, the panel's own copy — *"the grass giants arrive without warning… blades roar… escape is the only option… wait until it is safe to return"* — already matches the telegraph → onset → persistence structure and the "machinery is evaded, never fought" rule; it's only the *placement as a season* that needs adjusting. (Bonus: "the grass giants" is a nice extension of the "Giants" folk vocabulary from C13.)

## I2. Contested-encounter scene (opossum over a spilled cache)  [ART, with drift to fix]

A party confronting a hissing **opossum** guarding a spilled sack of berries along the vine-covered sound wall, rain, trucks, "Exit 17 Pine Ridge." As mood and staging this is an excellent **contested-node encounter** reference (§9.6) — the scale, wet-highway realism, and "party meets a territorial animal over a food cache" beat are spot-on, and the consistent "Exit 17 Pine Ridge" signage across images is nice world-continuity.

Drift to correct if this becomes canon reference:
- **Mixed-species party** (rabbit + squirrel + raccoon + mouse together) contradicts single-species colonies (Decision 23). A party should be *one* core species plus recruited Guests.
- **Raccoon as a party member** — raccoon is neither a core species nor a canon Guest (roster is Weasel/Fox/Mink/Crow/Hedgehog/Snake). Remove or reassign.
- **Opossum as the antagonist here is fine** — note it is *not* a v0.4 Guest (it was a dropped v0.1/v0.3 candidate), but using it as contested antagonist fauna is perfectly legitimate.
- The rabbit's **cloak/staff** is borderline: simple cloaks fit the *Mouse Guard* material-culture reference, but keep it restrained — no default cute anthropomorphic dress (§15.1).

## I3. The Rest-Stop Metropolis at night — definitive establishing art  [ART]

A sprawling, terraced, lantern-lit rodent city built on a mound beside an all-night truck-plaza, diesel pumps and parked rigs glowing behind it. This is the **best Rest-Stop Metropolis reference produced so far** (§13) and it nails the brief almost line-for-line: "an urban animal ecosystem distributed among the neglected margins of a human rest stop… extraordinary but plausible… not a fantasy capital beside human foot traffic." The dense population is *correct here* — the Metropolis is explicitly a distant, large, multi-species settlement, not the player's low-population Home colony, so the crowd reads right rather than violating the named-cast ceiling.

Two small notes for final art: use a **fictional plaza name** rather than a real chain, and push the **multi-species** variety (canon makes the Metropolis pointedly multi-species; the sketch skews mostly mice/rats).

## Where these leave the direction

I1 in particular closes the loop on Section H: the "preserve v0.3 layout, adopt Set 2 feel" path isn't hypothetical — it's already been executed convincingly in one poster. The practical recommendation tightens to: **use the "Turn of the Seasons" poster as the house-style template** for the v0.4 remake (fixing only the Mowing-as-season placement), lean on I3 as the Metropolis key-art anchor, and treat I2 as a usable encounter reference once the party/roster drift is corrected.

# MEDIAN v0.4.4 — Running Decisions

Canon revisions arising from a read-through of v0.4.3. **This is a design log, not the Phase 2 art log** — that one lives in `running_decisions.md` and numbers P2-*.

**Status:** notes on Sections 1–7 processed. Sections 8–38 pending a later pass.

**Convention:** decisions carry a section reference and a verdict — **SETTLED**, **PROPOSED** (my reading, awaiting confirmation), or **OPEN** (genuinely needs discussion). Settled items get GDD decision numbers from 94 upward when the edition is cut.

## The governing principle

### V4-1 — The book states what is, never what was rejected · **SETTLED**

Five separate notes turned out to be the same note. Each flags a passage where the document argues with an earlier version of itself:

- §2 — *"Two recurring ideas are deliberately not pillars…"*
- §4 — *"There is no cross-recruitment among the core species at any point…"*
- §4 — *"Species is not a chapter a reader can skip…"*
- §4.1 — *"Population-per-Upkeep is not a separate stat… No new tracked stats were added in v0.4.1 or v0.4.2."*
- §4.4 — *"The mouse's expedition identity is deliberately not a divided-attention macro-mechanic. The rejected 'pheromone macro-swarm' concept is not canonical."*

**The rule: a reader of the concept book should not be able to tell what earlier drafts said.** Rationale, negation and revision-history belong in Appendix B or in endnotes.

**But three of the five carry a fact that must survive, restated positively.** Deleting them wholesale would lose real canon:

| Passage | Fate |
|---|---|
| *Not pillars* — sound wall, real ecology | **Cut.** Both are stated properly elsewhere; this only explains why they aren't pillars |
| *No cross-recruitment* | **Restate.** → *"Each campaign is one species."* Stated as a rule, not as a rebuttal. The fact is load-bearing — it is why the Guest system exists |
| *Species is not a chapter to skip* | **Cut** from body; the point is editorial, addressed to a reader about document structure |
| *Population-per-Upkeep* | **Restate.** → the stat frame is three dials, and expendability falls out of the food ratio. Drop the version archaeology |
| *Pheromone macro-swarm* | **Cut entirely.** Pure history. The mouse's identity is stated positively in the same section already |

**A sweep for this pattern is needed across all 38 sections**, not only the five caught here. The tell is a sentence built on *deliberately not*, *is not*, *was rejected*, *no longer*, or a version number.

## Structural changes

### V4-2 — A world primer opens the book, before the registers · **PROPOSED**
*Addresses the §3-versus-§7 ordering question, and the §7 note.*

The objection is sound: **Crossing, Field and Colony are defined by what the player does, but each happens somewhere**, and the reader meets all four before learning what a Margin or a Reach is. Explaining a verb before its object.

But moving Part II ahead of the registers would cost the countdown structure — four registers, three species, two loops, one folklore — which is doing real work as a shape.

**The lighter fix: a short world primer before §3.** The cross-section in a paragraph and a diagram — Sound Wall → Margin → Highway → Home Median → Highway → Margin → Sound Wall — and the corridor as a chain of Reaches. Perhaps 200 words. Every register then lands on named ground: *a Crossing takes a party across the Highway, from the Median to the Margin.*

Part II keeps the full treatment. This also satisfies the §7 note directly — summary at the front, detail in place.

### V4-3 — The origin brief goes very early · **SETTLED**

Immediately after the elevator pitch:

> *Three young members of the chosen species escape the wreck of their ancestral home, destroyed by road construction. They flee to a green island between two highways, and begin again.*

Two sentences that give the reader a situation before any system. Currently the Founding Escape is not described until §35, some thirty sections after it becomes relevant to understanding anything.

### V4-4 — Species summaries precede the stat frame · **SETTLED**
*§4.*

§4.1 currently opens with the shared stat table before the reader has met a rabbit. Reorder: **who each species is, then how they compare.** The comparison is only meaningful once there are three things to compare.

### V4-5 — The Two Loops get a purpose statement before their steps · **SETTLED**
*§5.*

Both loops currently open as numbered procedure. Each needs a sentence on what it is *for* first — the domestic loop is how a place becomes worth protecting; the expedition loop is how it grows and what it costs.

### V4-6 — §5.2 reorganized around the pressure, not the corridor · **SETTLED**

The bridge between the loops is one idea: **the colony can always survive at home, and can never advance there.** The corridor and its Reaches are the *consequence*, not the concept. Currently the section leads with median connections and buries the pressure.

## The narrative track

### V4-7 — Three example colonies, one per species · **PROPOSED — and the largest idea in these notes**

A named colony of each core species, with named citizens, recurring throughout the book. Every worked example, illustration caption, Record excerpt and story beat draws on one of the three rather than inventing a fresh anonymous instance.

**Why this is more than flavor.** It is the device that makes a sourcebook feel *observed rather than invented*, and it solves several problems already logged:

- The **worked examples** (Phase 2, P2-46) need protagonists. Five examples about "a citizen" are five unrelated fragments; five about the same colony are a history.
- **Part IX — Stories of Survival** needs subjects, and this supplies them.
- The **Records** — Almanac, Chronicle, Likeness, Tale — are hard to illustrate in the abstract. With three colonies they have actual content.
- The **species asymmetry** becomes concrete: the same events befalling a rabbit, a squirrel and a mouse colony read differently, which is the argument §4 is making anyway.
- **Species-balance** in the artwork (the standing Appendix D review) becomes structural rather than a thing to audit afterward.

**Open sub-questions:** how much of each colony to fix in advance versus letting it accrete as the book is written; whether all three run the full campaign arc or each illustrates a different phase; and whether the Founding Escape is depicted once or three times.

### V4-8 — The book opens with narrative · **PROPOSED**

A short story segment before any system material — most likely the Founding Escape, from inside one of the three colonies. This is standard sourcebook practice and it does a job nothing else can: it establishes register and stakes before the reader is asked to hold any rules.

### V4-9 — The Founding Escape is depicted, with a named citizen · **SETTLED**
*§6.1, §35.1.*

Narratively and visually, featuring one of the three example colonies' founding trio. Already a planned plate; this fixes its content and ties it to V4-7.

## Canon changes with consequences

### V4-10 — Encounter tempo: two rounds and one decision is the target · **PROPOSED**
*§25.1, and resolves a deferred numeric in Appendix D.*

§25.1 currently calls the one-round, no-Turn encounter "the common case." **Invert that.** The majority should be **two rounds and one Turn** — the player chooses an Approach, sees a round resolve, and then makes a real decision.

**The argument for:** a one-round encounter asks the player for one choice at entry and then plays out without them. For a game whose Launcher question is *"who do I trust with this?"*, the Turn is where that question actually gets answered — *who goes back for them* (§25.4). Most encounters should reach it.

**The cost to watch:** pacing. A Field run visits several nodes; if every node runs two rounds, traversal slows. The three-round cap protects the ceiling, but the floor moving from one to two is a real change to how long a run takes.

**Mechanically this is a tuning decision, not a structural one.** §25.1 says a round that lands *close* continues the encounter, and Appendix D defers "the close window — how near the middle a round must land to continue." **This note sets that target: wide enough that two-and-one is the mode.**

**And the Situation beat wants an example in the book.** From the notes, and it is a good one:

> *You have found a truck tire with edible flowers growing inside it. Two large weasels are already there. One is favouring a hurt leg.*

Note what it does: the adversary is a Presence with a count given as flavor (§25.3), and the hurt leg is an unforced detail that suggests an Approach without disclosing a difficulty.

### V4-11 — Death is rare, and the reason is the pillar · **SETTLED**
*§2.5.*

State explicitly in §2.5 that citizen death is uncommon, with the reconciliation the note asks for:

**"Red in tooth and claw" is about consequence, not body count.** The world is genuinely dangerous, and that danger expresses mostly as fear, wounds, maiming, lost cargo and changed relationships — outcomes that persist and are carried home. A high death rate would make citizens replaceable, and replaceable citizens destroy the attachment the entire game is built on. The harm ladder exists precisely so that danger has three rungs before it has a fourth.

This is more *Watership Down* than a high body count, not less: that book's force comes from animals who survive damaged.

### V4-12 — Birds of prey become a distinct threat · **OPEN — needs a design pass**
*§6.1, Fifth Law.*

**The observation is sharp: birds are the only predator the world's geometry does not constrain.** Sound walls, lane count, cover and culverts shape every other threat. A hawk ignores all of it.

**And canon has already promised this and not delivered.** Four of the five Laws teach a system the game actually runs — the First teaches Crossing, the Second Road Work, the Third sustenance and the Pond Hollow, the Fourth the pooled economy. **The Fifth teaches predators by time of day, and almost nothing implements it.** A Law that teaches nothing is the weakest link in the folklore-as-tutorial argument.

**Proposed shape** — an *aerial threat* present across registers rather than a new register, in keeping with Pillar 2.6:

- **Colony** — telegraphed by the Watchkeeper and the lookout network; defensible; the Owl Guest's detection radius finally matters.
- **Field** — an overhead pressure that makes open ground genuinely different from covered ground, giving the Edge Effect a second axis.
- **Encounter** — rarely, as a mid-round event.

**Why it is flagged rather than settled:** Base Defense already exists, and its consequences are themselves an open item. Aerial threat could be a reskin of existing machinery — most likely a Presence type with an unusual availability profile, since Contest against a hawk is not a real option and Evade is — or it could be the fifth system the Fifth Law implies. **That is a design decision, not an art one, and it should not be resolved inside a book-revision pass.**

---

**Update, 2026-07-26 — half of this is now settled.** Two decisions in the v0.4.5 pass have resolved the mitigation side and sharpened the case for the threat:

- **V5-36** establishes that ground contest falls with Colony Tier — and that an aerial threat would **not**, because nothing a colony builds affects what comes from above. That asymmetry is now a visible structural hole in Tier progression, not merely a gap in the folklore.
- **V5-41** settles the counter: **an Ambient Guest**, not a building. The Owl already carries night-weighted perimeter detection, the Songbird is the natural day-watch counterpart, and WHOOT was written as the embodiment of this before the threat was ever specified.

**The design pass therefore only has to specify the threat.** The defence exists, costs a capped slot, extends to outposts for free, and requires no new system.

## Refinements and clarifications

### V4-13 — The register pairs are a zoom relationship, stated · **SETTLED**
*§3.5.*

**Field is to Encounter as the Home Median's Field layer is to the Colony.** In each pair, one register surveys ground and the other inhabits a point on it. Reaching a node in the Field opens an Encounter exactly as entering the colony opens the inhabited view of home.

Worth stating: it makes four registers read as two symmetrical pairs rather than four arbitrary modes, and it reinforces §3.5's spatial-fidelity rule.

### V4-14 — Colony attenuates the highway · **SETTLED**
*§3.5.*

Traffic remains present in the Colony register — the never-off-screen rule holds — but **quieter, more distant, more filtered.** Sanctuary is not the absence of the road; it is the road held at arm's length. This is the register where the player is allowed to stop bracing, and the sound design carries it.

### V4-15 — The Giants' vocabulary is metaphor, not nomenclature · **SETTLED**
*§6.3.*

Extends a principle already in the section: *Rivers of Thunder / Roaring Rivers* is deliberately a variant pair rather than one fixed term. **Generalize it.** These are things a storyteller reaches for, not proper nouns — the phrasing varies with the speaker, the season and the occasion.

**With one constraint, or the world becomes unquotable:** a stable core with variable ornament, in the manner of Homeric epithet. *The Giants*, *Roaring Iron* and *the Rivers of Thunder* recur often enough to be learned; around them the pool grows and shifts. The concept is constant; the words are not.

### V4-16 — Colony naming offers three registers, one of each · **PROPOSED**
*§22.3.*

Currently two registers — grounded-realistic and aspirational-hopeful — drawn from a combined pool of three candidates. **Add a third: Tribute.** *New [Ancestral Home Name]*, or a variant carrying the lost place forward.

**And present one of each rather than three from a pool.** The player chooses between a name that describes the ground, a name that hopes, and a name that remembers — which is a far more legible choice than three samples from one bag, and each reroll keeps the shape.

**Not too prescriptive**, on the contrary: it turns an aesthetic pick into a small characterisation of the colony's founding temperament. It also gives the Founding Escape a consequence that reaches the present, which it currently lacks — the ancestral home is destroyed in §35 and then barely referenced again.

### V4-17 — The documentary layer is not a fifth register · **SETTLED**
*§2.7.*

Reading the Chronicle, opening a Likeness, browsing the Records — these feel distinct in kind, and the question of whether they constitute a fifth mode will keep recurring. **They do not, and canon already answers it:** §32.1 places the Records at the Story Circle and the species hub, which are hotspots *inside the Colony register*.

Worth a sentence in §2.7 so the question stops being asked. The four-register rule governs presentation architecture, not every activity a player undertakes.

### V4-18 — "Tharn" breaks §1.1, and the fix already has a precedent · **DEFERRED**
*§1.1, §18.2, and 29 instances across canon.*

> **Standing decision, 2026-07-25: tharn stays for now.** The term is not changed in v0.4.4 and no replacement is adopted. The inconsistency with §1.1 is acknowledged and carried, to be settled in a later pass. Everything below is the analysis behind that deferral, kept so the question does not have to be re-argued from scratch.
>
> **While it stands, §1.1 and §18.2 contradict each other.** A one-line acknowledgment in §1.1 — noting tharn as a single conscious exception, designer-facing in origin — would remove the contradiction without committing to a replacement, if the contradiction becomes irritating before the question is settled.

**The inconsistency is real.** §1.1 states: *"No real Watership Down character name, place name, or **invented word** appears anywhere in MEDIAN's fiction."* Tharn is an invented Lapine word. It appears in a section heading, in Appendix A, in the canonical summary, and in the list of core inspirations.

**On whether it has transcended, as "orc" did: no.** Orc's escape had a specific mechanism — Tolkien revived an Old English word, D&D adopted it in 1974, and D&D's vocabulary became the shared vocabulary of an industry. Nothing did that for Lapine. Within Tolkien's own coinages, only the words that got that third-party adoption escaped: *warg* partly, *ent* barely, *hobbit* never. A reader meets *tharn* and thinks *Watership Down* — and MEDIAN's own pitch names the book, so they are primed to.

**The stronger objection is register, not borrowing.** Every term MEDIAN has invented is compound-descriptive and plain: Roaring Iron, the Rivers of Thunder, the Mowing, the River's Spume, Wind Draft, Warren Flow, After-name. §22.2 fixes the register as *"homely and plain, never ornate — the 'The Hill / Bywater' end of Tolkien rather than the elvish end."* Tharn is an opaque coinage from a Welsh-inflected constructed language: it describes nothing and must be taught. **It is the only word in the lexicon that does not sound like the others.**

**The document already contains the pattern that resolves this.** §22.1: *"The designer-facing term for this field is the agnomen; After-name is what the game says."* Apply the same split — **tharn stays as designer-facing shorthand**, precise and honest about lineage, while the fiction gets its own plain word.

**Constraint on any replacement: it must be a name, not a description.** *Fear paralysis* or *the freeze state* would be worse than tharn. A culture having one word for the thing is what makes it folklore rather than a status effect, and §37 lists players quoting the Laws back at the game as a success criterion. The word has to be sayable.

**Candidates in MEDIAN's register**, none adopted: **going stone** — plain, a verb phrase in the same shape, physical, and stone belongs to the world's material vocabulary of concrete and gravel and barrier. **Going white** — headlights are the cause, and the word carries the blankness. **The Long Second**, **going still**, **the Hold**.

*Also worth considering: whether a Law or Saying teaches the word, which would give the folklore layer a sixth piece of work and make the term learnable the way the others are.*

**Scope if adopted:** 29 instances in the GDD, plus references in `book/Contents.md`, `book/Production_Plan.md` and `log/running_decisions.md`. One planned plate and one planned artwork carry it in their titles.

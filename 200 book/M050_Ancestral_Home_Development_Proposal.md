# MEDIAN v0.5.0 — Ancestral Home Landmark Reach Development Proposal

Status: active noncanonical authorial discussion document under granular
authorial review

Purpose: development of the generated Ancestral Home as one heavily authored
Landmark Reach

Authority: the generated ancestral Colony, Landmark Reach category,
Reach-wide identity, Sourcebook-only status of named story subjects, and
Interchange-like TRAVEL geometry are adopted. This document develops their
still-open realization for granular discussion; proposals not yet adopted
here do not independently amend the GDD.

## Existing foundation

The Ancestral Home is the destroyed original Colony from that particular
campaign's Founding Escape. Its name, Founders, Places, civic arrangement,
material culture, and remembered particulars are generated for the campaign.
The selected Core Species shapes that generation but does not select one of
three fixed lost societies.

**Elderbank, Nine Beeches, and Chaff End—and every named Citizen and Place
associated with them—belong only to the Concept Sourcebook's illustrative
story civilizations. None appears as fixed content in the game.** Those
examples may demonstrate how an Ancestral Home works, but the player's return
is always to the Colony that their own opening established.

The resulting **Ancestral Home** is generated once at its opening-established
position some ways down the Corridor. It occupies an entire distinctive Median
Reach reached through ordinary travel when the new Colony can practically
support the expedition. Its physical TRAVEL layout uses the same crisscrossed,
multi-Roadway grammar as an Interchange Landmark Reach, but it is not itself an
ordinary generated Interchange. Its Node slate is much more heavily authored:
specialist Nodes and story situations arise from the generated remains of the
actual old Colony rather than from the ordinary Node and encounter mix.

A Founder or authored Wanderer escapee may open a Remembrance MEET through
lived connection. The low-frequency possibility of one lone escapee remains.
The new Colony remains the campaign's sole Home.

## Proposed governing idea

The Ancestral Home should be the one campaign horizon whose present
geography is already known emotionally before it is known spatially. The
player first experiences it as Home in the opening. Much later, ordinary
travel returns them to the same ground after destruction.

Its proposition is:

> **The place survives. Its society does not.**

This prevents two weak readings. It is neither a generic ruin dungeon full of
salvage nor a restoration objective waiting to become a second Home. It is a
large place to explore, with present life, recoverable material, and an
unusually dense authored Node slate. The Reach matters because physical
continuities let particular Citizens meet what remains of a life that cannot
be resumed.

## Physical extent and navigation

The Ancestral Home occupies the entire Median Reach containing the original
Colony and its destruction footprint. It is not a small ruin parcel
inside an otherwise generic Reach, a separate map, or a destination accessed
through a Special Journey command. The Founding Escape has already fixed its
place in the continuous Corridor; returning means physically travelling back
through the intervening Reaches and Crossings.

The whole Reach strongly reflects the lost Colony and the forces that destroyed
it. Its TRAVEL layout is built from the same broad grammar as an Interchange:
several Roadways may cross or divide the Median. Ground-level Roadways require
ordinary Crossing; elevated roads strengthen Staging Post vistas and Node MEET
backplates while remaining decorative; and a below-grade road may become an
impassable interior boundary. Ramps, barriers, embankments, pillars, drains,
divided grades, debris, vegetation, altered water, and animal paths create
several connected grounds. This shared geometry does not merge the two
Landmark Reaches. Ancestral Home applies the reusable Interchange travel
grammar to its own authored history.

Within the territory, direct-drive TRAVEL replaces the opening's guided DWELL.
The generated old civic plan remains partially readable through persistent
landmarks, but destruction and interchange construction have broken its former
paths and relationships:

1. **Approach ground** — the first distant confirmation that this is the lost
   place: recognizable vegetation, grade, human infrastructure, and one
   surviving Colony landmark.
2. **The destruction edge** — displaced earth, cut growth, broken shelter,
   drainage work, or machinery trace establishes how the catastrophe entered
   the Colony.
3. **Former civic ground** — several recognizable Places or paths survive only
   as material relationships. They are Nodes now, not selectable Home
   Practices.
4. **The interior Roadways** — one or more ground-level Roadway crossings and
   overhead or otherwise non-crossed roads divide the territory and make
   movement through the Reach an expedition problem in its own right.
5. **The deepest wreck** — the most altered and hazardous ground, where
   physical recovery may be possible but movement and visibility are worst.
6. **The far remainder** — regrowth, drainage, debris, abundant Resources, or
   new animal use shows that the territory has continued after the Colony
   ended.

These are navigational functions rather than mandatory named zones. The exact
topology is derived from the intact generated Colony shown in that campaign's
opening, then transformed by its specific destruction through the shared
Landmark-Reach roadway grammar; it is not selected from one of three fixed ruin maps or designed
backward from a standard dungeon plan.

## Specialist Node set

The territory combines three sources of persistent Node content:

- generated remains derived from the actual Places, paths, objects, and civic
  relationships established in the opening Colony; and
- authored story Nodes whose roles are filled by compatible generated
  particulars in this campaign; and
- an elevated present-tense set of programmatic Sustenance and Scrap Nodes,
  together with shelter, inhabitants, and hazards supported by the current
  physical ground.

Landmark Reach resource abundance remains separate from contest and story
content. The Ancestral Home assigns Contested state to a much lower percentage
of its programmatic Resource Nodes than an ordinary Reach or standard
Interchange. Its generated remains and authored story functions remain the
territory's defining content despite the abundant present-tense Resources.

The Reach is generated as a coherent place and persists. It does not roll a
fresh field of unrelated generic Nodes whenever the party enters. The
following are authored functions rather than fixed names or guaranteed
one-to-one placements.

### 1. The first recognition

A landmark visible during the opening survives strongly enough to establish
continuity before any label does. Reaching it can remain TRAVEL, open quiet
observation, or frame a Remembrance MEET when an eligible Citizen is present.

The Sourcebook may demonstrate this function through Elderbank's lesson ground
and **The Place Where the Lesson Was**. That is an illustrative Rabbit history,
not a Place or Citizen encounter installed in the game.

### 2. A former Practice or Place

One old civic function remains materially legible: Stores, Gathering Place,
Workshop, Hearth, Watchpost, Garden, or Residence according to the selected
Colony. It no longer functions as a Practice. Its present condition may support
recognition, shelter, careful recovery, competing claims, or evidence of later
animal occupation.

The game should resist making every former Place recoverable. One or two
legible civic remains carry more force than a complete checklist of ruins.

### 3. The catastrophe scar

This Node makes the destructive event physically specific. It can involve cut
roots, compacted earth, torn structure, drainage change, buried material,
machine residue, or a new exposure to Roadway weather. Its MEET substance is
immediate condition—instability, moving water, renewed human work, a trapped
object, or another animal's use—not a replay of the opening catastrophe.

### 4. The sheltered remainder

A small part of the former Home or adjacent ground still offers cover. It may
be occupied, contested, or empty. This is the credible locus for traces of
survival and for the low-frequency lone-escapee encounter, but the place never
advertises that possibility before it occurs.

### 5. The changed living place

Regrowth or later inhabitants have made one portion of the territory ecologically
active. This Node prevents the Ancestral Home from becoming frozen memorial
scenery. It may support Sustenance, another species' claim, predation, or a
relationship whose relevance belongs wholly to the present.

## MEET substance

The Ancestral Home supports present-tense contextual MEETs as well as its
signature Remembrance material. Its distinguishing quality comes from the
collision between current conditions and the generated old Colony—not from a
special response matrix.

### Remembrance

A Remembrance MEET becomes eligible when all three conditions hold:

- the party includes a Founder or authored Wanderer escapee with lived history
  at the exact place;
- the present location or event materially touches that established history;
  and
- more than one meaningful response to the present situation exists.

The MEET may allow attending to what survives, searching a bounded site,
speaking or withholding a known memory, recovering an eligible particular
object, leaving the ground undisturbed, yielding a claim, or withdrawing. Its
responses come from the actual place. **Remember** should not become a
universal sixth response family or a glowing interaction attached to every
ruin.

An attentive player may understand why the situation matters, but the game
does not announce an emotional outcome. Remembrance records what the Citizens
did there; it does not certify one account, reveal an objective complete past,
or impose grief and closure.

### Present-tense situations

Other MEETs may arise from:

- unstable or newly exposed ground;
- runoff, flooding, heat, cold, traffic weather, or River Spume acting upon
  damaged shelter;
- another animal's current territory or recovered possession;
- predators using broken sightlines and cover;
- a particular recoverable object whose existence is already authored;
- evidence of a later visitor or survivor;
- current human inspection, maintenance, clearing, or renewed Road Work; or
- disagreement within the party over a materially consequential action.

The last category remains a collective situational choice, not an autonomous
Citizen veto. Animal voice can make competing meanings legible while the
player retains authority over the response.

## Lone escapee

The lone escapee is a shocking low-frequency possibility, not the territory's
prize or a secret population to be harvested. The encounter becomes possible
only after the present scene supplies credible evidence that someone has
survived here or returned here. It receives no map icon, silhouette, rumor
counter, probability, completion condition, or repeated search action.

If selected for a campaign, the escapee is one authored individual:

- their survival has a material explanation tied to the wreck;
- they possess their own relationship to the lost Colony and do not simply
  verify the Founders' account;
- recognition may be difficult, mutual, partial, or delayed;
- meeting them does not guarantee immediate adoption or return;
- any later Citizenship follows ordinary relationship and Home processes; and
- once the encounter resolves, the possibility is spent for that campaign.

The encounter can deepen uncertainty as readily as answer it. One survivor's
memory is another particular account, not recovered omniscience.

## Recovery without looting the past

Material recovery belongs here, but should remain bounded and particular.
Ordinary Resources may be recovered where the present physical state supports
them. A Tool, Keepsake, or other singular object appears only when authored as
part of a known life or place. The game does not generate sentimental relics
merely because the party searches ruins.

The most valuable returns can be non-fungible without becoming a Knowledge
currency:

- a changed understanding between Citizens;
- a particular object returned to a particular life;
- confirmation that a Place or Citizen survived for some time;
- a new relationship with a present inhabitant;
- a practical observation relevant to one Home improvement; or
- one resolved event eligible for Chronicle or Tale selection.

The territory never offers a completion percentage. Material removal changes
the actual Node and persists. Later visits encounter the amended place rather
than a replenished ruin container.

## Persistence across visits

The Ancestral Home should visibly continue between expeditions:

- recovered material remains absent;
- disturbed, cleared, flooded, or collapsed ground retains its new state;
- seasonal growth and weather alter visibility and access;
- present inhabitants remember applicable encounters;
- human activity can further change or erase remains;
- authored objects remain where they were left or move only through a resolved
  cause; and
- Remembrance events enter Campaign Memory without turning their sites into
  exhausted quest markers.

Returning with different Citizens changes the meaning of the visit without
resetting its facts. A Founder remembers. A later-born Citizen inherits. A
Guest witnesses. A Wanderer escapee may remember another portion or version.
None borrows another's memory merely by joining the party.

## Stopover, Outposts, and repeated access

A Stopover anywhere within the Ancestral Home is uninterrupted. This removes
only the regular-Field chance that sleep is interrupted by a situated MEET: it
does not make the territory a safe center or grant automatic Exposure relief
or Cargo transfer. The locked Stopover screen still depicts the party's actual
ground, shelter, weather, and bodily state. A natural refuge may improve those
conditions where the physical ground supports one. An Outpost remains possible
only at an ordinarily eligible known site and through the normal remote Builder
Project; it should sit near or at the edge of the ruined territory rather than
converting the old Colony's civic center into a managed annex. A usable Outpost
adds its ordinary Stopover effects, including bounded Exposure relief and the
option to drain fungible Cargo from Carry into Colony Stores.

This is an art-direction and content preference, not a new prohibition. The
existing geography and Outpost eligibility rules remain authoritative.

Repeated visits need present reasons: a changed Node, relationship, particular
recovery, seasonal access, human activity, or a different eligible witness.
The player is never asked to sweep the territory until all content has fired.

## Species-shaped generation and Sourcebook illustration

### Rabbit

A generated Rabbit Ancestral Home should preserve ground-level civic
relationships: courts of cover, runs between Places, former gathering or
learning ground, stored material, and escape sightlines. Destruction reads as
violent breach and displacement. Rabbit animal voice gathers competing
fragments into story.

Elderbank, Bramble, Shadepatch, Twig, Nutmeg, and the later-born Citizens are
the Sourcebook's illustrative realization of these possibilities only.

### Squirrel

A generated Squirrel Ancestral Home is remembered through vertical
connection and progressive erasure. Surviving anchors, cut trunks, severed
spans, altered canopy, and grounded material make absence spatially legible.
Squirrel animal voice reaches among incomplete lines, compares what still
connects, and disputes what a restored relation would mean. Navigation should
repeatedly confront the party with former height collapsed into ground without
turning the zone into a platforming exception.

Nine Beeches and its named Citizens are the Sourcebook's illustrative
realization only.

### Mouse

A generated Mouse Ancestral Home should make population and joined
adjacency painfully specific. Many small rooms, passages, thresholds, and
records of ordinary work can survive in partial clusters while the larger
civic whole has vanished. Mouse animal voice joins evidence, counts what can
still be named, and notices gaps that remain unfillable. Density must never
reduce lost Mice to anonymous mass; every named recollection remains one
complete Citizen even where the opening could not show every life.

Chaff End and its named Citizens are the Sourcebook's illustrative realization
only.

## Audiovisual identity

The Ancestral Home is presented in the ordinary material world. It
receives no sepia layer, spectral figures, flashback filter, mournful status
effect, or music cue that tells the player what to feel.

Its identity comes from:

- visual rhyme between intact opening views and altered return views;
- persistent landmarks seen from compatible camera logic;
- ordinary Roadway sound continuing beside exceptional personal history;
- broken shelter changing wind, water, light, and River Spume;
- long quiet intervals interrupted by current animal or human activity;
- species-specific remnants of former civic form; and
- its present breadth, River Spume, internal Roadway divisions, and animal
  traffic; and
- Landscape Voice describing present terrain and simple events only.

Animal speech, Chronicle, Tale, and Remembrance may carry the past. Landscape
Voice does not remember the generated Ancestral Home for them. A Sourcebook
example may name Elderbank, Nine Beeches, or Chaff End only as part of its
clearly illustrative continuity.

## Campaign timing and function

No Tier unlocks the Ancestral Home. Its position is physically
established by the opening, while practical access emerges from the Colony's
ability to bear distance, ordinary Crossings, the Ancestral Reach's internal
Roadways, Stores demand, party subtraction, Stopover needs, and return.
The horizon should usually become plausible after the player has learned
ordinary expeditions well enough for the return to feel chosen rather than
tutorial-directed.

Nothing requires immediate return once it becomes practical. Going early can
mean a strained expedition with little capacity for careful recovery. Going
later can mean stronger preparation but further environmental change and more
Citizens whose relationship to the place is inherited rather than lived. The
game need not label this tradeoff; the continuous world and campaign history
produce it.

The horizon's campaign function is not closure. It can change the new Colony's
memory, relationships, objects, practical knowledge, and sense of itself while
leaving the old society lost and the Corridor open.

## Bounded content package

A credible v0.5 concept treatment can describe the horizon without pretending
to author an unlimited ruin simulation. Each generated version
should imply:

- one complete Landmark Reach derived from its intact opening Colony and
  transformed through the shared multi-Roadway TRAVEL grammar;
- one or more internal ground-level Crossing hazards and additional overhead
  or illustrative Roadways fitted by the Interchange design pass;
- four to six persistent specialist Nodes, including first recognition,
  former civic ground, catastrophe scar, shelter, and changed living ground;
- a small set of present-condition MEETs reusable only where their concrete
  facts fit;
- several species- and history-specific Remembrance situations;
- one optional lone-escapee encounter path;
- one matched intact/ruined visual continuity study; and
- enough persistent amendments to make a second visit visibly different.

This is a content-shape target, not a production quota or a new mechanical
table.

## Proposed decisions for adoption

1. Every campaign generates its own ancestral Colony name, Founders, Places,
   civic arrangement, material particulars, and history. The selected Core
   Species shapes those results without selecting one fixed lost society.
2. Elderbank, Nine Beeches, Chaff End, their named Citizens, and their Places
   are Sourcebook illustrations only and never fixed game content.
3. Ancestral Home and Interchange are separate Landmark Reach types. The
   Ancestral Home is generated once at the position established by the opening;
   Interchanges may generate elsewhere in the Corridor.
4. It is navigated through ordinary TRAVEL and persistent Nodes; the former
   Colony supplies no DWELL functions.
5. Its physical TRAVEL layout uses the Interchange's multi-Roadway grammar,
   including ground-level Crossing hazards and overhead or illustrative roads,
   without sharing the Interchange's Node-generation emphasis.
6. Its Node slate emphasizes generated remains and authored story-Node
   functions bound to the campaign's actual old Colony. Current Resources and
   encounters remain subordinate to that identity.
7. Physical recognition precedes labels. Each version preserves a small number
   of strong civic landmarks rather than a complete ruin checklist.
8. Remembrance is a contextual MEET enabled by an eligible Citizen and exact
   present circumstance, not a universal response or interaction marker.
9. The lone escapee is one low-frequency, once-per-campaign authored
   possibility with no advance legibility and no guaranteed adoption.
10. Recovery is bounded by actual established material. Singular sentimental
   objects are never procedurally invented as loot.
11. Every visit persistently amends the same territory; there is no completion
   score, content reset, restoration project, or path to making it a second
   Home.
12. Rabbit, Squirrel, and Mouse versions share campaign function while their
   physical ruins and memory voices remain species-specific.
13. Landscape Voice describes only the present terrain. The past belongs to
   particular animal voices and Campaign Memory.
14. Practical access emerges from continuous-world expedition capacity rather
   than Tier, quest, or Special Journey gating.

## Questions left deliberately open

- Which minimum opening facts must be retained so generation can later produce
  convincing recognizable remains without fixing the old Colony's layout?
- Which Interchange geometries can the Ancestral Home reuse while preserving a
  traversable, recognizable old Colony?
- How are authored story-Node functions bound to generated Places, objects,
  and histories without making every campaign follow one ruin sequence?
- Is the lone escapee generated with the opening and merely unrevealed, or
  generated only if the campaign selects the encounter?
- Which present-tense Node first makes each return more than remembrance?
- How much of the territory's change is seasonal simulation and how much is
  authored event amendment?
- Can one Home Practice improvement be learned here in each Species campaign,
  or should practical knowledge remain possible but not guaranteed?

These questions affect content and emphasis but do not require another
mechanical subsystem.

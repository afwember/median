## ⚠ Four items that conflict with existing canon

Flagged first because they are reversals rather than additions, and two of them reverse decisions made yesterday.

**1. §18.1 — an Ameliorated Maiming granting a compensating boost.** Canon rejects this explicitly and at length: *"The game must never imply that disability automatically grants genius or supernatural compensation."* Appendix B lists the v0.1 line pairing every maiming with a compensating trait as **superseded on record**. The note proposes bringing it back. **This one I would push back on — see the discussion group.**

**2. §33.1 — backing off the no-clothes rule, permitting staffs, cloaks and pouches.** This reverses **Decision 86**, made yesterday, and the Three Species plate and the house-style block were both built on it. Not a contradiction — the author may revise — but the cost is real and worth counting before committing.

**3. §20.4 — removing the Guest clothing distinction entirely.** Reverses **Decision 93**, also made yesterday. Consistent with note 2, so these two move together.

**4. §7.4, §8.1, §9.3 — the Edge Effect challenged three times.** §9.3 calls it *"the world's organizing ecological principle."* The objection is that plant richness would really be highest in sheltered interiors, and that the sound-wall side should be rich too. **I think there is a version of the Edge Effect that survives this — see the discussion group.**

## Group A — The numbers *(do first; everything downstream depends on it)*

- **GENERAL — expedition party size.** Pictured as 3 citizens but never quantified. Foundational.
- **§13 — the carry scaling is broken.** *1 weight = 1 slot* was calibrated before stats gained an exponent. A squirrel expedition currently has 60 carry slots. Needs a 10× pass, or normal items need to weigh more than 1.
- **§31.1 — advancement resource costs need the same 10× jump**, and should be genuinely expensive.
- **§9.0 — a global expedition cap?** Low population may make it automatic.
- **§10.1 — one Nesting Season or two.** Wants a full population-through-tiers pass.

## Group B — The encounter *(second; it is the most-touched system)*

- **§25.1 — eliminate the 1-round-0-choice encounter.** Every encounter carries a player choice, with one exception: a chance of instant resolution for *uncontested resource* nodes only.
- **§25.1 — may the player change Approach at the choice moment?** Negotiate after fighting.
- **§18.2 — tharn is a bombshell**, narratively centred the instant it fires.
- **§18.2 — tharn only occurs where rescue is possible.** Never on a final round, regardless of exposure.
- **§18.2 — the choice is *who* rescues, not *whether*.** Losing a citizen to untended tharn should not happen under normal play.
- **§18.2 — tharn needs a citizen-level focus inside the encounter cycle**, which the group-layer model does not currently provide.
- **§18.2 — smaller lasting repercussions**, e.g. a penalty next time that citizen meets the same species.
- **§25.3 — adversary numbers may be *depicted* freely** for compelling imagery; they remain gameplay-irrelevant.
- **§25.8 — carry capacity is the natural expedition-ender**, which addresses the node-to-node creep this section warns about.
- **§23.1 — nodes after the first get harder, tied to carry load.** A weighed-down party is less agile.
- **§26.1 — antagonist/non-antagonist ratio varies with conditions.**
- **§26.1 — Guest names known in encounters before recruitment.**
- **§18.3 — a Distinction reflects the character of the encounter that earned it.**

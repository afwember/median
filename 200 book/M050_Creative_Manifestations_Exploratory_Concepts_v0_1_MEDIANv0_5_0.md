# MEDIAN Creative Manifestations

## Exploratory concepts for games in other forms

**Document version:** 0.1

**Discussion basis:** MEDIAN v0.5.0 authorial development

**Status:** Noncanonical creative exploration held outside the active GDD

**Authority:** This document records speculative original game concepts. It is
not part of the MEDIAN v0.5.0 Game Design Document, does not amend Appendix J,
and creates no production commitment.

## Purpose and boundary

These concepts began with a different question from the translation work in
the MEDIAN v0.5.0 Concept Sourcebook. Rather than asking how the primary game’s
rules could be faithfully expressed in another medium, they ask:

> What distinct game could be rooted in MEDIAN’s world and themes while being
> designed natively for another form?

Each concept therefore retains the setting of animal Colonies living on median
strips, the contrast between sanctuary and the surrounding human world, named
and particular lives, credible consequence, return, accumulated history, and
an attachment-forward pace. None attempts to reproduce the full v0.5.0 rule
set.

The desktop and console game is intentionally absent. It remains the primary
game described by the Concept Sourcebook rather than a candidate for this
speculative exercise.

The four creative Manifestations are:

1. **Tabletop role-playing game — _MEDIAN: Things Carried Home_**
2. **Spatial board game — _MEDIAN: The Narrow Country_**
3. **Cooperative fixed-content card game — _MEDIAN: What Passes, What
   Remains_**
4. **Standalone mobile game — _MEDIAN: One Small Place_**

The titles are working titles. The concepts may be retained, transformed, or
set aside independently.

## 1. Tabletop role-playing game — *MEDIAN: Things Carried Home*

### Pitch

A spoken Colony chronicle about the lives animals build between dangerous
journeys.

Three to five players each portray one named Citizen. A Guide serves as the
Landscape Voice: describing the country, weather, traffic, passing time, and
simple events while portraying Guests, strangers, predators, and neighboring
communities. The Guide is the world’s voice rather than an adversary.

Every substantial story asks:

> **What is worth leaving Home for—and what will this journey bring back?**

The game follows a small Colony across seasons rather than sending an
adventuring party through unrelated scenarios. Players gradually learn its
routines, improve familiar Places, welcome new lives, and acquire obligations
beyond the median.

Some sessions remain entirely at Home. Others begin with an absence,
curiosity, material need, neighboring animal, or change in the landscape that
draws particular Citizens outward.

### The table as a remembered place

The group begins with a long, mostly blank illustrated sheet representing one
Median Reach. Two Roadways bound it. Players draw, label, or place cards for:

- Home and its accumulating Places;
- familiar paths and crossings;
- discovered Nodes and shelters;
- neighboring animals; and
- sites of injury, friendship, loss, or return.

The map becomes densely particular through play. Marks are dated and seldom
erased. A repaired culvert, a fox’s den, a dangerous drainage grate, and the
place where a Citizen was found all remain present after their immediate
stories end.

Character sheets and the shared map together form the campaign’s physical
memory.

### Citizens rather than classes

Each Citizen begins with three concise truths:

- **What my body knows**
- **What I contribute at Home**
- **What has become particular to me**

The third truth might name a friendship, Place, object, remembered event,
recurring fear, or responsibility. It is neither an attachment score nor an
automatic source of bonuses. It tells the table what kinds of consequences
would matter to this life.

New abilities arise from events in play: something learned from a Guest, a
practiced craft, a bodily adaptation, a hard-earned understanding of the
Roadway, or a responsibility accepted within Home.

### Native resolution

When an outcome is uncertain, a player gathers dice by explaining which
established truths materially help:

- the Citizen’s body;
- something they genuinely know how to do;
- a particular carried object; and
- another present Citizen’s direct help.

The roll answers two separate questions:

1. Does the intended action succeed?
2. What becomes exposed because it was attempted?

Success and safety are not the same result. A Citizen may secure the seeds
while losing the basket, reach the far Margin while becoming Wounded, persuade
a stranger while accepting an obligation, or finish a shelter while damaging
a relationship.

Consequences become named facts rather than depleted hit points. They attach
to bodies, objects, relationships, Places, or the Colony itself and remain
until the fiction credibly changes them.

### Spoken encounters

Important situations are played as full conversations. The Guide makes the
circumstances and stakes clear; players speak and act as their Citizens.

Different approaches remain genuinely available:

- contesting another animal;
- speaking and seeking understanding;
- exchanging something of value;
- yielding the immediate claim; and
- leaving before the situation worsens.

The game does not require every scene to become negotiation or conflict.
Sometimes the important choice is who steps forward, who remains silent, what
is offered, or whether the party decides the matter is worth continuing.

### Crossing as a table ritual

A Crossing changes the physical and vocal behavior of the table.

The Guide describes traffic in short, concrete observations. Players may ask
what their Citizens can perceive, arrange their order, and state the signals
they will follow. Once the first animal enters the Roadway, discussion becomes
limited to brief in-world calls.

Resolution proceeds animal by animal. Players cannot revise the whole plan
with shared hindsight after seeing how the first movement resolves.

The ritual should be quick, quiet, and tense. It uses silence and incomplete
information—qualities a spoken game can make unusually powerful—without
becoming a miniature traffic simulator.

### Ordinary life as play

Every session opens or closes with at least one ordinary-life scene chosen by
the players:

- eating together;
- repairing something familiar;
- teaching Young;
- showing a Guest around Home;
- telling a story badly;
- revisiting a place after an absence; or
- sitting quietly while traffic changes with the evening.

These scenes do not exist merely to recover resources. They establish the life
for which danger matters.

A player whose Citizen is absent, resting, or dead can portray another Colony
Citizen during these scenes. Over time the table becomes capable of speaking
for a recognizable community without turning every inhabitant into a complete
player character.

### Species and campaign character

Each campaign chooses one Core Species for its Colony.

- A **Mouse** campaign emphasizes adjacency, close cooperation, crowded joined
  spaces, and many small lives participating in common work.
- A **Rabbit** campaign emphasizes open common ground, longer journeys,
  visible collective life, and the social importance of gathering.
- A **Squirrel** campaign emphasizes elevation, connection, distant movement,
  stored objects, and a Home understood as a network.

Species changes the framing of scenes and the development of the shared map
rather than acting as a package of numerical modifiers.

Guests complicate those habits. Their bodies, perceptions, customs, and
singular capabilities create new possibilities while leaving the Colony
recognizably rooted in its Core Species.

### Homecoming and memory

At the end of a consequential return, the group performs a brief Homecoming:

1. Say who returned.
2. Place what was carried Home on the table.
3. Name what changed in a Citizen.
4. Alter the shared map if Home or the known world changed.
5. Record one sentence that the Colony will remember.

Only established events enter the Chronicle. Players may interpret their
meaning differently, and those interpretations may change later.

A Citizen’s death does not remove their sheet from the play materials. It
moves into the Colony record, where later characters may inherit an object,
repeat a saying, misunderstand a story, or live in something that Citizen
helped build.

### Campaign form

The game supports an eight-to-twelve-session named season, with campaigns
continuing across several seasons if desired. A season ends when the Colony
gathers to decide what it has become and what it now hopes to sustain.

There is no level ladder and no mandatory final crisis. The campaign succeeds
when the table can point to Places and objects on the shared map and remember
particular lives without consulting its notes.

> **The adventure is important because someone must carry it Home.**

### Form-native thesis

The tabletop role-playing form makes spoken interpretation, changing
relationships, authored voice, silence, and shared memory central. A dedicated
Guide acting as Landscape Voice gives the country a consistent narrator while
leaving the other players free to remain attached to continuing Citizens.

## 2. Spatial board game — *MEDIAN: The Narrow Country*

### Pitch

A cooperative campaign game played on a long strip of living ground physically
confined between two moving Roads.

One to four players build and remember a single animal Colony across eight to
twelve sessions. The game’s central object is not an efficiency tableau. It is
the increasingly crowded, damaged, repaired, and familiar place assembled in
the middle of the table.

### The board

The play surface is unusually long and narrow:

> **Margin | Roadway | Median and Colony | Roadway | Margin**

Two traffic tracks run along its outer edges. Vehicle pieces move independently
around them, creating sound, wake, danger, dropped material, temporary shelter,
and changing opportunities.

The median begins as mostly open terrain. Players construct Home directly upon
it with physical Place pieces, pathways, gardens, shelters, found objects, and
species-specific structures. By the campaign’s end, the board should look
unmistakably like the history of one Colony rather than a solved arrangement.

> **The Roads keep moving. The Colony has to make a life in the space left
> between them.**

### Traffic as the clock

After every completed Citizen action, something moves on one of the Roadways.
Players therefore feel human activity surrounding Home even during peaceful
turns.

The Roads are neither a conventional enemy nor a random damage generator.
Their movement changes the local world:

- a truck’s wake bends exposed plants;
- stopped traffic temporarily quiets one lane;
- road work introduces unfamiliar material and sustained disturbance;
- rain sends water and debris toward lower ground;
- litter or useful scrap appears along a Margin;
- a vehicle temporarily blocks another driver’s view; and
- something thrown from a window lands dangerously close to Home.

Players gradually learn patterns without ever controlling them.

### Named Citizens as playable pieces

Every Citizen has:

- a portrait standee or shaped wooden figure;
- a name;
- one bodily distinction;
- one familiar activity;
- one relationship or remembered association; and
- a small personal board that physically acquires consequences.

Players do not permanently own individual Citizens. Any player may move or
speak for any available Citizen, although familiarity may form naturally
between particular players and animals.

Citizens normally stand within a panoramic Home row beside the board. Moving a
Citizen into the Field removes them from that panorama, leaving their
illustrated space visibly empty. Absence is felt before any mechanical effect
is counted.

### One meaningful action

Each Citizen ordinarily performs one meaningful action in a round:

- carry something;
- work on a physical addition to Home;
- tend a living space;
- visit or assist another Citizen;
- investigate a change;
- receive a stranger;
- travel toward a Margin;
- attempt a Crossing; or
- spend time with something personally important.

Actions are concrete and spatial. Material must be physically moved. Citizens
must reach one another. A Place helps because of where it stands and how
animals can use it, rather than by adding an abstract icon to a total.

The cooperative problem arises from limited time and actual geography. The
players cannot accomplish every desirable thing before the season changes.

### A historically specific Home

Construction uses layered pieces. A shelter might begin as a foundation tile,
acquire walls during a later round, and eventually receive a roof made from a
particular recovered object.

The finished piece records its provenance through small fitted markers:

- who began it;
- what unusual material it incorporates;
- which event damaged it;
- who repaired or altered it; and
- what later memory became attached to it.

These markers usually provide little or no numerical benefit. Their primary
function is to prevent the board from forgetting why one Workshop, bridge,
room, or gathering ground matters.

### Species as physical construction

The three Core Species use genuinely different construction sets.

#### Mouse — a Manor House

Mouse Home grows through compact pieces joined directly together. Rooms share
edges, passages open between structures, and useful adjacency creates
increasingly dense interiors. Individual Mouse figures are small and numerous,
making cooperation powerful while creating painful empty spaces when several
leave together.

#### Rabbit — a Cul-de-Sac

Rabbit construction gathers around open common ground. Buildings and shelters
gain meaning from the shared space preserved between them. Filling every
available space is actively undesirable: the Colony needs room to gather, see
one another, and move rapidly across Home.

#### Squirrel — a Web

Squirrel Home uses raised supports, bridges, and connective pieces. Its Colony
becomes a physical network spanning several heights. A new Place matters partly
because of the lines it opens or interrupts. Weather and damage can alter the
Web without simply removing whole structures.

One terrain frame can therefore support three radically different construction
kits. Choosing a species changes the board’s spatial problem rather than
adjusting a few values.

### Crossing as a board event

A Crossing uses the traffic pieces already circling Home.

A Citizen enters the first Roadway from their present location. Each subsequent
action advances the animal, advances traffic, or helps create an opportunity
from the roadside. Because traffic moves after actions, every player can see
some immediate danger while remaining uncertain about what enters the Road
next.

The table must decide whether to:

- continue;
- wait and accept lost time;
- send another Citizen to assist;
- abandon something being carried;
- retreat; or
- use a species-specific movement possibility.

Consequences ordinarily leave the Citizen on one side or the other with a
Wound, lost object, separation, or altered opportunity. Death remains possible
in explicitly lethal circumstances, but Crossing is not a routine pawn-removal
mechanism.

The result feels like a physical crossing because the named piece must actually
traverse the Roads surrounding its Home.

### Possibilities rather than victory points

Each session introduces several Possibilities:

- welcome an unfamiliar animal;
- establish a new living space;
- retrieve something visible beyond the Road;
- improve a threatened part of Home;
- answer a neighboring Colony;
- investigate a seasonal change; or
- spend time on an ordinary communal undertaking.

The Colony cannot pursue them all. Unchosen Possibilities may disappear,
return differently, or continue without the players.

The session succeeds if Home remains viable and the Colony meaningfully
answers at least one Possibility. There is no reward for maximizing the number
completed. What the players choose gradually determines what kind of Colony
occupies the board.

### Quiet rounds

Some rounds are explicitly quiet. Traffic still moves and ordinary work
remains available, but no crisis or scenario demand arrives.

During a quiet round, players may open a scene attached to a Citizen,
relationship, object, or Place. These scenes can reveal a preference,
establish a routine, begin a friendship, or give new meaning to something
already on the board.

They are not optimization actions disguised as character moments. Taking one
may mean relinquishing useful labor. The campaign gives players enough safety
to make that choice without treating it as incorrect play.

### Persistent consequence

Wounds, altered movement, learned capabilities, important objects, and changed
relationships use transparent overlays, fitted markers, and entries in a
Colony folio. Earlier states remain recoverable without requiring players to
destroy cards.

When a Citizen dies, their piece moves into the campaign folio rather than
returning to the box. Their former position in the Home panorama may later hold
a memorial, inherited object, young relative, or intentional emptiness.

The board continues to contain what they built.

### Seasonal campaign

Each session represents a meaningful interval within one season. Between
sessions:

- weather and vegetation change;
- traffic patterns develop;
- young animals grow;
- unfinished structures remain unfinished;
- Guests reconsider their relationship to Home;
- consequences persist; and
- established Places generate new possibilities.

At the end of each season, the players photograph the Colony through a supplied
frame. These images become its visual campaign archive.

A full campaign concludes after enough seasons for the Colony to feel
established. Players may close its Chronicle, continue with another year, or
begin a later campaign on the same Corridor with the original Colony preserved
as a neighboring place.

### Form-native thesis

The spatial board makes constrained ground, moving traffic, physical absence,
species geometry, construction provenance, and shared pointing immediate.
Someone should be able to indicate a patched shelter and say:

> **That is where Juniper lived after the flood. We built it from the sign
> Bramble brought across the west Road.**

A reusable base game, removable overlays, a Colony folio, and photographed
seasonal states would allow a materially particular Home without requiring
irreversible stickers or limiting the box to one campaign.

## 3. Cooperative fixed-content card game — *MEDIAN: What Passes, What Remains*

### Pitch

A cooperative campaign card game in which every Citizen changes the deck
simply by being present.

The Colony is not represented by a miniature map. It exists as a changing
shared deck assembled from its Citizens, relationships, familiar Places,
possessions, season, and accumulated history.

When someone leaves Home, their cards leave the Colony deck with them. When a
Guest stays, new cards enter its ordinary life. When someone dies, their active
cards disappear—but some of their objects, sayings, relationships, and
remembered acts remain.

> **The cards drawn are the lives currently making up Home.**

### Citizen packets

A Citizen is a small packet rather than a single worker card:

- a portrait and identity card;
- several characteristic action or observation cards;
- one ordinary-life card; and
- space for acquired objects, relationships, consequences, and Tale cards.

A six-Citizen Colony therefore feels like six recognizable lives mixed
together. Players come to recognize a Citizen before reading the name because
that animal’s familiar images, phrases, habits, and possibilities keep entering
their hands.

### Building the Colony deck

At the beginning of a campaign, the shared deck contains:

- the founding Citizens’ packets;
- a few basic Home cards;
- the current season;
- the immediate Median environment; and
- a small number of unresolved circumstances.

The deck changes only when the fiction changes.

A Garden adds living cards that recur with season and weather. A shared shelter
adds refuge and ordinary-life cards. A friendship creates a card that enters
the deck only while both Citizens are present. A recurring Guest may add one
card before becoming a resident and contributing their complete packet.

Growth makes the deck richer, stranger, and less predictable. More cards are
not automatically better.

### Playing a Day

Each round represents one meaningful Day. Players draw individual hands from
the shared Colony deck and reveal a small row of circumstances from the two
Road decks surrounding it.

Cards may generally be used in three ways:

1. **Attend** — use the card to answer something happening now.
2. **Keep** — commit it beneath a Citizen, object, or part of Home so it becomes
   persistent.
3. **Let Pass** — allow its immediate possibility to leave or develop without
   intervention.

Players cannot attend everything. A ripening patch, an arriving stranger,
damaged shelter, two Citizens seeking time together, and an unusual quiet in
traffic may all appear during the same Day.

Letting something pass is not automatically failure. It defines what this
Colony noticed, valued, and had the capacity to answer.

### The two Roads

Two narrow traffic decks run in opposite directions along the top and bottom
of the play area. Cards move through short queues, making some future human
activity visible while leaving later cards unknown.

Traffic cards describe passing effects rather than attacks:

- wake and River Spume;
- sudden shade or glare;
- noise;
- stopped traffic;
- rain thrown from tires;
- road work;
- dropped objects;
- headlights;
- a brief opening; and
- direct danger near the verge.

The combination of the two visible queues creates the current character of the
highway. Players learn to read the Road without ever mastering it.

### Departure changes both decks

When Citizens leave, the players physically remove their packets from the
Colony deck. Those cards combine with carried objects and a small selection of
Field cards to become the Away deck.

This produces two simultaneous truths:

- the expedition possesses the particular bodies, habits, and objects it
  brought; and
- Home can no longer draw upon those Citizens’ familiar cards.

No subtraction formula is required. Their absence is the altered composition
of play.

A Citizen closely associated with tending a particular part of Home may leave
its corresponding cards harder to use. A relationship card disappears when
one of its two Citizens is absent. Another Citizen may temporarily take up an
unfamiliar responsibility, creating a new card if the pattern persists.

### Away as a compressed deck journey

The Away deck moves through a sequence of landscape and traffic cards. The
party is not advancing across a board. It is trying to keep enough of itself
together to reach something, act there, and still form a viable return deck.

Cards played for immediate effect become unavailable until Rest. Cards
abandoned to avoid danger may be lost. Wound or Exposure cards enter the deck
and begin obstructing future hands. A carried Remedy may remove that
obstruction from immediate circulation without erasing what happened.

The central Away tension is attrition of possibility. The party can continue
while its deck becomes narrower, more burdened, and more dependent upon a few
remaining combinations.

### Crossing as opposing card flow

A Crossing occurs when the two Road queues produce a possible opening. The
party plays cards into the space between them over several passes.

Players may commit quickly, wait while useful cards exhaust, or spend precious
capabilities to alter the opportunity. Once the first card enters the Crossing,
it cannot return to the hand unless the whole party withdraws.

Mouse, Rabbit, and Squirrel Crossing decks use different relationships:

- **Mouse** cards work in paired or grouped combinations. Their safety comes
  from coordinated multiplicity, but separation can strand useful halves of a
  combination.
- **Rabbit** cards carry momentum. A committed sequence can cross several
  traffic passes rapidly, but interrupting that sequence is dangerous.
- **Squirrel** cards create links among verge, structure, carried material,
  and elevated opportunity. Their Crossings are constructed as chains rather
  than bursts.

The Road remains the same pair of decks. The animals make different games of
reading it.

### Home is made from cards removed from circulation

A useful card can remain available for future hands or become part of Home.

A sheet of plastic might:

- protect the party during rain;
- be exchanged with another animal;
- cover an unfinished shelter; or
- become a remembered object associated with a rescue.

Committing it beneath Home removes that immediate option from the deck but
makes the Colony more particular. Its visible edge remains exposed, preserving
its name and provenance.

The Colony tableau gradually becomes a layered physical archive of things that
once could have been used differently.

### Relationships between cards

Relationship cards lie between two Citizen portraits rather than belonging
wholly to either one. They enter the Colony deck only while both Citizens are
at Home and able to participate.

Some represent practical familiarity: two animals who work efficiently without
explanation. Others create scenes, tensions, jokes, rituals, shared grief, or
unfinished conversations.

If one Citizen dies or permanently leaves, the relationship card turns over.
Its reverse is a Memory card belonging to the survivor and the Colony. The
relationship stops functioning as an active exchange but does not vanish from
the game.

### Quiet Days

When the Road queues and Colony draw produce no urgent situation, the players
may declare a **Small Day**.

During a Small Day, cards are played to discover ordinary combinations:

- two Citizens sharing a task;
- a Guest joining a familiar routine;
- an object finding a habitual owner;
- a young animal learning a Place; or
- someone returning to a location associated with an earlier event.

A Small Day creates no score and need not improve the deck. It may establish a
new relationship card, descriptive fact, or Chronicle entry if something
genuinely becomes particular.

The game protects enough uneventful time for players to recognize the deck as
a community rather than a machine for answering hazards.

### Consequence enters circulation

Consequences are cards that join a Citizen’s packet:

- Wound;
- altered movement;
- damaged or lost possession;
- obligation;
- separation;
- an unfamiliar responsibility;
- a changed relationship; or
- a remembered Roadway experience.

Because these cards recur in later hands, consequence affects play through
presence rather than through a detached penalty counter.

Recovery may eventually turn a Wound card over, but its reverse remains in the
packet as part of the Citizen’s history. What obstructed one season may later
become knowledge, caution, or a detail in a Tale without becoming a universal
upgrade.

### Fixed content and living combinations

The box contains a complete authored set:

- three Core-Species founding groups;
- Guest packets;
- Citizen and relationship cards;
- Home and Place cards;
- seasonal environments;
- two opposing Road decks;
- Field circumstances;
- consequences; and
- campaign envelopes or indexed additions.

There are no randomized boosters and no collectible rarity. Replay comes from
changing Colony composition, card relationships, Road order, player
priorities, and the different histories attached to the same authored
material.

A Guest card is interesting because of what that life changes, not because it
is rare.

### Campaign Memory

At Homecoming, returning cards are reconciled with the Colony deck one at a
time:

- objects are restored, committed to Home, inherited, or acknowledged as lost;
- consequences enter Citizen packets;
- new relationships are placed;
- Guests determine whether they remain present; and
- one returning event may become a Chronicle card.

The campaign folio records the order in which cards entered or left the Colony.
The tableau shows the current Home; the folio preserves earlier configurations.

When the campaign ends, players can spread out its Citizen packets,
relationships, layered Home, and Chronicle cards and read the Colony’s history
directly from the altered set.

### Form-native thesis

The design uses cards to make social presence and historical change structural:

- a Citizen’s presence changes what the Colony can draw;
- departure physically divides one deck into Home and Away;
- relationships exist as shared cards between particular lives;
- consequence recurs because it enters circulation;
- building Home means giving up a card’s other possible uses;
- death removes active possibilities while leaving material and memory behind;
  and
- fixed authored cards acquire different meanings through combination and
  history.

> **A Colony is the deck made by everyone still living there—and everything
> they have left behind.**

Private hands with unrestricted conversation, but without verbatim reading of
card text, would give each player partial knowledge of the Colony’s present
possibility. Players would describe what they can contribute rather than allow
one person to calculate and direct the entire table.

## 4. Standalone mobile game — *MEDIAN: One Small Place*

### Pitch

An intimate Colony chronicle played through short, deliberate visits to a
living median.

The portrait screen becomes the world’s geography. Home occupies a narrow
vertical strip through its center while traffic moves continuously along both
edges. The player holds the Colony between the Roads in their own hand.

This is a slow game about noticing a particular place often enough for it to
become familiar.

> **Each visit lets the player notice one thing, accompany one life, and leave
> one mark.**

### Voluntary return

A visit lasts roughly five to eight minutes, although the player may remain and
observe for as long as desired. Time advances only when the player deliberately
turns the next Page. Closing the application leaves the Colony safe at its
current moment.

No Citizen becomes hungry because the player was busy. No project finishes
through idle accumulation. No notification summons the player back.

### Home as a vertical living illustration

The main screen opens directly onto Home. The central median contains:

- visible shelters and familiar Places;
- moving, individually recognizable Citizens;
- plants changing with the season;
- objects retained from earlier events;
- traces of damage and repair;
- visiting animals;
- memorials and alterations; and
- ordinary activity.

Traffic at the screen edges supplies continual movement, light, sound, wake,
rain, litter, and disturbance. It makes the narrow safety of the center
perceptible without requiring constant emergencies.

The player may slide through the length of Home, pinch closer, or hold on a
location to listen and watch. The same doorway, gathering ground, Garden,
overlook, or patched roof appears repeatedly under different conditions.

### Invitations rather than tasks

Each Page contains several subtle Invitations. The game does not immediately
list them.

A Citizen may pause near something unfinished. Grass may move where no resident
is visible. Someone may be setting out an extra portion. A familiar object may
be missing. An animal may repeatedly look toward the Road. The soundscape may
reveal something the image does not.

Touching and holding an Invitation opens its moment.

The player may discover several but follow only one before turning the Page:

- help with a part of Home;
- accompany a Citizen;
- welcome or observe a stranger;
- investigate a change;
- spend time within an ordinary routine;
- prepare for something beyond Home; or
- revisit something remembered.

Unchosen Invitations are not failed quests. Some recur, some develop
independently, and some pass. The Colony’s identity emerges partly from what
the player repeatedly chooses to notice.

### One lasting mark

Most visits culminate in one small, persistent change:

- an object is placed;
- material becomes part of a shelter;
- two Citizens establish a routine;
- a Guest becomes familiar;
- someone learns a capability;
- a wound alters movement;
- a Citizen begins using a different Place;
- an absence becomes recognized; or
- a memory attaches to something visible.

The player sees the change rather than receiving a reward summary. A new strip
of fabric remains on the roof. Two animals begin sitting together at dusk. A
returning Citizen favors one leg. An object changes hands.

Many marks provide no mechanical advantage. They make this Colony increasingly
unlike every other one.

### Citizens in the hand

Touching a Citizen opens a compact portrait with three layers:

- **Now** — where they are and what presently occupies them;
- **Familiar** — established habits, relationships, objects, and Places; and
- **Remembered** — selected moments that changed how the Colony knows them.

There are no affection bars. Repeated attention does not compel friendship or
manufacture intimacy through a meter. Familiarity grows because the player
recognizes continuity across appearances.

The player may follow different Citizens on different Pages. The Colony
remains the continuing subject; individual Citizens provide the lives through
which it becomes known.

### Accompanying a Citizen

Some Invitations lead beyond Home. The presentation becomes a sequence of tall
illustrated spaces through which the player moves upward with the Citizen.

A journey is a chain of embodied moments:

- moving beneath wet grass;
- listening beside a Sound Wall;
- waiting under a discarded container;
- encountering another animal;
- deciding whether to continue into unfamiliar ground;
- carrying something awkward; or
- finding a different way back.

The player advances by touch, pause, observation, and occasional directional
choice. The sequence can last several Pages, leaving that Citizen visibly
absent from Home until their return.

### The phone turns when the world does

Home is held in portrait orientation: a narrow sanctuary between two moving
edges.

When a Citizen attempts a Roadway Crossing, the player rotates the phone into
landscape orientation. The Road suddenly fills the screen.

Traffic passes laterally through image, sound, and haptic vibration. The player
watches for movement, feels approaching weight, and uses a small number of
deliberate gestures to move, shelter, wait, or retreat. The Crossing lasts less
than a minute, but the physical reorientation makes it feel like entering a
different and overwhelming world.

After reaching the opposite Margin or returning Home, the player rotates the
phone upright. The median becomes visible again.

Accessible visual, audio, and simplified-control alternatives preserve the
same dramatic transformation without requiring sound, vibration, precise
timing, or physical rotation.

### Species transforms the phone

Each Core Species reorganizes the same portrait device.

#### Mouse — joined detail

Mouse Home becomes a dense sequence of linked interiors and adjacent spaces.
The player moves through it by slipping between close views rather than
surveying broad ground. Several Mice are often visible together, and an
accompanied moment may involve a pair whose small differences gradually become
recognizable.

#### Rabbit — open center

Rabbit Home preserves a broad vertical commons through the center of the
screen. Citizens cross one another’s paths visibly and gather in changing
groups. Away movement covers long stretches through fluid upward gestures,
while Crossing emphasizes decisive bursts.

#### Squirrel — vertical connection

Squirrel Home climbs the screen. Bridges, branches, supports, and hanging
Places create several elevations inside the portrait frame. The player follows
connections upward and downward, with the device naturally becoming a view
into the Web.

Species changes what the player’s thumb learns to do and what kind of space
becomes familiar.

### Quiet Pages

Some Pages contain no consequential Invitation.

The player may watch a meal, follow a Citizen’s ordinary route, listen to rain
under shelter, find animals sleeping in unexpected arrangements, or remain
with the changing sound of evening traffic.

A **Stay** gesture delays the next Page for as long as the player wishes.
Nothing is earned for staying, and nothing is lost by moving on.

Quiet is part of the game’s content rather than an interval between rewards.

### Illustrated memory

Every lasting mark produces a visual amendment to the Colony or a Citizen’s
current image. At the end of a season, the game assembles selected Pages into
an illustrated vertical Chronicle.

The player can scroll backward through:

- earlier forms of Home;
- arrivals and departures;
- recovered objects;
- relationships becoming visible;
- dangerous Crossings;
- ordinary repeated moments;
- seasonal transformations; and
- Citizens who are no longer present.

The Chronicle is generated from established play. The player does not select
flattering highlights or write captions to prove that something mattered.

Earlier images remain available, but ordinary play always opens on the Colony
as it exists now.

### Campaign shape

A complete first campaign might contain sixty to one hundred Pages, experienced
whenever the player chooses. It follows one Colony through a year of
settlement, relationships, departures, Guests, weather, and change.

Completion produces a final current portrait and a readable seasonal
Chronicle. The player may continue into another year, close this Colony’s book,
or begin again with another Core Species.

A five-minute visit remains meaningful because it belongs to a history
accumulated over weeks or months of voluntary return.

### Characteristic visit

The player opens the game after ten days away. No time penalty has accumulated.
Home resumes at a rainy morning Page.

Holding on the Kitchen reveals that its roof is dripping. Holding on a young
Citizen reveals that she is watching a Groundhog Guest preparing to leave.
Holding near the Road reveals a bright piece of waterproof material caught
against the verge.

The player follows the young Citizen and the Groundhog toward it. At the
Roadway, the phone rotates. They wait through the vibration of two passing
trucks, retrieve the material, and return.

Back in portrait view, the player places it over the Kitchen. The Page closes
on the two animals sheltering beneath the newly patched roof while rain
continues along both Roads.

Weeks later, the same blue material remains visible.

### Product ethic

The game is sold as a complete work. Its return rhythm comes from attachment
rather than pressure:

- no streaks;
- no expiring daily rewards;
- no deterioration during absence;
- no push notification claiming that a Citizen needs help;
- no premium currency;
- no randomized purchases; and
- no payments attached to recovery, survival, or affection.

Optional notifications may announce only a reminder deliberately requested by
the player. The Colony never impersonates emotional distress to compel
engagement.

### Form-native thesis

The design uses the device itself as part of MEDIAN’s meaning:

- the portrait screen resembles the long, narrow territory of a median;
- the player physically holds Home between two Roads;
- touch encourages attention to small movement and detail;
- rotation lets the Roadway overwhelm the device during Crossing;
- sound and haptics make human traffic physically present;
- brief Pages support a relationship built through recurring voluntary visits;
  and
- the image archive allows a Colony to remain literally carried in the
  player’s pocket.

> **The Colony waits without demanding. The player returns because it has
> become somewhere worth visiting.**

Time advances only through player-advanced Pages. Real-world light or season
may gently recolor presentation, but absence never advances needs, ends
opportunities, or harms Citizens. This protects the difference between
attachment and coercion.

## Closing note

These four games share MEDIAN’s world, emotional direction, and concern for
particular lives while deliberately rejecting rules fidelity as their measure
of success. Their common question is not whether every primary-game system has
been preserved. It is whether each medium can create its own credible reason
for a player to know a small Home, leave it, return to it, and care that it has
changed.

They remain outside the v0.5.0 GDD unless Asa expressly reopens their status.
